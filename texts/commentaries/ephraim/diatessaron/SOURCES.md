# Ephraim the Syrian — Commentary on the Diatessaron: source provenance

## source/ (public domain — tracked)

**mosinger_1876_latin.txt / .pdf** — *Evangelii concordantis expositio facta a Sancto
Ephraemo doctore Syro*, Latin translation by J. B. Aucher, revised and annotated by
G. Moesinger (Venice: Mechitarist Press, 1876). 316 pp.
Translated from the two Armenian MSS of AD 1195, which preserve the **complete**
commentary. Public domain. This is the base text for translation.
archive.org: `evangelifconcord0000ephr`

**harris_1895_fragments.txt / .pdf** — J. Rendel Harris, *Fragments of the Commentary
of Ephrem Syrus upon the Diatessaron* (London, 1895). Public domain.
Moesinger's Latin excerpts set in parallel with Syriac fragments recovered from
Isho'dad and Bar-Salibi; English appears only as glosses on the Syriac. Use as a
control on the Armenian, not as a base text.
archive.org: `cu31924029334442`

PDFs are gitignored (size); the OCR .txt files are tracked.

## benchmark/ (in copyright — gitignored, do not redistribute)

**mccarthy_1993_english** — Carmel McCarthy, *Saint Ephrem's Commentary on Tatian's

and Notes*, Journal of Semitic Studies Supplement 2 (Oxford University Press, 1993).

The only complete English translation. QC benchmark only — same role Stade plays for

## Notes on the witnesses

- Chester Beatty Syriac MS 709 preserves roughly three quarters of the commentary;
  the Armenian version behind Moesinger preserves the whole.
- Ephraim's lemmata follow Diatessaron harmony order, not the sequence of any single
  Gospel — verse mapping must account for this.
- `texts/diatessaron/` holds ANF09 (Hogg's translation of the Arabic Diatessaron) —
  the harmony text itself, not this commentary on it.

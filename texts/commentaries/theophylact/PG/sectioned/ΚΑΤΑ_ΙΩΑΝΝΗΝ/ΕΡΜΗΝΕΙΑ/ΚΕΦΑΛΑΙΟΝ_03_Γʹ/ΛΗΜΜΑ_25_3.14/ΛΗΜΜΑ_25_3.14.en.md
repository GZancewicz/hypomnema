# John 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 25 · John 3:14

> **And just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up; that everyone who believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life. (John 3:14-15)**

Having first spoken concerning the regeneration through baptism, he now speaks also concerning the benefit that has come to us through the cross. For the cross and the death are for us the cause of the grace that comes through baptism, seeing that, being baptized, we image forth the death of the Lord. But he does not say openly that "I am about to be crucified"; rather he makes mention of the serpent and of the Old *Testament* history (Num. 21:9), at once teaching us that the old things are akin to the new, and that one and the same is the lawgiver of both the Old and the New Covenant—even though Marcion and Manes and the rest of the catalogue of such heretics reject the Old *Covenant*, saying that it is the legislation of the evil creator; and at the same time also instructing us that, if the Jews, looking to a bronze image of a serpent, escaped death, much more do we, looking to the crucified one and believing, escape the death of the soul.

But observe for me the type in relation to the truth. There [was] a likeness of a serpent, having the form of a beast but not having the venom; so here too, the Lord [is] man, but free of the venom of sin, having come in the likeness of sinful flesh—that is, in the likeness of flesh subject to sin, yet not being flesh of sin. And then those who looked [escaped] bodily *death*, whereas we escape the death of the soul; and then the one hanging [there] healed the bites of serpents, but now Christ cures the wounds of the intelligible dragon.[^1]

[^1]: Greek τοῦ νοητοῦ δράκοντος, "the intelligible" (or spiritual, mental) dragon—the devil, contrasted with the perceptible bronze serpent of the type.

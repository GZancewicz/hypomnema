# John 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 9 · John 3:3

> **"Unless someone be born of water," he says, "and of Spirit." (John 3:3)**

For man, being twofold, out of soul and body, has also a twofold manner of the birth. For the water, received visibly, is understood *as being* for the cleansing of the body; but the Spirit, concurring invisibly, for the [regeneration] of the invisible soul.[^1] ...to give birth,[^1] I too will ask: How can the seed, being itself also watery, be formed into a man—a living being[?][^2]? As, therefore, in the case of the bodily seed the whole is of the grace of God, so also in the case of baptism, water is set beneath, but the Spirit works all things, and *so does* the invocation through the prayers—or rather the presence of God. For symbols and an image of burial and resurrection are accomplished in this water. The three immersions are symbols of the three-day burial; then the man rises up again, as *did* the Lord, wearing the brighter and more radiant garment of incorruption, and having sunk corruption down into the water.

[^1]: The OCR here has a lacuna. The legible text runs εἰς τὴν τῆς ἀοράτου ψυχῆς … γεννῆσαι, ἐρωτήσω κἀγώ ("for the … of the invisible soul … to give birth, I too will ask"). A clause is missing between ψυχῆς and γεννῆσαι; on the sense of the passage it supplied something like "for the *regeneration* (ἀναγέννησιν) of the invisible soul. But if you say, How can the water give birth *to a soul*?—I too will ask…". The bracketed "[regeneration]" is a reconstruction, not in the surviving text.
[^2]: OCR corrupt: εἰς ἄυθρωποιι ἄια πλασθῆναι, read εἰς ἄνθρωπον … πλασθῆναι, "to be formed into a man." The intervening word ἄια is unclear; it is plausibly ζῷον ("a living being/creature"), hence the tentative rendering "a man—a living being[?]".

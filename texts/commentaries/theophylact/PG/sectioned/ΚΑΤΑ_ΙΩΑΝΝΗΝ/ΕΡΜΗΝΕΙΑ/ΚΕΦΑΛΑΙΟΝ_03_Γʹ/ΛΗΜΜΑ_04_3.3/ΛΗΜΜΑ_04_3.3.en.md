# John 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 4 · John 3:3

> **Jesus answered, and said to him: Amen, amen I say to you, unless someone be born from above, he cannot see the kingdom of God. (John 3:3)**

The things said by the Savior to Nicodemus seem to have no connection with what Nicodemus had said; but to one who attends closely a great connection will appear. For since Nicodemus had a lowly opinion concerning Christ—that he is a teacher, and that God is with him—the Lord says to him, that: With reason you have such an opinion concerning me. For you have not yet been born from above[^1]—that is, [with] a spiritual birth from God—but you are still fleshly, and the knowledge which you have concerning me is not spiritual, but psychical and human.[^2] But I say to you, that both you, and any other whosoever, unless, having been born from above and from God, you obtain the estimation worthy of me, are outside the kingdom. For the birth through baptism, working illumination in the soul, gives one to see—that is, to understand—the kingdom of God, or rather his only-begotten Son. For just as the Son is the wisdom of God, so indeed he himself may also be called the kingdom of God. And this kingdom no one could see—that is, understand—O Nicodemus, unless he be born of God. So that you also, since you have not yet been born spiritually, for this reason do not see me, the kingdom of God, as one ought, but have a lowly estimation concerning me.

[^1]: ἄνωθεν carries both "from above" and "anew/again"; Theophylact takes it as birth "from God" (ἐκ Θεοῦ), i.e. a spiritual regeneration, while Nicodemus (next lemma) hears it as being born a second time.
[^2]: ψυχική ("psychical," of the soul) is set against πνευματική ("spiritual"): a natural, merely human mode of knowing as opposed to one wrought by the Spirit.

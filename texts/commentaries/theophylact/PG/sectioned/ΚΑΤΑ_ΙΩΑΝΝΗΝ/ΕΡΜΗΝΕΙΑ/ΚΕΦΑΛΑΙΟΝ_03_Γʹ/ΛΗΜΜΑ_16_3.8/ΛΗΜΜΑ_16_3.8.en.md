# John 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 16 · John 3:8

> **Where it wishes it blows, (John 3:8)**

he says — not as though the wind were possessed of choice and had a will, but, as I said, wishing to show the course belonging to it from nature and its unhindered authority. If therefore you are ignorant of the wind — this very "spirit" that falls under perception — how and where it blows, how do you scrutinize the regeneration that is from the divine Spirit? If this spirit cannot be restrained, much more will the grace of the Holy Spirit not be subjected to the laws of nature.

Let Macedonius the Spirit-fighter be ashamed, and before him Eunomius: the former makes the Spirit a slave, and yet he has heard here that "the wind blows where it wishes" — much more, therefore, will the Spirit have a more self-determining movement, and will act where it wishes and as it wishes; while Eunomius[^1] the Spirit itself, and moreover, having grown so insolent as to know God as he himself *knows* himself. Let him hear, then, that he does not know even the course and the impulse of the wind; how then do you presume against the essence of God, you wretch?

[^1]: The Greek of this clause is corrupt: ὁ δὲ Εὐνόμιος αὐτά τε Πνεῦμα, καὶ πρός γε ἀπαυθαδιασάμενος, οὕτω γινώσκειν Θεὸν, ὡς ἑαυτὸν αὐτός. A verb governing "the Spirit itself" appears to have dropped out; the sense is that Eunomius rejected/denied the Spirit and arrogantly claimed to know God as he knows himself. Rendered here as legible, with the gap left unfilled.

# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 115 · John 1:18

> **"I saw the Lord"? (Isa. 6:1)**[^1]

The prophet saw, but not the essence itself, but a certain likeness and a certain appearance, as he was able to see. And again, one saw in one form, and another in another. Whence it is clear that they were not looking upon the truth itself; for they would not have beheld in different shapes that which is simple and without shape. But neither do the angels see the essence of God, even if they are said to see the face of God; for this is indicative only of their being ever presented with an appearance of God.[^2] The Son, therefore, alone sees the Father, and expounds him to all men. But hearing of a paternal bosom, suspect nothing bodily in the case of God. For the evangelist, wishing to show the genuineness, and the inseparability, and the co-eternity of the Son with respect to the Father, made mention of this name.

[^1]: The lemma proper is John 1:18, «No one has seen God at any time». Theophylact here raises an objection drawn from Isaiah, who said «I saw the Lord» (Isa. 6:1); the Greek marks it as a question — «Εἶδον τὸν Κύριον;» — and the following sentence answers it.
[^2]: Greek τοῦτο γὰρ ἐνδεικτικόν ἐστι μόνου τοῦ φαντάζεσθαι ἀεὶ Θεόν — literally "for this indicates only their always being presented with an appearance of God," i.e. beholding God in a representation, not in his essence.

# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 59 · John 1:9

> **"the light, the true *light*." (John 1:9)**

And you also, O Manichaean, who say that we are creatures of the evil demiurge, hear that the true light enlightens every man. If, then, the evil demiurge is darkness, he would not enlighten anyone; so that we are creatures of the true light. And how, one says, does it enlighten every man, seeing that we see some who are darkened? As far as it lies with itself, it enlightens all. For tell me: are not all rational? are not all by nature knowing the good and its opposite? do they not have the power to conceive the Creator from the creatures? So that the reason[^1] given to us — which trains us by nature, and which is also called natural law — this may be called light, the *light* given to us from God; but if some have used the reason badly, they have darkened themselves. But some resolve this objection thus: The Lord enlightens, it says, every man who comes into the world — that is, into a better order, and who is eager to adorn his own soul and not to leave it unadorned and without beauty.[^2]

[^1]: «ὁ λόγος» — here the rational faculty (reason) given to man, glossed by Theophylact as the "natural law" (φυσικὸς νόμος), not the divine Λόγος. Rendered "reason" to keep the argument (all men are «λογικοί», rational) transparent.

[^2]: A play on «κόσμος»: the "world" (κόσμος) is also "order/adornment," so that to come "into the world" is glossed as coming "into a better order" (τάξιν βελτίονα) and adorning (κοσμῆσαι) the soul, leaving it not "unadorned/disorderly" (ἄκοσμον) nor "without beauty" (ἀκαλλῆ).

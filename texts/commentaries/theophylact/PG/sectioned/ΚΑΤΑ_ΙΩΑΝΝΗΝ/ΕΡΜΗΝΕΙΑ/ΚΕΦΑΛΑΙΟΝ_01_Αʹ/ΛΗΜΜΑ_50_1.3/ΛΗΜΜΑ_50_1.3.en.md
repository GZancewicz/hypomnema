# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 50 · John 1:3

> **Life was, and the life was the light of men. (John 1:4)**

And the evangelist names the Lord "Life," as holding together the life of all things, but also as the procurer of spiritual life for all rational beings. And *he names him* "Light" — not such a one as is perceptible, but intelligible, illumining the soul itself. But he did not say that it is the light of the Jews only, but of all men. For all men — inasmuch as we received mind and reason from the Word who created us — in this respect are said to be illumined by him. For the reason given to us, on account of which we are also called rational, is a light guiding us into the things to be done and the things not to be done.

> **And the light shines in the darkness [...]. (John 1:5)**[^1]

The light, that is, the Word of God, shines in the darkness — that is, in death and in error. For indeed, having come to be even in death, he so prevailed over it that he compelled it to vomit up even those whom it had already managed to swallow; and in the Greek error the preaching shines.

[^1]: The section break here is garbled in the OCR, which preserves only the clause's tail «αὐτὸ οὐ κατέλαβεν» ("did not comprehend it"). The exposition that follows expounds the first half of the verse, «τὸ φῶς ἐν τῇ σκοτίᾳ φαίνει» ("the light shines in the darkness"), which is supplied here; the second half is taken up in the next lemma.

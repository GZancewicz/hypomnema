# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 169 · John 1:34

> **And I too did not know him, (John 1:34)**

removing all[^1] suspicion; but that, he says, he might be made manifest to Israel, for this reason I came baptizing. That, he says, all might be guided to the faith in him, and that he might be pointed out to the peoples, for this reason I baptize. For while I am baptizing, the peoples run together; and to those being gathered, Christ is made manifest to them, being proclaimed by me, and, being present, is shown to those present. For if they were not running together because of the baptism, how would the Lord have been pointed out by John? For he would not, going around the houses and leading Christ about by the hand, have shown him to each one. For this reason, therefore, he says, I came baptizing in water, that through the baptism, to the peoples coming together, he might be made manifest by me. And from this we learn that the so-called childhood miracles of Christ[^2] are false, and were composed by those who wish to mock the mystery. For if they were true, how was the Lord unknown while doing these things? For surely it was not likely that one working such wonders should not be renowned. But this is not so, it is not so. For before he was baptized he neither did signs nor was he known.

[^1]: The OCR reads «πὰς», recoverable as «πάσας» ("all"), agreeing with «ὑποψίας».
[^2]: «τὰ λεγόμενα παιδικὰ τοῦ Χριστοῦ θαύματα» — the alleged wonders of Christ's infancy/childhood, i.e. the miracles recounted in the apocryphal infancy gospels, which Theophylact here rejects as fabrications.

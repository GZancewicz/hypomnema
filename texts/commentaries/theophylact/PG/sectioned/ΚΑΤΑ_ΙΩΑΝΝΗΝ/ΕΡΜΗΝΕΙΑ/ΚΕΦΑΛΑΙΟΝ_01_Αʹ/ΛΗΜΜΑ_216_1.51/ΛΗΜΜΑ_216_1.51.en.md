# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 216 · John 1:51

> **"You are the King of Israel." (John 1:51)**

Do you see that he had not yet attained to the perfect knowledge of the true divinity of the Only-begotten? For he believes him to be a God-loving man, and King of Israel. But if he had truly confessed him God, he would not have called him King of Israel, but King of all; wherefore neither is he blessed as Peter was. Whence the Lord also, correcting him and leading him up to conceive something worthy of his divinity, says: "You shall see the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man." For do not, he says, suppose me a mere man, but the Master of the angels. For if angels minister *to him*, he would not be a mere man, but true God. And these things came to pass at the cross and the ascension. For indeed at the time of the passion an angel from heaven strengthened him; and at the tomb, an angel; and at the ascension, as Luke recounts.

But some understood the fig tree to be the law, as having fruit that is sweet for a season, yet covered—as if with leaves—by the roughness of the legal observances and by the hard-to-accomplish nature of the commandments. They say, therefore, that the Lord "saw" Nathanael in the sense that he looked upon him graciously, and perceived his understanding, while he was still under the law. But do you also fix your mind on this—if indeed you are charmed[^1] by such things—that the Lord saw Nathanael under the fig tree, that is, under the law, that is to say, within the law and searching out its depths. For if he had not been searching the depths of the law, the Lord would not have seen him.

Know this also, that Galilee is interpreted "rolling-down."[^2] The Lord, therefore, went out into the "rolling-down" region, as it were, of this world, and into human nature—and us, being under the fig tree, that is, pleasurable sin (with which is joined no small bitterness on account of the repentance and the judgments there)—he, as the lover of mankind, saw and chose out, we who confess him Son of God and King of the Israel that sees God.[^3] But if we intensify our zeal, he will deem us worthy of greater sights also, and we shall see the angels ascending to the height of the knowledge of his divinity, but descending again through their not attaining to comprehend the unknowable essence. And in another way too: one ascends when he dwells upon the words concerning the divinity of the Only-begotten; but he descends when he lingers upon those concerning the flesh and the descent into Hades.

[^1]: The text reads θέλγῃ; the sense intended is "if you are charmed/delighted by such things" (or, reading θέλῃς, "if you wish for such things").
[^2]: Greek κατακυλιστή, from κατακυλίω ("to roll down"); Theophylact's etymological gloss of "Galilee."
[^3]: Greek τοῦ Ἰσραὴλ τοῦ ὁρῶντος Θεόν, playing on the traditional etymology of "Israel" as "one who sees God."

# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 67 · John 1:12

> **But as many as received him, to them he gave authority to become children of God, to those who believe in his name. (John 1:12)**

Whether slaves, whether free, whether children, whether old men, whether barbarians, whether Greeks—as many as received him, to them he gave authority to become children of God. But who are these? Those who believe in his name. So that those who received the Word and the true light, received and welcomed *him* through faith.

But for what reason did he not say that he made them children of God, but that he gave them authority to become children of God? For what cause? Hear: that being baptized does not suffice for the guarding of purity, but there is need also of much diligence, so as to preserve unstained the image of the adoption that was stamped in baptism. Wherefore many received the grace of adoption through baptism, but having grown slothful did not become children of God unto the end.

And perhaps someone might say this also, that many receive him through believing only—such as are those called Catechumens—but have not yet become children of God; they have, however, the authority, if they should wish to be baptized, to be deemed worthy also of this grace—of adoption, I mean. And someone will say this also, that even if through baptism we receive the grace of adoption, yet we receive back the perfect *gift* in the resurrection, and we hope then to receive back the most complete adoption; just as indeed Paul also says.[^1]

[^1]: The lemma runs on into the next section (Lemma 68), where the Pauline citation—ὅτι τὴν υἱοθεσίαν ἀπεκδεχόμεθα, "that we await the adoption" (cf. Rom. 8:23)—is supplied. The OCR here reads several forms corruptly; the exposition has been rendered from the evident underlying text: οὐκ ἐξαρκεῖ (for "οὐκέξαρκεῖ"), τὸ τέλειον (for "πὸ τίλειον"), ἐντελεστάτην (for "ἐντελεστάστην"), καθὰ (for "καθα").

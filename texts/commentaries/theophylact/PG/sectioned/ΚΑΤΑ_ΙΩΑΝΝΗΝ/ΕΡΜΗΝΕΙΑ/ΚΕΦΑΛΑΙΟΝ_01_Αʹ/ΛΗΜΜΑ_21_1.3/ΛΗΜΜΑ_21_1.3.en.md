# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 21 · John 1:3

> **And the Word was with God. (John 1:1)**

For plainly the great John here proclaims that the Word is one [person] and the Father another, even if not another thing and another thing.[^1] For the "one and another" is said of persons, but the "one thing and another thing" of natures. For example — that I may set forth the thought more clearly — Peter and Paul are one and another, for they are two persons; but not another thing and another thing, for one nature belongs to them, their humanity. So then, concerning the Father and the Son also it must be laid down as doctrine: one and another, for they are two persons; but not another thing and another thing, for the nature is one, the Godhead.

[^1]: The argument turns on a grammatical gender contrast that English cannot carry directly. Greek uses the masculine «ἄλλος καὶ ἄλλος» ("one man and another") for a distinction of *persons*, but the neuter «ἄλλο καὶ ἄλλο» ("one thing and another") for a distinction of *natures*. Father and Son are «ἄλλος καὶ ἄλλος» (two persons) but not «ἄλλο καὶ ἄλλο» (they share one nature, the Godhead).

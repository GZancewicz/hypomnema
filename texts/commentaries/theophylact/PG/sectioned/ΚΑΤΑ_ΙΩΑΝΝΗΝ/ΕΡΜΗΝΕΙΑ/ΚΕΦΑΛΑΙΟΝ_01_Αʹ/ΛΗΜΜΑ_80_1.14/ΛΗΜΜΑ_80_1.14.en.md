# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 80 · John 1:14

> **The Word became flesh, (John 1:14)**

naming the man from a part *of him*, the *man* composed of soul and body. And perhaps, since the flesh is alien to the divine nature, the evangelist, wishing to make plain the boundless[^1] condescension of God, made mention of the flesh, that we might be astounded at his unspeakable love for mankind—that, for our salvation, he took on what is alien to his nature and utterly foreign, I mean the flesh. For the soul has a certain kinship with God, but the flesh is entirely without communion *with him*. For this reason, therefore, I think the evangelist here made use only of the name of "flesh," not as though that which was assumed was without a share of a soul, but for a fuller display of the wondrous and awesome mystery. Since, if the Word, having become incarnate, did not assume a human soul, our souls are still unhealed; for what he did not assume, neither did he sanctify. And what a laughingstock[^2] *would it be*, the soul having been the first to suffer! For it was the soul that yielded to the words of the serpent and was deceived in paradise, and then the hand too laid hold *of the fruit*, following after its mistress and lady, the soul—that the maidservant, the flesh, should be both assumed and sanctified and healed, whereas the mistress be left unassumed and unhealed?

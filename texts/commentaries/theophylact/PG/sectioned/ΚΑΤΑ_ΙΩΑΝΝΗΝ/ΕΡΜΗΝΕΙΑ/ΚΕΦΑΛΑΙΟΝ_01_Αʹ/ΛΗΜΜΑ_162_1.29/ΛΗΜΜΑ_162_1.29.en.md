# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 162 · John 1:29

> **that he was, (John 1:29)**

Senseless too is the faction of the Samosatene [i.e. Paul of Samosata], *who hold* that the Lord did not begin to be from Mary, but was prior to the Forerunner according to his pre-eternal existence.[^1] For if, as you babble, the Lord took the beginning of his being from Mary, how was he prior to the Forerunner? For this one, quite evidently, precedes the Lord by six months as to the birth in the flesh. And the Lord is called "a man,"[^2] perhaps both because of the completeness of his age — for he was baptized at thirty years old — and perhaps also as the husband of every soul, and the bridegroom of the Church.

[^1]: The Greek is compressed: Ἄνους καὶ ἡ τοῦ Σαμοσατέως φατρία, ὅτι οὐκ ἀπὸ Μαρίας ἤρξατο τοῦ εἶναι ὁ Κύριος, ἀλλὰ πρῶτος ἦν τοῦ Προδρόμου. The ὅτι-clause states the truth against which the "faction" is senseless: *the* Lord did *not* begin to be from Mary, but *was* prior to the Forerunner. "Senseless" renders ἄνους (lit. "mindless"), a play on the following polemic.
[^2]: "A man" renders ἀνήρ (from the lemma «ὀπίσω μου ἔρχεται ἀνήρ», v. 30), the same word Theophylact then exploits: the Lord is ἀνήρ ("man"), and he is also the ἀνήρ ("husband") of every soul and bridegroom of the Church.

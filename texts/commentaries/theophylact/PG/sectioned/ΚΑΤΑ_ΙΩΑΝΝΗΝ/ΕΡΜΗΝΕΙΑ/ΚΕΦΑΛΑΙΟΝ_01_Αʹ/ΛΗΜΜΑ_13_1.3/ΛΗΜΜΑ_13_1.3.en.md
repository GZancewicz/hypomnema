# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 13 · John 1:3

> **the Word, (John 1:3)**

Hear: that because of the weakness of those hearing, lest, having heard immediately from the beginning "Son," we should conceive of a passible and bodily begetting, for this reason he called him "Word"; so that you might learn that just as the word is begotten from the mind impassibly, so also this one was begotten from the Father impassibly. And in another way too he called him "Word," since he announced to us the things of the Father, just as also every word announces the counsels of the mind; and at the same time also so that he might show him to be coeternal with the Father. For just as it is not possible to speak of a mind ever existing without a word, so neither the Father and God without the Son. And he said it with the article: *The* Word. For there are also many other words of God, such as, I mean, the prophecies and the commandments; according as it is said concerning the angels, that they are "mighty in strength, doing his word" (Ps. 103:20), that is, his commandments; but the Word properly so-called is a certain substance, subsisting in its own hypostasis.[^1]

[^1]: Greek οὐσία τις ἐστὶν ἐνυπόστατος: "is a certain substance, enhypostatic" — i.e. having a real, personal subsistence (a hypostasis) of its own, as against the "words" (prophecies, commands) that are only utterances.

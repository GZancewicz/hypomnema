# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 24 · John 1:3

> **All things came into being through him. (John 1:3)**

Do not suppose, he says, a word poured out into the air and dissolved, but the maker of all things intelligible and sensible. But again those around Arius fasten on, saying that, just as we say the door came to be through the saw as through an instrument, while the craftsman who moves the instrument is another — so too all things came to be through the Son, not as though he himself were the maker, but an instrument, as the saw is in that case, while God and the Father is the maker, using the Son as an instrument. So that the Son is a thing made, they say, created for this very purpose, that all things might come to be through him — just as the saw comes to be for this purpose, that the works of carpentry might be wrought through it. Such, then, are the things of the wicked faction of Arius.

But we shall say something simple and straightforward against these people: that if, as you say, the Father created the Son for this purpose, that he might have him as an instrument for the completion of the creation, the Son will be less honorable than the creation.[^1] For just as, the saw being an instrument, the things made through it are more honorable — for the saw came to be for the sake of those things, not those for the sake of the saw — so too the creation will be more honorable than the Only-begotten. For it was for its sake, as they themselves say, that the Father created him; so that, if God had not been about to create all things, he would not have brought forth the Only-begotten either. What could be more senseless than these words? "Yes," he says, "and how is it that he did not say, 'The Word himself…'"[^2]

[^1]: Lines 17–18 of the Greek are badly corrupted by OCR: «ἵοα ἡργανον ἔχη τοῦτον πρὺς ουμπλήρωσιν τῆς κτί.εως, ἀιμότεμος ἔττκιὃ ίὸς τῆς κτίσεως». Reconstructed as «ἵνα ὄργανον ἔχῃ τοῦτον πρὸς συμπλήρωσιν τῆς κτίσεως, ἀτιμότερος ἔσται ὁ Υἱὸς τῆς κτίσεως» — "that he might have him as an instrument for the completion of the creation, the Son will be less honorable than the creation," which the following sentences confirm.
[^2]: The lemma breaks off in the middle of the Arian's objection, and the last words are garbled in the OCR («Αὐτὸς ὁ Λόθέσει τῇ,» for something like «Αὐτὸς ὁ Λόγος…»). The quotation ("The Word himself…") leads into the scripture and discussion of the following lemma.

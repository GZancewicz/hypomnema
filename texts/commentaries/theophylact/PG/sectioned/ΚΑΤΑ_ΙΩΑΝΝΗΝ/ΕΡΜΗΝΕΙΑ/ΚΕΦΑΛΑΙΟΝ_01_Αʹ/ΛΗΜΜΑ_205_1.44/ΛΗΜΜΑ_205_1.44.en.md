# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 205 · John 1:44

> **Now Philip was from the city of Andrew and Peter.[^1] (John 1:44)**

Now this was some small city, and rather fittingly to be called a village. Whence it is also worthy to marvel at the power of Christ, that from those bearing no fruit he chose out the choice ones of the disciples. Accordingly, neither does Philip keep the good thing by himself, but shares it also with Nathanael. And since Nathanael was learned in the law, Philip refers him to the law and the prophets; for indeed he was exact and well-versed in the matters of the law. But he calls the Lord son of Joseph, since he was reckoned to be his child; and he names him from Nazareth, even though properly he was from Bethlehem. For in that place he was born, but in Nazareth he was brought up; yet nevertheless, since his birth was unknown to the many, whereas his upbringing was known, they name him from Nazareth, as having been reared in it.

[^1]: The OCR reads Ἀνβρέου; read Ἀνδρέου, "of Andrew." Theophylact's lemma here omits the ἀπὸ Βηθσαϊδά ("from Bethsaida") of the received text (ἦν δὲ ὁ Φίλιππος ἀπὸ Βηθσαϊδά, ἐκ τῆς πόλεως Ἀνδρέου καὶ Πέτρου).

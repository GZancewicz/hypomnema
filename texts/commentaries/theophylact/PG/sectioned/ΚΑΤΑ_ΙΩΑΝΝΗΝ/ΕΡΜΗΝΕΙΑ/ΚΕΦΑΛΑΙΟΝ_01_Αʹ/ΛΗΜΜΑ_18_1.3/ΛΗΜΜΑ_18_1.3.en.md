# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 18 · John 1:3

> **with God, (John 1:3)**

understand it in place of, he was with God, together with God, in his bosom. For it is not admissible that God should ever be without-word[^1] or without-wisdom, or without-power. For this reason we believe that, since the Son is the word and wisdom and power of the Father, he always was with God—in place of, together with the Father and with the Father. "And how," someone says, "being a son, is he not later than the father?" How? Learn from the material example. The radiance of the sun—is it not from the sun itself? Yes, certainly. Is it then also later than the sun, so that a sun could ever be conceived without it? It cannot be said. For how could there even be a sun not having radiance? What therefore *holds* in this case will much more be understood in the case of the Father and the Son. For the Son, being the radiance of the Father, as Paul says (Heb. 1:3), will be believed always to shine forth together with the Father, and will not be later than him. And consider for me also Sabellius the Libyan being overturned by this saying. For that man taught as doctrine that the Father and the Son and the Spirit are one person, and that this one person at one time appeared as Father, at another as Son, and at another as Spirit. Such nonsense did that son of the father of falsehood babble, being full of the evil spirit. But he is manifestly refuted from this—I mean, from the *saying*—[^2]

[^1]: Greek ἄλογον, "without word/reason," playing on Λόγος: to say God ever lacked the Word (the Son) would be to make God ἄλογος. The following pair ἄσοφον ("without wisdom") and ἀδύνατον ("without power") sets up the identification of the Son as the Father's Word, Wisdom, and Power.

[^2]: The sentence breaks off; the "saying" continues into the next lemma.

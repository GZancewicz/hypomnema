# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 211 · John 1:50

> **"Come and see," he says (John 1:50)**

For he knew that if *Nathanael* should taste of the words of Christ, he will not withdraw. And Nathanael is praised by Christ as truly an Israelite, since he said nothing for favor or for hostility. For his words were not of unbelief, but of exactness and of a mind learned in the law, knowing that the Christ comes not from Nazareth but from Bethlehem. What then does Nathanael do? Was he then puffed up[^1] by the praise? Not at all, but he persists, wishing to learn[^2] something more clearly and more exactly; wherefore he also asks:

[^1]: Greek ἐχαυνώθη (from χαυνόω), "was made vain/slack/puffed up"; the sense is being inflated or made complacent by flattery.
[^2]: The text reads καθεῖν, evidently an OCR corruption of μαθεῖν ("to learn"), adopted here.

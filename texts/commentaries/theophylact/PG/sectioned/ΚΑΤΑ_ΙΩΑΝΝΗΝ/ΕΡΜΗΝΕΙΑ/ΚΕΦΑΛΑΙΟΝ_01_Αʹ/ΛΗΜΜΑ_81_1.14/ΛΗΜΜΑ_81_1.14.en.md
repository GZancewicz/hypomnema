# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 81 · John 1:14

> **The Word became flesh, (John 1:14)**

we believe that *he became* a complete man, since Scripture is accustomed to name the composite man by a single part, both *by* "flesh" and *by* "soul."[^1] And by this saying Nestorius too is overthrown. For that man used to say that it was not God the Word himself who became man, conceived from the all-pure blood of the holy Virgin, but that the Virgin bore *a mere* man; and *that* this man, graced with every form of virtue, had the Word of God conjoined to him and granting *him* authority against unclean spirits; and from this he taught *the doctrine of* two sons—one the *son* of the Virgin, Jesus the man, and another the Son of God, conjoined to the man and being inseparable from him, but *only* according to grace and relation and love, inasmuch as the man was virtuous. These things *he taught*, willfully deafening himself to the truth. For had he been willing, he too would have heard this blessed evangelist saying that

[^1]: Greek μονομερῶς, "by a single part": Scripture designates the whole composite human being (τὸν σύνθετον ἄνθρωπον) sometimes by "flesh," sometimes by "soul." Hence "the Word became flesh" means the Word became a complete man, soul and body alike.

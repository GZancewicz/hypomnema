# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 75 · John 1:14

> **became flesh, (John 1:14)**

do not suppose that he departed from his own nature and was changed into flesh; for he would not be God, if indeed he were being changed and altered—but that, remaining what he was, he became what he was not.[^1] But Apollinaris of Laodicea, from this, put together a heresy. For he dogmatized that our Lord and God did not assume the perfect nature of man—that is, a body with a rational soul—but flesh only, devoid of a rational and intellectual soul. For what need had God of the soul, having the divinity governing the body, in the manner in which we have the soul conducting our body? And he had for support, as he supposed, the present saying:

[^1]: The classic patristic formula: «μένων ὃ ἦν, ἐγένετο ὃ οὐκ ἦν» — "remaining what he was, he became what he was not." (The manuscript's «ἀγένετο» is corrected to «ἐγένετο».)

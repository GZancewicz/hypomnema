# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 25 · John 1:3

> **«Through»? (John 1:3)**

How is this?[^1] In order that you might not suppose the Son to be unbegotten and without beginning and a rival god,[^2] for this reason he said [it]—that the Father through the Word made all things. For suppose some king who has a son, and who, being about to found a city, entrusts to the son the founding of it. Just as, therefore, the one who says "Through the son of the king the city was founded" does not introduce the son of the king as a slave, but shows that this son has also a father, and is not alone; so also here the evangelist, having said that through the Son all things came to be, made it clear that the Father used him as mediator toward the creation, so to speak—not as one lesser than himself, but rather as equal in power, and as being strong to accomplish so great an undertaking.[^3] And this I say to you, that if the

[^1]: The opening of the fragment is OCR-damaged: «διά;» Πῶς. Theophylact is quoting and questioning the preposition «διά» ("through") from John 1:3 (πάντα δι' αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο), then asks Πῶς; ("How [is this]?"). The rendering treats «διά» as the quoted lemma and Πῶς as the rhetorical question introducing the answer.
[^2]: Greek (reconstructed from corrupt OCR) ἀντίθεον, "opposed to God / a rival god." The lines read: ᾑπονοήσης εἶν αι τὸν ΥΙὲν καὶ ἀντίθερν τούτοι ἔνενιν, reconstructed as ὑπονοήσῃς εἶναι τὸν Υἱὸν καὶ ἀντίθεον, τούτου ἕνεκεν [εἶπεν].
[^3]: Greek ἐπίτασιν, lit. a "straining/exertion," here the great task or undertaking of creation.

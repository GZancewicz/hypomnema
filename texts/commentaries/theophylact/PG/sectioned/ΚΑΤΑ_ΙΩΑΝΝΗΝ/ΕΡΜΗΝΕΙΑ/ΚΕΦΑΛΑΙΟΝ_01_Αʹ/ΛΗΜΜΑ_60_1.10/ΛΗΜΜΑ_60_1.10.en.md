# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 60 · John 1:10

> **He was in the world, and the world came into being through him, and the world knew him not. (John 1:10)**

He was in the world, as God present everywhere; but indeed he might also be said to be in the world in a providential and sustaining manner. And yet why do I say this, one says, that he was in the world, seeing that the world itself would not even have been, had he himself not made it? And from every side he shows him to be the Creator: at once restraining the madness of Manes, who says that an evil demiurge produced all things, but indeed also that of Arius, who said[^1] ... leading every man to confess the Maker, and not to serve the creatures but to worship the Creator.

[^1]: The Greek is defective here: «ἀλλὰ δὴ καὶ τὴν τοῦ Ἀρείου, τοῦ φήσαντ[ος]...» breaks off, the clause stating Arius's error being lost, and resumes with the participle «πάντα ἄνθρωπον ἐνάγων» ("leading every man"). The gist: he also refutes Arius, leading all to confess Christ as Maker.

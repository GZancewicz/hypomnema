# John 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 65 · John 1:10

> **He came unto his own, and his own received him not. (John 1:10)**

Here he most manifestly launches into the discourse concerning the dispensation in the flesh,[^1] and the whole texture of the thought is as follows: The true light was in the world without flesh, and was not being recognized; then it also came unto its own with flesh. And by *his own* you will understand either the whole world, or Judea, which he chose as a cord of inheritance,[^2] and as a portion, and as his special possession.

[^1]: «τῆς ἐνσάρκου οἰκονομίας» — the "in-the-flesh economy," the technical patristic term for God's saving arrangement worked out through the Incarnation; rendered here "the dispensation in the flesh."
[^2]: «σχοίνισμα κληρονομίας», literally "a measuring-cord of inheritance," echoes the Septuagint, where the allotted portion of God's people is marked off by a surveyor's line (cf. Deut. 32:9; Ps. 77[78]:55). The following «περιουσιασμὸν αὐτοῦ» ("his special possession") likewise picks up the LXX notion of a λαὸς περιούσιος, a peculiar people.

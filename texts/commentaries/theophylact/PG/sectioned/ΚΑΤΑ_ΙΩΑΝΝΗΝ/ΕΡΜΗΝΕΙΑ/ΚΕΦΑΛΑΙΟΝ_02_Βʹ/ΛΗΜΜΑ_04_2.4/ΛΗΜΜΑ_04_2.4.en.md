# John 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 4 · John 2:4

> **And the mother of Jesus and his brothers were there.[^1] (John 2:4)**

As therefore they invited her and the brothers, so also they called the Lord. But the Lord comes, and does not deem the invitation unworthy, because he was looking not to his own dignity but to our profit and benefaction. For he who did not deem it unworthy to come to be among slaves, much more would not have deemed it unworthy to come to a marriage. But the mother urges him to a work of wonder, having conceived a great estimation concerning his power both from the conception and from the birth. For she kept all things in her heart (cf. Luke 2:19), and from these she reckoned that the Son was able to do beyond a man. For it was not indeed from Jesus' having done other miracles too that the mother was led on to urge him to this; for being a child, he had worked no wonder—otherwise he would have been conspicuous to all. And at the same time the mother also remembered the testimonies of John, which he testified concerning him, and she already saw disciples following him too; and from all these she inferred the power of the Child. But he rebukes her not without reason. For if, he says, there is no wine, those very ones who have none ought to have come forward and made the request, not you the Mother. For entreaty on behalf of one's own kin becomes a stumbling-block[^2] to those who see it; but when those who have the need themselves make the request, the matter becomes free of suspicion.

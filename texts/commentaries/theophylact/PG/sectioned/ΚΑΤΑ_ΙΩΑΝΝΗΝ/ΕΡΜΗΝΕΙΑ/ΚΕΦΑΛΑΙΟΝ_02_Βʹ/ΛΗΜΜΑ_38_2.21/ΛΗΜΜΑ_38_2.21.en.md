# John 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 38 · John 2:21

> **The Lord wills to cleanse him from the stroke, and to show him light. (Isa. 53:10–11)**[^1]

For these Scriptures treat most manifestly concerning the resurrection. But Apollinaris from hence attempts to find an advocacy for his own heresy. For, wishing to establish that the Lord's flesh was without a soul,[^2] he says that: Since the flesh is called a temple, and the temple is soulless, therefore this *flesh* also is soulless. O senseless and wily one! Will you then make the Lord's flesh even wood and stones, since it is from these that the temple *is*? But when you hear the Lord,

[^1]: A loose Septuagint conflation of Isaiah 53:10–11: καθαρίσαι αὐτὸν τῆς πληγῆς (53:10) and δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς (53:11). Theophylact's «ἀπὸ τῆς πληγῆς» renders the affliction/stroke of the suffering servant; πληγή = blow, wound, stroke.
[^2]: Greek ἄψυχος, "soulless, inanimate," rendered consistently here as "without a soul / soulless." In the Apollinarian argument the term denies Christ's flesh a rational soul.

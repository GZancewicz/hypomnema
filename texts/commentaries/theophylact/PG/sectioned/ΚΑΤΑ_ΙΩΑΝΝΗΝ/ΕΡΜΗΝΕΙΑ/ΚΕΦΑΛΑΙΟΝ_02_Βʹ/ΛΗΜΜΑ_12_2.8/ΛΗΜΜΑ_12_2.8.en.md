# John 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 12 · John 2:8

> **according to the purification of the Jews. (John 2:8)**

*He added this* so that none of the unbelievers might suspect that some wine-lees had remained behind in them, and then, when the water had been poured in and mixed, a very thin wine had resulted; he shows that those *vessels* had never been receptacles of wine. Was he, then, not able, even without the waterpots being filled with water, to create wine out of things that are not? He was able indeed, but in many places he curtails the greatness of his wonders, so that they might become more readily accepted; and at the same time so that he might also have the servants as witnesses of the wonder, those who had poured the water into the waterpots; and further, that we might learn that he it is who also fashions the moisture of the vine — being altogether water — into wine.

[?So that the servants might not seem to affect][^1] the tasting; not without reason, but so that none might suspect that it was a gathering of drunken men, and that their perception had been corrupted, and that, as men corrupted *in their senses,* [?they had judged unreliably. So that][^2] such a suspicion *might have no* place, he entrusts the tasting to the master of the feast, to the sober one. For indeed in gatherings of this kind those entrusted with this service are especially sober, so that all things may come to be in order and good arrangement at their command.

But let us pray that these things may be accomplished in us also: that there may now come to be, as it were, a wedding — that is, a joining of God to our soul — while we are still in Cana of Galilee, I mean in the region of this lower and tumbling world, which now this way, now that, is rolled about and rolls *others* about. And this wedding does not come to pass without the presence of Christ, and of his mother, and of the disciples. For unless someone believes in Christ, who was born of Mary and who spoke in the apostles, how shall he be joined to God? Let us see, therefore, what the Lord works as a wonder in such a wedding, and in such a joining of God to the soul. He transforms the water into wine; he fills our six stone waterpots. By water, then, you might understand what is watery and fluid and dissolute in us, both in life and in doctrines; and by the six waterpots, the five senses, through which we stumble in our actions, and, as a sixth waterpot, the reasoning faculty, through which we are disposed erroneously in matters of doctrine. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then — the evangelical word, I mean — healing our failings, those both in the practical and in the doctrinal, that is, the contemplative, *sphere,* transposes what is watery and easily-dissolved in these into wine: I mean the astringent[^3] and gladdening life and word; and thus our six waterpots are filled with this good drink — both the senses, that we may not stumble in the practical, and the reasoning, that we may not stumble against the doctrines.

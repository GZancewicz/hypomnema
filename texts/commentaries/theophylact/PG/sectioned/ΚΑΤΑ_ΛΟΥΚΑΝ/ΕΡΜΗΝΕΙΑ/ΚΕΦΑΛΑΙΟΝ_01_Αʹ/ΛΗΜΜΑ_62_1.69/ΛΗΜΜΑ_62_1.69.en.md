# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 62 · Luke 1:73-75

> **Blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you. (Gen. 22:17)**[^1]

For Abraham has now indeed been multiplied, all the nations having been adopted to him through faith; for as that one believed, so also these were made his own through faith.[^2] And he rescued us from the enemies without fear (Luke 1:74); for often some are rescued, but with fear and many struggles and war; but Christ, when we had toiled at nothing, was crucified on our behalf; and thereafter he rescued us without fear, that is, without danger.

But why did he rescue us? Was it for the sake of luxuriating? Not so; but in order to serve him (Luke 1:74), not once, nor for two days, but all the days (Luke 1:75); and to serve, not through bodily worship and ministration only, but in holiness and righteousness. Now holiness is what is just toward God, and righteousness what is toward men: for instance, if someone abstains from *profaning* the sacred things, and does not lay hands upon the divine things impiously, but preserves every honor toward the things that are honorable, such a one is holy; likewise also the one who reveres his parents is holy, for these too are household gods;[^3] but if someone is not covetous, not rapacious, not a thief, not an adulterer, not a fornicator, such a one is righteous. So then one must serve God in holiness—I mean, the reverence toward the divine things—and in righteousness—the praiseworthy conduct toward the human things; and to serve before him, not before men, as the men-pleasers and hypocrites do.

[^1]: The oath to Abraham of the previous lemma (Luke 1:73), cited from Genesis 22:17 (LXX: εὐλογῶν εὐλογήσω σε, καὶ πληθύνων πληθυνῶ σε).
[^2]: The OCR of this clause is corrupt (ὡς γὰρι ἐνεῖνο· ἐλίστευσεν ρὥτω καὶ ταῦτα διὰ τῆς πίστεως ᾠκειώθησαν). Reconstructed as ὡς γὰρ ἐκεῖνος ἐπίστευσεν, οὕτω καὶ ταῦτα διὰ τῆς πίστεως ᾠκειώθησαν, "for as that one [Abraham] believed, so also these [the nations] were made his own through faith" — an allusion to Genesis 15:6 / Romans 4. The verb ᾠκειώθησαν means "were made kin / appropriated as one's own."
[^3]: θεοὶ κατοικίδιοι, "household gods" — a striking expression applied to one's parents, whom one is to revere as domestic deities.

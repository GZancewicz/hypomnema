# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 1 · Luke 1:5

> **It came to pass in the days of Herod the king of Judea, a certain priest by name Zacharias, of the course of Abia, and his wife of the daughters of Aaron; and her name was Elisabeth. (Luke 1:5)**

He makes mention of the reign of Herod, partly narrating according to the pattern of the prophets — for those begin thus: "In the days of Ahaz and Hezekiah, and of such a one, these things came to pass"[^1] — and partly, since he is about to speak concerning Christ, on this account he mentions Herod, in order to show that Christ truly came in the time of Herod. For this Herod came to be, when the rulers from among the Jews had failed, as the prophecy of Jacob makes clear (Gen. 49:10); and by this Christ is shown to have come.[^2] And he accomplishes yet another thing as well: he shows the Gospel to be true through his stating of the time. For he gives license to those who wish to examine, and to learn the truth of the Gospel from the time. But he begins from Zacharias and the birth of John, and reasonably so; for since he is about to speak concerning the birth of Christ, and John is the Forerunner of Christ, reasonably he narrates the *birth* of John before the birth of Christ. Nor was even this without a miracle. For since a Virgin was about to bear, grace ordered beforehand that an old woman should bear, not according to the law of nature, even if with a husband.

But what is the meaning of "of the course of Abia"? Some understand it thus, that there were two priests fulfilling the liturgy in succession: the one so called Abia, and Zacharias. Since therefore Abia had ministered as priest in the liturgy, after his course Zacharias would perform the liturgy; but it seems not to be so. For Solomon, having completed the temple, appointed also courses, that is, weeks; and in this week, say, he appointed the sons of Korah, and in that one Asaph, and in that one Abia, and in another another. So then, when he says that Zacharias was "of the course of Abia," it must be understood thus, that he was from the week of Abia — not that after the week of Abia he himself took over the liturgy; for *then* he would have said "after the course of Abia"; but as it is, having said "of the course of Abia," he indicates that he was of the order of Abia and of his week. And wishing to show that John was on both sides lawfully of priestly stock,[^3] he says, "And his wife of the daughters of Aaron"; for it was not permitted to take a wife from another tribe, but from the same one. And Elisabeth is interpreted "God's rest," and Zacharias, "remembrance of the Lord."

[^1]: The prophetic formula is garbled in the OCR («Τφάχαζ»); read Ἄχαζ (Ahaz). Theophylact alludes loosely to prophetic superscriptions such as Isa. 1:1 and Hos. 1:1 ("In the days of … kings of Judah, this came to pass"); «τοῦ δεῖνος» = "of such a one, so-and-so."
[^2]: The OCR here is corrupt: «Ῥεν δείκνυται ὁ Χριστὸς ἐλθών». The sense is evidently "in this / whereby Christ is shown to have come" (perhaps ᾧ or ὅθεν δείκνυται); the point is that, Judah's own rulers having ceased (Herod being a foreigner), Shiloh had come per Gen. 49:10.
[^3]: One line of OCR at this point is illegible («ΟΑΓὗΤἰ»); it appears to be a corrupted word or column-marker and is omitted from the rendering.

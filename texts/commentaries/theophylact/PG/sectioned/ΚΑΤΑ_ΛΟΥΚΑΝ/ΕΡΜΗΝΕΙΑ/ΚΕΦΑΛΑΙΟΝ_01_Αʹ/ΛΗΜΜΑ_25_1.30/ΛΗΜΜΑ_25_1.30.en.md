# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 25 · Luke 1:30

> **the angel said to her: Fear not, Mary; for you have found favor with God. (Luke 1:30)**

By the sixth month he means the one reckoned from the conception of John. And he says that the virgin was betrothed to a man of the house of David, in order to show that she too was descended from the lineage of David; for it was a law that both parties be from the same lineage and the same line. And since the Lord said to Eve, "In pains you shall bear children," this sorrow is loosed by the joy which the angel brings to the Virgin, saying: Rejoice, favored one;[^1] and since Eve had been cursed, this woman hears "blessed." And she was pondering concerning the greeting, of what sort it might be, whether perhaps some improper and lewd one, as from a man to a girl, or a divine one; since indeed mention of God accompanies the greeting, the "The Lord is with you."

But first he calms her heart from fear, so that, having come into a state of untroubledness, she might receive the divine response; for it was not possible for her, being troubled, to hear accurately the things that were to happen. And that which I said above, "favored one," he says as if interpreting it: For you have found favor before God; for this is what "to be favored" is, namely to find favor with God, that is, to please God. But this is common; for many others also found favor with God, but that which is added on is no longer *so*.

[^1]: The angel's greeting (Luke 1:28), Χαῖρε ("Rejoice"), plays on the χαρά ("joy") that undoes Eve's λύπη ("sorrow"); and κεχαριτωμένη ("favored one," here glossed) together with χάρις ("favor") shares the same root χαρ-. English cannot reproduce the single Greek root across "rejoice / joy / favored / favor."

# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 27 · Luke 1:33

> **he shall reign over the house of Jacob unto the ages, and of his kingdom there shall be no end. (Luke 1:33)**

"Behold, you shall conceive"; this is the extraordinary thing, of which no other woman was deemed worthy. And he said "in the womb," in order to show that the Lord was made flesh substantially from the very belly of the Virgin. And rightly was he called Jesus, who came for the salvation of our race; for this name, when translated into the Greek tongue, is called "salvation of God." Jesus, therefore, is interpreted "Savior," since salvation is also called *iaō*.[^1]

He shall be, he says, great, and shall be called Son of the Most High. Great indeed was John also, but not moreover Son of the Most High; whereas the Savior *is* great, insofar as he taught, and Son of the Most High both on account of this—for he himself taught as one having authority—and on account of the marvel-working of the miracles. And the one who appears is called Son of the Most High [?…];[^2] for the man, the Son of the Virgin, was of the Most High. For the Word was Son of the Most High even before the ages, but he was not called so, nor was he known; but when he was made flesh and was manifested in flesh, then indeed he was also called Son of the Most High—the one who appears and works the miracles.

And when you hear "throne of David," do not think of a perceptible kingdom, but a divine one, which he reigned over all the nations through the divine preaching; for "house of Jacob" is also those from among the Hebrews who believed—yes indeed, and those from the other nations as well; for these are properly Jacob and Israel. But how is he said to sit upon the throne of David? Listen: David was small among his brothers; and the Lord was despised, and slandered as a glutton and a wine-drinker and a carpenter's son, and dishonored among his brothers, the sons of Joseph; for neither did his brothers, it says, believe in him. That one was persecuted while doing good; and the Lord, while working miracles, was slandered and stoned. That one conquered and reigned through gentleness; and the Lord, having accepted the cross through gentleness, reigned. Do you see how he is said to sit upon the throne of David? For as David received the perceptible kingdom, so also the Lord the intelligible one, of which there shall be no end; for there shall be no end of the kingdom of Christ, that is, of the knowledge of God and of Christianity. For even while being persecuted, we shine by the grace of Christ.

[^1]: Greek ἰαὼ. Theophylact derives Ἰησοῦς ("Jesus / Savior") from a Hebrew name for salvation, transliterated here as *iaō*; the etymology is his, connecting the name to the root of "to save."
[^2]: OCR gap. The manuscript runs "καλεῖται δὲ Υἱὸς Ὑψίστου ὁ φαινόμενος … Ὑψίστου ἦν ὁ ἄνθρωπος ὁ Υἱὸς τῆς Παρθένου," with material apparently lost after ὁ φαινόμενος. The sense is that the visible one—the man who is the Son of the Virgin—is called Son of the Most High.

# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 65 · Luke 1:80

> **And the child grew and was strengthened in spirit, and was in the deserts until the day of his manifestation to Israel. (Luke 1:80)**

It appears strange that Zechariah says to the child the things which he says; for surely, since the child was without perception, one ought not to have spoken to it. It is possible, then, to say that, just as the child had a strange birth, having leapt and prophesied in the womb at the coming of Mariam (Luke 1:41-44), so it is nothing incredible that it should also perceive the things spoken by its father after the birth.

"And you shall go before," he says, "in front of the Lord, leaving me before long." For Zechariah knew that he would be deprived of him after a little while, since he was about to flee into the deserts. But why "shall you go before"? For the sake of preparing the ways of the Lord; and the ways are the souls upon which the Lord journeys; therefore the Forerunner prepared the souls, so that Christ might walk about in them. And how did he prepare them? By giving knowledge of salvation to the people; now the salvation is the Lord Jesus, and the knowledge of the salvation, that is, of Christ, was given to the people by John; for John testified concerning Jesus. And the knowledge came about in the remission of sins. For otherwise the Lord would not have been recognized to be God, if he had not remitted the sins to the people; for it belongs to God to remit sins. And he remitted these to us through the bowels of mercy; for it was not through our works, for we did nothing good; but he visited us from on high, being called *dayspring*;[^1] for he is the Sun of righteousness (Mal. 4:2).

And he appeared to us who are in darkness, that is, in ignorance, and sitting in the shadow of death, that is, in sin; for two evils held sway over the nature of men: ignorance of God, which the Greeks had,[^2] and sin, which the Hebrews had, even though they did recognize God. He appeared, therefore, to human nature, so that he might enlighten both those in the darkness, that is, in ignorance and godlessness, and those sitting in the shadow of death, that is, in sin. And sin is a shadow of death according to this reasoning, as I think: just as the shadow follows the body, so also, where there is death, there is sin as well. For instance, Adam died—there, consequently, is also sin; and not even the death of Christ will you find apart from sin, for Christ died, but on account of my sins; so that with good reason sin is said to be a shadow of death, as always accompanying it. And there are other things too to say on this point; and I think we said them in expounding Matthew.

But is it enough only to appear to those who have been darkened? No; rather, it is necessary also to guide our feet into the way of peace, that is, of righteousness; for just as sin is enmity toward God, so righteousness is peace. The way of peace, therefore, is the conduct of righteousness, toward which Christ, who dawned from on high, guided the feet of our souls. And the child grew according to bodily stature, and was strengthened in Spirit; for together with the body the spiritual gift too was growing, and by however much the more the boy grew, by so much also the operations of the Spirit were displayed, since the instrument was able to contain them. But why was he in the deserts? So that he might pass his life outside the wickedness of the many, and so that, standing in awe of no one, he might reprove with boldness; for if he had been in the world, he would perhaps have been defiled by the friendships and habitual associations of men; and at the same time also so that he might be worthy of trust, being about to preach concerning Christ, inasmuch as he was a desert-dweller and above the many. And he was hidden in the deserts until God was well pleased to manifest him to the Israelite people.

[^1]: ἀνατολή, rendered "dayspring," means the rising/dawn (also "east"); Theophylact glosses it by "Sun of righteousness" (Ἥλιος δικαιοσύνης, Mal. 4:2). The same word stands in the lemma's "dayspring from on high" (Luke 1:78).
[^2]: οἱ Ἕλληνες, "the Greeks," here in the customary patristic sense of the pagans/Gentiles as opposed to the Hebrews.

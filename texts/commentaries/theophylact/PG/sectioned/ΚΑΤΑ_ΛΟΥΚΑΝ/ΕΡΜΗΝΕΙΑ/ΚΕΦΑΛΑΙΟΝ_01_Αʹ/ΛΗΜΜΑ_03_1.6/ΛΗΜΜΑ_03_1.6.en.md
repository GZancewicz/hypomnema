# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 3 · Luke 1:6

> **Walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord, blameless. (Luke 1:6)**

Commandments are such as, "You shall not commit adultery," "You shall not steal"; and ordinances such as, "He who speaks evil of father or mother, let him die the death"; for this is just. But know that the commandment too can be named an ordinance,[^1] as making the man righteous, or rather as being also a just decree of God. For in that day God, having the commandments like a written legal deed, condemns us. "For if I had not come," He says, "and spoken to them, they would not have had sin" (John 15:22); and again, "The word that I have spoken to them, that shall judge them" (cf. John 12:48). But for what reason is "blameless" added to "walking in all the commandments"? Listen: often some walk in the law of God, but do everything in order to be seen by men; such, then, are not blameless; but Zacharias both did the commandments and did them blamelessly, not fulfilling them for the sake of pleasing men.

[^1]: The wordplay turns on δικαίωμα, which spans "ordinance / righteous requirement / just decree / legal deed": «δύναται καὶ ἡ ἐντολὴ δικαίωμα ὀνομάζεσθαι … καὶ τοῦ Θεοῦ οὖσα δικαίωμα … ὥσπερ τι δικαίωμα ἔγγραφον». I keep "ordinance" in the lemma and vary it here where the sense shifts toward a "just decree" and a "written deed."

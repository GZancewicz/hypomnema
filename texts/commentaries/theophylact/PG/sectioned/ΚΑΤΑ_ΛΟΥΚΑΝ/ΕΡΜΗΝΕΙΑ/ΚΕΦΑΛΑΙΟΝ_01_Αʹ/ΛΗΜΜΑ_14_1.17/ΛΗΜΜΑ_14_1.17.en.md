# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 14 · Luke 1:17

> **and he himself will go before him in spirit and power of Elias, to turn hearts of fathers to children, and *the* disobedient in *the* understanding of *the* righteous, to prepare for the Lord a people made ready. (Luke 1:17)**

The angel promises that John will be great, but before the Lord; for many are called great before men, and not before God — such as the hypocrites; but John was great according to the soul, just as also everyone who is scandalized is small according to the soul; for no one great is scandalized, but the small and faint-hearted. The Lord accordingly says: Whoever shall scandalize one of the little ones (Matt. 18:6). And just as those who begot John were righteous before God, so also their child was great before the Lord; and "strong drink" is said of everything able to produce drunkenness, but not being from the vine.[^1] And while still being in the womb of his mother, he was filled with the Holy Spirit, when the Mother of the Lord came to Elizabeth, and the babe leaped, exulting greatly because of the presence of the Lord.

And he turned *the* hearts of fathers to children, that is, of the Hebrews to the apostles; for the Jews were the fathers, but the children of these were the apostles. He turned therefore the hearts of the Jews to the apostles, teaching about Christ and bearing witness; and he who bears witness about Christ altogether makes his disciples also worthy of trust. But John did not turn all, but many; the Lord, however, enlightened all. And in *the* Spirit of Elias he came forth, inasmuch as, just as the grace worked in Elias, so also in John; for both Elias is the forerunner of the second coming, and John of the first. And in *the* power of Elias too, inasmuch as the comings of both accomplish the same thing, both that of Elias and that of John; for they gather *men* toward Christ. And in another way too John came in the power and spirit of Elias, because he too was a dweller of the desert, and without excess, and reproving like that one.

And he turned also the disobedient Jews in the understanding of the righteous, that is, by the teaching of the apostles; for the understanding of the apostles is the grace of the Spirit in them, by which they were governed. And he prepared for the Lord — that is, for Christ — a people made ready, that is, in readiness to receive the preaching. To give an example of what I mean: whenever some prophet would come preaching, not all believed, but those made ready, that is, those who had prepared themselves beforehand for this. For just as, if someone should come into a house by night, not all receive him, but those who are awake and expecting him and prepared to receive him — so also John prepared a people for the Lord, but not the disobedient one, rather the one made ready, which is prepared beforehand, for the reception of Christ.

[^1]: The lemma's word is σίκερα ("strong drink," Luke 1:15), an indeclinable loanword (from Hebrew *shēkār*); Theophylact glosses it here as πᾶν τὸ μέθην ἐμποιεῖν δυνάμενον, οὐκ ὂν δὲ ἐξ ἀμπέλου — anything able to intoxicate that is not made from the vine.

# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 36 · Luke 1:42

> **cried out with a loud voice and said: Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb. (Luke 1:42)**

The Virgin, having heard from the angel that Elizabeth had conceived, ran to her, at once rejoicing over the good of her kinswoman, and at the same time, being most prudent, wishing to be fully assured whether he who had appeared to her had spoken true things, so that from this she might also not be in doubt concerning her own case. For being on her guard, she feared lest she be deceived; and not as one disbelieving, but as one wishing to learn the matter more accurately. Now Zacharias dwelt in the hill country, and for this reason she ran there; but John, having received an exceptional gift beyond the rest of men, leaps in his mother's womb, whence also *he is* greater than the prophets. For they prophesied after being born, whereas he, while yet being in his mother's womb, was deemed worthy of such a gift.

For see: the Virgin greeted Elizabeth, that is, she addressed her. The voice therefore of the Virgin was the voice of the God being made flesh in her; and on this account it also endowed the Forerunner with grace in the womb, and made him prophesy.[^1] For whatever things Elizabeth said prophetically to Mary were not the words of Elizabeth, but of the babe; but the mouth of Elizabeth only served, just as the mouth of Mary also served the Son of God who was in her womb. For when the babe had leaped in the belly, then Elizabeth was filled with the Spirit. For if the babe had not leaped, she would not have prophesied. Just as therefore they say that the prophets, being first in ecstasy and inspired, then prophesy,[^2] so perhaps John also first leaped, as it were being inspired, then prophesied through the mouth of his mother. But what did he prophesy?

[^1]: «ἐχαρίτωσε» — "filled with grace, endowed with grace," echoing the angel's «κεχαριτωμένη» (Luke 1:28); the same grace-root recurs.
[^2]: «ἐξισταμένους καὶ ἐνθουσιῶντας» — technical terms for prophetic rapture: "standing outside themselves" (ecstasy) and "being god-possessed / divinely inspired."

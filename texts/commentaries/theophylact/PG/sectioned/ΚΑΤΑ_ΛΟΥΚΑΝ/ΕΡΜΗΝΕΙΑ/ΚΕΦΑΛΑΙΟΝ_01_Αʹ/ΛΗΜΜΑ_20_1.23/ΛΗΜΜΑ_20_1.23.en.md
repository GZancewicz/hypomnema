# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 20 · Luke 1:23

> **And it came to pass, when the days of his ministration were fulfilled, he departed to his own house. (Luke 1:23)**

Do you see how the Jews awaited and waited until the high priest should come out? But we Christians, as soon as we have entered into the church, if we do not go out, think we are suffering something dreadful. And Zacharias was making signs to the people, who were perhaps asking the cause of his silence; for, not being able to speak, he indicated it by a gesture. But observe that until the days of his ministration were completed he did not depart to his house, but continued in attendance at the temple; for the hill country was indeed truly remote and distant from Jerusalem. Yet nevertheless, even if the priest had had his house in Jerusalem itself, it was not permitted to him to depart outside the court of the temple during the time of his course *of service*; but we — alas! — how we despise the divine services! And that Zacharias could not speak, but made signs to the people, signifies the irrational polity of the Jews;[^1] for, having killed the Word, they cannot render an account concerning what they do or say, but even if you ask them something prophetic, they are muzzled, and cannot give you word and answer.

[^1]: A wordplay runs through the sentence. The Jews' conduct is ἄλογος ("irrational," literally "without word/reason"); and having killed *the Word* (τὸν Λόγον) they can no longer *render a word/account* (λόγον ἀποδοῦναι). The single root λόγ- binds together "reason," "the Word," and "account."

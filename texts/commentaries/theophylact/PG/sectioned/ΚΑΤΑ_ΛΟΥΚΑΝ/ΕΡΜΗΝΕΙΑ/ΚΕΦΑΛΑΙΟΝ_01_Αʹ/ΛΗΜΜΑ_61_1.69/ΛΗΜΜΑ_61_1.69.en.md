# Luke 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 61 · Luke 1:69

> **And you, Bethlehem, are by no means least; for out of you shall come forth one who leads. (Mic. 5:2; cf. Matt. 2:6)**[^1]

And he performed mercy, not with the living only, but also with our fathers (Luke 1:72); for the grace of Christ ran back even unto those, the ones already dead. As, for instance, I mean this: he has given to us the living the hope of resurrection, and we shall be raised; but not we alone shall be counted worthy of this grace, but also those who have died before us; for the whole of nature has obtained the good. And in another way too he performed mercy with the fathers, in that he fulfilled their hopes. For as many things as they were expecting, in Christ they saw accomplished; but indeed the fathers also, seeing their children enjoying such great goods, rejoice, and, becoming partakers of the gladness, they take the mercy up unto themselves.

[^1]: Theophylact's citation is Micah 5:2 in a form close to Matthew 2:6 (Ἐκ σοῦ … ἐξελεύσεται ἡγούμενος). He adduces it to gloss the preceding verse, "a horn of salvation … in the house of David" (Luke 1:69): Bethlehem, the city of David, is that whence the ruler (ἡγούμενος) comes forth. The rendering "one who leads" keeps the participial ἡγούμενος; "a ruler" is equivalent.
[^2]: The sentence breaks off here and is completed by the oath quoted at the head of the next lemma (Luke 1:73; the citation "Blessing I will bless you…," Gen. 22:17).

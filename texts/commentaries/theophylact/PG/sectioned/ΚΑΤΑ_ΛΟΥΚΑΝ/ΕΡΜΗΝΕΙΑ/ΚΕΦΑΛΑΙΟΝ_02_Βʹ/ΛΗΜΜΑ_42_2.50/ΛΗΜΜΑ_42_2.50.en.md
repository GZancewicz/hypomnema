# Luke 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 42 · Luke 2:50

> **But they understood not the saying which he spoke to them. (Luke 2:50)**

He goes up together with his parents to Jerusalem, that through all things he might show that he is not opposed to God,[^1] nor sets himself against the things ordained by law; but when they had completed the days—namely the seven days of the Passover—he remained in Jerusalem. And he was discussing with the Scribes, questioning them from the law; and all were amazed. Dost thou see how he was advancing in wisdom, through being known by many and being marvelled at? For the manifestation of his wisdom, this is his advancement.

But the Theotokos names Joseph "father," knowing that he is not the father; nevertheless, on account of the Jews, that he might not be thought to be from fornication, she calls Joseph his father; and besides, since he had shown a father's care and service in the upbringing, she reasonably named him father—as if the Holy Spirit were honoring him with the appellation of father.

But why were they seeking him? Was it, then, that they held the supposition that he was lost or had gone astray like a child? Away with the thought! For this belonged neither to the all-wise Mary, who had received countless revelations concerning him, nor to Joseph, who himself also had had it revealed to him that he is from the Holy Spirit. But they were seeking him lest he had departed from them, lest he had forsaken them. And when they found him, see how he answers them; for when the Virgin had called Joseph his father, he says: He is not my true Father, for otherwise I would have been in his house; but God is my Father. And for this reason I am in his house, that is, in his temple. But they understood not what he spoke to them; for it was a mystery.

[^1]: Greek ἀντίθεος, literally "against God" or "an adversary of God"; here the sense is that Christ, by keeping the feast, shows he is not set in opposition to God or to God's Law.

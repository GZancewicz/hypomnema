# Luke 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 7 · Luke 2:7

> **she brought forth her firstborn Son, and swaddled him and laid him down in the manger; because there was no place for them in the inn. (Luke 2:7)**

On this account the census takes place, so that, when all had come to their own homelands, the Virgin too might go up to Bethlehem, as to her own homeland, and thus the Lord might be born in Bethlehem, and the prophecy be fulfilled (Mic. 5:2). And it was fitting that, since one God was about to put an end to polytheism, one king also should hold sway, namely Caesar.[^1] And Christ too is registered along with all; for it was fitting that the Lord also be registered together with the inhabited world, that he might sanctify those being registered, and abolish slavery. For just as, having been circumcised, he abolished circumcision, so, having been registered as a slave, he abolished the slavery of our nature. For those who serve the Lord are no longer slaves of men, as the Apostle says:

[^1]: The OCR here reads a bare `νὸς` (line 9), which I take to be ἑνὸς agreeing with Θεοῦ: «ἔδει δὲ Θεοῦ ἑνὸς μέλλοντος παῦσαι τὴν πολυθεΐαν, καὶ ἕνα βασιλέα κρατῆσαι», i.e. "since one God was about to abolish polytheism, one king also should prevail" — the "one God / one king" antithesis that governs the sentence.

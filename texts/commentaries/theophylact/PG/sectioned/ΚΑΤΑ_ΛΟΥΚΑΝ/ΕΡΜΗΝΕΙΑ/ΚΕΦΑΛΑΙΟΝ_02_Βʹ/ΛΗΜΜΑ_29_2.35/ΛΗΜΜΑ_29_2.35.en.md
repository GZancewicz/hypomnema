# Luke 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 29 · Luke 2:35

> **...a sword shall pass through her very soul, so that the thoughts out of many hearts may be revealed. (Luke 2:35)**

Simeon blessed both; but leaving aside the reputed father, he spoke to the true mother: "Behold," he says, "this one is set for the falling and rising of many in Israel"—the falling, of those not believing; and the rising, of those believing. Or also in another way: the Lord is set for the falling of the evils in our souls, and for the rising of the good things. Fornication falls, self-control rises. And in yet another way it can be understood: for Christ is set for a falling, meaning that He Himself is about to suffer and to fall into death, and many are about to rise when He has fallen. Therefore place a stop at "for a falling"; then begin, "and a rising of many."[^1] And the sign is the cross, which until now is spoken against, that is, not accepted by the unbelievers. And the incarnation of the Lord is also called a sign; indeed a strange sign. For God became man; and a virgin, a mother; and this sign, I mean the incarnation of Christ, is spoken against; for some say the body is from heaven; others, that it was according to appearance; others babble some other thing. "And of you yourself also," of the Virgin, "a sword shall pass through the soul"—perhaps the affliction that arose at the passion; and perhaps by "sword" he means also the offense at which she was scandalized, seeing the Lord being crucified. For perhaps she was inwardly wondering: How is it that He who was born without seed, who did wonders, who raised the dead, was crucified, and put to death, and spat upon? And the "so that the thoughts out of many hearts may be revealed," this signifies that the thoughts of many who are scandalized shall be revealed and made manifest, and, having been convicted, they will find a swift healing. As you also, O Virgin, shall be revealed and made manifest, as to what you think concerning Christ; then you shall be confirmed in the faith in Him. Likewise Peter also was revealed when he denied, but the strength of God was shown, He having taken him back through repentance. And in another way too the thoughts out of many hearts were revealed, when the betrayer appeared, and when those loving Him appeared; such as was Joseph, who came to Pilate; such as the women who remained by the cross.

[^1]: Theophylact gives a punctuation directive: Στίξον οὖν ἐν τῷ, εἰς πτῶσιν· εἶτα ἄρξαι, καὶ ἀνάστασιν πολλῶν — i.e. read "set for a falling" (Christ's own death) as a closed clause, and begin a fresh clause with "and a rising of many," so that the many rise on account of His fall.

# Luke 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 17 · Luke 2:21

> **And when eight days were fulfilled for circumcising him, his name also was called Jesus, the name called by the angel before he was conceived in the womb. And when the days of their purification were fulfilled according to the law of Moses, they brought him up to Jerusalem, to present *him* to the Lord; as it is written in the law of the Lord, that: Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord; and to give a sacrifice according to what is said in the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons. (Luke 2:21)**

Since the law had given commandments, those who transgressed them were under a curse. The Lord, therefore, is circumcised, so that in this too, having fulfilled the law and having left out nothing of what it had commanded, he might deliver us[^1] from the curse; and that from this those may be put to shame who say that he was made flesh in appearance only. For how was he circumcised, if he was made flesh in appearance only?

And it is unprofitable also to inquire where that severed member is; for the things which the Scripture has passed over in silence one must not inquire into — and that when there is no benefit at all. But one may say that then, when it was cut off, touching the earth, it sanctified the earth, just as did also the blood and the water which flowed from his side; and by whatever reasons he knows, he kept this member unharmed; and after the resurrection, taking it up again, he was raised, so that in this respect too he might be found lacking nothing — since we too shall receive back our own body perfect in the resurrection.

And take note that the Lord was not conceived at the same moment as the angel's saying, *Behold, you shall conceive a son*, but afterward, when he willed. For see what he says here: *the name called by the angel before he was conceived in the womb*; and from the very wording this is shown: for he did not say, *Behold, you conceive*, but, *Behold, you shall conceive*. So that from these things it is possible *to conclude that the Lord was conceived*[^2] — yet not at the very instant at which the Angel was speaking, but perhaps when he had completed his words. But these things we do not say dogmatically.

[^1]: The text as transmitted reads «ὑμᾶς» ("you"), evidently for «ἡμᾶς» ("us"), which the sense requires; rendered "us."
[^2]: The Greek here is elliptical and probably defective: «Ὥστε ἐκ τούτων ἔστι Κύριος», where «ἔστι» carries the sense "it is possible" but the governing infinitive is missing. The bracketed words supply what the argument requires.

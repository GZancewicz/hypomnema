# Luke 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 26 · Luke 2:32

> **...unto the revelation of the Gentiles, and the glory of Your people Israel. (Luke 2:32)**

Simeon was not a priest, but a God-loving man, and one awaiting that the Christ would come, to comfort the Hebrews, and to release *them* from the slavery under sin, and perhaps also from that under the Romans and Herod; for he who has believed in Christ is truly free, being honored by kings and by all men. See the apostles: were they not slaves of the Romans? But now the kings of the Romans honor and venerate them. Behold, therefore, Christ has become a comfort to these, who are Israelites. This Simeon, therefore, being moved by the Holy Spirit, went up *into* the temple, when the mother brought up the Lord; and having received Him, he confesses Him God; for the saying, "Now You release me, Master," belongs to one confessing that He Himself is Lord of life and death. But observe how the saints reckon the body a bond; wherefore also he says, "Now You release," as from a bond. And he said "according to Your word" because of the oracle which he had received, that he should not die until he saw the Christ. And the "in peace" stands for "in rest"; for as long as a man lives, he is troubled, just as David also says; but having died, he is at peace. And in another way too you will understand the "in peace," that is, in the receiving of the hope. For before I saw the Lord, he says, I was not at peace in my thoughts, awaiting Him and ever anxious as to when He would come; but now, having seen Him and having become at peace and having put off anxiety, so to speak, I am released. And by "salvation" he meant the incarnation of the Only-begotten, which God prepared before all the ages; and before the face of all the peoples He prepared this salvation. For in order that He might save the world, and that His incarnation might be made manifest to all, for this reason He was made flesh. And this salvation is a light unto the revelation of the Gentiles, that is, unto the enlightening of the darkened Gentiles, and a glory of the Israelites; for Christ is a glory of the true people of Israel, inasmuch as from them He has dawned forth; and in Him the truly right-minded glory. This much Simeon said. And it seems to me that the words of David also fit this Simeon: "With length of days will I fill him, and I will show him My salvation." (Ps. 90:16 LXX)

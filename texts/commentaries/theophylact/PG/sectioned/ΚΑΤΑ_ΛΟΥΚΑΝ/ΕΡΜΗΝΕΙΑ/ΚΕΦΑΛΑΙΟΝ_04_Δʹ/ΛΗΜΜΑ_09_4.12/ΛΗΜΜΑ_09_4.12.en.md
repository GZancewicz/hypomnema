# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 9 · Luke 4:12

> **And Jesus answering said to him that, It is said, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. (Luke 4:12)**

The Lord was baptized, that he might sanctify the waters, for our sake who are about to enjoy the grace; but after being baptized, he is led up by the Spirit into a desert mountain. For the Holy Spirit was leading him into the contest against the devil. And he was going[^1] into the desert, that he might give the devil a hold[^2] to attack him; for indeed it is especially against us when we are alone that he sets upon us. And he fasts forty days, and does not exceed the measure of the fast of Moses and Elijah, that he might not give Satan from thence already a suspicion that he is greater than these, but that *the devil* might assault him, supposing that he too is a man; and at the same time also that he might not seem to have been made flesh in appearance *only*.[^3]

But after the baptism he is tempted, showing us that after the baptism temptations will await us; and he fasts, because fasting is a great weapon in temptations, and because after the baptism we too must give ourselves not to indulgence, but to fasting. Therefore the enemy assaults him, first from gluttony, from which he assaulted Adam too; then from love of money, having shown him all the kingdoms. But how did he show them? Some say that he set these before him in *the* mind; yet not in the mind, I think, but perceptibly he showed these to him, making *them* appear in a phantasm, though the Lord was not deluded by the appearance.

A third time he assaults him, from love of glory: For if thou art Son of God, he says, cast thyself down. And flattering him, he babbled such things, *to see* if perhaps, being deceived by the flattery and wishing to show himself Son of God, he might cast himself down, and thus it might be discerned by him who he is. But the Lord—see how he repels him from the Scripture: For thou shalt not tempt, he says, the Lord thy God. For one must not cast oneself into manifest dangers, testing whether God will help him.

And note this too, that it is a great benefit to know the Scriptures; for indeed the Lord also repelled Satan from the Scriptures. For both the *saying*, Man shall not live by bread alone, is Mosaic, spoken concerning the manna—for the manna, not being bread, nevertheless fed the people—and the *saying*, But thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and the, Thou shalt not tempt: these too are Mosaic.

[^1]: OCR: the manuscript's ἀπειοιν is read as ἀπῄει, "he was going/departing."
[^2]: λαβήν — literally a "handle" or "hold," an occasion or purchase for the attack (as in wrestling); the imagery of a grip is deliberate.
[^3]: The two purposes guard against the two errors: that Satan might not too soon suspect his divinity (and so refrain from attacking a mere "man"), and that the incarnation might not be thought merely apparent (κατὰ φαντασίαν)—a rejection of docetism.

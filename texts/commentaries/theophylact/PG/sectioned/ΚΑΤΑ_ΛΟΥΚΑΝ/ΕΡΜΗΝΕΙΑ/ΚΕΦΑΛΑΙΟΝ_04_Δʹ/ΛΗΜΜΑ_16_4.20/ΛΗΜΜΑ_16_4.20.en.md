# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 16 · Luke 4:20

> **Having rolled up the book, having given it back to the attendant, he sat down; and the eyes of all in the synagogue were fixed intently upon him. And he began to say to them, that "Today this scripture is fulfilled in your ears." (Luke 4:20)**

The Lord wished to establish himself as manifest to the Israelites, and *to show* that he had been anointed by God and Father for the saving of those under heaven. And he arranges this too in a wondrous way: manifesting himself before the others to those in Nazareth, with whom he had been brought up; so that he might also teach us first to do good to our own, and to instruct these, then to pour out love of humankind upon the rest as well. And when the book had been handed to him he unrolled it, and found, not what came to hand by chance, but what he himself wished. For do not suppose that, when he opened the book by coincidence, that place was found where the things concerning him had been written; but this came to pass because he himself willed it.

And what had been written? That "the Spirit of the Lord anointed me," that is, consecrated me. "He appointed me to preach good news to the poor," that is, to the nations, which, having neither law nor prophets, were in reality in great poverty; but "those crushed in heart" were perhaps those from Israel, whose heart was formerly both great and lofty, and a house of God. But afterward, when they had committed idolatry and sinned in various ways, their heart was crushed, and was pulled down, and instead of being a house of God, became a den of robbers (cf. Matt. 21:13). Therefore the Lord came, that he might heal these too, and might give both release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, both to those from the nations and to those from Israel. For both these parts were both captive under Satan and blind.

But these things can also be understood concerning the dead; for these too, being captives and shattered, were released from the authority of Hades through the resurrection. And he also proclaimed an acceptable year. But what was the acceptable year? Perhaps indeed even the age to come, concerning which the Lord proclaimed, saying: "In that day you will ask me nothing" (John 16:23). And again: "An hour is coming when the dead will rise" (John 5:28). And that year is "acceptable," that is, longed for by the righteous and beloved; which all who labor here await.

# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 12 · Luke 4:15

> **he himself taught in their synagogues, being glorified by all. (Luke 4:15)**

Luke says that the Lord completed every temptation, although he was tempted with three temptations. Why? Because these three are the chief heads of all the temptations: that of love of money, that of gluttony, that of love of glory. Now the one of gluttony he repelled by saying that, Man shall not live by bread alone; for he brought this temptation to him first, as also to Adam. For Adam he could trip up neither from covetousness—for he had no one to be defrauded, being alone—nor from anger, for he had no one at whom to be angry, nor from envy; from the belly, therefore, he tripped him up.

And the one of love of money he repelled by not falling down nor worshiping him. For to *the devil*, who showed him all the kingdoms perceptibly in a phantasm, he did not stoop. Although indeed some understood this not concerning perceptible kingdoms, but concerning intelligible ones: as, for instance, that he showed him the kingdom of licentiousness, the kingdom of envy, and simply the kingdoms of all the sins; and it was as though he said such things to him: If thou wishest to reign over these passions, and hast come for this—to snatch away those held fast by me—fall down and worship me, and take all those ruled by me. But the Lord wishes to reign, and has come for this, yet to reign not with sin, nor without struggle, but having wrestled and conquered. These things some have understood; and to whomever it is agreeable, let him accept them. But the one of love of glory too he repelled, by speaking the scriptural saying. Deliver us also, then, O Lord, from these three heads of the dragon.

And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit. It seems to me that Jesus returned inspired;[^1] for this is *the meaning of* the, in the power of the Spirit. And perhaps—attend to the Scripture—that after he conquered the tempter and showed his power, then is written the, in the power of the Spirit. But the, he departed from him until a season, has such a meaning as this: the devil assaults the Lord through two passions, pleasure and pain; for indeed he assaulted the Lord through pleasure, as on the mountain; but he departed from him until a season, that is, until the season of the cross—for then he was about to assault him through pain.

[^1]: ἐνθουσιῶν—literally "being inspired," possessed or carried along by God (from ἔνθεος); Theophylact uses it to gloss "in the power of the Spirit."

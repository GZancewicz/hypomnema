# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 35 · Luke 4:44

> **…preaching in the synagogues of Galilee. (Luke 4:44)**

The Master of all was fed by being received as a guest among his disciples; just as now, then, being received as a guest with Peter as well, he heals that man's mother-in-law — so that he might train you too not to send away the kindnesses of the lowly. And he does not simply cure her from the disease, but also puts robustness and strength into her, for the ministering.

If we too, therefore, receive him, he will quench our fever as well — both that of anger and that of desire; and he will raise us up so as to minister to him, that is, to do the things pleasing to him.

But observe for me the faith of the crowd as well, how even as the sun was setting they were bringing the sick, not being hindered even by the time. And he does not permit the demons to speak — at once because he has no need of the praise that comes from the unclean (for praise is not comely in the mouth of sinners [Sir. 15:9]),[^1] and at the same time because he does not wish to kindle the envy of the Hebrews out of his being praised by all. But the crowds seek him even when he has departed into a desert place; yet he does not confine himself *to one place*.[^2] "But to the other cities also," he says, "I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God." Whence we too must not shrink back, nor be content when shut up in one place, but go about everywhere, if somehow we may benefit someone.

[^1]: A quotation of Sirach 15:9 (LXX): «οὐχ ὡραῖος αἶνος ἐν στόματι ἁμαρτωλοῦ». Theophylact has the plural «ἁμαρτωλῶν» ("of sinners").
[^2]: The OCR reads «οἱ δὲ ὄχλοι καὶ εἰς ἔρημον τόπον ἀπελθόντα ζητοῦσι περιορίζεται», where a word (and evidently the negative) has dropped out between «ζητοῦσι» and «περιορίζεται». The sense, confirmed by the following «Ἀλλὰ καὶ ταῖς ἑτέραις… πόλεσιν», is that though the crowds seek him, he does not restrict himself to one place; supplied *to one place* accordingly.

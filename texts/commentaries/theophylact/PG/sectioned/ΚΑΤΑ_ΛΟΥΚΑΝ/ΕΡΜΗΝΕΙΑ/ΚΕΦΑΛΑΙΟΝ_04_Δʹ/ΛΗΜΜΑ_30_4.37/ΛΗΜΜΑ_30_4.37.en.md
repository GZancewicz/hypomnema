# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 30 · Luke 4:37

> **a report went out about him into every place of the surrounding region.» (Luke 4:37)**

Since he does not draw the unbelievers to faith even by teaching,[^1] for this reason the Lord brings forward his wonder-workings, as a kind of most effective medicine. He works wonders, therefore, in Capernaum; for these were most unbelieving and in need of many things toward faith. Accordingly, since he had taught sufficiently, and this as one having authority (for he did not say, "Thus says the Lord," but "I say to you"), *showing* that he was not even as one of the prophets, but the true Son of God, after these things he brings on also the wonder, and heals the demoniac.

But the demon first confesses the enmity, so that the testimony might be trustworthy; then he adds the testimony: "I know thee, who thou art: the holy one of God." First, therefore, he accuses him: "Why hast thou come here to destroy us?" Then he flatters, thinking that the Lord, softened by the flattery, would let him be; so senseless is wickedness. But the Lord, teaching us that we should not need the testimony and commendation that comes from demons, says: "Be silenced, and come out of him." And he permits the demon to throw the man, so that the bystanders might learn that he really did have a demon. And the words were that one's, even though the tongue of the man served *as its instrument*.

And indeed all spoke together with one another, marveling at the things that had happened, and saying: "What is this word?" — that is, what is this command that he commands, that "Come out of him, and be silenced"?

But know that even now many have demons, as many as accomplish the desires of the demons. For example, there is someone wrathful; this one has a demon of wrath. But if Jesus comes into the synagogue — that is, when the mind is gathered together and not dispersed — then he will say to the demon of wrath, which is uncontrollable: "Be silenced"; and straightway, having thrown the man into the midst, it will come out from him. But what it is to throw into the midst, learn: the man ought neither to be altogether wrathful and bitter — for that is bestial — nor altogether without anger — for that is insensible — but to walk the middle road, and to have his wrath against wickedness. Whenever, therefore, the evil spirit throws someone into the midst, it comes out from him.

[^1]: The OCR here is corrupt: the text reads οὔτε. ιδασκαλίᾳ. Reconstructed as οὐδὲ διδασκαλίᾳ ("not even by teaching"), which the syntax and sense require.

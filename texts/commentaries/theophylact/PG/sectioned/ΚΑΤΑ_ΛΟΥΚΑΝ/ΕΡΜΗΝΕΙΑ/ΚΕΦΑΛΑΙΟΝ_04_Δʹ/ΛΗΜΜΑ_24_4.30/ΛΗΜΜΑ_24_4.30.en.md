# Luke 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 24 · Luke 4:30

> **But he, passing through the midst of them, went away. (Luke 4:30)**

The crowds, hearing the things spoken by Christ, marveled at the words of grace. But while marveling, they derided him, saying: Is not this the son of the carpenter? And yet what was there to prevent him from being wonderful and worthy of worship? Do you not see how many things he does? Do you not hear how many things he says? And then you deride his father? Truly it is right to say of them: Behold a foolish people and without heart; they have eyes, and see not; ears, and hear not (cf. Jer. 5:21).

The Lord, therefore, what does he say to them? "Doubtless you will say to me: In your own country do the wonders,[^1] such as also in Capernaum" (Luke 4:23). For this is the meaning of, "Physician, heal yourself"; for this was a common saying among the Jews, applied to physicians who were themselves sick. "But I say to you, that I would have done many signs also among you, my fellow-countrymen; but I know the affliction that is common and befalls all, that even the most excellent of things are despised, when they are not rare, but common and familiar, and all may freely enjoy them; for men are always accustomed both to care for and to marvel at things strange and rare, but to despise things common and familiar; for this reason no prophet is honored in his own country, but if he comes from somewhere else, they marvel at him. So too Elijah — the widows of Judea did not receive him, but the woman of Sarepta received him; and Elisha cleansed the foreign leper, who showed forth faith in him; for his familiars and fellow-countrymen did not believe in him, and for this reason were not cleansed. Likewise, therefore, I too, to you my fellow-countrymen do not seem wonderful, but am despised; therefore neither do I work signs; but to those in Capernaum, seeming wonderful, I both work signs and am acceptable to them."

But hearing these things in the synagogue, they were filled with wrath, when they ought to have marveled, and attempted to cast him down headlong. But he, passing through the midst of them, went away; not fleeing suffering, but awaiting the *proper* time. For he had come in order to suffer on our behalf; but now, the preaching having its beginning, he ought not to give himself over to death, but, having taught sufficiently, so to be brought to his consummation.[^2] So that from this it is clear, that not even when he was crucified was he crucified unwillingly, but willingly gave himself over to death.

And know that the fatherland of the prophets is the synagogue of the Jews, in which the prophets are dishonored; for neither are they accepted; but we, the strangers, received them. For the widow — I mean the Church from the nations — received Elijah, that is, the prophetic word, when a spiritual famine had come upon Judea, a famine of hearing the word of God (cf. Amos 8:11). And concerning this widow the prophet says, that many are the children of the desolate rather than of her that has the husband (Isa. 54:1); and again, that the barren has borne seven, and she that is abundant in children has grown weak (1 Sam. 2:5).

[^1]: The OCR reads θαυμάσια Ἐσα; read θαυμάσια ὅσα, "the wonders, such as (also in Capernaum)."
[^2]: Greek τελειωθῆναι — "to be perfected/completed," but in patristic usage regularly a euphemism for consummation by death (martyrdom); rendered here "to be brought to his consummation" to preserve both senses.

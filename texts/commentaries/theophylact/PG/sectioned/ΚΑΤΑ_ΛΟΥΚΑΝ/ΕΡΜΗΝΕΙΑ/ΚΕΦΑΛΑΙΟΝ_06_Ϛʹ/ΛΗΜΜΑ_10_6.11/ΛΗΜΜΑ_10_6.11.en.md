# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 10 · Luke 6:11

> **But they were filled with folly, and were discussing with one another what they might do to Jesus. (Luke 6:11)**

The things which we said in [the commentary] on Matthew are clear; but now we say this, that he has a withered hand who does not do any of the works of virtue. For the hand is an instrument of activity, but he who has withered it[^1] is wholly idle. Whoever, therefore, wishes to have his hand made whole,[^2] on the Sabbath he will have it made whole. Something like what I mean: it is not possible to work any of the works of virtue unless one ceases from evil. For turn away first from the evil, and then do the good (cf. Ps. 34:14).[^3] When, therefore, you keep the Sabbath—that is, you rest[^4] from the works of evil—then you will stretch out your hand to the *works* of virtue, and it will be restored to you. And fittingly he said, "It was restored." For there was a time when human nature had the working of the good, and had the hand—that is, the active power—sound. Then, having lost it, by the grace of Christ it received it back, and was restored to its former good.

[^1]: Greek ὃς δὲ ἐξήρανται ταύτην, perfect passive with active force: "he in whom it has been withered / who has let it wither."
[^2]: OCR: the text reads Βέλει and νιασθήσεται, read here as θέλει ("wishes") and ὑγιασθήσεται ("will be made whole"). The verb ὑγιάζω is rendered "make whole" throughout.
[^3]: The wording ἔκκλινον ... ἀπὸ τοῦ κακοῦ, καὶ ... ποίησον τὸ ἀγαθόν echoes Ps. 34:14 LXX (33:15): ἔκκλινον ἀπὸ κακοῦ καὶ ποίησον ἀγαθόν.
[^4]: ἀργήσῃς, "be idle / cease from work"; here rendered "rest," picking up the Sabbath (σαββατίσῃς) imagery. OCR ἀργήσῃε read as ἀργήσῃς.

# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 33 · Luke 6:38

> **Give, and it shall be given to you. Good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over, shall they give into your bosom. For with the same measure with which you measure, it shall be measured back to you. And he spoke also a parable to them: Can a blind man guide a blind man? Will not both fall into a pit? A disciple is not above his teacher; but everyone fully trained shall be as his teacher. (Luke 6:38-40)**

A most grievous disease of our souls the Lord cuts away — I mean the root of disdain. For he who does not examine himself, but looks only upon his neighbor's affairs, and wishes to find fault with these, it is evident that, being caught by disdain, he is unaware of himself. For he supposes altogether concerning himself that he does not sin, and on account of this he accuses the others who sin. If therefore you wish to be uncondemned, do not condemn others.

For tell me, why do you condemn another, as one altogether transgressing the divine laws? But do you yourself not transgress a divine law? I leave aside the other sins; but in this very thing, the judging of others — for a divine law altogether commands you not to judge your brother — so that you too transgress a law. And being a transgressor, you ought not to judge another as a transgressor; for the judge must be higher than the nature that sins.

Release, therefore, and you shall be released; give, and it shall be given to you. For good measure, pressed down and shaken together and running over, shall they give into your bosom. For the Lord does not measure back sparingly, but richly. Just as, therefore, if you should be about to measure, say, flour, you press it down and shake it and make it run over, if indeed you wish to measure it without stinginess — so also the Lord will give you a measure great and running over.

But someone more acute might inquire: how, having said that "With what measure you measure, it shall be measured back to you," does he again say that "a measure running over shall they give into your bosom"? For if it runs over, how is it the same? We say, then, that he did not say "With so great a measure shall it be measured back to you," but "with the same." For if he had said "with so great a measure," then the saying would have had difficulty and contradiction; but as it is, having said "with the same," he resolves the contradiction. For it is possible to give with the same measure, yet not with one so great. This at any rate the Lord says: that "You have done a kindness, you shall be shown a kindness" — this is the same measure; but the "running over" is that, having done a kindness once, you shall be shown a kindness ten-thousandfold.

The same holds also in the case of condemnation. For he who condemns, and then is condemned, receives back with the same measure; but inasmuch as he is condemned more, because he condemned one like himself, in this respect the measure is running over.

And having said these things, and having restrained us from condemning, the Lord speaks to us also a parable, that is, an example. For tell me, he says, you who judge another and yourself sin the same things — are you not like a blind man guiding a blind man? For if you judge the other, but you yourself fall into the same things, both are blind; and you seem to guide that one toward the good through condemning, but you do not guide him. For how will that one be guided toward the good by you, when you too stumble? For a disciple is not above his teacher. If therefore you, who seem to be the teacher and guide, stumble, then altogether, in some way, so also does the one being taught and guided. For the fully-trained disciple — that is, the one made perfect — shall be as his teacher.

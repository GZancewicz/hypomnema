# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 22 · Luke 6:26

> **—all men speak well of you. For according to these things their fathers did to the false prophets. (Luke 6:26)**

After appointing[^1] the disciples, he orders them—through both the beatitudes[^2] and the teaching—toward a more spiritual condition. For it is toward them that he directs his discourse. And first he pronounces blessed—that is[^3]—those who have a disposition free from love of money. And, put simply, all the beatitudes teach us frugality, and humility, and self-abasement, and reproach. Just as, therefore, the "Woe" is allotted to those others—namely to the rich in the present age, of whom he says also that they have their consolation in full, that is, that here in the present age they receive back their gladness—and to those who laugh, and live in luxury, and are praised.[^4] Let us shudder,[^5] brothers, at how those who are praised by men incur the "Woe." For one ought[^6] indeed to be praised even by men, but first by God.

[^1]: χειροτονῆσαι, literally "to elect by show of hands"; used of ordination/appointment to office.
[^2]: The OCR reads κακαοισμῶν; reconstructed as μακαρισμῶν ("beatitudes"), which the sense and the following μακαρισμοί require.
[^3]: The OCR reads τοὺτ ετε; reconstructed as τουτέστι ("that is").
[^4]: The OCR reads ἐπκινουμένοις; reconstructed as ἐπαινουμένοις ("who are praised"), matching the following sentence about praise from men.
[^5]: The OCR reads Ρρίξωμεν; reconstructed as Φρίξωμεν ("let us shudder").
[^6]: The OCR reads Δτῖ; reconstructed as Δεῖ ("it is necessary / one ought"). Here μέν … ἀλλά marks a genuine antithesis: praise from men is permissible, but praise from God must come first.

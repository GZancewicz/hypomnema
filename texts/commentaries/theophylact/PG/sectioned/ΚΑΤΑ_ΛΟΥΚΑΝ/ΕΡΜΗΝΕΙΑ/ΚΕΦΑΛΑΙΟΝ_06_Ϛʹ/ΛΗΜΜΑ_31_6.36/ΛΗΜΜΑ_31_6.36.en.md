# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 31 · Luke 6:36

> **Become therefore compassionate, just as also your heavenly Father is compassionate. (Luke 6:36)**[^1]

Since the apostles were about to be sent forth for the preaching, and there were expected from this many persecutors and plotters against them; if therefore the apostles, being distressed at the persecution, and then wishing to avenge themselves on those who had grieved them, were to fall silent and not teach, the sun of the Gospel would have been extinguished. On account of this, then, the Lord, anticipating, exhorts the apostles not to proceed toward vengeance on their enemies, but to bear nobly all the things that befall *them*, even if some insult *them*, even if *others* unholily plot *against them* — as also he himself did upon the cross, saying: Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do (Luke 23:34).

Then, so that the apostles might not have it to say that such a command is impossible — the loving of one's enemies — he says: What you would wish for yourself, this show also to the others. And become such toward others as you would wish others to become toward yourself. If you wish your enemies to be hard and unsympathetic and irascible toward you, be such yourself also; but if the contrary — good and sympathetic and unresentful of wrongs — do not consider it impossible for yourself also to be such.

Do you see a law implanted, inscribed in our hearts? So also the Lord said: In those days, giving my laws, I will inscribe them upon their understanding and upon their hearts.[^2] Then he imposes also another necessity upon us, namely: If you love those who love you, you are like sinners and gentiles. But if you love those who act wickedly toward you, you are like God, who is good toward the ungrateful and the wicked. Which, then, do you wish — to be like the sinners, or to be like God?

Do you see divine teaching? He first persuaded you from the natural law: for what you wish to happen to you, this do you also to others. Then he persuades you also from the end and from the gain;[^3] for as a reward he promises you the becoming like God.

[^1]: Theophylact's lemma reads ὁ Πατὴρ ὑμῶν ὁ οὐράνιος οἰκτίρμων ἐστίν, adding ὁ οὐράνιος ("heavenly"), which the common Lukan text lacks (cf. Matt. 5:48). οἰκτίρμων rendered "compassionate" (full of pity) throughout.
[^2]: A loose quotation of Jer. 38:33 LXX (MT 31:33), as cited also at Heb. 8:10; Theophylact conflates the wording.
[^3]: The Greek here is OCR-damaged: ἀπὸ τοῦ τέλους καὶ τοῦ κι[.]θοῦ. Reconstructed as ἀπὸ τοῦ τέλους καὶ τοῦ κέρδους ("from the end and from the gain/reward"), confirmed by the following μισθόν ("reward").

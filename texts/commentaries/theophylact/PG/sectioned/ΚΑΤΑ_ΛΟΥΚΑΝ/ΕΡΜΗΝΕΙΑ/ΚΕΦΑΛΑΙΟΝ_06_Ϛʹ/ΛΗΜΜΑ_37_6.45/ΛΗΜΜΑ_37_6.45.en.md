# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 37 · Luke 6:45

> **A good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth the good; and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings forth the evil. For out of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks. (Luke 6:45)**

Why do you look, he says, at the mote — that is, at the small sin of your brother — but the beam, that is, the great sin that is yours, you do not perceive? And this applies to all, but especially to teachers and rulers, who punish the sins of those under their hand even when these are small, but leave their own, even should they be great, unpunished. Therefore the Lord also calls these hypocrites, as appearing to be one thing — from their punishing the sins of others, for they appear righteous — but being another, from their own sinning yet worse.

Then he also confirms his word from an example. For just as, he says, there is no good tree producing corrupt fruit, nor a corrupt tree producing good fruit, so also he who undertakes to make others sober-minded and to correct them and to lead them over to what is better would not be evil; but if he were evil, he would not make others better.[^1] For the heart of each is a treasure, and if it have good things, the man too is good and speaks good things; but if the heart is full of evil things, the man too is evil and speaks evil things.

But you can also understand all these things as being spoken concerning the Pharisees. For directing himself at them, he says: Cast out first the beam from your eye, and then the mote that is in the eye of your brother (Luke 6:42) — just as he also says elsewhere: they who swallow the camel and strain out the gnat (Matt. 23:24). How then, he says, will you, O Pharisees, being corrupt trees, be able to bring forth good fruits? For your speech too is corrupt, as is your life; for out of the abundance of the heart you speak. How then will you correct others and punish the sins of others, when you yourselves sin the greater things?

[^1]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupt: the manuscript reads «καὶ μετάνειν ἐπὶ τὸ βέλτιον, οὐκ ἂν εἴς πονηρὸς, εἰ δὲ πονηρὸς αη». Read as «καὶ μετάγειν ἐπὶ τὸ βέλτιον, οὐκ ἂν εἴη πονηρός, εἰ δὲ πονηρὸς ᾖ» ("to lead over to what is better … would not be evil; but if he were evil"), which the translation follows.

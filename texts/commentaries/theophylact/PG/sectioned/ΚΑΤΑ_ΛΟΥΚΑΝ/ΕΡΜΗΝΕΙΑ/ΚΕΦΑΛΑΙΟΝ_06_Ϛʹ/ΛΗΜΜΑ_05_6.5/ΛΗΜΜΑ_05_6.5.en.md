# Luke 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 5 · Luke 6:5

> **he said to them that the Son of Man is Lord also of the Sabbath. (Luke 6:5)**

The Jews used to call every feast a Sabbath; for the Sabbath is a rest.[^1] Often, therefore, the feast fell on the Preparation, and they would call the Preparation a Sabbath on account of the feast; then the Sabbath properly so called they would name "second-first," as being second, since another feast and Sabbath had preceded. So it happened at that time too that such a thing occurred, and that Sabbath was named "second-first."

Now when the Pharisees were accusing the disciples that on the Sabbath they were eating while plucking — that is, pulling up the ears of grain — and rubbing, which is grinding *them* with their hands, the Lord brings forward David, who hungered and ate the loaves of the presentation [i.e. the shewbread]. For fleeing from Saul, he came to Abiathar the high priest, and deceived him, saying that the king was sending him on some most necessary task; and indeed, being hungry, he received from the priest the loaves of the presentation also, which each day they used to set out — twelve upon the sacred table, six on the right and six on the left. And he received also the sword of Goliath.

Having told them this history, the Lord shames them by the deed of David. For if you honor David, he says, how do you condemn my disciples? Besides, the Son of Man — that is, I — am Lord of the Sabbath, as Fashioner and Maker and Master and lawgiver, having authority to abolish the Sabbath. For no other could be called Son of Man except Christ, who, being Son of God, paradoxically counted it worthy also to become and to be called Son of Man for the sake of men. For that you and I are called son of man is nothing new; but it is fitting that he be called Son of Man — the one who was paradoxically made man.

[^1]: Greek ἀνάπαυσις, "rest, cessation"; the etymological point (Sabbath = rest) drives the explanation of why every festal day of cessation could bear the name "Sabbath."

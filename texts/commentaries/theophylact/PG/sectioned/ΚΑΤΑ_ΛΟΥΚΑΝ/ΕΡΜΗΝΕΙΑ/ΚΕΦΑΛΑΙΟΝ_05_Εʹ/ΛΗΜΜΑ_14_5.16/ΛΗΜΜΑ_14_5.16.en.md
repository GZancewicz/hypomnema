# Luke 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 14 · Luke 5:16

> **But he himself was withdrawing into the deserts, and praying. (Luke 5:16)**

Worthy of admiration is the leper, in that, holding a thought befitting God concerning the Lord, he says, *If you will, you can cleanse me.* For this shows that he was thinking about Christ as about God; for he did not approach him as a physician[^1] — for leprosy is not to be attempted by physicians' hands — but as God; for it belongs to him alone to heal such things. But the Lord touches him not without reason, but since according to the law the one touching a leper was deemed to be unclean, wishing to show that he has no necessity to keep such petty niceties of the law, but is himself the Lord of the law, and that the clean is in no way defiled by what seems to be unclean, but *it is* the leprosy of souls, that is what defiles; for this reason he touches; and at the same time, also that he might show that his holy flesh has divine power, being both cleansing and life-giving, as being the flesh of God the Word. But he charges the leper to tell no one, teaching us not to seek praise from those who are benefited. But *go*, he says, *show yourself to the priest, and offer the gift for a testimony to them*; for there was a law, that the priest should examine those stricken with leprosy, and judge whether they had been cleansed; and if within seven days he had been cleansed, the leper remained inside the city; but if not, he was cast out. This, then, the Lord says,

[^1]: The Greek here is corrupt: the text reads προσήρχετο γὰρ λέπρα, apparently a garbling of οὐ γὰρ ὡς ἰατρῷ προσήρχετο· ἡ γὰρ λέπρα ἰατρῶν χερσὶν ἀνεπιχείρητος ("for he did not approach him as a physician, for leprosy is not to be attempted by physicians' hands"), which the sense of the passage requires; translated accordingly.

# Luke 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 28 · Luke 5:32

> **Jesus saw Matthew the tax-collector sitting at the tax-office.[^1] (cf. Matt. 9:9)**

But Luke and Mark, honoring the evangelist, bring forward into the midst his other name, that of Levi. And marvel at God's love for man, how he plunders the vessels of the evil one. For a tax-collector is a vessel of the evil one, and a wicked beast. Those who have had experience of the bitter exactors know it. For these tax-collectors, who buy up the public taxes, do so with a view to making extra gain against their own souls. Not only Matthew, then, did the Lord gain, but he was eager to gain also the other tax-collectors with whom he was dining. For on this account he deigns to be dined with, that he might draw those men too to himself.

But when the Pharisees fault him, see what they hear! I have not come, he says, to call the righteous — that is, you who justify yourselves — but I came to call sinners, not that they should remain in sin, but that they should repent. And otherwise: I came not to call the righteous. For neither do I find any, since all have sinned. Since if it were possible to find righteous ones, I would not have come.

And a tax-collector is also everyone who serves the world-ruler [i.e. the ruler of this world] and offers dues to the flesh. The glutton renders foods to the belly as tax,[^2] the fornicator the filthy intercourse, and another renders another thing. When therefore the Lord — that is, the evangelic word — sees him sitting at the tax-office, that is, not advancing nor progressing and moving on into greater wickedness, but as it were idle, then he will rise up from the evil and will follow Jesus, and he receives the Lord into the house of his soul. But the Pharisees, the arrogant and severed demons — for this is what "Pharisee" means, one severed from the others[^3] — murmur, that he eats with the sinners.

[^1]: OCR — the fragment opens «Εδε», restored to εἶδε ("saw"). The clause is Matthew's own self-reference in his Gospel (cf. Matt. 9:9 «εἶδεν ἄνθρωπον καθήμενον ἐπὶ τὸ τελώνιον, Ματθαῖον λεγόμενον»), quoted here as Theophylact has it.
[^2]: A play on the tax-vocabulary running through the passage: τελώνης "tax-collector," τέλη "taxes/dues," τελεῖ "pays/renders as tax." The one enslaved to a vice "pays it as toll" to the flesh.
[^3]: Etymological wordplay: «Φαρισαῖος» is glossed by «ἀποκεκομμένος», "cut off, severed" (the Pharisee as the "separated one").

# Luke 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 24 · Luke 5:25

> **Immediately, rising up before them, having taken up that on which he lay, he went away to his house glorifying God; and amazement seized all. And they glorified God, and were filled with fear, saying that: We have seen incredible things[^1] today. (Luke 5:25-26)**

It was fitting, the enemies being gathered together, that the Lord should work some more novel wonder; therefore he heals a man sick with a disease hard to cure, so that through healing such a malady he might also cure the hard-to-treat madness of the Pharisees. First, then, he heals the things of the soul, saying, *Your sins are forgiven you*, so that we may learn that the many of our infirmities are born from the sins; then he heals also the things of the body, having seen the faith of those who brought *him*. For often through the faith of some he saves others.

But the Pharisees say, *Why does this man speak blasphemies? Who is able to forgive sins except God alone?* And these things they say, condemning him to death; for the law commanded that the one who spoke blasphemy against God should undergo death. The Lord then, showing them that he is truly God and does not boast in vain, persuades them from another sign. For the things which they were reasoning, these he himself perceives; whence it is altogether clear that he is God, for it belongs to God to know hearts.

He says, therefore: What seems to you easier, to forgive sins, or to make a body healthy? Certainly, according to your verdict, the forgiving of sins seems easier, as being invisible and not open to refutation, although it is the more difficult; but the healing of a body seems more difficult, as being visible, although it is easier by nature. Nevertheless, I will do both this and that. And through healing the body, which seems to you more difficult, I will give assurance also of the healing of the soul, which,[^2] even if it is difficult, seems to you easy, because of its being invisible.

But observe that on the earth sins are forgiven. For as long as we are on the earth, we are able to wipe out our sins; after we have risen away from the earth, however, we ourselves are no longer able through confession to wipe out our sins.[^3]

[^1]: Greek παράδοξα, "things beyond expectation, marvels"; rendered "incredible things."
[^2]: The OCR here is corrupt: line 34 reads Κοις εἰ καὶ δύσκολός ἐστι. The sense requires a relative referring back to "the healing of the soul" (τὴν θεραπείαν τῆς ψυχῆς), i.e. ἥτις εἰ καὶ δύσκολός ἐστι, "which, even if it is difficult." Translated on that reconstruction.
[^3]: The Greek that follows in the source (Ἀποπαεεῖςάπαζτύπο · ·τʹτ·τό) is garbled OCR and illegible [?]; it appears to be a corrupt trailing fragment and is not translated.

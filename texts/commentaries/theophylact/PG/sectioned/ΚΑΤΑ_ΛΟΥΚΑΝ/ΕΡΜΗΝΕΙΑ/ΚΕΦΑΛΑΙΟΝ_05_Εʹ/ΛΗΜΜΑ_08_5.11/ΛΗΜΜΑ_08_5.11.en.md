# Luke 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 8 · Luke 5:11

> **having brought the boats down to the land, having left all, they followed him. (Luke 5:11)**

The Lord flees glory, but *glory* so much the more pursues him. So then, when the crowd was pressing upon him, he enters into the boat, that from the boat he might teach those standing on the shore, all of them being over against his face, and none coming behind his back. But when he had taught the crowd from the boat, he does not let the owner of the boat go unrewarded, but doubly benefits him: both because he granted him a multitude of fish, and because he made him his disciple. But marvel at the Lord's dispensation, how he draws each one through what is his own and familiar to him; for as *he drew* the magi through the star, so also the fishermen through the fish.

And see the fairness of Christ, how he asks Peter to put out from the land. And *Peter* was so obedient,[^1] that a man whom he did not know he both received into his boat and obeyed in all things. For when he told him to put out into the deep, he did not take it ill, nor did he say, "Having toiled all night and gained nothing, shall I now be persuaded by you, and give myself over to a second round of labors?" He said none of these things, but rather, "At your word I will let down the net" — so fervent was he in faith even before *his* faith; whence he catches so many fish that, being unable to draw them up, *he filled* also the other boat;[^2] and by a nod he summons them, since because of the astonishment at the catch he could not even speak. And out of great reverence Peter begs Jesus not to be in the boat, calling himself a sinner and not worthy to be with him.

But understand these things also according to the anagogical *sense*, if you wish. The boat is the Synagogue of the Jews; Peter holds the type of the teachers under the Law. So the teachers before Christ toiled all night — for the time before Christ's coming was night — and took nothing. But when Christ had come, and day had dawned, the apostles, set in the place of the teachers of the Law, at his word — that is, at his command — let down the net of the Gospel, and catch alive a multitude of men. But the apostles alone cannot draw the net of the fish, and they beckon also to their partners and companions, and *these* lend a hand together with them. And these are the pastors and teachers of the Churches in each successive age, who, interpreting and teaching the apostolic *writings*, join with the apostles in catching men alive.

And consider also the wording of "They let down the net." For the Gospel is the net; but since it has a lowly and prosaic diction, condescending to the simplicity of the hearers, for this reason it is said to be let down.

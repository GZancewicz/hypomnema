# Luke 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 34 · Luke 5:39

> **And no one having drunk old immediately wants new; for he says: "The old is more agreeable."[^1] (Luke 5:39)**

We have spoken about these things in the *Commentary* on Matthew. But now also we will say concisely that he names the apostles sons of the bridechamber. For the coming of the Lord is likened to a wedding, because, having betrothed the Church, he took her *to himself*. Accordingly the apostles must not fast now; for the disciples of John are obliged to fast, inasmuch as their teacher achieved virtue by toil and labor. "For John came," he says, "neither eating nor drinking." But my disciples, as being with me, who am God the Word, do not for the present need the benefit that comes from fasting, having been graced from that very source and guarded by me. But when I am taken up, and they are sent forth to the preaching, then they will both fast and pray, as men who have stripped for great contests.

And in another way too: being now weak, and not yet renewed by the Spirit, they resemble old wineskins and an old garment. Therefore no heavier manner of life must be loaded upon them, just as neither is a new patch placed upon an old garment. You can, then, as I said, understand the apostles to be like old wineskins, inasmuch as they are still weak. But you can also understand the Pharisees to be likened to these, the Lord all but saying to them, that: My teaching is new wine; but you, being old wineskins, cannot receive it, but having drunk the old wine—that is, the old tradition—you do not want the new, which is mine, but you say that the old wine—that is, the Old Covenant—is more agreeable.

[^1]: The Greek is «Ὁ παλαιὸς χρηστότερός ἐστιν». The comparative χρηστότερός covers "better, kindlier, more useful"; of wine it carries the sense "more mellow." The same word (χρηστοτέρα) recurs below of the Old Covenant, and is rendered "more agreeable" throughout for consistency.

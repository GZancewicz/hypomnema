# Luke 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 4 · Luke 3:3

> **Now the word of God came, he says, upon John; (Luke 3:2)**

—so that you may learn that he came not self-summoned[^1] to bear witness concerning Christ, but moved by a divine Spirit; for by "the word" you will understand either the Holy Spirit, or the command of God. But in the wilderness came the word of God to John; for since the children of the wilderness — I mean of the Church of the nations — were about to become more than those of her who has the husband, that is, of the Jewish Synagogue, fittingly the word and the command of God comes to John while he is in the wilderness.[^2] And he proclaimed a baptism of repentance to the peoples, that is, of confession. And this baptism cooperated toward the remission of their sins — the remission given through the baptism of Christ. For John's baptism did not indeed have remission of sins, but contributed toward remission; that is, it prepared the people so that they might receive the baptism of Christ, which has remission of sins.

[^1]: αὐτόκλητος — "called by oneself," i.e. coming of his own accord rather than sent.
[^2]: A play on ἔρημος: John receives the word in the "wilderness" (ἐρήμῳ), and the "children of the wilderness/desolate" (τὰ τέκνα τῆς ἐρήμου) are the Gentile Church, contrasted with "her who has the husband," the Synagogue — an allusion to Isa. 54:1 (cf. Gal. 4:27), "the children of the desolate are more than the children of her that has the husband."

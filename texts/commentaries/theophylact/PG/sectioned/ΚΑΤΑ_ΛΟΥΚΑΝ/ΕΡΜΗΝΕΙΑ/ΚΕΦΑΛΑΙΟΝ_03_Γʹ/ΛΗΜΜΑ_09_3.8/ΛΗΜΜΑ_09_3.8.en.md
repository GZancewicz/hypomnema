# Luke 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 9 · Luke 3:8

> **Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance, and do not begin to say within yourselves, "We have Abraham for father"; for I say to you that God is able even from these stones to raise up children to Abraham. And already the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree therefore not bringing forth good fruit is cut down and cast into fire. (Luke 3:8)**

*Offspring of vipers* he calls the Jews, as ones who destroy their fathers and mothers.[^1] For they say that this animal too, gnawing through the womb, is thus born; but they also killed the prophets and the teachers. And by the *coming wrath* he means the eternal punishment. But *fruits worthy of repentance* are not only the avoidance of evil, but also the working out of the good; for this is truly the fruit and offspring of repentance, to work the good.

*Do not begin to say within yourselves* that "we are well-born," and, trusting in the fathers, neglect virtue. For indeed even from these stones God will be able to give children to the patriarch, as he practically did before as well. For the womb of Sarah, being harder than stones, was nevertheless graced unto child-bearing. And the *axe* is the divine judgment, which cuts off the unworthy from life. If therefore you do not repent, he says, you shall be cut off from life. For it is laid upon the root of your trees.

[^1]: The Greek here is corrupt: ὡς τοὺς πατέρας αὐτῶν καὶ τὰς μητέρας λίβοντας. The participle λίβοντας is not a standard form; from the following sense (the viper gnaws through the womb in being born, and the Jews killed the prophets) it must mean "destroying/doing violence to" — rendered here "destroy."

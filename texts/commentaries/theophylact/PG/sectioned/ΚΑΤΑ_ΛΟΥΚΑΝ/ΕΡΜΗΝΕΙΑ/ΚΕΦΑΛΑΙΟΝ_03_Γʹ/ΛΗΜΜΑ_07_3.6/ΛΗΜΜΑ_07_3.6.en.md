# Luke 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 7 · Luke 3:6

> **All flesh shall see the salvation of God. (Luke 3:6)**

The *way* is the manner of life according to Christ, which he commands to be prepared. For the Lord was, before long, about to preach. But the *paths* are the commandments of the Law, as already worn,[^1] which he orders to make straight; for the Pharisees were perverting the commandments. But the *way* can also be understood as the soul, and the *paths* as the thoughts and the actions. Therefore we must both prepare the soul and make our actions and thoughts straight.

Then, as though someone were asking, "And how shall we accomplish this?" — for virtue is hard to work at, having many ravines and difficulties, both from the evil powers and from the passions dwelling within us — he says that nothing will be laborious, but all things easier. For the ravines shall be filled up, that is, our natural powers toward the good, having grown weak and been brought low, shall be filled up again; and *every mountain and hill* — I mean the opposing powers, which through pride have been lifted up — shall be brought low. For truly they grew weak, both the opposing powers and the desires that seem to be naturally implanted in us. And all things have become smooth, and the crooked things have been changed into straight. For Christ both abolished the opposing powers, which here are called mountains and hills, and revived our natural movements toward the good, which the evangelist called ravines being filled. For for this reason he was made flesh, that he might restore our nature to its proper state.

And *all flesh*, he says, *shall see the salvation of God* — no longer Jews only and proselytes, but also all flesh; for into all the earth the Gospel has come. There were many other things also to say, but for the sake of greater clarity let these things be said.

[^1]: The Greek plays on τρίβοι ("paths") and τετριμμέναι ("worn, trodden"): αἱ τοῦ νόμου ἐντολαί, ὡς ἤδη τετριμμέναι — the commandments of the Law as already "well-trodden."

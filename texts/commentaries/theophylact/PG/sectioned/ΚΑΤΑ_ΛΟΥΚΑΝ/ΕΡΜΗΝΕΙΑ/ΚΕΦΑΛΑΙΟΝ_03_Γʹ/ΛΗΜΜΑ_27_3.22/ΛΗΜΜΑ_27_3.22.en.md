# Luke 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 27 · Luke 3:22

> **of Methuselah, of Enoch, of Jared, of Mahalaleel, of Cainan, of Enos, of Seth, of Adam, of God. (Luke 3:22)**

Being thirty years old, the Lord was baptized, because this age is the most perfect; and at this age the man *shows himself* either approved or unapproved. And Luke gives his genealogy in reverse order to Matthew, in order to show that the one now born in flesh is from God. For observe the genealogy, how it comes down to God; and at the same time, that we may learn that it was for this reason that he was made flesh — namely, in order that he might lead up all the fathers in between to God and make them sons.

But I am able to speak also in another way. The birth of the Lord was disbelieved, as being without seed; therefore the evangelist, wishing to show that at another time also a man came into being without seed, ascends from the ones below up to Adam and to God, all but saying to us: If you disbelieve how the second Adam came to be apart from seed, ascend for me in mind to the former Adam, and you will find him having come to be without seed by God, and henceforth, do not disbelieve.

But some inquire how Matthew says Joseph to be the child of Jacob, whereas Luke *says he is the child* of Heli; for it is impossible, they say, for the same person to be the child of two fathers. Therefore they say in response to this, that Jacob and Heli were brothers of the same mother, but of one father and another;[^1] therefore, when Heli had died childless, Jacob took his wife, *in order* to beget a child from her; and thus Joseph is said to be the child of Jacob by nature, and of Heli by law. For Jacob begot him by nature and truly, and so is henceforth his natural son; but of Heli he was a child according to the law only. For the law commanded, if someone should die childless, that his wife be joined to his brother, and that the child to be born from this union be reckoned to be *the child* of the deceased, even if by nature it was *the child* of the living *brother*; so that the evangelists speak rightly and do not contradict one another. For Matthew recorded the natural father of Joseph, whereas Luke *recorded* the one reckoned his father according to the law, that is, Heli, in order that both might show that for this reason the Lord was born: that he might sanctify both nature and law.

[^1]: Greek ἄλλου δὲ καὶ ἄλλου πατρός, literally "but of one and of another father" — i.e., they shared a mother but had different fathers, so that Joseph could be legally reckoned to one and naturally begotten by the other.

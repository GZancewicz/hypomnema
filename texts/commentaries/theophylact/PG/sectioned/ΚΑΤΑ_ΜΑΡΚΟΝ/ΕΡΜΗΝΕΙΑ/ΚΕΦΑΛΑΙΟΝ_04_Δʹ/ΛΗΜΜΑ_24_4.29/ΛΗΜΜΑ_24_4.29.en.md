# Mark 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 24 · Mark 4:29

> **But when the fruit yields itself,[^1] immediately he sends the sickle, because the harvest is at hand. (Mark 4:29)**

By "the kingdom of God" understand the dispensation of God concerning us. For the man is God himself, who for our sake became man. And the seed he cast upon the earth is the preaching of the Gospel; and having cast this, he sleeps, that is, he ascended into the heavens; but nevertheless he rises night and day. For even if God seems to sleep, yet he rises: by night, when through trials he raises us up to the knowledge of him; and by day, when through pleasant and joyful things he orders our life. And the seed lengthens as he himself knows not; for we are self-determining,[^2] and it lies in our own choice whether the seed grows or not. Since we bear fruit not by necessity, but of our own accord — that is, from ourselves we bear fruit — first blossoming as the blade and showing the beginning of the good, when we are infants and have not yet attained to the measure of the stature that is according to Christ; then as the ear, when we are able also to stand against trials. For the ear is already girded even with joints,[^3] and stands upright, and is more perfect. Then also the full grain in the ear, when one bears fruit that is perfect. And when the harvest yields, then the sickle gathers the fruit. And by "sickle" understand the word of God; and by "harvest," the time of the consummation.

[^1]: Greek «Ὅταν παραδῷ ὁ καρπός». The verb παραδίδωμι is used idiomatically of the crop "giving itself up," i.e. becoming ripe / permitting harvest; hence "yields itself." Cf. the exposition below, «Ὅταν δὲ παραδῷ τὸ θέρος», "when the harvest yields."
[^2]: Greek «αὐτεξούσιοι», the technical term for those possessing free self-determination (free will).
[^3]: Greek «γόνασιν διαζώννυται»: γόνυ ("knee") is here the node or joint of the stalk; the ripening ear is "girded" at its joints and so stands erect.

# Mark 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 31 · Mark 4:40

> **He said to them: Why are you so fearful? How is it you have no faith? And they feared a great fear, and said to one another: Who then is this, that both the wind and the sea obey him? (Mark 4:40)**

Matthew narrated these things in one way, and Mark in another; for what the former told more expansively, this one contracted, and, conversely, what the former contracted, this one expanded. He takes along, therefore, only the disciples, making them spectators of the miracle that was about to be; but lest they be lifted up that, having sent the others away, he had kept them, he allows *the boat* to be tossed by the waves, and at the same time that he might teach them to bear trials. And he sleeps, that the miracle might appear greater to them, who had first been thrown into confusion; for if the surge had arisen while Christ was awake, either they would not have been afraid, or they would not have entreated *him*. He allows them, therefore, to fall into fear of danger, that they might come to a perception of his power. For since they saw the others being benefited, but they themselves enjoyed nothing of the sort, there was a danger of their becoming more careless. He permits, therefore, the storm to come upon *them*; and he sleeps upon the cushion of the boat — and this was certainly of wood. And having been roused from sleep, he rebukes first the wind — for it is this that makes the sea grow wild — then the sea also. And he rebukes the disciples too, as not having faith; for if they had had faith, they would have believed that even while sleeping he was able to preserve them unharmed. And they said to one another: Who then is this? For they still stood doubtful concerning him; for inasmuch as he lulled the sea to rest by command alone, and neither with a rod, as Moses, nor by invocation, as Elisha *did* the Jordan, nor with the ark, as Jesus [i.e. Joshua] the son of Nave [i.e. Nun], for this reason he seemed to them above man; but inasmuch as he slept, he appeared to them again as man.[^1]

[^1]: The three Old Testament parallels: Moses parting the sea with his rod (Exod. 14:16); Elisha striking the Jordan with Elijah's mantle and calling upon the Lord (2 Kings 2:14); and Joshua ("Jesus the son of Nave," the LXX form of Joshua son of Nun) crossing the Jordan by means of the ark (Josh. 3). The Greek names Ἐλισσαῖος (Elisha) and Ἰησοῦς ὁ τοῦ Ναυῆ (Joshua, lit. "Jesus of Nave").

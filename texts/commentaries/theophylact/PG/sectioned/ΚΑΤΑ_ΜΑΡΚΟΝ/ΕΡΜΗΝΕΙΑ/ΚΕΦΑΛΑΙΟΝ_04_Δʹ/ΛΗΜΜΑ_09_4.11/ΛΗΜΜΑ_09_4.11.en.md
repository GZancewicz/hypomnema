# Mark 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 4:11

> **he said to them: To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God, but to those outside, all things come to be in parables, that seeing they may see and not perceive, and hearing they may hear and not understand, lest they turn back and their sins be forgiven them. (Mark 4:11)**

He sets down this parable first, the one about the seed, in order that he might make the hearers more attentive. For since he is about to say that the seed is the word, and that, falling into heedless persons, it is destroyed, on this account he says such things before the others, so that the hearers might be earnest not to be made like that ground which destroys the seed.

Who, therefore, is the sower? Christ himself, who went forth from the paternal bosom by love for mankind and condescension, without departing.[^1] But he went forth not that he might burn up the accursed ground and the wicked hearts; nor that he might cut out the thorns, but that he might sow the seed. But what kind of seed? Perhaps that of Moses? perhaps that of the prophets? No, but his own, that is, that he might proclaim his own Gospel.

He, therefore, sowed. And of the seeds, one fell into a soul trodden down by many; and the birds of heaven — I mean the demons that hold the air — devoured the seed. For such are the men-pleasers, a road trodden down by many; for when someone does everything so as to please this one and that one, he is trodden down by many. But observe: he did not say that He threw it upon the road, but It fell. For the sower throws as onto good ground, but that ground, being found wicked, destroys the seed, that is, the word. And some have gracefully understood the "It fell beside the way" to mean that it fell into an unbelieving heart; for the way is Christ, and beside the way are the unbelievers, those outside the way, that is, of Christ.

And another falls upon a stony soul — I mean those who readily receive, then cast away; who are stony, resembling in some small measure the rock — I mean Christ — insofar as they received the word; but insofar as they receive for a season, then cast it away, they are unlike him. And another into a soul full of cares; for the cares are thorns. But the fourth, into good ground. Observe, then, how rare are the good things, and few those who are being saved; for only the fourth part of the seed is found to have been preserved.

To the disciples, therefore, who asked him privately, he says that To you it is given to know the mysteries. Is it, then, by allotment and destiny from nature that to some it is given, but to others not? God forbid! But it is given to those, namely to those who seek.

[^1]: Greek ἀνεκφοιτήτως, a technical term of patristic theology: Christ "came forth" from the Father without ever leaving or being separated from him. Rendered here "without departing."

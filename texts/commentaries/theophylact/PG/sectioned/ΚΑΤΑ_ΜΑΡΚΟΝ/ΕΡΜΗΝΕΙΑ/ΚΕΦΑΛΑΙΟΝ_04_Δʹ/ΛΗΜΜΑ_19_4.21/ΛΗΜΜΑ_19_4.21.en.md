# Mark 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 19 · Mark 4:21

> **...to hear, let him hear. (Mark 4:21)**[^1]

Here he exhorts the apostles to be radiant in life and in conduct; for just as the lamp, he says, is set for shining, so also your life shall be conspicuous to all, and all look toward it, so that *it is required*[^2] to have a good life. For you do not sit in a corner, but you are a lamp. But the lamp is not hidden under a bed, but, placed on the lampstand, it shines. And each of us also is a lamp, which ought to be placed upon the lampstand, that is, upon the height of the conduct that is according to God, so that it may be able to shine to others also, and not under the measure, that is, under gluttony and the concern about food, nor under the bed, that is, repose. For no one who is busy about food and longing for ease is able to be a lamp shining to all through his own life. For there is nothing hidden which shall not be made manifest. Whether one does something good or something wicked in secret, it shall be made manifest both here, and much more in the age to come. For what was more hidden than God? But nevertheless he too was made manifest in the flesh.

[^1]: The source fragment opens mid-quotation with «ἀκούειν, ἀκουέτω» (the OCR reads ἀνουέτω for ἀκουέτω); only the closing words of the scripture lemma survive here, the opening «... having fallen in the preceding section. The full formula is "he who has ears to hear, let him hear."
[^2]: The connecting word is OCR-corrupt: «ὥστε αποιλλάσασἀγαθὸν βίον ἔχειν». The sense is "so that [one must] have a good life"; the exact verb (perhaps ἀναγκαῖον/ἀπαιτεῖ, "it is necessary / it demands") cannot be recovered with confidence.

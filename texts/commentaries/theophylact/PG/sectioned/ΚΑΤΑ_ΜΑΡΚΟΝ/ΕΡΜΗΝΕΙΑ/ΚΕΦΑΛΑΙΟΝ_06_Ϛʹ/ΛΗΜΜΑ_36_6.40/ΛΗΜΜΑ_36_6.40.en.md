# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 36 · Mark 6:40

> **they reclined in garden-plots, plot by plot, by hundreds and by fifties. (Mark 6:40)**[^1]

The Pharisees, being ravening wolves, were not shepherding the people, but devouring them. On account of this they are gathered together to Christ, the true shepherd, fleeing from those *others*; whence he gives them nourishment, foremost the more profitable and more precious *nourishment*, that through the word, then also the bodily. But observe the disciples, how they advance toward the more compassionate. For having had mercy on the crowds, they come to Christ, and make request concerning them. And the Lord, testing them and proving whether they had recognized his power, that he is able to nourish them fully, says: Give them, you, to eat. But they even find fault with him, as not knowing how great the lack was for them,[^2] and how great also the multitude of the crowd. For, being weighed down, they say the *saying*, Shall we buy two hundred denarii of loaves, and give them to eat? He, however,[^3] makes all recline upon the grass, separated apart. For this is *the meaning of* the *phrase* "Company by company" (συμπόσια συμπόσια), instead of "at various tables." And this also is what the *phrase* "They reclined in garden-plots" signifies, that is, various sections and parts. For "garden-plots" (πρασιαί) is the name for the various divisions in gardens, in which various vegetables are often planted.

[^1]: The Greek reduplicates distributively: πρασιαὶ πρασιαί, literally "garden-plots, garden-plots," i.e. group by group, as with συμπόσια συμπόσια ("company by company") in the preceding verse. Theophylact glosses πρασιά below as a garden-bed.
[^2]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupt: «πόση μὲν μὸ τοῖς ἡ ἔνδεια» — "how great … the lack for them." The word rendered "was for them" (μὸ τοῖς, perhaps for ᾖ / αὐτοῖς) is uncertain; the sense ("how great the lack") is secure. The balancing clause is «πόσον δὲ καὶ τὸ τοῦ ὄχλου πλῆθος» ("and how great also the multitude of the crowd").
[^3]: The Greek reads «λος μέντοι ἀνακλίνει», where "λος" is corrupt; read Ὁ μέντοι, "He, however."

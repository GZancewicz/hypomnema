# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 13 · Mark 6:11

> **Amen I say to you, it shall be more tolerable for Sodom or Gomorrah in the day of judgment than for that city. (Mark 6:11)**

Not in the cities only did the Lord teach, but also in the villages, that we might learn neither to despise the small things, nor always to seek out the great cities, but to sow the word even in the paltry little hamlets. And not only does he himself teach, but he also sends the twelve two by two, that they might be the more eager. For if he had sent one, that single one would have been without eagerness; again, if *he had sent* more than two, the number of the apostles would not have sufficed for many villages. Accordingly he sends two; for "the two are better than the one," says Ecclesiastes (Eccl. 4:9). And he charges them to take nothing — neither bag, nor copper *money*, nor bread — training them by these things to freedom from love of money, and so that those who saw them, *the apostles* teaching possessionlessness from the fact that they themselves had nothing, might be put to shame by them. For who, seeing the apostle taking neither bag nor bread — the most necessary thing — would not have been put to shame, and stripped himself *as for the contest* toward possessionlessness?[^1] And he bids them to remain in one house, lest they should seem to be unstable on account of gluttony, exchanging one *host* for another. And from those who did not receive them, he tells *them* to shake off the dust, showing that they had walked a long road for their sake and profited nothing; or that they got nothing from them, not even dust, but shook off even this, so that this might be for a testimony against them, that is, for a reproof. For amen I say to you that it shall be more tolerable for Sodom or Gomorrah in the judgment than for those who did not receive you. For the Sodomites, having been punished here, shall be punished more bearably there; and at the same time, no apostles were even sent to them, whereas those who did not receive the apostles shall suffer worse things than they.

[^1]: The verb is ἀπεδύσατο, literally "he stripped himself"—the athletic metaphor of stripping down to contend in the arena; here, of readying oneself for the ascetic discipline of possessionlessness (ἀκτημοσύνη).

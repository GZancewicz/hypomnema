# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 22 · Mark 6:19

> **knowing him to be a righteous and holy man; and he kept him safe, and having heard him he did many things,[^1] and gladly heard him. (Mark 6:19)**

Mark here inserts the narrative concerning the death of the Baptist, having seized the occasion. As for Herodias, some say that Herod tore her away while Philip was still living, and that on this account he was rebuked as one transgressing the law, in unyoking the wife from a living brother; but others say that Philip did indeed die, yet leaving a daughter—and, there being a daughter, Herod ought not to have married the wife of his brother, not even after his death. For the law at that time commanded the brother to take the wife of the brother when there was no child; but here the daughter existed—therefore the marriage was unlawful. But see how great is the power of the raging madness of erotic passion,[^2] so that even Herod, though he had so much reverence and fear toward John, despised him, only to gratify his licentiousness.

[^1]: Theophylact's text reads «πολλὰ ἐποίει» ("did many things"), as against the reading «πολλὰ ἠπόρει» ("was much perplexed") found in many witnesses. Translated per his text.
[^2]: Greek «ἡ λύσσα τοῦ ἔρωτος»: λύσσα is the "frenzy/rabid madness" of a beast; ἔρως here is specifically carnal desire.

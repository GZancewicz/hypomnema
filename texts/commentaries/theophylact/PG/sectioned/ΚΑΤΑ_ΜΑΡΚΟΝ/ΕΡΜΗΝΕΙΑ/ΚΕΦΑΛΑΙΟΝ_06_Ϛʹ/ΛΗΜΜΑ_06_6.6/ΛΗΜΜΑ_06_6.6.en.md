# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 6 · Mark 6:6

> **And he marveled because of their unbelief. (Mark 6:6)**

Universally the Lord says that all the prophets are without honor in their homelands. For whether they have illustrious kinsmen, these envy them, and on this account dishonor them; or whether they be low-born, again on account of the low birth they are dishonored. Therefore he could not do mighty works there, not because he himself was weak, but because those people were unbelieving. For sparing them, in order that the signs might not be for their greater condemnation, since they would not believe even so, on this account he does not do mighty works there; and besides, in works of wonder there is need both of the power of the one who works them, and of the faith of those who receive them. Accordingly, since there one of the two was lacking, namely the faith of those to be healed, it was not possible for Jesus to do signs. For thus we ought to understand the "He could not," in the sense of "it was not possible."[^1]

[^1]: Theophylact glosses the Evangelist's οὐκ ἠδύνατο ("he could not," 6:5) by οὐκ ἐνεδέχετο ("it was not admissible / not possible"): the impossibility is not a defect of Christ's power but a condition set by the recipients' unbelief.

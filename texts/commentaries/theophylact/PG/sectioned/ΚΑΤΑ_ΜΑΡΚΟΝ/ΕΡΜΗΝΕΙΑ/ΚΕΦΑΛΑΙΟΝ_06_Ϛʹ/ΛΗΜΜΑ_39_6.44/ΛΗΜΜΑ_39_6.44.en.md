# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 39 · Mark 6:44

> **who ate the loaves, five thousand men. (Mark 6:44)**

He looks up into heaven, at once both teaching us also to ask for our food from God, and not from the devil — which is what those do who are sustained out of unjust gains; and at the same time showing the crowds then that he is not opposed to God,[^1] but calls upon God. And he gives *the loaves* to the disciples, so that they might not forget the miracle, having received the loaves with their own hands. And the twelve baskets are left over, for this very reason, that each one, lifting a basket upon his shoulders, might keep the miracle beyond forgetting. And it is an abundance of power, not only to feed so many, but also to leave over a surplus. For Moses, even though he gave the manna, gave it *only* according to the need of each; for what was left over bred worms (cf. Exod. 16:20). And Elias too, when he sustained the widow, supplied *only* what was sufficient (cf. 1 Kings 17:8-16). But Jesus, as Master, even makes there to be a surplus.

These things, then, are according to the history [i.e. the literal sense]. But according to the anagogy [i.e. the higher spiritual sense], the five loaves are the Mosaic words. For five are the books of Moses: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy. And the two fishes are the words of the fishermen, the Apostle [i.e. the Epistles] and the Gospel. By these, then, are nourished our five senses, for these are the five thousand. But we cannot eat everything, but many things are left over, which the apostles are able to bear. For the more difficult of the meanings of the Law and of the Gospel we, who are still enslaved to the five senses, do not bear, but the apostles do.

[^1]: Greek ἀντίθεος — "opposed to God," a rival or counter-divinity. Theophylact's point is that in looking up and invoking God, Jesus shows the crowds he is not setting himself up against God as a rival power, but calling upon him.

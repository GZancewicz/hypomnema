# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 28 · Mark 6:29

> **And when his disciples heard, they came and took up his corpse, and laid it in a tomb. (Mark 6:29)**

A drinking-bout[^1] is got up, but Satan dances through the girl. And an oath is performed, lawless and godless, or rather senseless; and the wicked woman says, "Give me, forthwith" — that is, on the spot, at that very hour. But the senseless and love-smitten Herod fears the oaths, and on account of this murders the righteous man. Yet here he ought to have broken his oath, and not to have wrought such an abomination. For to keep one's oath is not good in every case. And a "speculator"[^2] is what the executioner-soldier is called, who is appointed for putting to death. And by "corpse" he means the body; for after being beheaded the body, falling, is called a "corpse."[^3]

But it is also possible to take these things according to contemplation. For Herod is the leathern[^4] people of the Jews. And he married a wife, the false and licentious opinion, whose daughter even now still dances and is set in motion among the Jews — the knowledge of the Scriptures that leads them astray. For they seem to know the Scriptures; but this is not so; for they beheaded John, that is, the prophetic word; for the chief point[^5] of the prophecy — I mean Christ — they did not receive; so that even if they do have the prophetic word, yet they have it without the head, that is, without Christ.

[^1]: The OCR reads Πέτος; read Πότος, "a drinking-bout, banquet" — set opposite συγκροτεῖται ("is got up, is mustered").
[^2]: σπεκουλάτωρ, a Latin loanword (*speculator*), a soldier of the guard detailed to carry out executions.
[^3]: A wordplay: πτῶμα ("corpse") is derived from πίπτω ("to fall") — the fallen body.
[^4]: δερμάτινος, lit. "of skin, leathern" — i.e. the carnal, fleshly people, alluding to the garments of skin (Gen. 3:21).
[^5]: κεφάλαιον ("chief point, sum, capital"), punning on κεφαλή ("head"): the "head" of the prophecy is Christ, whom they beheaded by rejecting.

# Mark 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 46 · Mark 6:52

> **For they did not understand concerning the loaves; for their heart was hardened. (Mark 6:52)**

He constrained the disciples. For they did not wish to be drawn away from him, but were being separated by force, both because of their love toward him, and also because of being at a loss how he might come to them, there being no boat. Having therefore dismissed the crowds, he went up to pray in private. For prayer needs leisure and freedom from disturbance. But he lets the disciples be tried, that they may learn to endure. For on this account he does not even immediately come to stand by them, but through the whole night allows them to be tossed by the waves, that he may teach them to persevere, and not, at the beginning of hardships, to hope for relief. But observe also another thing: that when he is about to put an end to the terrors, then rather he casts them into greater fear. For having seen him, they cried out, being troubled as at a phantom; and immediately through his voice he heals them, saying: Do not be afraid.[^1] Then also, through his getting into the boat, he procures for them the more complete freedom from disturbance. For immediately the wind ceased. And a great marvel is the walking upon the sea, and truly *the work* of God; but that also, there being turmoil and the wind being contrary, is an intensification of the marvel. But the apostles, not understanding concerning the loaves, understood from this marvel that was upon the sea; it seems, therefore, that Christ allowed them to be tried for this reason also, that, since they had not recognized him from the loaves, they might recognize him from the sea, and be benefited.

[^1]: The OCR of the re-quoted lemma here reads «Μὴ σοβεῖσθε», corrected to «Μὴ φοβεῖσθε» ("do not be afraid"), matching the lemma of Mark 6:50.

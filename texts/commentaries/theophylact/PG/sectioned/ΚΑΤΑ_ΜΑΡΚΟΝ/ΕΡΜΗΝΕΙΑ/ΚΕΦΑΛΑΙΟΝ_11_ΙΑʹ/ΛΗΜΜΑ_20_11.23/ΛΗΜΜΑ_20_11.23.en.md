# Mark 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 20 · Mark 11:23

> **"And immediately the fig tree withered." (Matt. 21:19)**

Stop, therefore, up to this point; then say, "And the disciples, seeing it, marveled." When? Seeing it — not immediately, but on the next day. To one who understands it thus, then, nothing insoluble will present itself. And observe how from this too Christ is shown to be God. For the Lord says through the prophets: "I dry up the green tree, and make the dry tree flourish" (cf. Ezek. 17:24). And marvel at the divine love for mankind, how even to us, who are made like him through faith, he gives the power of working miracles, which he himself possesses by nature, so as to be able even to remove mountains. And a mountain is also the proud mind, being something lofty and hard. Whoever, therefore, sees the passion of pride troubling him and eager to cast him out of God's oversight and providence — for that man is proud who says that he himself accomplishes everything, and not through God — such a one, therefore, ought to rebuke this mountain, pride I mean, and to say to it: "Be lifted up, and be cast into the sea," that is, into worldly men, those who are in the sea of this life and are unbelieving. And this man shall surely not be divided,[^1] that is, be separated from God. For the proud man is separated from God, saying, "I have nothing from God, nor do I need his alliance..."

[^1]: The lemma's word is διακριθῇ, "doubt/waver" (Mark 11:23), but Theophylact exploits the verb's other sense: he glosses it διαχωρισθῇ ἀπὸ τοῦ Θεοῦ, "be separated/divided from God." The rendering "be divided" preserves the wordplay he draws out; in the scriptural sense it means "does not doubt."

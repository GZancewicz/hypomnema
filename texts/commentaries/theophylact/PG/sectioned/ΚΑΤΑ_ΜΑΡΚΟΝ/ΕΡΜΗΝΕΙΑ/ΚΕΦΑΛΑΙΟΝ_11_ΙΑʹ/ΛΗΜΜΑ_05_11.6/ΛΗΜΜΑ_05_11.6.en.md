# Mark 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 5 · Mark 11:6

> **spoke to them just as Jesus had commanded them; and they let them go. (Mark 11:6)**

Many times indeed and on other occasions he had come to Jerusalem, but never with such great splendor as now. For formerly he was hiding himself because of their envy; but now, since the time of the Passion has drawn near — the time which he himself had appointed for himself — he comes more splendidly, so that, if they should be willing, they might understand his glory, and from the prophecies concerning him being fulfilled might recognize that he is the true God. But if they should not be willing to understand, it would be for a greater condemnation to them, that not even after so many splendid miracles did they believe.

For see how many signs are here. He told them that "You will find a colt." He told them that they would be hindered, and then, upon their saying that "The Lord has need of it," they would be let alone. For neither is even this a small thing, that the apostles were permitted to lead away the colt — which would not have happened unless some divine necessity lay upon the owners of the colt, compelling them, poor and perhaps farmers as they were, to release the animal.

Nevertheless, learn that the Lord did not do this in vain. For he had no need of the colt, he who often had gone about all Galilee and Judea; but *he did it* showing that he would be seated upon the gentile people, which was disobedient and untaught, and was bound by its own sins in the open street — that is, in this life — at the door, or rather outside the Church. And it was loosed by the disciples through baptism and faith; and the Lord was carried upon it, when the apostles had placed on it their garments, that is, all the comeliness that comes from virtue. For formerly, being naked, those of the gentiles were unseemly, doing lawless things. But when they were clothed[^1] by the apostles, then indeed, then did they learn to walk becomingly, and on account of this Christ also is carried, being borne upon them.

And who were the former masters of the colt, hindering the apostles, but assuredly the demons? But the apostles become mightier even than these.

[^1]: The Greek verb here is corrupt in the OCR (Πχθησαν); the sense required by the context — being adorned with the apostles' garments — is "were clothed" / "were robed" (perhaps ἐστολίσθησαν or ἐνεδύθησαν). Only the meaning "clothed, arrayed" is secure.

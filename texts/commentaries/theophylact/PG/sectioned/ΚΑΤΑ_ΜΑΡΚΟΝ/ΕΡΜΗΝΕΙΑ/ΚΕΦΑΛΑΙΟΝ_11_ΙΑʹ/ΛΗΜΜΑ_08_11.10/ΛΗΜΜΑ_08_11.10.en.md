# Mark 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 8 · Mark 11:10

> **Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord. Blessed is the coming kingdom in the name of the Lord of our father David; Hosanna in the highest. (Mark 11:9-10)**[^1]

The crowd, so long as it remains uncorrupted, recognizes what is fitting. Wherefore they also honor Jesus, each according to the power he had. But let us see what those who were singing praise were saying. This hymn they took from David; but the "Hosanna," according to some means "save now," while according to others it means "a hymn." Yet the first is better. For in the one hundred and seventeenth psalm it stands: "O Lord, save now" (Ps. 117:25 LXX); which in the Hebrew is written "Hosanna." And by "kingdom of David" they meant the kingdom of Christ, both because the Lord was descended from the seed of David, and because "David" is interpreted "mighty in hand," that is, "sufficient in hand." And who else is "sufficient in hand," if not the Lord, whose hands wrought so many miracles?

But indeed let us also spread our garments, that is, the flesh — for the flesh is the garment of the soul — and let us subject it to the Lord. And let us spread the road of our life, cutting branches from the trees, that is, imitating the lives of the saints. For the saints are trees, from which he who imitates their virtue cuts branches. But let also our actions, both those that go before and those that follow, be unto the glory of God. For some in their *earlier* life[^2] displayed a good beginning, but the life that followed — the things I speak of as later — was not such, nor unto the glory of God.

[^1]: The lemma conflates the acclamation: to «εὐλογημένος ὁ ἐρχόμενος ἐν ὀνόματι Κυρίου» Theophylact's text joins «Εὐλογημένη ἡ ἐρχομένη βασιλεία ἐν ὀνόματι Κυρίου τοῦ πατρὸς ἡμῶν Δαβίδ», inserting «ἐν ὀνόματι Κυρίου» into the phrase about the kingdom (the common Markan text reads simply «ἡ ἐρχομένη βασιλεία τοῦ πατρὸς ἡμῶν Δαβίδ»). Rendered as he quotes it.
[^2]: OCR: «ἐν μὲν τῷ προάγοντι Ἅῳ», where «Ἅῳ» is corrupt for «βίῳ» ("life"), matching the following «ὁ δὲ ἀκολουθῶν βίος». A stray «Τ.» at the end of the passage is left untranslated as OCR debris.

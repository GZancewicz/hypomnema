# Mark 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 12 · Mark 11:14

> **Jesus answering, said to it: No longer may anyone eat fruit from you unto the age. And his disciples were listening. (Mark 11:14)**

Jesus entered into the temple, but again he went out, showing that he was now abandoning it desolate and to plundering. And he goes out to Bethany, which is interpreted "house of obedience." For having left the insubordinate and hard-hearted Jews, he comes, with his disciples, to those who obey him. But let us also look at the matters concerning the fig tree; for many absurdities appear to arise. First, that he hungered in the early morning; then, that when it was not the season of figs, he demanded fruit; and further, what reason there is for a senseless plant to be punished. They say therefore that all this is a matter of dispensation. For since he frequently did miracles for the benefit of men, but the disciples had not seen his power exercised in the harming of anything, for this reason here, wishing to show the disciples that he is able also to harm, and that if he wills he will annihilate those about to crucify him in a single turn of a moment, he displays his power upon the senseless tree. Which is also a great marvel: how so moist a tree was all at once dried up. For the fig tree is moister than almost all trees.

And he hungers in the early morning, by dispensation yielding to the flesh, and he seeks fruit from the tree that had not the season of fruit, in order that, as I said above, he might show the disciples that he is able also to chastise. And this fig tree bears also a type of the Synagogue of the Jews, who had only leaves, that is, the law furnishing them a shadow, but had no fruit. And Jesus hungered for their salvation. "For my food," he says, "is that I do the will of my Father" (John 4:34). And the will of God is to turn back those who sin. Since therefore the synagogue had no fruit, it was cursed and dried up, having neither prophet nor teacher.

# Mark 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 14 · Mark 11:18

> **the Scribes and the chief priests heard, and were seeking how they might destroy him. For they were fearing him, because all the crowd was astonished at his teaching. (Mark 11:18)**

That the sellers were cast out by Jesus, John also relates (John 2:14-16). But that one *relates it* in the beginning of the Gospel, whereas this one near the end. It is likely, therefore, that this happened a second time — which is also a greater accusation against the Jews, seeing that, though the Lord did the same thing many times, they were not turned back.

And he names the temple a den of robbers on account of *their* love of gain. For the robber race is all agog about gaining.[^1] Since, therefore, they too were selling the animals offered for sacrifice for the sake of gain, he named them robbers. And the money-changers [kollybistai] were those who exchanged the coins; for a *kollybos* is a kind of small copper coin. And he sets over them as accuser also the prophet Isaiah, saying: My house shall be called a house of prayer.

But may it not happen that we too be cast out outside the temple. For many of us also enter into our own temple, selling the good things and buying the worse. And there are also those who have tables full of petty coins — those who manage and administer the ecclesiastical affairs, but do everything on account of love of money. But also the seats of those selling the doves are overturned — that is to say, the chief priests who sell the spiritual gifts; for the dove is a symbol of the Spirit. The Lord thrusts *them* out of the high-priesthood, for he who ordains through the giving of gold is deposed. But also whoever betrays to the devil the grace and purity *that comes* from baptism, this one has sold his dove, and on account of this is also cast out of the temple.

[^1]: The verb is ἐπτόηται (from πτοέω), "is fluttered/excited/agog" — the robber kind is in a flutter of eagerness over gain.

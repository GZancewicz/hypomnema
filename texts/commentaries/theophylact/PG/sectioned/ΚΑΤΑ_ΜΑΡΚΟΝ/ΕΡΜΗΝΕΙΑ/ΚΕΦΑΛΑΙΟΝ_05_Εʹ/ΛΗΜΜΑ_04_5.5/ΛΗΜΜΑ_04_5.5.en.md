# Mark 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 4 · Mark 5:5

> **—continually, night and day, in the mountains and in the tombs he was, crying out and cutting himself with stones. (Mark 5:5)**

The more accurate of the copies have *into the country of the Gergesenes*.[^1] Now Matthew says that the demoniacs were two, but Mark and Luke *say* one; for, having picked out the more grievous one, they narrate concerning him. The demoniac comes, then, confessing him to be Son of God. For whereas there, in the boat, they had been in doubt what sort of man this is (Mark 4:41), the testimony from the enemies becomes the more trustworthy—I mean, that of the demons. And he dwelt among the tombs, *the demon* wishing to implant an evil supposition in men, that the souls of those who die become demons.[^2] Which God forbid that anyone should believe!

[^1]: Theophylact notes a manuscript variant in the place-name: the reading he expounds is Γαδαρηνῶν ("of the Gadarenes"), but the more accurate copies read Γεργεσηνῶν ("of the Gergesenes").
[^2]: OCR: the manuscript reads ὀνθρώποις (read ἀνθρώποις, "men"), and a stray ἐ precedes Ὃ μὴ γένοιτο ("which God forbid"), rendering the idiom ὃ μὴ γένοιτο, lit. "which may it not be."

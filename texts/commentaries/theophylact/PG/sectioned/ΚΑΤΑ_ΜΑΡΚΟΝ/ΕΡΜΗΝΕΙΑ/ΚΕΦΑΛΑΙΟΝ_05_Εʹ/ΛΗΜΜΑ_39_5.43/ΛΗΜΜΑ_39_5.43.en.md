# Mark 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 39 · Mark 5:43

> **He charged them much that no one should know this, and said that something should be given her to eat. (Mark 5:43)**

Those of the ruler of the synagogue [i.e. the messengers from his house], thinking Christ to be one of the teachers, for this reason were entreating *him* that, having come, he might pray over the little girl; then, since she died, they thought there was no longer any need of him, because she had died. But that one encourages the father, and says, Only believe. And he does not permit any other to follow along, except the three disciples. For indeed the humble Jesus does not wish to do anything for display. And when he says that the little girl did not die, but sleeps, they laugh *him* to scorn — so that they might not afterward be able to say that she had merely fallen into a swoon,[^1] and *that* it was nothing marvelous if he raised her, but rather that they themselves, testifying against themselves, are convicted that he truly raised one who was dead, seeing that they even laughed him to scorn when he said that she did not die. But he takes hold of the hand, so that he might put power into her. And he gives her *food* to eat, so that he might confirm the resurrection, that it truly came to pass and not according to appearance.[^2]

[^1]: Greek «κάτοχος ἐγένετο», literally "became held/seized." The word denotes being gripped by a cataleptic swoon or trance rather than truly dying; the malicious scoffers, Theophylact says, wished to leave open the claim that the girl had only been in such a stupor, not dead.

[^2]: The Greek of this section is corrupt in the OCR at several points, reconstructed here: line 4 «xριστὸν» read «χριστὸν»; line 7 «θαῤῥόνει» read «θαῤῥύνει» ("encourages"); line 9 «Κτερον» read «ἕτερον» ("other"); line 19 «ὼαὰν» read «ἵνα» ("so that") and «ἀλσθῶς» read «ἀληθῶς» ("truly"). «κατὰ φαντασίαν» rendered "according to appearance" (i.e. not a mere apparition or illusion).

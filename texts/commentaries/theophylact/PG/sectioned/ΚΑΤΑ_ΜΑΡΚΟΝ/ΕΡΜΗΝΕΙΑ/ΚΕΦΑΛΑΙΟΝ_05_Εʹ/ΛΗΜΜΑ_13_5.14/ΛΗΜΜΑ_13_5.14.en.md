# Mark 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 13 · Mark 5:14

> **…feeding the swine, fled, and reported it in the city and in the fields. (Mark 5:14)**

The demons ask the Lord not to send them out of the country, but to release them into the herd of swine; and he permits this. For since our life is a wrestling-school, the Lord did not wish to release the demons out of this life,[^1] so that, wrestling with us, they might render us more approved. He permitted them to enter into the swine, that we might learn that, just as they did not spare the swine, so neither would they have spared that man, had there not been a divine power that preserved him; for the demons, being enemies, would have cut us to pieces at that very hour, if God did not preserve us. And learn that the demons *can do* nothing even against swine,[^2] let alone against men, unless God permits it. And understand that into the men also who live like swine, wallowing in the mire of pleasure — into these the demons enter, and hurl them down the cliff of destruction, into the sea of…[^3]

[^1]: The manuscript reads ἔξω τοῦ βίου τοῦτο ἀπολῦσαι τοὺς δαίμονας; τοῦτο is likely a corruption of τούτου agreeing with βίου ("out of *this* life"), the reading adopted here.
[^2]: The Greek is defective here: Μάνθανε δὲ ὅτι οὐδὲ κατὰ χοίρων … κατὰ ἀνθρώπων, εἰ μὴ συγχωρήσει ὁ Θεός. A verb of power/ability (e.g. δύνανται or ἔχουσιν ἐξουσίαν οἱ δαίμονες) has dropped out after χοίρων; the sense supplied is "the demons can do nothing even against swine, let alone against men."
[^3]: The fragment breaks off mid-clause at ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ τοῦ… ("into the sea of…"); the continuation is lost at the section boundary.

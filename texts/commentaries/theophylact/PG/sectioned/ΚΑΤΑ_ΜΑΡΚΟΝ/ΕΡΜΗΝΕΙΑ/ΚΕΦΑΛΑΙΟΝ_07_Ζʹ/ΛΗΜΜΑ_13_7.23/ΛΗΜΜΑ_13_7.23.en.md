# Mark 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 13 · Mark 7:23

> **All these evil things come forth from within, and defile the man. (Mark 7:23)**

The Lord, teaching men that the observances concerning foods which the law prescribes we ought not to understand bodily, begins from here to reveal moderately the intention of the law. And he says that nothing of the things that enter in is able to defile, that is, to pollute, anyone, but the things that come out from the heart, those are the things that pollute; and he enumerates them. And an evil eye he calls either envy or licentiousness; for indeed the one who envies has an evil eye, and through the eye, seeing the evil, pursues it. And blasphemy he calls the insult against God, as when someone says that "There is no providence" — this is blasphemy; therefore he attaches pride, for pride is, as it were, a contempt of God, whenever someone, having done some good, ascribes this not to God, but to his own power. And foolishness understand as the insult against men. All these passions, therefore, defile the soul, springing from it and coming out.

To the crowd, then, the Lord spoke more obscurely, therefore also he kept saying: "He who has ears to hear, let him hear"; that is, "He who understands, let him understand." But to the apostles, who supposed the Lord was saying something deeper, and who came and asked about the parable — that is, the obscure saying (for a parable is an obscure saying) — first he rebuked *them*, saying: "So are you also without understanding?";[^1] then he resolved the difficulty.

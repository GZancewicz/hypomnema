# Mark 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 23 · Mark 7:37

> **they were beyond measure astonished, saying: "He has done all things well; he makes both the deaf to hear and the mute to speak." (Mark 7:37)**

He does not tarry in the regions of the gentiles, but quickly moves away, so that, as I said, he might not give the Jews occasion to say that he transgresses the law by mingling with the gentiles.[^1] Having therefore departed from the borders of Tyre and Sidon, he approaches Galilee. And indeed he heals a deaf man who spoke with difficulty[^2] — and the affliction was from a demon. He took him aside privately; for he who humbled himself down to our poverty was not fond of display, nor did he wish, for the most part, to work wonders before many, except indeed for the benefit of those who saw. And having spat he touched his tongue, so that he might show that all the parts of his holy flesh were divine and holy, just as the spittle too now loosed the bonds of the tongue; and yet all spittle is a waste product, but in the Lord's case all things are more novel and divine.

And having looked up into heaven he groans — perhaps both as calling upon the Father to have mercy on the man, and teaching us, whenever we are about to do a miracle, to look toward God and to ask from there for the power of miracles; and perhaps also as pitying human nature, how it was so handed over to the devil as to be thus mocked and to suffer such things. Whence also, having healed, he is proclaimed by those being healed, even though he himself charges them and commands *them* to say nothing. For from this we are taught that when we do good, we ought not to demand applause and public acclaim; but when we are benefited, to proclaim and make famous those who have done us good, even though they do not wish it.

[^1]: OCR corruption: the manuscript reads Οόκἐιβοαδύνει, reconstructed as οὐκ ἐμβραδύνει ("he does not tarry/linger").
[^2]: Greek μογιλάλον, "speaking with difficulty," a hapax-type term describing impeded speech; the same word appears in the Marcan narrative (Mark 7:32). Rendered "who spoke with difficulty."

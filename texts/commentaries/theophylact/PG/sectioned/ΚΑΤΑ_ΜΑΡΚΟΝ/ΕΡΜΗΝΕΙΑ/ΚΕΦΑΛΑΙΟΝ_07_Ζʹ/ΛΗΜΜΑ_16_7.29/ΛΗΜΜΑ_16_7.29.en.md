# Mark 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 16 · Mark 7:29

> **He said to her: For this saying, go; the demon has gone out of your daughter. And having departed into her house, she found the demon gone out, and the daughter laid upon the bed. (Mark 7:29-30)**

After the Lord had spoken about foods, and saw the Jews disbelieving, he passes over to the borders of the gentiles. For, the Jews having disbelieved, salvation was about to come to be for the gentiles. He himself, however, principally is eager to be hidden, so that the Jews might not have grounds later to accuse him, as having run over to the unclean gentiles. Yet he could not be hidden; for it was not possible for him to be hidden and not be known by someone. So the woman, having heard about him, displays a warm faith. For this reason, therefore, the Lord also does not immediately hearken, but defers the gift, in order to show the woman's persevering faith, and that, although being sent away, she perseveres — so that we too may learn not to leap away at once whenever, having prayed, we do not immediately obtain, but to persevere until we receive.

And he names the gentiles "little dogs," as being reckoned defiled by the Jews; and "bread," the benefaction which God set apart for the children, that is, for the Hebrews — for the benefactions he sent to the Hebrews. So the Lord says that the gentiles must not partake of the benefaction which has been set apart for the Jews; but since the woman answered intelligently and faithfully, she obtained her desire. For "the Jews," she says, "have the bread — that is, you whole and entire, who came down from heaven, and your benefactions; but I ask for crumbs, that is, a partial benefaction." And observe the Lord also, how he did not say, "My power has saved you." But what does he say? "For this saying" — that is, for your faith — "go, for your daughter has been cleansed."

Learn, therefore, you too, from here a profitable lesson. For each of us, whenever he sins, is a woman, that is, a weak soul; and a Phoenician indeed, as having the crimson sin — both bloody and murderous.[^1] And such a soul has a daughter, the evil deed, which has a demon; for evil deeds are of demons. Being sinners, then, we are also named "little dogs," full of uncleanness; and for this reason we are not even worthy to receive the bread of God, that is, to partake of the undefiled mysteries. If, however, we recognize ourselves through humility, and confess that we are little dogs, and confess out our sins, then the daughter will be healed — that is, the demoniacal deed.

[^1]: A wordplay: «Φοινίκισσα» (Phoenician) is glossed by «τὴν φοινικὴν ἁμαρτίαν», the "crimson/scarlet sin" (from φοῖνιξ, purple-red), evoking the color of blood and murder (cf. Isa. 1:18, sins "as scarlet"). The pun cannot be reproduced in English.

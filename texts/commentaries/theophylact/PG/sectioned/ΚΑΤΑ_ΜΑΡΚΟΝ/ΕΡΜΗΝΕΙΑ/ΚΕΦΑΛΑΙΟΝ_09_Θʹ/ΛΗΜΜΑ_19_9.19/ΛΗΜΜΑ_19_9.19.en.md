# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 19 · Mark 9:19

> **And if your hand cause you to stumble, cut it off. It is good for you to enter into life maimed, than having the two hands to go away into gehenna, into the unquenchable fire, where their worm does not die, and the fire is not quenched. And if your foot cause you to stumble, cut it off; it is good for you to enter into life lame, than having the two feet to be cast into gehenna, into the unquenchable fire, where their worm does not die, and the fire is not quenched. And if your eye cause you to stumble, cast it out; it is good for you to enter one-eyed into the kingdom of God, than having two eyes to be cast into the gehenna of fire, where their worm does not die, and the fire is not quenched. For everyone shall be salted with fire. And every sacrifice shall be salted with salt. Salt is good. But if the salt become saltless, with what will you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another. (Mark 9:19)**

Having threatened those who cause stumbling, that it were profitable for them if they were cast into the sea, now he exhorts those who are made to stumble to guard themselves from those who are ready to cause them to stumble and to trip them up. For whether it be foot, or hand, or eye that is causing you to stumble — that is, whether it be one of your nearest kin, or of those near by blood and serviceable *to you* — cut it off, that is, renounce your friendship and kinship toward him.

But the worm and the fire that punish the sinners are the conscience of each one, and the memory of the shameful things done in this life; which, like a worm, devours, and, as fire, burns. For everyone, he says, shall be salted with fire, that is, shall be tested. Which Paul also says, that everything shall be tested by fire (1 Cor. 3:13). And every sacrifice too, he says, shall be salted with salt. This he said from Leviticus. For there God says, Every gift of your sacrifice shall be salted with salt (Lev. 2:13).[^1] It is good, therefore, for us to salt our sacrifices with the salt of God, that is, not to offer loose and slack sacrifices, but pungent and savory ones.

And the Lord calls the apostles also salt, and, simply, all who have a holding-together and binding power. For just as salt holds the flesh together, and does not permit it to breed worms, so also the teaching word, if it be pungent, binding tight the fleshly men, does not permit the sleepless worm to be bred in them. But if the teacher be saltless, and have not the pungency and the binding-power, with what shall he be salted, that is, be seasoned? Have therefore in yourselves salt, namely the savory and holding-together grace of the Spirit, that you may be at peace with one another. For he who is bound tight to his neighbor by the bond of love, this one has salt, and…[^2]

# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 18 · Mark 9:19

> **For whoever gives you a cup of water to drink in my name, because you are Christ's, amen I say to you, he shall not lose his reward. And whoever offends one of the little ones who believe in me, it is better for him rather, if a millstone is set about his neck, and he is cast into the sea. (Mark 9:19)**

Not only, he says, do I not forbid the one working mighty deeds in my name, but even if someone gives you some very small thing for the sake of my name, and receives you on my account—not for the sake of human or worldly favor—he shall not lose his reward. And he said *a cup* of water on account of those who plead poverty as an excuse. For even if, he says, you give a cup of water, the most trifling thing, not even this will you lose. And just as, if you honor one of the little ones, you are well-pleasing to God, so if you offend one of the little ones—that is, dishonor him, trip him up—it is expedient for you that a donkey's millstone[^1] were hung upon your neck; for we shall undergo, as it seems, a worse punishment. And he spoke of a perceptible punishment, frightening us through this perceptible example.

[^1]: μύλος ὀνικός, a large millstone of the kind turned by a donkey, heavier than the domestic hand-mill; the lemma above has the plainer λίθος μυλικός ("a mill-stone").

# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 11 · Mark 9:19

> **And he answering him says: O unbelieving generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I bear with you? Bring him to me. And they brought him to him. And seeing him, immediately the spirit convulsed him. And falling on the ground, he rolled about foaming. And he asked his father: How long a time is it since this has come upon him? And he said: From childhood, and often it has thrown him both into fire and into waters, that it might destroy him. But if you can do anything, help us, having compassion on us. And Jesus said to him: *As for* the "If you can"—all things are possible to the one who believes. And immediately the father of the child, crying out with tears, said: I believe, Lord; help my unbelief. And Jesus, seeing that the crowd was running together, rebuked the unclean spirit, saying to it: You mute and deaf spirit, I command you, come out of him and enter no more into him. And crying out and convulsing him much, it came out, and he became as if dead, so that many said that He is dead. But Jesus, taking hold of him by the hand, raised him up, and he arose. (Mark 9:19-27)**

That man, having come forward, blamed the disciples as not having been strong enough to heal. But Christ turns the charge back upon that man, all but saying this: that, being unbelieving yourself, you became the cause of your son's not being healed. Yet he says this not to that man only, but makes the statement general, as if reproaching all the Jews in common for unbelief. For it was likely that many of the bystanders were scandalized.

And he shows that death was desirable to him, from his saying: How long shall I be with you—that is, I have grown weary living among you unbelievers. Nevertheless he does not stop at blaming them, but also applies the cure—not undertaking to heal the child for display, but rather with much modesty. For see how he attributes the healing not to his own power, but to that man's faith, saying that: All things are possible to the one who believes.

But there is also another point: for seeing the crowd gathering together, he rebuked the spirit; for he did not wish to heal before a crowd, as one craving display and ostentatious. And by rebuking and saying, Come out and enter no more, he hints at this: that on account of the man's unbelief the demon would have entered again, had it not been prevented by his command. And he permits *the spirit* to convulse the child, so that all might know the malignity of the demon, and that it would have killed the man, had it not been held back by the divine hand.

And one is thrown by the demon into the fire of anger and of desire, and into the water—the surging flood of the affairs of life. And this demon is mute and deaf: deaf, as not wishing to hear the words of God; and mute, as not even able to teach others what is fitting. But if Jesus—that is, the Gospel word—takes hold of the hand (I mean the practical faculty), then we shall be set free from the demon. And see that God helps us, and then we ourselves are required to work the good. For it says: Jesus raised him up—this is the help of God; and he arose—this is that man's own zeal toward the good.

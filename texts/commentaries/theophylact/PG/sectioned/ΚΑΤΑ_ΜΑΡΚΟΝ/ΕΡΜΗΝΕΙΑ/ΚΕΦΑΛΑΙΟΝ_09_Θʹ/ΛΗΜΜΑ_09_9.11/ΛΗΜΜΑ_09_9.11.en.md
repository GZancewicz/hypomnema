# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 9:11

> **Elias indeed, coming, restores all things; and how is it written concerning the Son of man, that he should suffer many things? (Mark 9:12)**

And what he says is something like this: Elias the Tishbite, when he is about to come, is to make peace for the disobedient Jews, and to lead these to faith; so that he will be the forerunner of the second coming. For if the Tishbite were the forerunner of the first coming, being about to restore all things, how then is it written that the Son of man is to suffer this and that? Of the two, therefore, one alternative remains: either that Elias is not the forerunner of the first coming—if indeed the Scriptures speak truly, that Christ must suffer—or that the Scriptures do not speak truly, those which say that Christ will suffer, if indeed we should believe according to the word of the Pharisees, that the Tishbite will be the forerunner of the former coming. For Elias is to restore all things, and there will not then be any disobedient Hebrew, but all will believe the preaching, as many as hear Elias. Therefore the Lord, overturning the supposition of the Pharisees, says that Elias has already come—that is, John—and they did to him whatever they wished. For they set upon[^1] him, and in the end he was beheaded, becoming the prize of a game.[^2]

[^1]: The OCR reads Ἐπείθησαν, which as it stands would mean "they obeyed/were persuaded" and does not suit the context ("they did to him whatever they wished"). The sense requires something like ἐπέθεντο, "they set upon / attacked him"; rendered accordingly.

[^2]: παιγνίου ἔπαθλον, "the prize (reward) of a game / of sport" — an allusion to the dancing at Herod's banquet, for which John's head was given as the reward.

# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 1 · Mark 9:2

> **And after six days Jesus takes along Peter and James and John, and brings them up into a high mountain apart, alone, and was transfigured before them, and his garments became gleaming, exceedingly white as snow, such as a fuller upon the earth cannot whiten. (Mark 9:2)**

But Luke says "after eight days," not contradicting this, but rather agreeing exceedingly. For the one [Luke] counted both the very day on which he spoke and that on which he led them up; but Mark counted only those in between these. Having taken, then, the chief ones, he brings them up into a high mountain: Peter, as having confessed and as loving; but John, as being loved; and James, as being himself also most loud-voiced and most given to divine utterance. For he was so burdensome to the Jews that Herod[^1] too, wishing to please the Jews, killed him.

And he brings them up into a high mountain, so that the miracle might be the more glorious. And apart; for he was about to show a mystery. But understand the transfiguration not as a change of the form, but that, the form remaining such as it also was before, an addition of ineffable light came upon it.

[^1]: The manuscript reads a truncated «ρώδης»; reconstructed as Ἡρώδης, "Herod." The reference is to the killing of James the son of Zebedee (Acts 12:2).

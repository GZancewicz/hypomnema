# Mark 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 4 · Mark 9:7

> **…there came a cloud overshadowing them, and a voice came out of the cloud: This is my beloved Son; hear him. And suddenly, having looked around, they no longer saw anyone, but Jesus alone with themselves. (Mark 9:7-8)**

Elijah and Moses appear talking together *with him*, for many reasons; but two suffice. For since the disciples had said that the crowds — some say he is Elijah, and others, one of the prophets — he shows them the chief ones of the prophets, so that from this too they might learn the difference between the servants and the Master; this, then, is one reason. And another: since the many considered him opposed to God, as loosing the Sabbath and transgressing the law, he shows on the mountain the prophets — of whom the one [Moses] was the lawgiver, and the other [Elijah] a zealot *for it*. Such prophets, therefore, would not have conversed with one who seemed to loose the law, if what he says did not please them.

But Peter, fearing to go down from the mountain (for he dreaded the crucifixion of the Lord) says: It is good for us to be here, and not to go down there into the midst of the Jews. For even if those who rage against you should come here too, we have Moses, who [?drowned] the Egyptians;[^1] we have Elijah, who brought down fire from heaven and destroyed the captains of fifty. But what were the prophets talking about with him? The things concerning his cross and his death. And Peter said what he said, not knowing what he was saying; for they became terrified from the ineffable light and the glory. For he was not truly in his own mind, not wishing Jesus to go down to be crucified for our salvation, but to remain always on the mountain.

But the passage must be brought up also to *spiritual* contemplation. After the consummation of this world, which was created in six days, Jesus will bring us up too, if indeed we are his disciples, into the high mountain — I mean heaven — and will manifest himself to us more brightly. For now he appeared in dishonor, as one crucified and a carpenter's son; but then we shall see his glory as of the Only-begotten. And we shall see also the law and the prophets talking together with him — that is, the things spoken concerning him by both Moses and the prophets; then we shall understand and find them in agreement with the facts, and then we shall truly hear the Father's voice, the Father revealing to us the Son, and teaching that…

[^1]: OCR-corrupt: the manuscript reads «προπωαάμενον», an unrecoverable participle governing «τοὺς Αἰγυπτίους» ("the Egyptians"). The sense is a deed of destruction paralleling Elijah's; most likely a form such as καταποντίσαντα, "who drowned/sank *the Egyptians in the sea*" (the Red Sea). The rendering "[?drowned]" is a reconstruction.

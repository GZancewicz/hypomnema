# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 13:10

> **And first must the gospel be preached among all the nations. (Mark 13:10)**

Many shall come, such as Judas was, such as Theudas,[^1] who said of themselves that they had been sent by God. "And you shall hear of wars," he says, which Josephus too records to have taken place before the capture *of Jerusalem*. For the nation rose in revolt and would not pay the tributes to the Romans. But they, being angered, came against *them* and made continual assaults; yet not yet was there an end of Jerusalem, for the Romans showed forbearance.[^2] And not only did wars occur, but also God-sent plagues, famines and earthquakes, God plainly showing them that he himself was warring against them. And all these things are the beginning of birth-pangs, that is, of the evils that were to befall them.

"But take heed to yourselves; for they shall deliver you up to councils." Opportunely he inserted the account concerning these things—that they shall deliver you up to councils—so that they might have some consolation from the common *calamities* amid their own misfortunes. But also in saying, "For my sake you shall be led before governors and kings," he implanted in them no small consolation, namely that they are to suffer for his sake. And the *phrase* "for a testimony against them" means: for their being without excuse and thenceforth already condemned; because, even while torturing you, they were not able to prevail over the truth. Then, lest they should think that the dangers and the afflictions hinder the preaching, he says that the gospel must be preached among all the nations too, and then Jerusalem shall be taken. [^2]: ἐφιλανθρωπεύοντο γὰρ οἱ Ῥωμαῖοι — literally "for the Romans were acting benevolently/humanely," i.e. they still dealt leniently and had not yet brought the siege to its final end.

# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 31 · Mark 13:31

> **Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words shall by no means pass away. (Mark 13:31)**

Do you see that the Son too sends forth the angels, just as the Father does? Where then are those who say that he is not equal to God and Father? The angels then will go forth[^1] gathering together the elect, so that these, having been caught up in clouds, may meet the Lord. And from the fig tree learn what I say: just as, when the fig tree already has leaves, immediately *comes* the summer, so also, after the affliction of the Antichrist, immediately, with nothing intervening, *comes* the coming of Christ, which is truly summer for the righteous after winter, but for the sinners winter after summer.

*Verily I say to you that this generation* — that is, of all the faithful, of the Christians I mean — *shall by no means pass away, until all these things* come to pass, both those concerning Jerusalem and the coming of the Antichrist. For surely he does not mean the generation of the apostles. Since the apostles do not live until the consummation of the world, seeing that not even until the consummation of Jerusalem did the greater part of them attain to live. Therefore he means the generation of the Christians, consoling the apostles. For lest they suppose that, so many evils occurring, the faith would perhaps fail, he says: Take courage, the generation of the faithful will not pass away, nor will it fail. For sooner would these immovable elements — heaven, I mean, and earth — pass away, than my words fail in any respect. For all things whatsoever I said will be accomplished.

[^1]: The printed πλεύσονται means "will sail"; the sense requires a verb of motion (πορεύσονται, "will go," or ἐλεύσονται, "will come"), rendered here "will go forth."

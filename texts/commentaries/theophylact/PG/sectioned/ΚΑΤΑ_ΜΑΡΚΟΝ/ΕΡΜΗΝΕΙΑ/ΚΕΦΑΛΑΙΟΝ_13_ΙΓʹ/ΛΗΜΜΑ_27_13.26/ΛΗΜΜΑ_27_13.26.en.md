# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 27 · Mark 13:26

> **And then they will see the Son of Man coming in clouds with power and much glory. (Mark 13:26)**

He has already completed the things concerning Jerusalem; but he now goes on to speak concerning the coming of the Antichrist. As for the word "then," do not understand it to mean that, when the things spoken above concerning Jerusalem come to pass, then, "if anyone says to you: Behold, here is the Christ," you are not to believe; but rather know that this is an idiom of Scripture,[^1] just as Matthew too, after the birth of Christ, says: "In those days comes John" (Matt. 3:1). What days? Surely those following directly upon the birth of Christ? No, but *it is spoken* indefinitely. So therefore here too he says "then"—not when Jerusalem is laid desolate, but at the times of the coming of the Antichrist. Do not be led astray. For many will usurp the name of Christ,[^2] so as to lead astray even the elect. But after the coming of the Antichrist, all creation will be altered, the stars being darkened because of the surpassing excess of the light of Christ. And the angelic powers will be shaken—that is, they will be astonished—seeing so great a change taking place, and their fellow-servants being judged.[^3] And then they will see the Lord, as Son of Man, that is, in a body; for that which is beheld is altogether a body. But even if he comes embodied, as a man, yet *he comes* with power and much glory.

[^1]: The point is grammatical: the adverb «τότε» ("then") does not mark strict temporal sequence upon the fall of Jerusalem. The embedded saying «ἐάν τις ὑμῖν εἴπῃ· Ἰδοὺ ὧδε ὁ Χριστὸς, μὴ πιστεύσητε» ("if anyone says to you, Behold, here is the Christ, do not believe") echoes Matt. 24:23 / Mark 13:21; Theophylact cites it as an instance of the loose apocalyptic "then."
[^2]: Greek «ὑποποιήσονται» — will feign, appropriate, or lay false claim to (the name of Christ).
[^3]: The Greek of this clause is badly corrupted by OCR: after «ὁρῶσαι τοσαύτην μεταβολὴν γινομένην, καὶ τους» the line reads «νωιῖει. αλετῖί Δπει οὴμΟιοειαίεφίοτῖ ρ. Ο συνδούλους αὐτῶν κρινομένους». The legible tail «συνδούλους αὐτῶν κρινομένους» ("their fellow-servants being judged") is translated; the intervening garbled matter is illegible.

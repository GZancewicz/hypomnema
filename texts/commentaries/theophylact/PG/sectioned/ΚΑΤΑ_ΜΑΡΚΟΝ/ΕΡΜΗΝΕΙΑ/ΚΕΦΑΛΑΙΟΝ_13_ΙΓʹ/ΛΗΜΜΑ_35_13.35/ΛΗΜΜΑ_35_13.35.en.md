# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 35 · Mark 13:35-37

> **Watch therefore, for you do not know when the lord of the house comes, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning; lest, coming suddenly, he find you sleeping. And what I say to you, I say to all: Watch. (Mark 13:35-37)**

The Lord, wishing to restrain the disciples from asking about that day and hour, says that neither the angels nor the Son knows. For if he had said, "I know indeed, but I do not wish to reveal it to you," he would have grieved them. But now he handles it more wisely, and altogether prevents them from seeking to learn and from troubling him, in saying, "Neither the angels nor I know."

And from a certain example you will understand what is said. Often little children see their fathers holding something in their hands, and they ask for it. But the fathers do not wish to give it. And the children cry, as not receiving it; yet at last the fathers hide that which they hold, and, showing their hands empty to the children, make them cease from weeping. So also the Lord, dealing with the apostles as with children, hid the day. For if he had said, "I know, but I do not tell," they would have been vexed, as not learning it from him. But that the Lord knows the day and the hour is plain. For he himself made the ages; that which, therefore, he made, how does he not know it?

But indeed God has also profitably hidden the consummation of life, whether the universal one or that of each individual, in order that, the end being uncertain, we may always struggle, awaiting it and fearing lest it come upon us unprepared. And let us consider the words also. In the evening the end presents itself, when someone dies having grown old. At midnight, when someone is of middle age. At cockcrow, when the reason within us is brought to completion; for the cock is reason, which rouses us from the sleep of insensibility. When therefore the child begins to live according to reason and to think, then the cock crows within him. And the morning is the wholly childish age. All, therefore, must take forethought for the end. For even if it be an infant, forethought must be taken for this one also, lest it depart unbaptized.

And the Lord enjoins these things upon all, both upon the more worldly and upon the anchorites. We ought therefore both to watch and to pray, both these things together. For many watch indeed, but spending the nights on wicked things. And see how he did not say, "I do not know when the time is," but, "You do not know." For on account of our benefit he hid it. For if even now, the end being uncertain, we rage against one another, what would we not do if we knew the end? For we would spend the time before death on the utmost wickedness, and then, showing repentance on a single day, we would be harmed in the worst things.

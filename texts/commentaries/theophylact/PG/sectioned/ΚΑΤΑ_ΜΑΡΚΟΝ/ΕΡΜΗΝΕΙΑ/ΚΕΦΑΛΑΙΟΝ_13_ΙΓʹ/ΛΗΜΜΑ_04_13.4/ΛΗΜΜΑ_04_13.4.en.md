# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 4 · Mark 13:4

> **these things shall be? And what shall be the sign when all these things are about to be accomplished? (Mark 13:4)**

Since the Lord had been saying many things concerning the desolation of Jerusalem — that, *Your house is left to you desolate* (Matt. 23:38), and that magnitudes and beauties of buildings so great would be brought to nothing[^1] — they point out to him the temple's great comeliness. But he, none the less, foretells that these things will be destroyed, and so utterly that not even a stone will remain upon a stone. And indeed some say that many remnants remained of Jerusalem the old city, so that they attempt to show Christ false; but this is not so. For even if some remnants were left behind — which is not true — yet at any rate, until the universal consummation, there shall by no means remain a stone upon a stone. Nevertheless it is recorded that Aelius Hadrian[^2] razed the city and the temple from their very foundations, so that this too was fulfilled in his time: that not even a stone was left upon a stone.

And as he was sitting on the Mount of Olives, they came to him asking, When shall these things be? — that is, when shall the things of Jerusalem be accomplished? But he, before answering them concerning the things they had asked, secures their understanding, that they might not be led astray. For since, when the affairs throughout Judea began to fall sick, certain men rose up who proclaimed themselves teachers, the Lord says: Watch that you be not led astray.

[^1]: The printed Greek here is corrupt — «Ἀφίεται ὁ οἶκος ὑμῶν ἔρητοσαῦτα μεγέθη καὶ κάλλη οἰκοδομημάτων ἀφανισθήσεται»; read «Ἀφίεται ὁ οἶκος ὑμῶν ἔρημος, καὶ τοσαῦτα μεγέθη καὶ κάλλη οἰκοδομημάτων ἀφανισθήσεται» ("Your house is left desolate, and such great magnitudes and beauties of buildings shall be made to vanish").
[^2]: The printed «Αλιος Ἀδριανὸς» = Αἴλιος Ἀδριανός, the emperor Aelius Hadrian, who after the revolt razed Jerusalem to its foundations and refounded it as Aelia Capitolina.

# Mark 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 20 · Mark 13:20

> **And unless the Lord had shortened the days, no flesh would have been saved; but for the sake of the elect whom he chose, he shortened the days. (Mark 13:20)**

For if the flight should happen in winter, those wishing to flee will be hindered by the difficulty of the season, and altogether there will be an affliction more grievous than any that have ever happened or shall happen. And unless God had shortened, that is, had cut short in brief the war of the Romans, no flesh would have been saved, that is, no Jew would have been left; but for the sake of the elect, that is, those from among the Hebrews who had believed, or who were also going to believe later, the war was cut short in brief. For God, foreknowing that many from among the Hebrews would believe after the capture, for this reason did not permit the race to be utterly wiped out.

But these things can also be carried up to a more ethical sense.[^1] The abomination of desolation is every satanic thought, standing in a holy place, our mind. Then, accordingly, let him who is in Judea flee to the mountainous regions, that is, let him who has made confession run up to the mountains of the virtues. For Judea means "confession."[^2] And let him who stands on a height not come down. For when some passionate thought stands within us, we must through confession run up to the heights, and not come down from the height of virtue. And let him who works the good not turn back so as to take up again the old conscience, which is a garment that he has stripped off. But woe to him who flees in winter; for we must flee from sin with warmth, and not coldly and unmoved. For such is what fleeing in winter hints at.

[^1]: ἀναχθῆναι ἠθικώτερον: "to be carried up to a more ethical (moral) sense" — the anagogical/tropological reading, whereby the literal events are raised to signify the inner moral life.
[^2]: Ἰουδαία γὰρ ἐξομολόγησις λέγεται. The etymology derives Judah/Judea from the Hebrew root *ydh* ("to praise, confess"), hence "confession" (ἐξομολόγησις); on which the whole moral reading of the passage turns.

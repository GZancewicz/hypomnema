# Mark 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 10 · Mark 3:11

> **unclean spirits, whenever they beheld him, would fall before him and cry out, saying that, You are the Son of God. And he charged them much, that they should not make him known. (Mark 3:11)**

Who were the Herodians? Either the soldiers of Herod, or some newly-appeared sect teaching as doctrine that Herod himself was the Christ, because under him the succession of the Jewish kings received its end.[^1] For the prophecy of Jacob had determined that, when the rulers from Judah should fail, then the Christ would come (Gen. 49:10). Since therefore in the times of Herod there was no Jewish ruler, but he himself ruled, being of another race — for he was an Idumean — some supposed him to be the Christ, and they formed a sect. These, accordingly, were attempting to destroy the Lord.

But he withdraws; for the time of the passion was not yet. And at the same time also, so that he might benefit more people, for this reason he withdraws from the thankless ones. For indeed many followed him, and he healed them; but also the Tyrians and the Sidonians, being of another race, were benefited. But his own kinsmen were persecuting him. Thus there is no benefit in kinship, unless the character be good. And the foreigners were coming to him from afar, whereas the Jews were persecuting him as he came to them.

But observe also his freedom from love of glory: for indeed, so that the crowds might not trouble him, he seeks a small boat, so that, having embarked in it, he might be kept undisturbed. And by "scourges" he means the diseases; for indeed truly diseases contribute much to our instruction, God scourging us as a father his children.

But understand also that the Herodians, the fleshly and skin-clad ones, wish to kill Jesus — for "Herod" is interpreted "skin-clad."[^2] But those who have gone out from their houses and from their fatherlands, that is, from the relations according to the flesh — those follow him; whence also their scourges are healed (the sins, I mean, which scourge the conscience), and the unclean spirits are driven out.

But attend to the fact that Jesus commands his disciples that a small boat should attend upon him because of the crowds, so that they might not press upon him. For Jesus is the reason within us,[^3] who commands that our small boat, that is, the body, should attend upon him, and not be cast into the surge of the affairs of life, so that the crowds — the disturbances of affairs — might not press upon the Christ within us.

[^1]: "Succession" translates διαδοχήν; the lemma has the corrupt διαἑαμήν, an OCR distortion of διαδοχήν ("succession, line of descent").

[^2]: An etymological gloss on the name Ἡρώδης, glossed δερμάτινος ("of skin, skin-clad, leathern"); the wordplay ties Herod and the "Herodians" to τὸ σάρκινον, the fleshly/carnal, and to the "garments of skin" (Gen. 3:21). Cf. οἱ σάρκινοι καὶ δερμάτινοι just above.

[^3]: ὁ ἐν ἡμῖν λόγος — "the reason (Word) within us." λόγος is doubly loaded: Christ the Logos, and the rational faculty in the human being; the rendering "reason" is a compromise that the following lines (Christ within us, the body as boat) play upon.

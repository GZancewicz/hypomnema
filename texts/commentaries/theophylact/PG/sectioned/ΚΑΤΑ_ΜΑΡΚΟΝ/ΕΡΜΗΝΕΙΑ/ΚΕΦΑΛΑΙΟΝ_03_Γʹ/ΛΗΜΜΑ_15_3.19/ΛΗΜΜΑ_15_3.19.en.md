# Mark 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 15 · Mark 3:19

> **Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him. (Mark 3:19)**

He goes up into the mountain so as to pray. For since he had worked miracles, after the display of the miracles he prays, teaching us all that we must give thanks to God whenever we have succeeded in accomplishing something good, and attribute this to the power of God; or, since he was about to ordain[^1] the apostles, for this reason he goes up to pray, that we may learn that we too, whenever we are about to ordain certain persons, must first pray that the worthy one be revealed to us, and that we may not share in others' lawless deeds (cf. 1 Tim. 5:22). And he puts forward Judas too as an apostle, that we may learn that God does not turn away from the one who is going to work the evil on account of the wickedness that is to come, but on account of the virtue that is now present in him deems him worthy of honor, even if later he should turn out bad. And he enumerates the names of the apostles on account of the false apostles, that the true ones may be recognizable. And he names the sons of Zebedee "sons of thunder," as great heralds and most consummate in theology.

[^1]: χειροτονῆσαι — literally "to stretch out the hand," used of appointing by vote and, in ecclesiastical usage, of ordaining; rendered "ordain" here.

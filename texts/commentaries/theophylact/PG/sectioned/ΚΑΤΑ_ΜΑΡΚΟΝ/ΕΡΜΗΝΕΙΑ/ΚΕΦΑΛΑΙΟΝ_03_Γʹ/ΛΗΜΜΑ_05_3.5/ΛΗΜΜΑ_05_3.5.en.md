# Mark 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 5 · Mark 3:5

> **and having looked around on them with anger, being grieved together at the hardening of their heart, he says to the man: Stretch out your hand. And he stretched it out, and his hand was restored, healthy, as the other. (Mark 3:5)**

The disciples having been accused by the Jews, because on the sabbath they were plucking the ears of grain, the Lord muzzled the accusers, both through the example concerning David.[^1] But shaming them yet more now also, he works a miracle, showing that: So far are my disciples from sinning, that I myself also work on the day of the sabbath, displaying a miracle; so that, if to work a miracle is evil, then simply to do the necessary things also on the sabbath is evil; but truly, to work a miracle for the salvation of a man is divine — therefore he does not transgress the law who on the sabbath does nothing evil. On account of this, therefore, he also asks them whether it is lawful to do good, shaming them as hindering him from doing good.

But everyone who does not do the works of the right-hand portion has his right hand withered. To such a one Christ says: Rise up — that is, Stand up from sin — and stand in the midst, that is, into the mean of the virtues.[^2] For every virtue is a mean, inclining neither to deficiency nor to excess; then, accordingly, whenever he stands in the mean, his hand shall be restored healthy. But observe also the *was restored*. For indeed there was a time when we had healthy hands — that is, the practical activity — when there was not yet transgression; but from the time our hand was stretched out[^3] toward the forbidden fruit, from then it was withered as regards the working of the good. Again, therefore, it shall be restored to its former health, whenever we stand in the midst of the virtues.

[^1]: The word rendered "muzzled" is «ἐπεστόμισε» (literally, put something over the mouth, hence "silenced"). Here the OCR of «ὑποδείγματος» ("example") is corrupted by an intruding running-head fragment («ὑποδείΚΟΑΡΝΤΙ.γματος»); read «ὑποδείγματος».
[^2]: A wordplay: the Lord's «εἰς τὸ μέσον» ("into the midst") is read as «μεσότης» ("the mean," the middle state), the Aristotelian mean between deficiency and excess.
[^3]: The manuscript reads «ἐξετάσθη» ("was examined"), but the sense — the hand reaching toward the forbidden fruit — requires «ἐξετάθη» ("was stretched out"), which is here adopted.

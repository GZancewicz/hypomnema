# Mark 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 22 · Mark 3:26

> **Satan has risen up against himself, and has been divided, he cannot stand, but has an end. No one is able, having entered into the house of the strong man, to plunder his goods, unless he first bind the strong man, and then he will plunder his house. (Mark 3:26)**

From irrefutable examples he overturns the malicious Jews. For how, he says, is it possible for a demon to cast out demons? Just as we see also in households: as long as those in them are at peace, they hold together; but whenever they are divided, they are dissolved. But how, he says, can anyone plunder the goods[^1] of the strong man, unless he first bind him? And what he says is such: The strong man is the demon; and his goods are the men who make room for him. If therefore someone does not first bind the demon and destroy him, how will he plunder his goods—I mean the demon-possessed—from him? So that, since I plunder his goods—that is, freeing the men from the demonic affliction—I have accordingly first bound and destroyed the demons, and I am their enemy. How then do you say that I have Beelzeboul—that is, that being a friend of demons and a sorcerer, I cast out the demons?

[^1]: σκεύη, literally "vessels/chattels," here the strong man's household goods or property; Theophylact glosses them as the men whom the demon inhabits.

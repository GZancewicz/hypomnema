# Mark 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 7 · Mark 10:10

> **And Jesus, having looked around, says to his disciples: How hardly shall those who have riches enter into the kingdom of God? But the disciples were amazed at his words. And Jesus again answering says to them: Children, how hard it is for those who trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God. It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. But they were exceedingly astonished, saying among themselves: And who can be saved? But Jesus, looking upon them, says: With men it is impossible, but not with God. For all things are possible with God. (Mark 10:10)**

Not riches are evil, but those who have them are worthy of blame; for one must not "have" them, that is, hold them fast, but use them for what is needful. For "riches" are so named from their being for the use of men, not from being held fast.[^1] Those, then, who have and shut these things up shall hardly enter into the kingdom of God. But here understand "hard" in the sense of "impossible." For it is impossible for a rich man to be saved. And this is clear from the example which the Lord sets forth, saying that it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than a rich man into the kingdom of God. For it is impossible for a camel to go through the eye of a needle. And by "camel" understand either the animal itself, or a certain thick rope, which they use for the largest of ships.[^2] It is impossible, therefore, for a man, so long as he is rich, to be saved.

With God, however, this is possible. For Christ said: Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness (Luke 16:9). You see how, when we hearken to God, this becomes possible? But with men it is impossible — that is, when we think human things, this is impossible.

But for what reason were the disciples amazed at these words? For surely they themselves were not rich. To me it seems *they were amazed* because they were anxious on behalf of all men. For they had already begun to become lovers of mankind.

But some are here perplexed as to how he said, All things are possible with God. We say, therefore, that when he says "all things," he means the things that are; but sin is a non-being. For sin is a thing without essence and without subsistence; and besides, sin is not of power but of weakness, as also the Apostle says: Christ, while we were weak, died (Rom. 5:6). And again David: Their weaknesses were multiplied.[^3] So that sin, being weakness, is not possible with God. Then, they say, can God also make what has happened as not having happened? We say, therefore, that God is truth. But to make what has happened as not having happened is a falsehood. How, then, shall the truth make the falsehood? For it would first destroy its own nature; so that those who say this are as good as saying: Can God, then, not be God? For you see the ridiculousness of the question.

[^1]: An etymological wordplay: «Χρήματα γὰρ εἴρηται παρὰ τὸ εἰς χρῆσιν εἶναι ἀνθρώποις, οὐχὶ παρὰ τὸ κατέχεσθαι» — χρήματα ("riches/goods") is derived from χρῆσις ("use"), not from κατέχεσθαι ("being held/retained"). Untranslatable in English, hence rendered discursively.
[^2]: The Greek plays on κάμηλος ("camel") and its near-homophone κάμιλος ("ship's cable, hawser"); Theophylact glosses the word as either the animal or «σχοῖνόν τινα παχεῖαν» ("a certain thick rope"). Note also that his text varies the phrase for "eye of a needle" between τρυμαλιὰ ῥαφίδος and τρύπημα ῥαφίδος/βελόνης.
[^3]: «Ἐπληθύνθησαν αἱ ἀσθένειαι αὐτῶν» (Ps. 15:4 LXX). Theophylact's argument depends on the LXX reading ἀσθένειαι ("weaknesses, infirmities"); the Hebrew of Ps. 16:4 rather has "sorrows."

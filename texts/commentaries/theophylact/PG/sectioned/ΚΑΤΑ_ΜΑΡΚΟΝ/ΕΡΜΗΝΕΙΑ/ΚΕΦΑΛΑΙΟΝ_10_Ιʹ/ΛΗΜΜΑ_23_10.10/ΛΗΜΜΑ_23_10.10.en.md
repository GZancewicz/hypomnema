# Mark 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 23 · Mark 10:10

> **answering, Jesus says to him: What do you want me to do for you? But the blind man said to him: Rabbouni, that I may see again. But Jesus said to him: Go, your faith has saved you. And immediately he saw again and was following Jesus on the road. (Mark 10:10)**[^1]

But Matthew says two blind men; and it is possible that those who were healed were two, but that the more distinguished of them is this one now mentioned by Mark. And see how even the crowds honor Jesus, so that they rebuke the blind man crying out, as if some king were passing by. But the Lord asks him, so that no one might say that the Lord gives other things to one who wills other things.[^2] And grateful was the soul of the blind man; who indeed, after being healed, did not leave the Lord, but was following him.

But this can also be understood anagogically. Jericho is this place below. And the blind man sitting here is human nature, which was son of the God who is above all honor.[^3] This *nature*, then, cried out to Jesus as he journeyed through Jericho, that is, through this life. And he had mercy on it and saved it through faith, when it had stripped off the old garment of sin. And having been saved, it followed him, working the commandments on the road, that is, in this life. For here it is possible to follow Christ; but after these things all are shut out from working the commandments.

[^1]: OCR corruptions in the lemma, all emended: «σέκωκέ» read as σέσωκε, «ἀνἐβλεψε» as ἀνέβλεψε, and «Ὑαγε» as Ὕπαγε.
[^2]: The Greek is compressed: «ἄλλα βουλομένῳ ἄλλα δίδωσιν ὁ Κύριος» — literally "the Lord gives other things to one wishing other things," i.e. that the Lord bestows something other than what the man actually wants. The Lord asks the question so that the healing manifestly answers the man's own request.
[^3]: A wordplay on the name Timaeus (Τιμαῖος), father of Bartimaeus: Theophylact reads human nature as "son of Timaeus," glossing this as son «τοῦ ὑπὲρ πᾶσαν τιμὴν Θεοῦ» — "of the God who is beyond all honor (τιμή)." The OCR «Θευῦ» is corrupt for Θεοῦ.

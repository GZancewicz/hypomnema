# Mark 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 10:10

> **—a hundredfold, now in this present time, houses, and brothers, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and fields, with persecutions, and in the age that is coming, eternal life. But many that are first shall be last, and the last first. (Mark 10:10)**

Peter, even though he left few things, nevertheless names all these. For even few things have a bond of attachment, so that blessed is even he who leaves few things. But though Peter alone asked, the Lord makes the saying universal, saying: Everyone who has left either wife or father. And he says these things, not that we should leave our fathers without help, and separate from our wives, but training us to prefer piety toward God above all things of the flesh. For since war was being kindled from the preaching, and children were about to deny their fathers for the sake of piety, he says: Whoever shall leave his fleshly kindred, and simply all bodily things for the sake of the Gospel, shall receive all a hundredfold in the present age, and in the age to come eternal life.

Then shall he also receive a hundredfold wives? Yes; even though the accursed Julian mocked this.[^1] For tell me, what does the wife contribute to the household of the husband? Surely she takes care of the husband's food and clothing, and the husband is brought by these to complete freedom from care. If, then, this was so also in the case of the apostles — how many women cared for their clothing and their food, and ministered to them, who cared for nothing except only the word and the teaching? Likewise the apostles had many fathers and mothers, all those who loved them and were tenderly affectionate toward them; but also Peter, having left one house, afterward had the houses of all his disciples. And now, too, he has as splendid houses the temples everywhere on earth in his name; and what is greater, that with persecutions the saints inherit all these things — that is, being persecuted, and not living in ease, but being afflicted. Hence also those who seem to be last in the present age because of being afflicted and persecuted shall be first because of their hope in God; but also the Pharisees, being first, became last. But those who left all and followed Christ became first.

[^1]: Julian the Apostate (emperor 361–363), who in his polemic against the Christians ridiculed the promise of a hundredfold of wives. «κατάρατος» = "accursed."

# Mark 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 14 · Mark 10:10

> **—said to them: What do you wish me to do for you? And they said to him: Grant to us that we may sit, one at thy right hand and one at thy left, in thy glory. But Jesus said to them: You know not what you ask. Are you able to drink the cup which I drink, and to be baptized with the baptism with which I am baptized? And they said to him: We are able. (Mark 10:10)**

Another evangelist says that their mother approached Christ (Matt. 20:20). And it is likely that both took place: both that the apostles, being ashamed, put their mother forward, and then that they themselves also approached privately; wherefore the evangelist too notes this, saying, *They come to him*, instead of, they approach privately, having withdrawn from the others.

But let us learn what it was that they were asking. They supposed that this—that he goes up to Jerusalem—he goes up in order to reign over a perceptible kingdom. And after reigning, then he would suffer what he was about to suffer. Therefore, thinking these things, they ask for both the seat at the right and the seat at the left; wherefore the Lord also rebukes them as seeking something senseless. For "you know not," he says, "what you ask." For you suppose my kingdom to be perceptible, and you ask for a perceptible seat. But this is not so; rather all these things are beyond understanding. And to sit at my right hand is a great thing, surpassing even the angelic ranks; and you gape after glory, but I call you to death. For he names the cross "cup" and "baptism": a cup, as leading straightway into sleep and as being gladly received by him;[^1] and a baptism, as effecting a cleansing of sins. But they, not understanding what he meant, promised, supposing that he was speaking about a perceptible cup, and about a baptism such as the Jews were baptized with, washing themselves before eating.

[^1]: ποτήριον μὲν, ὡς εἰς ὕπνον εὐθὺς ἄγον καὶ ὡς ἡδέως ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ προσδεχθέν. The image is of the cup (of the Passion) as a draught that quickly brings on sleep (i.e. death), one that Christ willingly accepted.

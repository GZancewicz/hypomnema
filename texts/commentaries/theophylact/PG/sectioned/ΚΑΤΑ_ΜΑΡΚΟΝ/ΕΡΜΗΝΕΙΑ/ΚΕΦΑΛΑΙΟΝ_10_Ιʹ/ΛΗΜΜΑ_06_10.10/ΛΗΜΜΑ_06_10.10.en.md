# Mark 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 6 · Mark 10:10

> **And as he was going out into the way, one ran up, and having knelt before him, questioned him: Good Teacher, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life? But Jesus said to him: Why do you call me good? No one is good except one, God. You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not bear false witness, do not defraud. Honor your father and your mother. But he answering said to him: Teacher, all these I have kept from my youth. But Jesus, looking upon him, loved him, and said to him: One thing you lack; go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven, and come, follow me, having taken up the cross. But he, saddened at the word, went away grieving, for he was one who had many possessions. (Mark 10:17-22)**

*Some suppose this young man to have been someone* base[?] and wicked and given to testing.[^1] But this is not so; rather the man was money-loving, but not a tester. For hear the evangelist, that Jesus, looking upon him, loved him. Why then did Christ answer him thus, saying: No one is good? Because he came to him as to a man, and as to one of the many teachers. For this is what Christ says: If you think me to be good as a teacher, no man is good, in comparison to God; but if you think me to be good as God, why then do you name me teacher? So that this is what Christ wills through these words: to make the man's understanding higher, unto recognizing him as God.

But he accomplishes something else as well: to teach him, whenever he is about to converse with someone, not to converse flatteringly, but to know God as the root and the fountain of goodness, and to refer the honors to him. And I marvel at this young man, that while all the others came to him for the healing of diseases, he himself seeks the inheritance of eternal life, even if he was held fast by a most terrible passion, the love of money, on account of which, having heard, Go, sell, and give to the poor, he went away grieving.

But observe: he did not say, Go, sell what you have piecemeal and give, but sell at one stroke and give—and to the poor, not to flatterers and prodigals. But also: come, follow me, that is, pursue all the rest of virtue as well. For many are without possessions, but not humble-minded; or even humble-minded, but drunkards or having some other evil. Therefore for this reason the Lord says: Sell, and give to the poor, and follow me, having taken up the cross—that is, having prepared yourself for death on my behalf.

But he, saddened at the word, went away. For he was one who had many possessions; and not in vain is this word "many" added. For those who have many things and those who have few are not held fast alike, but more terrible and harder to loose are the bonds of many possessions. But even if someone be a rather young man, light in understanding and not settled in his wits, let him sell his belongings—such as anger and desire, and whatever evils grow out of these—and let him give these and cast them off to the demons, who are poor, deprived of every good and fallen out of the wealth of the goodness of God. And then let him follow Christ. For he who has cast off the wealth of sins to the demons—that is, casting off the evil wealth to the poor powers of the demons—that one is able to follow Christ; and do good: this is to follow Christ and to take up the cross.[^2]

[^1]: The fragment begins mid-sentence in the OCR: "…λόν τινα καὶ πονηρὸν καὶ πειραστικόν." The opening word survives only as "-λόν"; the sense (confirmed by the following "But this is not so; rather the man was money-loving, but not a tester") is that Theophylact is reporting and rejecting an opinion that this man was wicked and out to test Jesus. The reconstructed adjective is probably φαῦλόν ("base, worthless"); rendered here "base[?]."
[^2]: Lines 61–64 of the Greek are OCR-scrambled and partly duplicated: "Ὁ γὰρ ἀποῤῥίψας τὸν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν πλοῦτον τοῖς δαίμοσιν, ἐκεῖνος δύναται ἀκολουθεῖν / τὸ ἀποῤῥίψαι τὸν κακὸν πλοῦτον ταῖς πτωχαῖς δυνάμεσι τῶν δαιμόνων· καὶ ποίησον ἀγαθὸν, τοῦτο τὸ ἀκολουθεῖν τῷ Χριστῷ, καὶ ἆραι τὸν σταυρόν." The received sense is that one who casts off the wealth of his sins onto the poor powers of the demons is thereby able to follow Christ; the translation reflects this, noting the duplicated gloss.

# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 25 · Mark 8:26

> **Woe to you, Chorazin; woe to you, Bethsaida, for if in Tyre and Sidon the mighty works had been done which were done in you, they would long ago have repented. (Matt. 11:21)**[^1]

Accordingly, when the Lord had come here, they bring to him a blind man. But the faith of those bringing him was not genuine, whence also the Lord leads the blind man away out of the village, and thus heals him. And he spits into the eyes of the blind man and lays on his hands, so that we may learn that a divine word, and an action following upon the word, is able to accomplish the miracles. For the hand is a symbol of action, but the spittle of the word, which is from the mouth. Nor, however, did the blind man have perfect faith; for this reason neither does he make him recover his sight at once, but by degrees, as one not having whole faith; for according to the faith the healings also come about.

But he charges him not to enter into the village, because, as I say, since those in Bethsaida were unbelieving, the man would have been harmed in his soul; but neither to tell anyone what had happened to him, lest they, disbelieving, draw upon themselves a worse judgment. And we, therefore, are often blind in soul, being in the village, that is, in this world. Then, being led away outside the village—that is, the world and its affairs—by Christ, we are healed. But after we are healed, he tells us no longer to return into the village, but into the house. And the house of each of us is heaven and the mansions there.

[^1]: The lemma captured here is not Mark 8:26 itself but the woe over Bethsaida (Greek: «Οὐαί σοι, Χοραζίν· οὐαί σοι, Βηθσαῖδά…»), which Theophylact quotes as a lead-in to the healing of the blind man of Bethsaida (Mark 8:22–26). The wording follows Matt. 11:21 (cf. Luke 10:13), though this section is filed under Mark 8:26.

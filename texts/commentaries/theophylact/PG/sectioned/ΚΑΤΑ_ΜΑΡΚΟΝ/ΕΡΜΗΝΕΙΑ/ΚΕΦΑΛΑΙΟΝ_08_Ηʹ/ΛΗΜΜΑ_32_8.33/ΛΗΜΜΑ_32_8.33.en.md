# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 32 · Mark 8:33

> **having turned about and seen his disciples, he rebuked Peter, saying: Get behind me, Satan, for you do not mind the things of God, but the things of men. (Mark 8:33)**

When he had secured them, once they had confessed that he himself is the true Christ, then he uncovers to them also the mystery of the cross, even if not altogether nakedly; for not even so did they yet understand what he was saying,[^1] nor did they conceive what the rising again is, but they thought it better to suffer nothing at all. Therefore also Peter begins to blame him, as though he were casting himself into death when it was possible to suffer nothing. But the Lord, showing that his passion will come to pass for salvation, and that Satan alone does not wish him to suffer—so that he might not save men—names Peter "Satan," as one minding the things of Satan and not wishing him to suffer, but opposing him. For "Satan" is interpreted "adversary." And "Get behind me," he says, that is, "Follow my will, and do not array yourself against me, nor come as an opponent to me, but follow behind me." And he said that Peter minded the things of men, inasmuch as, minding certain lowly and carnal things, he wished the Lord to be at ease, and not to be crucified and to fall into trial for the salvation of the world.

[^1]: OCR corrupt here: the text reads «οὐδὲ γὰρ, οὐδὲ οὕτω συνίεσαν πι λένιι», where "πι λένιι" is unintelligible; reconstructed as «συνίεσαν τί λέγει» ("understood what he was saying").

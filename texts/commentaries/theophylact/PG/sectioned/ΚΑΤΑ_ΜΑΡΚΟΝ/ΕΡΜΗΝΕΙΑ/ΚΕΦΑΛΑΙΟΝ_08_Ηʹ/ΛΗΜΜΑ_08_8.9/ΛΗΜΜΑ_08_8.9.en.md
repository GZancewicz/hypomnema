# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 8 · Mark 8:9

> **had eaten, about four thousand, and he dismissed them. (Mark 8:9)**

Already before, the Lord had worked some such wonder, and now too he works a wonder, having seized upon a reasonable occasion. And it was this: that the crowd remained three days, and the provisions of each had been used up. For he did not always work wonders concerning food, so that the crowds might not [follow him merely for the sake of it].[^1] And now therefore, if there had not been a danger expected to come upon the crowd from lack of food, he would not have worked the wonder. But observe the disciples too, how they are still in a foolish state, not yet believing in his power from the previous events. But he reproaches them in nothing, teaching us too not to rebuke the ignorant too severely, but in some matters even to be forgiving toward them as being foolish.

And consider this too, that Christ wishes to dismiss no one fasting. For he wishes all to luxuriate in his gifts, and especially those who remained with him three days, that is, those who have been baptized. For since baptism is called illumination, and baptism is accomplished in three immersions, those who are illumined through baptism are within three days. Accordingly he takes the seven loaves — I mean the more spiritual words. For the number seven is a symbol of the Spirit. For the Spirit is that which perfects all things, and in the number seven our life is perfected, and this age. And the illumined eat and are satisfied, and they leave leftovers. For they cannot eat all the divine thoughts.

But there, in the wonder of the five loaves, the leftovers were twelve baskets; for they were five thousand, that is, those enslaved to the five senses. And on account of this they were not able to eat much, but were content with little. Wherefore also the things left over were many; whereas here the baskets are seven and the remnants few, because they were four thousand, that is, those trained in the four virtues, and on account of this, as being stronger, they ate much and left little. For those things alone they could not eat, whatever were more spiritual and deeper; for this is what the seven baskets are. And learn this too, according to the literal narrative, that we must stop at need, and seek nothing more. For behold, the crowds, having eaten and been satisfied, did not take away with them the remnants of the loaves, but the disciples took these up, as it were to keep to the proportion of the need.[^2]

[^1]: The Greek here is defective: «Οὐ πάντοτε γὰρ ἐθαυματούργει ἐπὶ ταῖς τροφαῖς, ἵνα μὴ … ὄχλοι» — a verb governing ὄχλοι has dropped out. The supplied sense is that the crowds should not follow him merely for the sake of food.
[^2]: «ὥσπερ κεῖσθαι τῇ συμμετρίᾳ τῆς χρείας» is compressed: literally "as it were, to be laid up by the proportion of the need," i.e. to abide within the measure of what is needed.

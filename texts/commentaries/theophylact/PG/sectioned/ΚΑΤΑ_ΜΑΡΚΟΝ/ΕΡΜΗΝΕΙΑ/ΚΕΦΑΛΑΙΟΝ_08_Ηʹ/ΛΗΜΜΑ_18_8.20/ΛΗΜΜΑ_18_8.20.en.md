# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 18 · Mark 8:20

> **four thousand, the fillings of how many baskets of fragments did you take up? And they said: Seven. And he said to them: How is it that you do not yet understand? (Mark 8:20)**[^1]

The Lord dismisses the Pharisees, as incorrigible. For where there is hope of correction, there one must linger; but where the evil is incorrigible, from thence one must leap away. And by *providential* dispensation[^2] the disciples forget to take loaves, so that, being rebuked, they might become better, and might come to a perception of the power of Christ. For when the Lord had said to take heed of the leaven of the Pharisees—that is, of their teaching—they supposed that the Lord was forbidding them the leaven of loaves. Whence they are reasonably rebuked as not understanding the power of Christ, that he is able to make loaves from things that are not.

And he names the teaching of the Pharisees and of the Herodians "leaven," as being rank-smelling[^3] and full of old wickedness. For everyone who has grown old in wickedness, and who is able to say nothing spiritual so as to sweeten the throat of the hearer—such a one has leaven: a teaching of old wickedness that bites, and afterwards leads the one who has been persuaded into regret.

But who were the Herodians? Certain newly-appeared teachers, who said that Herod is the Christ, and that one must believe in him.

[^1]: For the two feedings Theophylact's text keeps the Gospel's distinction of vessels: κόφινοι (the "baskets" of the five thousand, Lemma 17) versus σπυρίδες (the "baskets" here, of the four thousand). Both fall together as "baskets" in English; the phrase πόσων σπυρίδων πληρώματα κλασμάτων is literally "the fillings of how many baskets of fragments."
[^2]: Greek οἰκονομικῶς — "by (divine) economy/dispensation," i.e. by providential arrangement rather than mere chance.
[^3]: Greek ὀζώδη, "giving off an odor, rank," playing on the fermenting, sour quality of ζύμη ("leaven").

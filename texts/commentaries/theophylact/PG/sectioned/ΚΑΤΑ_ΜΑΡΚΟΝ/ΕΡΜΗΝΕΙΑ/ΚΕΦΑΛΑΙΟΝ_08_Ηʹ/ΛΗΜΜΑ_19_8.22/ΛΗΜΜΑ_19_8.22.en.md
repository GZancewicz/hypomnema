# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 19 · Mark 8:22-26

> **And he comes to Bethsaida, and they bring to him a blind man, and they entreat him, that he might touch him. And having taken hold of the hand of the blind man, he led him out outside the village. And having spit into his eyes, having laid his hands upon *him*, he asked him if he saw anything. And having looked up, he said: I see the men, for as trees I behold *them* walking. Then again he laid his hands upon his eyes, and made him see again. And he was restored, and saw all things clearly. And he sent him away to his house, saying: Neither enter into the village, nor tell anyone in the village. (Mark 8:22-26)**[^1][^2]

[^1]: The Greek here is lightly garbled by OCR; read ἀποκατεστάθη for the printed «ἀποκατκστάθη» ("he was restored"), ἀναβλέψαι for «ἁναβλέψαι», and Εἶτα for «Εἰτα». In the clause «ἐπιθεὶς τὰς χεῖρας αὐτοῦ;» the printed semicolon is a comma.
[^2]: «Βλέπω τοὺς ἀνθρώπους, ὅτι ὡς δένδρα ὁρῶ περιπατοῦντας» — literally "I see men, that as trees I see *them* walking." The ὅτι-clause explains the manner of seeing; hence "for as trees I behold *them* walking." τηλαυγῶς ("clearly," properly "far-shining, at a distance") in the following clause is rendered "clearly."

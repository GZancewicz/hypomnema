# Mark 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 29 · Mark 8:30

> **he charged them, that they should tell no one about him. (Mark 8:30)**

Having led them far away from the Jews, then he asks concerning himself, so that, fearing no one, they might confess the truth. They answer, therefore, that some suppose him to be John; and others, Elijah. For the many thought that John had risen—just as indeed Herod also supposed—and that he had taken on from the resurrection also the working of signs; for while he was living John did no sign. After, therefore, he had asked them the suppositions of the others, he asks them also their own opinion. All but saying this: Those, being deceived, hold such an opinion about me, but you—say something else.

What, then, does Peter do? He confesses him to be the Christ proclaimed by the prophets. But what the Lord said in response to Peter's confession, and how he blessed him, Mark omitted, lest he seem to say these things out of favor to Peter, who was his teacher; but Matthew has recounted everything without omission. And he charged them that they should tell no one about him. For he wished the report about himself to be kept veiled, lest many be scandalized at him and, on this account disbelieving, become liable to a greater punishment.[^1]

[^1]: "The report about himself": Greek τὴν περὶ αὐτοῦ δόξαν. Here δόξα is the opinion/repute of who he is rather than "glory"; the sense is that he wished the growing recognition of his identity to be kept hidden for a time.

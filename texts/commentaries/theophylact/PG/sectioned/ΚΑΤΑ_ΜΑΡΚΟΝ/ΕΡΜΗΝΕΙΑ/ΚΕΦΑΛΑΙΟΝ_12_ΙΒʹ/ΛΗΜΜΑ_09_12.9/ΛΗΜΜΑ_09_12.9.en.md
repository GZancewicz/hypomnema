# Mark 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 12:9

"Having brought them in, plant them in thy holy mountain" (Exod. 15:17). And the hedge is the law, preventing them from mingling with the other nations. And the tower is the temple, being conspicuous. And the winevat is the altar, where the blood was poured out. And he let out the people to husbandmen, to the teachers and rulers of the Jews from time to time. And he sent one servant — perhaps the prophets around Elijah, such as Micaiah, whom Zedekiah the false prophet struck (1 Kings 22:24). And a second servant, whom, having stoned, they wounded in the head[^1] — that is, they completed and brought to a head the outrage — the prophets around Hosea and Isaiah. And a third servant, those in the captivity, the prophets around Daniel and Ezekiel. And last of all God sent his Son, who was named "man" on account of his love for mankind, saying that "They will reverence my son." But he said this not as though ignorant of what was to come, but stating what ought to have been done and what was reasonable. But the wicked husbandmen, having learned that this is the Son of God, cast him out of the vineyard, that is, outside of Jerusalem, and killed him. For outside the city he was crucified (Heb. 13:12). Therefore the lord of the vineyard, the Father of the murdered Son — or rather the murdered Son himself — will destroy the husbandmen, delivering them over to the Romans. And he will let out the people to other husbandmen, that is, to the apostles. For do you wish to see how the apostles cultivated the vineyard? Read the book of Acts, and you will find three thousand and five thousand all at once believing (Acts 2:41; 4:4) and bearing fruit to God.

[^1]: Theophylact glosses the Markan word ἐκεφαλαίωσαν (Mark 12:4, "they wounded in the head") by its other sense, "to sum up, bring to a head": «ἐκεφαλαίωσαν, τουτέστι, συνετέλεσαν καὶ ἐκορύφωσαν τὴν ὕβριν» — "they wounded in the head, that is, they completed and brought to a head (ἐκορύφωσαν) the outrage." The rendering keeps the primary sense in the body and reflects the wordplay in the following clause.

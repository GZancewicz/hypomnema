# Mark 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 15 · Mark 12:16

> **brought it. And he says to them: Whose is this image, and the inscription? And they said to him, Caesar's. And answering, Jesus said to them: Render the things of Caesar to Caesar, and the things of God to God; and they marveled at him. (Mark 12:16)**

We said at another time also concerning the Herodians, that there was a certain newly-appeared sect of those who said Herod to be the Christ, because in his times the successions of the Jewish kings had failed. But others say that the Herodians are here called the soldiers of Herod, whom the Pharisees took to themselves, so that they might be witnesses of the things about to be said by Christ, and might thereafter seize him and lead him away. But observe their malice, how with flattery they attempt to deceive the Lord: *We know that you do not look upon the face of man* — so that you will not stand in awe even of Caesar. And this whole thing was a contrivance, having precipices on both sides, so that if he should say that it is lawful to pay to Caesar, they might slander him before the crowd as enslaving them; but if he should say not to pay, they might accuse him as one stirring up the people against Caesar. But the fountain of wisdom escapes their wiles. *For show me,* he says, *the coin.* And seeing that it has an image of Caesar, he says, *Render that which has the image to him who is imaged,* that is, to Caesar, *and the things of God to God* — that is, the paying to Caesar in nothing hinders you as regards piety toward God. For you can both pay tribute to Caesar and render to God the things that are his own. But Caesar is, for each of us, also the inescapable necessity of the body. The Lord therefore commands to render to the body also its own foods and necessary coverings, and the things of God to God — vigilance, such prayer as is possible,[^1] and the rest. But to the devil also, who is a Caesar, cast back the things given to you by him — wrath, evil desire — and the things of God, offer to God.

[^1]: Greek ἀγρυπνίαν ἐνδεχομένην προσευχήν. The participle ἐνδεχομένην ("admissible, such as is possible") is ambiguous: either "prayer so far as is possible" or "fitting/becoming prayer"; the syntax may also link it to ἀγρυπνίαν ("wakefulness").

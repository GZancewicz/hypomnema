# Mark 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 22 · Mark 12:26

> **dead, that they are raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, at the bush, how God spoke to him, saying: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living. You therefore greatly go astray. (Mark 12:26)**

A sect of the Jews, the Sadducees, saying there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit. And obliquely, as crafty men having come forward, they fabricate this narrative, ostensibly overturning the resurrection through it, having woven a supposition that never happened, nor perhaps ever will happen. And they put together seven men who married the woman, so that they might the more disparage the resurrection. The Lord therefore, since those men put forward the law of Moses, shows them to be ignorant of the Scriptures. For you do not understand, he says, what sort of resurrection the Scripture speaks of. For you suppose that again such a more bodily condition is going to be, but it is not. Thus, then, you are ignorant of the meaning of the Scripture; but you are ignorant also of the power of God. For you perhaps look to the difficulty of the matter, and on account of this you are utterly at a loss how the dissolved bodies will be able to come together with the souls. But to the power of God this is as nothing. There will therefore be a resurrection, and not a bodily one, but a more divine and angelic condition of life. For since we are not going to undergo corruption, marriage will not be instituted;[^1] for now marriage comes about on account of corruption, so that, being maintained by the succession of the race, we might not be brought to naught. But then, just as the angels, without marital succession, are the same and never cease, so also those who rise, without marriage, remain undiminished.

And in another way too they are convicted of not knowing the Scriptures. For if they had understood these, they would have understood also how it is said, "I am the God of Abraham," namely as *the God* of the living. For he did not say, "I was," but, "I am"—as though those men too are risen and standing, and not perished. But perhaps someone might say that the Lord has said this concerning the soul of Abraham alone, not also concerning the body, so that the bodies do not rise. I say therefore, first, that Abraham is spoken of as the both-together, soul and body, so that He is God also of the body, since it too is living with God and has not altogether passed into non-being. Then, that the Sadducees were in doubt concerning the resurrection of the bodies. So that the Lord too spoke concerning the bodies, that they live with God, not concerning the souls; for this was already agreed even among the carnal Sadducees. Then consider this also: the resurrection is a rising-up of that which has fallen. But it was not the soul that fell, for it is immortal, but the body; this therefore also will rise, being joined again to its yoke-fellow soul.

[^1]: The Greek here is corrupt: after «οὐ μέλλομεν φθείρεσθαι» ("we are not going to undergo corruption") the text reads only «θήσεται» ("it will be set/instituted"), evidently a truncation. The sense required by the following clause ("for now marriage comes about on account of corruption") is that marriage will no longer be needed/instituted; rendered "marriage will not be instituted" as a best guess.

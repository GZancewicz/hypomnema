# Mark 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 27 · Mark 12:33

> **to love him with the whole heart, and with the whole understanding, and with the whole soul, and with the whole strength, and to love the neighbor as oneself, is more than all the whole burnt offerings and the sacrifices. And Jesus, seeing that he had answered sensibly, said to him: You are not far from the kingdom of God. And no one any longer dared to question him. (Mark 12:33-34)**

Matthew says that he came testing *him*, but Mark here says that he answered sensibly. Do they then contradict one another? No, but it is likely that at first he asked as one testing, and then, having been benefited by Christ's answer and having answered sensibly, he was praised. But observe also the praise, how it does not testify that he is perfect. For he did not say, "You are within the kingdom of God," but "You are not far."

For what reason did this lawyer put forward this question to Christ at all? Thinking to catch Christ as one who would emend the law, and on account of this to accuse him as opposed to the law. But the Lord, showing him that it is from not having love, but from being consumed with envy, that he comes to such questions, says that the first and great commandment is to love God, and the second, like it, to love the neighbor. But how is it like it? Because they are held together by one another. For he who loves God loves also his creation; and the most kindred of his creations is man, so that he who loves God will love also all men, and he who loves the neighbor much more *loves* God. For if he loves men, who often provide occasion for offenses and hatred, much more *will he love* God, who ever does good.

But hear also the Lord: "He who loves me will keep my commandments" (John 14:15). Do you see that from loving God we keep his commandments, and his commandments all treat of the affection toward one another? And again, "By this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another" (John 13:35). Do you see again how, from loving one another, there is established the having of friendship toward Christ, and being his disciples and friends?

But consider how he enumerated all the powers of the soul. For there is a power of the soul, the vital one, which he hints at in saying, "with the whole soul." For he wishes the *incensive* faculty[^1] and the desire to be given wholly to the love of God. There is also another power of the soul, the one called vegetative, which is called nutritive and augmentative; and this too, therefore, must be given wholly to God. And there is also the rational power, which the law named "mind" (διάνοια). Accordingly, all the powers of the soul must be dedicated to love.

[^1]: Greek τὸν θυμόν, the "spirited" or incensive part of the soul (anger/passion) paired here with ἡ ἐπιθυμία, "desire"; the two together make up the ζωώδης (vital/animal) power.

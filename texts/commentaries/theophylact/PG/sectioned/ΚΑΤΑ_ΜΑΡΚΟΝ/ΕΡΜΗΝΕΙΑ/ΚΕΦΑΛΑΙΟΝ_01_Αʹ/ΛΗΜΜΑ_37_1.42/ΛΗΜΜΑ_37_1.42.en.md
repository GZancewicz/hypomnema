# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 37 · Mark 1:42

> **When he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed. (Mark 1:42)**

The leper was right-minded[^1] and faithful, wherefore he did not say, "If you ask God," but, "If you will," believing in him as God. And Christ touches him, in order that he might show that nothing is unclean; for the law commanded not to touch the leper, as unclean. But the Savior, showing that nothing is unclean by nature, and that the observances of the law must be abolished, and that these prevail over mere human beings *only*, touches the leper — and yet Elisha so feared the law that he did not endure even to see Naaman (2 Kings 5:9-10), though he was a leper and in need of healing.

[^1]: εὐγνώμων: "right-minded, reasonable, well-disposed." The word shares its root with εὐγνωμονεῖν ("to be grateful"), rendered "to be grateful" in the exposition on Mark 1:45 (Lemma 40); here the sense is the leper's sound judgment in ascribing power directly to Christ.

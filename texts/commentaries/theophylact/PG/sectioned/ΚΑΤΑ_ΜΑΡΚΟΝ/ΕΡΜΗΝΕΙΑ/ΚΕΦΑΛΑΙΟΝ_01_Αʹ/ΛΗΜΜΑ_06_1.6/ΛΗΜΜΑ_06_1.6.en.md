# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 6 · Mark 1:6

> **And John was clothed with camel's hair, and a leather girdle about his loins, and eating locusts and wild honey. (Mark 1:6)**

We spoke about these things in the commentary on Matthew, and now we will say too what was passed over there: that the garment was a symbol of mourning, the prophet showing that the one repenting must mourn. For sackcloth is *a sign* of mourning; but the leather girdle signifies the deadness of the Jewish people. And that the robe signified mourning, the Lord also says, *We mourned to you, and you did not lament* (Matt. 11:17), calling the Forerunner's manner of life a dirge. For he says, *John came neither eating nor drinking, and you said: He has a demon* (Matt. 11:18). But the food of John also, altogether, shows self-control of itself. But perhaps it was also a symbol of the people's rational[^1] food at that time—not eating any of the clean birds of heaven, nor thinking anything lofty, but nourished by a word that seems to travel aloft and to leap up, but falls down again; for such is the locust, leaping as if toward the height, then falling down again. Just as, therefore, the people also ate honey produced by the bees—that is, by the prophets—but not cultivated and tamed through being rightly understood and sought and grasped. For the Hebrews had the Scriptures as a kind of honey; but they did not cultivate them, nor search them.

[^1]: λογικῆς: "rational" in the sense of "of reason / of the word (λόγος)," pertaining to spiritual understanding.

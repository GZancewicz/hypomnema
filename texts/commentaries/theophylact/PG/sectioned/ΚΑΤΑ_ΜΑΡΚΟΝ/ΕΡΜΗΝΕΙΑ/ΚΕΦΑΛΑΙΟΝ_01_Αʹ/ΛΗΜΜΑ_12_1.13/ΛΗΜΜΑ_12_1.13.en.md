# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 12 · Mark 1:13

> **And he was there in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan; and he was with the wild beasts, and angels ministered to him. (Mark 1:13)**

The Lord, teaching us not to be vexed if after baptism we fall into temptations, for this reason goes up *into* the mountain[^1] to be tempted. Or rather, he does not go up, but is led up by the Holy Spirit, showing to us also that we must not leap upon temptations, but, when they come on, receive them. And for this reason he goes up into the mountain, that the devil might take courage from the desolation of the place and approach; for he is accustomed, whenever he sees us alone, then to set upon us. And the place was untrodden to such a degree that it even teemed with wild beasts; but the angels ministered to him after the conquering of the tempter.[^2] And these things are set out more fully in the *Gospel according to Matthew*.[^3]

[^1]: Theophylact writes εἰς τὸ ὄρος (\"into the mountain\") here and again just below, although the Markan lemma speaks of the wilderness (ἔρημος). The wording is his own, drawing on the tradition of the mountain of the temptation; it is rendered as he gives it rather than harmonized to the lemma.
[^2]: The Greek of the object is OCR-corrupt: μετὰ τὸ νικῆσαι θοῆὐραστὴν. Read τὸν πειραστήν (\"the tempter\"), which suits the sense (\"after conquering the tempter\").
[^3]: OCR καὶ τοῦτα ἐν τῷ κατὰ ἐατοσίον κετται λατύτερον, read as καὶ ταῦτα ἐν τῷ κατὰ Ματθαῖον κεῖται πλατύτερον—a cross-reference to Theophylact's fuller treatment in his commentary on Matthew (cf. Matt. 4:1-11). The name ἐατοσίον is corrupt; Ματθαῖον is the expected reading given the parallel.

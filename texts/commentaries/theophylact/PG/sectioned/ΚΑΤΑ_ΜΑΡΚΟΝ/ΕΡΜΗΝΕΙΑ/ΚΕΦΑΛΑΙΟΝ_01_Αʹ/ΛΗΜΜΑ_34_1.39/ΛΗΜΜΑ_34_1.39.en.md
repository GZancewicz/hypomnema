# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 34 · Mark 1:39

> **And he was preaching in their synagogues throughout all Galilee, and casting out the demons. (Mark 1:39)**

After he had healed the sick, he withdraws privately, teaching us not to do anything for display; but even if we do some good thing, to be eager to escape notice.[^1] And he also prays, showing us that we must attribute it to God if we do any good thing, and say to him, "Every good giving is from above, coming down from you"[^2]—for surely he himself did not stand in need of prayer. But though the crowds seek him and long for him, he does not give himself up *to them*, but welcomes them indeed, yet nonetheless proceeds also to the others who need healing and teaching.[^3] For one must not circumscribe the teaching within one place, but everywhere extend the rays of the word. But see how he interweaves action with the teaching: for preaching, he then also cast out the demons. So therefore you also, along with teaching, act as well, that your word may not be in vain. For if Christ had not also displayed miracles, his word would not have been believed.

[^1]: The Greek here is corrupt in the OCR: πρὸς ἐπίεαι.ξίν and λανΒάνειν are read as πρὸς ἐπίδειξιν ("for display, for show") and λανθάνειν ("to escape notice, to be hidden"); the sense is secure.
[^2]: An adaptation of James 1:17, «Πᾶσα δόσις ἀγαθὴ… ἄνωθέν ἐστι καταβαῖνον»; Theophylact recasts it in the second person, ἐκ σοῦ καταβαίνουσα ("coming down from you"), as a form of address to God.
[^3]: The Greek Ζητοῦσι δὲ αὐτὸν τοῖς ὄχλοις καὶ ποθοῦσιν is grammatically loose (the datives τοῖς ὄχλοις where a genitive absolute or nominative subject would be expected); rendered here as "though the crowds seek him and long for him." The clause ἀποδέχεται μὲν, βαδίζει δὲ ὅμως is a plain μέν…δέ contrast: "he welcomes them indeed, yet nonetheless proceeds."

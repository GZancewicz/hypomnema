# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 20 · Mark 1:23-27

> **And there was in their synagogue a man in an unclean spirit, and he cried out, saying: Ah! what have we to do with you,[^1] Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know you, who you are, the Holy One of God. And Jesus rebuked him, saying: Be muzzled,[^2] and come out of him. And the unclean spirit, having convulsed him and having cried with a loud voice, came out of him. And they were all amazed, so that they questioned among themselves, saying: What is this? What is this new teaching, that with authority he commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey him? (Mark 1:23-27)**

[^1]: τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί — literally "what to us and to you," a Semitic idiom of dissociation ("what have we in common with you?"); rendered here by the customary "what have we to do with you."
[^2]: φιμώθητι — literally "be muzzled" (from φιμόω, to put a muzzle on); the literal image of stopping the mouth is retained rather than the smoother "be silent."

# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 3 · Mark 1:3

> **of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.» (Mark 1:3)**

John, the last of the prophets, he says is a beginning of the Gospel of the Son of God. For the end of the Old is the beginning of the New Covenant; but the testimony concerning the Forerunner was taken from two prophets. For from Malachi is the, *Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who shall prepare your way before you*; but from Isaiah the, *A voice of one crying*, and what follows. And the word is from God and the Father to the Son, saying the Forerunner to be a messenger,[^1] because of his having an angelic and almost immaterial life, and his announcing and declaring Christ as coming; and he prepared the way of the Lord, making ready the souls of the Jews through baptizing to receive Christ. But the *before your face* means this, that Near you shall be your messenger; whence the intimacy of the Forerunner toward Christ is shown; for indeed those most intimate go before, near, the kings also. But *A voice of one crying in the wilderness*—perhaps in the wilderness of the Jordan, but perhaps also in the Synagogue of the Jews, which was desolate of every good. And the way is the New Covenant; but the paths are the Old, as worn out. Toward the way, therefore, they ought to have been prepared, that is, toward the New Covenant, but to make the paths of the Old straight—having received these of old, then having shown themselves crooked and having gone lame away from their paths[^2]

[^1]: ἄγγελος means both "messenger" and "angel"; Theophylact exploits the ambiguity, glossing the title by the Forerunner's *angelic* (ἀγγελικήν) life and by his *announcing* (ἀγγέλλειν). English cannot hold both senses in one word; "messenger" is kept in the body, with the play carried by the following clause.

[^2]: The fragment breaks off here mid-sentence. OCR readings restored in the lemma and exposition: *ὀδὶν*→ὁδόν, *πὺθείας*→εὐθείας, *Ἡσοΐου*→Ἡσαΐου, *λἱόν*→Υἱόν, *ῥδόν*→ὁδόν.

# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 40 · Mark 1:45

> **But he, having gone out, began to proclaim many things, and to spread abroad the word, so that he[^1] could no longer openly enter into a city, but was outside in desert places, and they came to him from every quarter. (Mark 1:45)**

And through these things we are taught not to make a display when we do some good to someone. For behold, he himself also charges the one cleansed not to spread report of him. And yet he knew that that man would not be persuaded, but would spread it abroad; nevertheless, as I said, teaching us freedom from ostentation,[^2] he charges *him* to tell no one. Yet the one who has been benefited ought to be grateful and to give thanks, even if the benefactor has no need *of it*. For indeed the leper, even though the Lord charged him *otherwise*, yet himself spread abroad the benefaction. But he sends him to the priest, because the law commanded that, unless the priest should declare the former leper to have become clean, it was not permitted for him to enter into the city otherwise, but *he was* to be driven away. And he commands also to offer the gift, which by custom those who were cleansed used to present, so that this might be a testimony that he is not opposed to the law, but so cherishes it that even the things *enjoined* by the law…[^3]

[^1]: The OCR here reads αὐπὴν; the received text is αὐτὸν, referring to Jesus — "so that he [Jesus] could no longer openly enter into a city."
[^2]: τὸ ἀφιλόκομπον: literally "the un-boastful *disposition*," i.e. the avoidance of ostentation or vainglory.
[^3]: The fragment breaks off mid-sentence at τὰ ὑπὸ τοῦ νόμου ("the things [enjoined] by the law…"); the thought is completed in the following section.

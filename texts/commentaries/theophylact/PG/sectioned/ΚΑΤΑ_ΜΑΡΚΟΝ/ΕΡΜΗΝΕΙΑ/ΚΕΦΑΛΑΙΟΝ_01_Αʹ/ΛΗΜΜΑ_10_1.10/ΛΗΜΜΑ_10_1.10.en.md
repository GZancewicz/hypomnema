# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 10 · Mark 1:10

> **And immediately, coming up from the water, he saw the heavens being rent, and the Spirit as a dove descending upon him; and a voice came from the heavens: You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. (Mark 1:10-11)**[^1]

Neither for the sake of having sins forgiven does Jesus come to the baptism, for he committed no sin; nor as one about to partake of the Spirit—for the baptism of John, as I said, since it did not cleanse sins, how could it have given the Spirit? But indeed neither does he come to be baptized for the sake of repenting, for he was far better even than the Baptist. Why then does he come? Surely in every way that he might be made manifest to the people by John. For since many were streaming together, he was pleased to come, so that it might be testified before these many who he is; and at the same time that he might fulfill all righteousness—that is, all the commandments of the law. For since this too seems to be a commandment, namely to obey the prophet who baptizes as one sent by God, he fulfills this also. And the Spirit comes down, not because Christ had need of this—for how, seeing it was remaining in him essentially?—but that you might learn that when you are baptized, upon you too the Holy Spirit will come. And at the same time the descent of the Spirit effected this also: for since the Father said from above, *This is my Son*,[^2] lest those hearing should suppose that he speaks concerning John, the Spirit comes down upon Jesus, showing that it was said concerning him. And the heavens are rent, that we might learn that when we are baptized, they are opened for us also.

[^1]: OCR corrections: εἶδε σχιζομένους τοὺς οὐρανούς, καὶ τὸ Πνεῦμα ὡσεὶ περιστερὰν καταβαῖνον ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν, καὶ φωνὴ ἐγένετο ἐκ τῶν οὐρανῶν· Σὺ εἶ ὁ Υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός, ἐν ᾧ εὐδόκησα. The quotation embraces both verse 10 and verse 11; the (ref) is a locator only.
[^2]: Theophylact here reports the heavenly voice as Οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ Υἱός μου (\"This is my Son\"), the form given in Matthew 3:17, although the Markan lemma he has just quoted reads Σὺ εἶ ὁ Υἱός μου (\"You are my Son,\" Mark 1:11). His argument—that the voice pointed away from John to Jesus—turns on the demonstrative \"this,\" so the Matthaean wording is deliberate; the divergence from his own lemma is preserved rather than corrected.

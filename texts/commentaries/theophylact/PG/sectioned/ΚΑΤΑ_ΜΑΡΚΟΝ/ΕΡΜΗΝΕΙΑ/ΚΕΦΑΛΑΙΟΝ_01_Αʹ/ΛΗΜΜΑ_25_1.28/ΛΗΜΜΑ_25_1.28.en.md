# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 25 · Mark 1:28

> **And the report of him went out immediately into the whole surrounding region of Galilee. (Mark 1:28)**

The evil spirits are called unclean because they take pleasure[^1] in all shameful deeds. And the demon regards his going out from the man as his own destruction. For, being merciless, the demons suppose that they suffer some evil if they themselves are not permitted to do harm to men. But indeed, being also lovers of the flesh and accustomed to be nourished by the material vapors, they have great want of nourishment whenever they do not dwell in a body; wherefore also the Lord says that by fasting the demonic kind goes out (cf. Mark 9:29). And the foul one did not say, "Thou art holy," — for many even of the prophets were holy — but "The Holy One," with the addition of the article, the one and only and determinate one (cf. Mark 1:24). But Christ muzzles him, that we may learn that even if the demons speak true things, they must be muzzled. And the demon throws him down and convulses him, so that those who see, having beheld from what an evil the man is set free, might believe on account of the miracle.

> **And immediately, having gone forth out of the synagogue, he came into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. And Simon's mother-in-law lay sick with a fever, and immediately they speak to him about her. And— (Mark 1:29-30)**

[^1]: The OCR reads ἐφδεσθαι, a corruption of ἥδεσθαι, "to take pleasure in, delight in" (with dative). The sense is that the spirits are called "unclean" because they delight in shameful deeds.

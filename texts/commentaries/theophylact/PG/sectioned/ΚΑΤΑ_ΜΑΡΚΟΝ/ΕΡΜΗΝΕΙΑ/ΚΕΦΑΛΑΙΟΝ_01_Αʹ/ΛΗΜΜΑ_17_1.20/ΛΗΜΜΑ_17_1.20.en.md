# Mark 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 17 · Mark 1:20

> **immediately he called them. And having left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men, they went away after him. (Mark 1:20)**

Peter and Andrew were disciples of the Forerunner. But having seen Jesus witnessed to by John, they came to him. Then, since John was delivered up, being grieved, they withdrew again to their own trade. Whence Christ calls these a second time; for this is a second calling. But observe them being nourished from just labors, and not from unjust ones; for such are those worthy to become the first disciples of Christ. Having therefore immediately left the things in their hands, they followed him; for one must not delay, but follow immediately. But after these he takes James and John in his net,[^1] who, though they too were poor, nevertheless supported their father in his old age. And they left their father, not because it is good to leave one's fathers, but because he was about to hinder them with respect to the following *of Christ*. So therefore you also, whenever you are hindered by your parents, leave them and run to the good. For Zebedee appears not to have believed; the mother of the apostles, however, *appears* to have believed, for she also followed *him*, perhaps once Zebedee had died. And know this too, that first the practical *life* is called, then the contemplative. Now those around Peter are a symbol of the practical *life*; for this man is most fervent, and leaping ahead of the others, such as indeed the practical *life* also is. But those around John hint at contemplation; for John is most theological.

[^1]: Greek σαγηνεύει, "he catches with a dragnet" (σαγήνη), continuing the fishing imagery of the passage; rendered here "takes in his net."

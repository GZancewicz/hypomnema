# Mark 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 23 · Mark 14:24

> **…he said to them: This is my blood, that of the New Covenant, which is poured out for many. Amen I say to you, that I shall by no means any longer drink of the fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God. (Mark 14:24-25)**

Some say that Judas did not partake of the mysteries, but went out before the Lord delivered the mysteries.[^1] But others say that he imparted the sanctified elements even to him, the ungrateful one. And *having blessed*, in the sense of *having given thanks*, he broke the bread. Which we also do, adding prayers over it. *This is my body*—this which you now receive. For the bread is not an antitype of the Lord's body, but is changed into that very *body* of Christ. For the Lord also says: *The bread which I shall give is my flesh* (John 6:51). He did not say, "It is an antitype of my flesh," but, "It is my flesh." And again: *Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man…* (John 6:53). And how? one says; for is not flesh seen there?[^2] Because of our weakness, O man. For since bread and wine are customary to us, but seeing blood set before us and flesh, we could not have borne it, but would have recoiled; on this account the Lover of mankind, condescending to us, preserves the appearance of bread and wine, but transelements them into the power of flesh and blood.[^3] And he said *blood of the New Covenant*, by contrast with the Old. For the Old Covenant too, that one also had blood, by which both the people and the book of the law were sprinkled (cf. Heb. 9:19). And *I shall by no means drink of the wine*, he says, until the resurrection. For he names the resurrection *kingdom*, as having then reigned over death. At any rate, after the resurrection he ate and drank with the disciples, giving assurance that he himself is that same one who suffered. And he drinks it *new*, that is, in a certain new and strange manner; for he no longer had a passible body, needing nourishment, but an incorruptible and immortal one. But understand it also thus: the vine is the Lord himself; and the fruit of the vine is the mysteries, and the hidden knowledge, which he himself begets, who *teaches man knowledge* (cf. Ps. 93:10 LXX). Accordingly, in the kingdom of God—that is, in the eighth age—he shall drink with his disciples the mysteries and the wisdom, teaching us certain new things, and revealing what is now hidden.

[^1]: The OCR here is corrupt: «ἐξελθεῖν πρὶν ΠΔ κήριοπαραδῷ τὰ μυστήρια». Reconstructed as «ἐξελθεῖν πρὶν ἢ ὁ Κύριος παραδῷ τὰ μυστήρια», "to go out before the Lord delivered the mysteries."
[^2]: The Greek is elliptical and voices an objector: «καὶ πῶς; φησίν· οὐ γὰρ σὰρξ καθορᾶται;»—literally "and how? he says; for is not flesh seen?" The sense is: how can this be flesh, since no flesh is visibly present?
[^3]: "Transelements" renders μεταστοιχειοῖ (from μεταστοιχειόω), a technical term for the transformation of the elements; there is no exact English equivalent.

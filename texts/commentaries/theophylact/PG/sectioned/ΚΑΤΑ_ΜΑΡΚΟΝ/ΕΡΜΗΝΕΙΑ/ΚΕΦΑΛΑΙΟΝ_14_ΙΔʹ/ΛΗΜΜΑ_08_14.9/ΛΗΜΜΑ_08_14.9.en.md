# Mark 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 8 · Mark 14:9

> **...this Gospel be proclaimed in all the world, what this woman did shall also be spoken of, in memory of her. (Mark 14:9)**

The Lord blames the apostles, as unseasonably hindering the eagerness of the woman. For why, he says, do you cause her troubles?—after she offered the gift, turning her away through the reproach. And at the same time he also strikes the betrayer, in saying that "For my burial she did this," and reproves him as one without conscience. All but saying this to him: You, betraying me to death, do not reprove yourself; but this woman, bringing the myrrh for my burial, as if moved by God, is she worthy to be reproved by you?

And here the Lord prophesies two prophecies: both that the Gospel shall be proclaimed in all the world, and that the work of the woman shall be proclaimed together with it. Let them therefore be ashamed henceforth, those who prefer the poor to Christ. For I myself have heard goldsmiths saying that "If, having broken up the sacred vessel, I give to the poor—and I too make my living[^1]—there is no condemnation." Let them therefore hear how Christ prefers the service of himself to the poor. And Christ's body, properly speaking, is that on the golden paten, and the blood that in the cup. Therefore he who takes away the precious paten, and compels the body of Christ to be placed in a cheaper one, while making the poor his pretext, let him know of what portion he is.

[^1]: Greek «καὶ ἐγὼ ζήσω», lit. "and I too shall live," i.e. and I too make my living by it. The goldsmith's excuse: that melting down a sacred vessel to give alms (while also profiting himself) incurs no judgment (κρίμα).

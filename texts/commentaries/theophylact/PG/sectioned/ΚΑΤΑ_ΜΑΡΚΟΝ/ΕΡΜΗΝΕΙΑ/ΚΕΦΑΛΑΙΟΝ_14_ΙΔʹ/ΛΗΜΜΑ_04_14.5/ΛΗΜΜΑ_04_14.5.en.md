# Mark 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 4 · Mark 14:5

> **For this could have been sold for above three hundred denarii, and given to the poor. And they were murmuring against her. (Mark 14:5)**

On the fourth day [i.e. Wednesday] the plot was formed. And for this reason we too fast on the fourth days [Wednesdays]. Those men, then, wished to postpone the time of the feast, but they were not permitted; rather he himself, keeping for himself the time of the passion, was pleased to be crucified at the Passover. For he himself was the true Passover. Whence one may marvel at his power. For when they wished to seize[^1] him, they were not able; but when they did not wish to on account of the feast, then he of his own will gave himself up.

*And while he was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper, there came a woman.* All four *evangelists* mention the ointment. And it seems to some to be one *woman*, but it is not; rather they are two. One, the one in John, who is also the sister of Lazarus; and another, the one in the remaining three. But take heed, and you will find these to be three: in John, one; in Luke, another; and in the two *evangelists* [Matthew and Mark], yet another one. For the one in Luke was both a harlot and *appears* around the middle of the preaching, whereas the one in Matthew *is* near the time of the passion. And she is not admittedly a harlot; but the Lord accepts her purpose, as *one who* spent so much on the nard. Understand πιστική[^2] nard either as a kind of nard, so called "pistic," or as the genuine nard, prepared with faith.

*But there were some who were indignant.* But John says that Judas was indignant. It is likely, then, that the other apostles too blamed the woman, as having heard Christ always teaching about almsgiving, but that Judas was indignant against the woman not with the same aim, but on account of his avarice and shameful love of gain. For this reason, then, John also mentions him alone, as having blamed the woman with a treacherous aim. *And they were murmuring against her* — that is, they were indignant, they reviled, they rebuked her.

[^1]: Here the printed text is broken by an intrusion of the running head (καΟΑΓΝΤ / ΚΙΝ.) splitting the word κατασχεῖν ("to seize, lay hold of"); read κατασχεῖν.
[^2]: The lemma reads νάρδου πιστικῆς. Theophylact explains πιστική either as the name of a species of nard or, by wordplay on πίστις ("faith"), as nard that is ἄδολος ("unadulterated, genuine") and "prepared with faith." Rendered "pure" in the scripture lemma above.

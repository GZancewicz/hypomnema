# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 15 · Mark 2:20

> **And days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast in those days. (Mark 2:20)**[^1]

And the disciples of John, being disposed in a more imperfect manner, continued in the Jewish customs; whence some come to Christ, bringing these forward as an example, and asking[^2] why his disciples do not fast on equal terms with them. But he says: For now I am the Bridegroom, and they must be of good cheer and not fast; but when I am taken away from this life,[^3] then, having fallen into trials, they will both fast and be afflicted.

So then he names himself Bridegroom, not only as wedding the virgin souls, but also because the time of his first coming is not one of mourning nor of gloominess for those who believe in him, nor does it involve toils, but easily and apart from the works of the law, through baptism, he gives us rest. For what toil is it to be baptized? But nevertheless through this easy thing we find salvation. And the sons of the bridechamber are the apostles, as being themselves also deemed worthy of the joy of the Lord, and partakers of every heavenly good and of all delight.

But understand it also thus: that every man, whenever he works virtue, is a son of the bridechamber. So then, as long as he has with himself the Bridegroom Christ, he does not fast — that is, he does not display the works of repentance. For how should he who does not stumble? But whenever the Bridegroom Christ is taken away from him — that is, clearly, when he has slipped into sin — then he fasts and repents, that he may heal the sin.

[^1]: The lemma here reads ἐλεύσονται καὶ ἡμέραι ("and days will come"); the common text has ἐλεύσονται δὲ ἡμέραι ("but days will come").
[^2]: Greek αἰτούμενοι (so read); the form in the text (αἰτώμενοι) could also point to αἰτιώμενοι, "faulting, bringing a charge," which the context of complaint would equally suit.
[^3]: Greek ἀπὸ τοῦ βίου τούτου (the text's ἂίου corrected), "from this life."

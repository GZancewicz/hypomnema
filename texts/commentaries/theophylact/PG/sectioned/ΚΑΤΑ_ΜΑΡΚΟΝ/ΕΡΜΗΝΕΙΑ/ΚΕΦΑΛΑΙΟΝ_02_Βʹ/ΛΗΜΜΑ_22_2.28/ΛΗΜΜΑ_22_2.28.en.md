# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 22 · Mark 2:28

> **...the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath. (Mark 2:28)**

The disciples of the Lord pluck the ears of grain, inasmuch as they were no longer accustomed to live according to the law; but the Pharisees, being indignant, are put to silence by Christ, who brings forward David, who out of necessity dissolved the law in the time of Abiathar the high priest. For the prophet David, fleeing from the face of Saul, came to this high priest, and deceived him, as though he had been sent by the king upon a necessary military errand. It was then that he ate the loaves of the presentation, and took away the sword of Goliath, which had been dedicated to the Lord. And twelve were the loaves set forth each day upon the table, six on the right and six on the left.

But some inquire how the evangelist here named the high priest Abiathar, whereas the book of Kingdoms names him Abimelech.[^1] It is possible, then, to say that Abiathar perhaps had two names, the same man being called also Abimelech. Or otherwise: the book of Kingdoms says that Abimelech was priest at that time, whereas the evangelist says that Abiathar was high priest. So that these things are not contradictory; for Abimelech was priest, but Abiathar was high priest.

But understand also according to the anagogical sense, that the disciples of Christ on the Sabbath — that is, in the rest of souls (for "Sabbath" is interpreted as "rest")[^2] — therefore whenever they have rest from the passions, not being warred upon by the demons, then they make a way; that is, they become guides for others also unto virtue, plucking and rooting up all things that sprout up from the earth and from the lower imagination.[^3] For unless one first rest from the passions, and establish himself in tranquility, he could not make a way for others, and become a guide unto the good.

[^1]: The "book of Kingdoms" (ἡ βίβλος τῶν Βασιλειῶν) is the Septuagint designation for the books of Samuel–Kings; the reference is to 1 Kingdoms (1 Samuel) 21:1-6, where the priest is named Ἀβιμέλεχ (Ahimelech), against Mark's Ἀβιάθαρ (Abiathar).
[^2]: The parenthesis is partly damaged in the OCR («ἀναπαύσειβατον γὰρ κατῶπαυσις ἐρμηνεύεται»); reconstructed as «Σάββατον γὰρ κατάπαυσις ἑρμηνεύεται» ("for 'Sabbath' is interpreted as 'rest'"), the standard Hebrew etymology invoked by Theophylact.
[^3]: The verb rendered "make a way" is ὁδοποιοῦσι, echoing the Markan phrase «ὁδὸν ποιεῖν» (Mark 2:23, the disciples making a path as they plucked): Theophylact turns the literal path-making through the grain-field into the spiritual sense of clearing a road of virtue for others (ὁδηγοί, "guides").

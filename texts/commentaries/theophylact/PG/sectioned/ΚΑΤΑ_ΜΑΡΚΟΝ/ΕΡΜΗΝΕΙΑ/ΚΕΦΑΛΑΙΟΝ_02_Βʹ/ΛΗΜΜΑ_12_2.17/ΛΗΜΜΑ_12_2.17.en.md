# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 12 · Mark 2:17

> **And Jesus, having heard, says to them, "Those who are strong have no need of a physician, but those who are ill; I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance." (Mark 2:17)**

After completing the miracle upon the paralytic, the Lord goes out beside the sea, perhaps wishing to be by himself, but the crowd runs together again, that you may learn that as much as you flee glory, so much does it pursue you. If, however, you pursue it, it will flee from you. Just as, therefore, the Lord also fled beside the sea, and again the crowd pursued him closely; yet not even so, but from there too he fled. And passing by, he caught Matthew alive;[^1] for the Levi now spoken of by Mark is Matthew, for he was of two names. Now Luke and Mark name him Levi, concealing the name; but that man himself is not ashamed, but announces the thing concerning himself; for "Jesus," he says, "beheld Matthew the tax-collector." Thus, therefore, let us also not be ashamed to confess our own sins.

Levi was sitting at the tax-office, as it seems, demanding payment from certain persons, or reckoning accounts, or doing some other of the things which tax-collectors are accustomed to do in their own workshops. But he was so aroused that, having left all, he followed; and, as one rejoicing, he called together many to a banquet. But the Pharisees find fault, making themselves out, forsooth, to be pure. But the Lord says, "I did not come to call the righteous" — that is, you who justify yourselves (for he says this ironically) — "but sinners"; and he calls them, not *that they should remain sinners, but that they should repent. For lest anyone should say that* he calls the sinners but improves them in nothing, he adds the phrase, "to repentance."[^2]

[^1]: «ἐζώγρησε» — "took alive, captured alive," a hunting/fishing metaphor (cf. ζωγρέω, Luke 5:10, "catching men alive").
[^2]: The OCR here is disordered and defective: after «καλέσαι δὲ αὐτοὺς, οὐχ» a clause is lost before «καλεῖ μὲν τοὺς ἁμαρτωλοὺς, οὐδὲν δὲ αὐτοὺς βελτιοῖ». The italicized words reconstruct the expected sense (that he calls them not to remain in sin but to repent, and that, lest anyone think he leaves them unimproved, he adds «εἰς μετάνοιαν»).

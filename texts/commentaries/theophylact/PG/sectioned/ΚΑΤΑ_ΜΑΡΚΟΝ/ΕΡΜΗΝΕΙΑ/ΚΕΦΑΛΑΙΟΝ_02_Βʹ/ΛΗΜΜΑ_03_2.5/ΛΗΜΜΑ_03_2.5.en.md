# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 3 · Mark 2:5

> **—Jesus, *having seen* their faith, says to the paralytic: "Child, thy sins are forgiven thee." (Mark 2:5)**

What is [the meaning of] "after some days"? [It stands] for "when some days had passed." But when he had entered, they, having heard that he is in a house, ran together, expecting to meet with him more easily. And great was the faith of the men, so that they even dug through the roof and let *him* down; wherefore the Lord also heals, having seen the faith of them who carried *him*, or also of that paralytic himself. For that one too would not have consented to be lifted up, if he had not believed that he would be healed. For often, on account of the faith of the one who brings, he heals those who are brought, though they are unbelieving; but often the contrary, on account of *the faith* of the one who is brought, although those who bring are unbelieving.

But first he forgives the sins of the sick man, then he also heals the disease. For indeed the more grievous of the illnesses arise, for the most part, from sins, just as the Lord says that the paralytic in John also was paralyzed from sins (John 5:14). For the one in John and the one now mentioned are not one and the same, but different and different;[^1] for the one in John had no man, but the one now has four; and the one was at the sheep-pool [i.e. Bethesda] (John 5:2), but the one now in a house; and the one in Capernaum, the other in Jerusalem; and you might find still other differences as well. Only know that the one in Matthew (Matt. 9:2) and the one now mentioned in Mark are one.

[^1]: Greek «ἀλλ᾽ ἕτερος καὶ ἕτερος» — literally "but another and another," an idiom asserting that they are two distinct men, not a single individual reported by both evangelists.

# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 10 · Mark 2:12

> **Your sins are forgiven you. (Mark 2:9)**

For assuredly, if he had been able, he would have healed the body, and would not have taken refuge in the unseen. The Savior, therefore, showing them that he is able to do both, says: "Which is easier, to heal a body or a soul?" Assuredly to heal a body; but to you it seems the opposite. I, then, through healing the body — which in truth is easy, but seems hard to you — will confirm also the healing of the soul, which is hard, but, because of its being unseen and unable to be tested, seems easy. Then, accordingly, he says to the paralytic: "Arise and take up your pallet," for a greater confirmation of the miracle, that it is not according to mere appearance; and at the same time that he might show that he not only healed, but also implanted power. Thus, therefore, he does also in the case of the soul's sicknesses: he not only delivers us from sins, but also puts in power for the working of the commandments.

I too, therefore, being paralyzed, am able to be healed. For even now Christ is in Capernaum, the house of consolation, that is, in the Church; for the Church is the house of the Paraclete;[^1] and I am paralyzed, having the powers of the soul inactive and immovable toward the good; but if I am taken up by the four evangelists, and brought to the Lord, then I shall hear it. For I become a child of God through the working of the commandments, and my sins shall be forgiven me. But how shall I be brought before Jesus? If they dig through the roof. And what is the roof? The mind, which lies above all the things within us. But this has much soil and tiles — I mean the earthly matters; yet if all these are cleared away, and the power of the mind in us is dug open and unburdened, then I am let down, that is, humbled; for one must not be lifted up because the roof of the mind has been unburdened, but after I am unburdened, I ought to be let down, that is, humbled. Then, accordingly, I shall be healed, and shall take up the pallet — that is, the body — moving it toward the working of the commandments. For one must not only arise from sin, and understand that we are sinning, but also take up the pallet — that is, the body — for the working of the good. Then, accordingly, we shall also be able to attain to contemplation, so that all the reasonings within us say, "Never did we see thus" — that is, we never understood thus as we do now, when, being paralyzed, we were[?].[^2] For he who has been cleansed of his sins, that one truly sees.

[^1]: Theophylact reads Capernaum (Καπερναούμ) as "house of consolation" and plays on παράκλησις (consolation) and Παράκλητος (the Paraclete, the Comforter); the pun cannot be reproduced in English.
[^2]: The Greek verb is corrupt in the OCR («ἄθημεν» / «ᾔθημεν»); the sense requires a finite verb such as "we were" or "we perceived." The clause is rendered tentatively.

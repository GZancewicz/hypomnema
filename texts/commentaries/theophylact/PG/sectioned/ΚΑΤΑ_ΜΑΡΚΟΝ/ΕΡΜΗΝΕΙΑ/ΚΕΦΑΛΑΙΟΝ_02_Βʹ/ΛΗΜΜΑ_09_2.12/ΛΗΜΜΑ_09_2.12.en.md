# Mark 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 9 · Mark 2:12

> **He arose immediately, and having taken up the pallet, went out before them all, so that all were amazed and glorified God, saying, "Never did we see thus." (Mark 2:12)**

The Pharisees were slandering the Lord as a blasphemer, because he was forgiving sins; for to forgive sins belongs to God alone. But the Lord gives yet another proof of his being God: the knowing of their hearts; for God alone knows the inward things of each, as also the prophet says: "You alone understand hearts."[^1] But those men, although the things in their hearts had been uncovered, nevertheless remain insensible, not conceding that he who knows their hearts is able also to heal sins. The Lord, however, confirms and makes sure, through healing the body, that he had also healed his soul, having confirmed the unseen through the seen, and the harder through the easier — even if to those men it did not seem so. For the Pharisees thought the healing of the body harder, as being visible; but to say that the soul had been healed was easy, because the healing was unseen. So that perhaps they were reasoning even such things as these: "Behold the deceiver — he has left off healing the body, the manifest thing, and heals the unseen soul, saying:"

[^1]: «Σὺ ἐπίστασαι καρδίας μονώτατος» — a loose echo of 3 Kingdoms (1 Kgs) 8:39 LXX, «σὺ μονώτατος οἶδας τὴν καρδίαν πάντων υἱῶν ἀνθρώπων». The superlative μονώτατος (lit. "most utterly alone," from μόνος) is here rendered "alone." Theophylact calls the speaker "the prophet," though the words are Solomon's prayer at the dedication of the temple.

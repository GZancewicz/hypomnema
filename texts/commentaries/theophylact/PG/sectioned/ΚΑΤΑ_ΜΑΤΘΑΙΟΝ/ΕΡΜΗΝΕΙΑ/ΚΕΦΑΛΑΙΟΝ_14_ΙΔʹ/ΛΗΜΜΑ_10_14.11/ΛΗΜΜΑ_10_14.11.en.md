# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 14:11–12

> **…his head was brought upon a platter, and was given to the girl, and she brought *it* to her mother. And his disciples, having come forward, took up the body and buried it." (Matt. 14:11–12)**[^1]

He was grieved on account of the virtue *of the man*. For even an enemy admires virtue; nevertheless, on account of the oaths, he gives an inhuman gift. And let us learn from this, that there are also *occasions* when it is better to break one's oath than, on account of oaths, to do something impious.[^2] Now the body of the Baptist was buried in Sebaste of Caesarea,[^3] but his precious head was at first deposited in Emesa. **And having come, they reported *it* to Jesus.** What did they report to Jesus? Not that John had died — for the narrative concerning John was inserted in the middle[^4] — but that Herod says concerning him[^5] that he is John.

[^1]: Greek «ἠνέχθη ἡ κεφαλὴ αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ πίνακι, καὶ ἐδόθη τῷ κορασίῳ, καὶ ἤνεγκε τῇ μητρὶ αὐτῆς. Καὶ προσελθόντες οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ, ἦραν τὸ σῶμα καὶ ἔθαψαν αὐτό» (OCR «θαψαν αλτό» = «ἔθαψαν αὐτό»). "The girl" renders *τῷ κορασίῳ* (diminutive, "the little girl / maiden"), Matthew's own word for the dancer.
[^2]: Greek «Μανθάνωμεν δὲ ἐντεῦθεν, ὅτι καὶ ὅτε κρεῖττον ἐπιορκεῖν, ἢ διὰ τοὺς ὅρκους ἀσεβές τι ποιεῖν». The syntax is compressed — literally "that also *there are cases* when *it is* better to forswear oneself (*ἐπιορκεῖν*) than…"; the sense is that a rash or wicked oath is better broken than kept. *ἐπιορκεῖν* means "to swear falsely / violate an oath."
[^3]: Greek «ἐν τῇ Σεβαστῇ Καισαρείᾳ» — the reference is to Sebaste, the Herodian name for Samaria, the traditional burial place of John the Baptist; "Caesarea" is added loosely (both were Herodian foundations). Rendered as it stands rather than emended.
[^4]: Greek «τὸ γὰρ κατὰ τὸν Ἰωάννην διήγημα παρενετέθη μέσον» — i.e. the whole account of John's death is a parenthetical flashback inserted into the narrative, so the disciples' report to Jesus is not the fresh news of the death but Herod's opinion (14:1–2) that Jesus is John risen.
[^5]: Greek «ὅτι ὁ Ἡρώδης λέγει περὶ αὐτοῦ ὡς Ἰωάννης ἐστί» — "that Herod says concerning him [Jesus] that he is John [returned]," referring back to Herod's words at Matt. 14:2.

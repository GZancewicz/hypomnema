# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 34 · Matt. 14:36

> **they besought him, that they might only touch the hem of his garment; and as many as touched *it* were saved.**[^1]

Jesus having come to sojourn in Gennesaret after a long time,[^2] the people, having recognized *him* not only from *his* appearance but also from the signs, display fervent faith; so as to desire to touch even the hem, and indeed, having done this, they were healed.[^3] So therefore you also, touch the extremity of the garment of Christ, that is,[^4] the end of his in-the-flesh sojourn.[^5] For if you believe that he was taken up,[^6] you shall be saved; for the flesh is *his* garment, and the hem of this is the end of *his* life on earth.

[^1]: Greek «παρεκάλουν αὐτὸν, ἵνα μόνον ἅψωνται τοῦ κρασπέδου τοῦ ἱματίου αὐτοῦ· καὶ ὅσοι ἥψαντο, διεσώθησαν.» (Matt. 14:36), completing the quotation begun in lemmata 32–33. The OCR *ἅύωνται* is securely *ἅψωνται* ("might touch"). *κράσπεδον* is the "hem / border / tassel" of the garment (the fringe or *tzitzit* of Num. 15:38–39); rendered "hem." *διεσώθησαν* is "were saved / were brought safely through," here with the concrete sense "were healed / made whole."
[^2]: Greek *Διὰ πολλοῦ χρόνου ἐπιδημήσαντα τῇ Γεννησαρὶτ τὸν Ἰησοῦν* — "Jesus having sojourned in Gennesaret after a long while." The verb *ἐπιδημέω* ("to be in residence, to be present as a visitor, to sojourn") is the same root Theophylact uses just below for the Incarnation (*ἐνσάρκου ... ἐπιδημίας*), which prepares the allegory. The OCR spells the place *Γεννησαρὶτ* (and, garbled, *Ιεννησαρὶτ*).
[^3]: The OCR *ἐθεοαπεύθησαν* is securely *ἐθεραπεύθησαν* ("they were healed / cured").
[^4]: Greek *ἤτοι*, "that is / or rather," introducing Theophylact's allegorical gloss.
[^5]: Greek *τοῦ ἄκρου τοῦ ἱματίου ... ἤτοι τοῦ τέλους τῆς ἐνσάρκου αὐτοῦ ἐπιδημίας*: "the extremity of the garment ... that is, the end of his in-the-flesh sojourn." *τὸ ἄκρον* ("the extremity, tip, outermost edge") answers to *κράσπεδον* above; the "garment" is glossed as the flesh (Incarnation), and its outermost edge as the end (*τέλος*) of the incarnate life.
[^6]: Greek *ἀνελήφθη*, "he was taken up" — the technical term for the Ascension (cf. Acts 1:2, 11; Mark 16:19). The allegory thus resolves: to touch the "hem" is to believe in the completion of the incarnate life, i.e. the Ascension.

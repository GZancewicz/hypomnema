# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 14:33

> **boat, having come, worshipped him, saying: Truly you are God's Son. (Matt. 14:33)**

Christ, showing that not the wind[^1] *was* the cause of the sinking, but the faintheartedness,[^2] rebukes not the wind, but the fainthearted Peter. Wherefore also, having lifted him up,[^3] he set him upon the water, having allowed the wind to blow. But Peter did not doubt with respect to the whole, but with respect to something — that is, partially; for on the one hand, in that in which he was afraid, in respect of this he disbelieved; but on the other hand, in that in which he cried out, "Lord, save me," in respect of this he healed the unbelief. Wherefore he hears *himself called*[^4] "of little faith," but not "unbelieving." Those, therefore,[^5] in the boat, they too were released from the fear. For the wind ceased; and indeed, having recognized Jesus from these things, they confess his Godhead.[^6] For to walk upon *the* sea *is* not of a man, but of God, as also David says:[^7]

[^1]: Greek «Δεικνύων ὁ Χριστός, ὅτι οὐχ ὁ ἄνεμος αἴτιος τοῦ καταποντισθῆναι…» (OCR *Αεικνύων* = *Δεικνύων*, "showing"). The participle is kept ("Christ, showing…").
[^2]: Greek *μικροψυχία* — "littleness of soul," faintheartedness, pusillanimity; the corresponding participle *μικροψυχήσαντι* below is rendered "the fainthearted *Peter*." The word is the opposite of *μεγαλοψυχία* (greatness of soul); "faintheartedness" is chosen to keep the noun/participle pair visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἀναβαστάσας* (from *ἀναβαστάζω*), "having lifted / borne up."
[^4]: Greek *ὀλιγόπιστος ἀκούει* — literally "he hears 'of little faith,'" a common Greek idiom in which *ἀκούειν* means "to be called / to have said of oneself" (as one "hears" the name applied to him). Rendered "he hears *himself called*," the supplied words marking the idiom; the point is that Christ names Peter *ὀλιγόπιστος* ("of little faith"), not *ἄπιστος* ("unbelieving"). Greek: «ὀλιγόπιστος ἀκούει, ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἄπιστος» (OCR *Δὰ* = *Διό*, "wherefore").
[^5]: Greek OCR *Οἱοῦν*, resolved as *οἱ οὖν* ("those, therefore") — the connective *οὖν* rendered "therefore"; conceivably *οἱ γοῦν* ("those, at any rate"). Either way the referent is the disciples in the boat.
[^6]: Greek *ὁμολογοῦσι τὴν αὐτοῦ θεότητα*, "they confess his Godhead / divinity" (OCR *Ῥν Ἱησοῦν* = *τὸν Ἰησοῦν*, "Jesus").
[^7]: The sentence breaks off before the citation; the words of David (Ps. 76:20 LXX) are quoted at the head of the next lemma.

# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 14:33

> **"I will by no means deny you;"**[^1]

so also here: "Command me to come upon the waters";[^2] and just as then he was permitted to deny, so also here he is permitted to be plunged *into the sea*;[^3] here the Lord gave him *his* hand, and did not allow *him* to be plunged *under*. And there too, through repentance, he drew him up from the depth of denial.

[^1]: This lemma opens by completing a sentence begun at the end of the previous lemma (30), which broke off at "for just as there he says, being emboldened:" (*ὥσπερ γὰρ ἐκεῖ λέγει θρασυνόμενος·*). The quoted words «Οὐ μή σε ἀπαρνήσομαι» ("I will by no means deny you") are Peter's boast during the Passion narrative — cf. Matt. 26:35 (there in the plural *οὐ μή σε ἀπαρνήσομαι*) and Mark 14:31. Theophylact adduces them as a cross-reference, not as a lemma from Matthew 14; the OCR *ἀπαρνήπομαι* is securely *ἀπαρνήσομαι*.
[^2]: Greek «Κέλευσόν με ἐλθεῖν ἐπὶ τὰ ὕδατα» — Peter's own request in this same episode, Matt. 14:28. The OCR *θδατα* is securely *ὕδατα* ("waters"). Theophylact sets the two utterances in parallel: the boldness of "command me to come" answers to the boldness of "I will not deny you."
[^3]: The verb is *ποντίζω* ("to plunge / sink in the sea"), here twice: *συγχωρεῖται ποντίζεσθαι* ("he is permitted to be plunged into the sea") and *οὐκ ἀφῆκε ποντισθῆναι* ("did not allow *him* to be plunged *under*"). The OCR gives *σνγχορεῖται* (= *συγχωρεῖται*), *πονσισθῆναι* (= *ποντισθῆναι*); the closing clause *κης ματανοίας ... εἴλνυσεν* is securely *τῆς μετανοίας ... εἵλκυσεν* ("through repentance ... he drew *him* up"). The point is a strict parallel: as denial was permitted and then remedied by repentance, so the sinking was permitted and then remedied by the Lord's outstretched hand.

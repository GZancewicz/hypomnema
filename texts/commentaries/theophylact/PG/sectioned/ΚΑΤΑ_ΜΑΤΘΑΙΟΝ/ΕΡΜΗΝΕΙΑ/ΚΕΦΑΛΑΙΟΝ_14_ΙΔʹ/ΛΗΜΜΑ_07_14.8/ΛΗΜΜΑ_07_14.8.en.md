# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 14:8

> **…having been prompted by her mother: "Give me," she says, "here upon a platter the head of John the Baptist." (Matt. 14:8)**[^1]

See *the* wantonness! The queen[^2] dances, and by however much she dances well, by so much *the more* wickedly.[^3] For *it is* a shame for a queen to do skillfully something *that is* unseemly. But consider also another folly of Herod: he swore to give whatever she might ask; but if she had asked *for* your *own* head, you would have given it.[^4] "Give me," she says, "here the head of John." For what reason did she add the "here"? She was afraid, lest Herod, having come to his senses, might afterward repent. For this reason she presses *him*, saying, "Give me here."

[^1]: This lemma continues the sentence begun in the previous lemma, which ends "But she," (Ἡ δὲ,). The Gospel verse (Matt. 14:8) reads in Theophylact «ἡ δὲ προβιβασθεῖσα ὑπὸ τῆς μητρὸς αὐτῆς· Δός μοι, φησίν, ὧδε ἐπὶ πίνακι τὴν κεφαλὴν Ἰωάννου τοῦ βαπτιστοῦ». The participle *προβιβασθεῖσα* ("having been pushed forward / prompted / instigated") is kept as a participle.
[^2]: Greek *ἡ βασίλισσα* ("the queen"). Strictly the dancer is the daughter of Herodias (Matt. 14:6, τὸ κοράσιον, "the girl"), not a reigning queen; Theophylact styles her a royal woman to sharpen the point that unseemly conduct is doubly disgraceful in the highborn. Rendered literally "queen."
[^3]: Greek «καὶ ὅσῳ καλῶς ὀρχεῖται, τοσούτῳ κακῶς» — a compressed antithesis playing *καλῶς* ("well, beautifully") against *κακῶς* ("badly, wickedly"): the more accomplished her dancing, the more shameful it is. The verb of the second clause is elided in the Greek; supplied here as "the more wickedly *she acts*."
[^4]: Greek «εἰ δὲ τὴν κεφαλήν σου ᾐτήσατο, δέδωκας ἂν αὐτήν» — an abrupt second-person apostrophe to Herod ("if she had asked for *your* head, you would have given it"), which could equally be read as a rhetorical question ("…would you have given it?"). The point is the same: the oath, taken thus recklessly, bound him to grant even an absurd or self-destructive request.

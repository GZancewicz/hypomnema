# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 14:27

> **…But immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying: Take courage,[^1] I am;[^2] do not be afraid.**

He did not immediately come upon them to dissolve the storm, training *us* not to ask quickly for a dissolving of misfortunes, but to bear *them* nobly — but *he came* around the fourth watch. For the night is divided into four *parts* among the soldiers who keep guard by succession, each watch having three hours apiece. The Lord, then, after the ninth hour of the night, walking upon the water, as God, came upon *them*. But they, on account of the unaccustomed and strange *sight*, thought *it* to be a phantom; for they did not recognize him from *his* appearance — at once, on the one hand, because of the night, and at once, on the other hand, because of the fear.[^3] But he first emboldens[^4] them, saying: I am *he*, the one who is able *to do* all things; take courage.

[^1]: Greek *θαρσεῖτε* — "Take courage / Be of good cheer."
[^2]: Greek *ἐγώ εἰμι* — "I am." Also renderable "It is I." The phrase carries the resonance of the divine self-naming ("I AM," cf. Exod. 3:14); since Theophylact repeats it below and glosses it "the one who is able *to do* all things," the emphatic "I am" is kept rather than the colloquial "It is I."
[^3]: Greek *ἅμα μὲν … ἅμα δὲ* — literally "at once (on the one hand) … at once (on the other hand)," i.e. "partly because of the night, partly because of the fear." The correlative *μὲν … δὲ* is rendered "on the one hand … on the other hand"; *ἅμα* ("at the same time, at once") is retained to show that both causes operate together.
[^4]: Greek *παραθαῤῥύνει* (present) — "encourages, emboldens, heartens."

# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 14:21

> **…*those* eating were men about five thousand, apart from women and children.[^1] (Matt. 14:21)**

He gives the loaves to the disciples, so that they might always hold the miracle in memory and it might not flow away from their mind — even though those men quickly forgot; and so that you might not suppose that he worked the miracle in appearance,[^2] for this reason *the fragments* are left over in abundance. And *there were* twelve baskets, so that Judas too might carry *one*, and might not be flung headlong into betrayal, *if* he pondered the miracle.[^3] And he multiplies both the loaves and the fishes, so that he might show that he himself is the Fashioner of land and sea, and that the things which we eat each *day*, we eat by his giving,[^4] and by him they are multiplied; and *he works* the miracle in a desert, so that no one might suppose that he bought the loaves from a neighboring city and distributed them to the multitude — for it was desert. And thus *it stands*, on the one hand, according to the history.[^5]

But according to the anagogical *sense*,[^5] learn that, since Herod — the fleshly and skin-clad mind of the Jews, for this is what "Herod" is interpreted *to mean*[^6] — beheaded John, the chief-point[^7] of the prophets — that is, he did not believe those who prophesied concerning Christ — Jesus then withdraws into a desert place, into the nations, which are desert of God, and heals those who are sick according to the soul, then also feeds them. For unless he shall forgive us *our* sins and heal *our* illnesses through baptism, he will not feed us by the imparting of the undefiled mysteries. For no unbaptized person partakes.

And the five thousand *are* those who are badly disposed in the five senses, who also are healed through the five loaves. For since the five senses were sick, as many as *were* the wounds, so many also *were* the plasters.[^8] And the two fishes *are* the words of the fishermen: one fish *is* the Gospel, the other fish the Apostle.[^9] But some understood the five loaves *to be* the Pentateuch of Moses — Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy. And the twelve baskets were those taken up and carried by the apostles. For whatever things we, the crowds, are not able to eat — that is, to understand — these the apostles carried and had room for.[^10] And "apart from women and children," for the Christian is required to have nothing childish or womanish and unmanly. And…[^11]

[^1]: The lemma begins mid-sentence, continuing «Οἱ δὲ» ("But those") from the end of the previous lemma; hence the supplied "*those*" (italic) opens the quotation. «ὡσεὶ» = "about, approximately." Rendered from Theophylact's Greek as it stands.
[^2]: Greek «κατὰ φαντασίαν» — "in appearance / by way of a phantasm," i.e. as a mere illusion rather than a real multiplication. The abundant leftovers prove the miracle was substantial.
[^3]: Greek «καὶ μὴ εἰς προδοσίαν ἐκτραχηλισθῇ, ἐνθυμούμενος τὸ θαῦμα». The verb «ἐκτραχηλίζομαι» is literally "to be thrown headlong (as over a cliff), to break one's neck." The participle «ἐνθυμούμενος» ("pondering / calling to mind") is here taken conditionally ("if he pondered"): carrying his own basket might have kept Judas from plunging into betrayal, had he reflected on the miracle.
[^4]: Greek «αὐτοῦ δι[δόντος] ἐσθίομεν» — the source is OCR-corrupt («διὲάνό-»), securely reconstructed as a genitive absolute «αὐτοῦ διδόντος» ("he giving," i.e. "by his giving"). Flagged as a reconstruction. «καθ᾽ ἑκάστην» = "each *day*," with «ἡμέραν» ("day") understood; the supplied "*day*" is italicized.
[^5]: Theophylact marks his standard twofold reading: «καθ᾽ ἱστορίαν» ("according to the history," the literal/narrative sense) versus «κατὰ ἀναγωγήν» ("according to the anagogical *sense*," the elevated/spiritual sense). The pair «μὲν … δὲ» links the two.
[^6]: Greek «τοῦτο γὰρ Ἡρώδης ἑρμηνεύεται» — "for this is what 'Herod' is interpreted *to mean*." The etymology ("Herod" = a fleshly, skin-clad mind) is Theophylact's own allegorical gloss, not a philologically secure derivation; translated as it stands. «δερμάτινος» ("of skin, leathern") evokes the "garments of skin" of Gen. 3:21 — the fallen, carnal condition.
[^7]: Greek «τὸ τῶν προφητῶν κεφάλαιον». «κεφάλαιον» means "chief point, sum, summation," but audibly echoes «κεφαλή» ("head"): John, the "head/summary" of the prophets, was literally beheaded. The wordplay is lost in English; "chief-point" is chosen to keep the head-imagery faintly audible. An alternative rendering is "the crown/summation of the prophets."
[^8]: Greek «ὅσαι αἱ πληγαί, τοσαῦται καὶ αἱ ἔμπλαστροι» — "as many as *were* the wounds, so many also *were* the plasters (poultices)." «ἔμπλαστρος» = a medicinal plaster/salve; the five loaves are the remedies matching the five wounded senses.
[^9]: Greek «ὁ Ἀπόστολος» — not the person but "the Apostle," i.e. the liturgical book/reading of the apostolic writings (Acts and the Epistles), paired with «τὸ Εὐαγγέλιον» ("the Gospel"). The two fishes = the two great classes of New Testament proclamation.
[^10]: Greek «ἐβάστασαν καὶ ἐχώρησαν» — "carried and had room for / contained." «χωρέω» here = "to make room for, to hold, to comprehend"; what the crowds cannot digest (understand), the apostles both bear and can contain.
[^11]: The comment breaks off at «Καὶ» ("And"), running on into the next lemma («εὐθέως ἠνάγκασεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς…», Matt. 14:22).

# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 14:13–15

> **"And Jesus, having heard *it*, withdrew from there in a boat into a desert place privately. (Matt. 14:13)**[^1]

Jesus withdraws on account of Herod's murderousness, teaching also us not to throw ourselves openly into dangers. And in order that he might not seem to have been incarnate in appearance *only*,[^2] for this reason he withdraws. For if Herod had seized him, he would have attempted to kill *him*; but, with *Herod* attempting to kill *him*, if Jesus had snatched himself out from the midst of the dangers — because the time of *his* death had not yet arrived — he would have seemed to be a phantom;[^3] therefore, then, he withdraws. And *he withdrew* into a desert place privately, in order that he might work the miracle concerning the loaves.

> **And the crowds, having heard *it*, followed him on foot from the cities. And Jesus, having gone out, saw a great crowd, and was moved with compassion over them, and healed their sick. (Matt. 14:13b–14)**

The crowds display faith, seeing that they run to Jesus even *as he is* withdrawing. Wherefore they receive the healings as a reward of *their* faith; but also the following *him* on foot, and the *going* without food — all these *are marks* of faith.

> **And evening having come, his disciples came to him, saying: "The place is desert, and the hour has already passed; dismiss the crowds, in order that, having gone away into the villages, they may buy food for themselves." But *he*— (Matt. 14:15)**[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Καὶ ἀκούσας ὁ Ἰησοῦς, ἀνεχώρησεν ἐκεῖθεν ἐν πλοίῳ εἰς ἔρημον τόπον κατ᾿ ἰδίαν». *κατ᾿ ἰδίαν* ("privately, by himself, apart") is rendered "privately" throughout.
[^2]: Greek «ἵνα μὴ δόξῃ κατὰ φαντασίαν σαρκωθῆναι» — "lest he seem to have been incarnate *only* in appearance / imagination" (*κατὰ φαντασίαν*). The point is anti-docetic: by fleeing real danger like a real man, Christ shows his flesh was genuine, not a mere semblance. On *φαντασία* / *φάντασμα* see the next note.
[^3]: Greek «ἔδοξεν ἂν φάντασμα εἶναι» — "he would have seemed to be a phantom / apparition" (*φάντασμα*). Theophylact's reasoning: had Christ miraculously escaped death before his appointed hour, onlookers would have concluded his body was unreal; so he avoids the danger by ordinary withdrawal instead. The chain «ἐπεχείρησεν ἂν ἀνελεῖν· ἐπιχειροῦντος δὲ ἀνελεῖν» keeps the repeated *ἀνελεῖν* ("to do away with, kill").
[^4]: The lemma breaks off at «Ὁ δὲ» ("But *he*—"); the sentence is completed in the next lemma, which supplies «Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν αὐτοῖς» ("Jesus said to them"). Greek «ἡ ὥρα ἤδη παρῆλθεν» = "the hour has already passed" (i.e. it is late).

# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 14:1–2

> **In that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report of Jesus, and said to his servants: This one is John the Baptist; he has been raised from the dead, and for this reason the powers[^1] are working in him. (Matt. 14:1–2)**

This Herod was son of the one who slew the infants in Bethlehem. But consider from this the vanity[^2] of the tyrannical life. For behold, after how long a time Herod hears the things concerning Jesus! For those *who are* in positions of power learn these things slowly, on account of not caring for those who shine in virtue. And this one appears to fear the Baptist. For this reason he does not even dare to speak *it* out to any others, but to his "servants,"[^3] that is, to his slaves. But since, when John was living, he did no sign, Herod supposed that from the resurrection he had received also this gift from God — the doing of wonders.

[^1]: Greek «αἱ δυνάμεις ἐνεργοῦσιν ἐν αὐτῷ». *δύναμις* spans "power" and "mighty work / miracle"; here the plural means the miraculous powers *at work* in Jesus, which Herod imagines to be the risen John's newly received gift. Rendered "powers" to keep it literal.
[^2]: Greek *τύφος* — "conceit, delusion, empty pomp, self-importance." The word carries a note of insubstantial vapor (its root sense is "smoke"), i.e. the puffed-up unreality of the tyrant's life. Rendered "vanity."
[^3]: Greek «τοῖς παισὶ, τουτέστι τοῖς δούλοις». *παῖς* means both "child / boy" and "servant / attendant"; the Gospel's *παισίν* here denotes household servants, which Theophylact glosses with the plainer *δούλοις* ("slaves"). Rendered "servants" in the lemma and glossed "slaves" in the comment, preserving his equation.

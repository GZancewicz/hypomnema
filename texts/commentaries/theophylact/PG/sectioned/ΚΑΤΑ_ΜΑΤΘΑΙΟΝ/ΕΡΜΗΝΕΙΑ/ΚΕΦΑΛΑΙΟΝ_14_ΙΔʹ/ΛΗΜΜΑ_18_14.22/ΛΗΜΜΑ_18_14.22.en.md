# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 14:22

> **…immediately Jesus compelled his disciples to embark into the boat and to go on ahead of him[^1] to the other side, until he should dismiss the crowds. (Matt. 14:22)**

Hinting at the inseparability of the disciples, he said the *word* "compelled," for they wished always to be with him. And he dismisses the crowds, not wishing to drag them along after *him*, so that he might not seem fond of display.[^2]

[^1]: Greek «προάγειν αὐτὸ⟨ν⟩ εἰς τὸ πέραν». The source reads «αὐτὸ» ("it," which would refer to the boat); the received text and sense give «αὐτόν» ("him"), i.e. the disciples go on ahead of Jesus. Translated "on ahead of him"; the final «ν» is taken as dropped in the OCR. «ἠνάγκασεν» = "compelled, forced," the very word Theophylact fastens on below.
[^2]: Greek «ἵνα μὴ δόξῃ φιλόκομπος» — "so that he might not seem φιλόκομπος," i.e. boastful, fond of pomp or ostentatious display. Rendered "fond of display."

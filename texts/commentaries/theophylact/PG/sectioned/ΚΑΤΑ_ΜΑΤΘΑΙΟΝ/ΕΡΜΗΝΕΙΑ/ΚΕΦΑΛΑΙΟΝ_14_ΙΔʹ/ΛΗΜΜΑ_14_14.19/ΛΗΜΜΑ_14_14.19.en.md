# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 14:19

> **…having commanded the crowds to recline upon the grass, having taken the five loaves and the two fishes, having looked up into heaven, he blessed.[^1] (Matt. 14:19)**

"Bring me the loaves here"; for even if it is evening,[^2] yet I, the Maker of the hours,[^3] am present; if the place is desert, yet I am the one who gives food to all flesh. But we learn from this that even if we have few things, we must spend these on hospitality. For indeed the apostles, having few things, gave to the crowds. But just as these few things were multiplied, so also your few things will be multiplied. And he reclines the crowds upon the grass, teaching frugality, so that you too may not rest upon costly couches and coverlets. And he looks up into heaven and blesses the loaves — perhaps, on the one hand, also so that he might confirm that he is not a rival god,[^4] but came from the Father and from heaven; and at the same time, on the other hand, also so that he might train us, when we take hold of a table, to give thanks and thus to eat.

[^1]: The quotation is the middle portion of Matt. 14:19, running on from the previous lemma (which ended at «Καὶ»). Rendered from Theophylact's Greek as it stands. The participles «κελεύσας», «λαβὼν», «ἀναβλέψας» are kept as English participles ("having commanded / having taken / having looked up").
[^2]: Greek here is OCR-garbled as «πἍῷία»; from the parallel structure (evening answered by the "Maker of the hours," the desert place answered by the "giver of food to all flesh") and from the disciples' twofold objection at Matt. 14:15 — that "the place is desert" and "the hour is now past" (ἡ ὥρα ἤδη παρῆλθε) — it is securely **«ὀψία»** ("evening, lateness"). Flagged as a reconstruction.
[^3]: Greek «ὁ τῶν ὡρῶν Ποιητής», "the Maker of the hours." The phrase answers the first of the disciples' objections at 14:15 (that the hour is now late): though it is evening, the very Maker of the hours is present.
[^4]: Greek «οὐκ ἀντίθεός ἐστιν». «ἀντίθεος» = "a god set against God, a rival/opposing god." The point is that Christ, by looking up to heaven, shows he is not a second, competing deity but comes from the Father.

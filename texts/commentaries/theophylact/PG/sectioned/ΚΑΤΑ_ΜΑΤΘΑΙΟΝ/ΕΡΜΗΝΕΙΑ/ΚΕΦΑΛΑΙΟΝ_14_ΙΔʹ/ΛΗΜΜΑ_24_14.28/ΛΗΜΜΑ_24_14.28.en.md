# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 14:28

> **And Peter, answering him, said: Lord, if it is you,[^1] command me to come to you upon the waters.**

Peter, being most fervent[^2] *in his* love toward Christ, desires to come near to him quickly, and before the others. And he believes that Jesus not only himself walks upon the waters, but will also give this to him. But he did not say, "Command me to walk,"[^3] but, "To come to you." For the one, on the one hand, was *a matter* of display;[^4] but the other, on the other hand, of love toward Christ.

[^1]: Greek *εἰ σὺ εἶ* — "if you are" / "if it is you." Peter's words pick up the Lord's *ἐγώ εἰμι* ("I am") just spoken; rendered "if it is you" for natural English, though the bare "if you are" is equally defensible.
[^2]: Greek *θερμότατος ὢν … τὴν πρὸς Χριστὸν ἀγάπην* — superlative "being most warm/fervent/ardent," with "*the* love toward Christ" as an accusative of respect ("with respect to *his* love toward Christ").
[^3]: Greek *βαδίσαι* — "to walk, to step, to go on foot"; distinguished from *ἐλθεῖν πρὸς σὲ* ("to come to you").
[^4]: Greek *ἐπιδείξεως* — "of display, showing-off, ostentation." The contrast is between a mere feat of self-display (walking on water) and love (coming *to Christ himself*).

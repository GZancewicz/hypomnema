# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 14:16

> **…Jesus said to them: "They have no need to go away; you *yourselves* give them *something* to eat." (Matt. 14:16)**[^1]

The disciples were lovers of humankind,[^2] for they care for the crowd, and for this reason they do not wish them to be fasting. What, therefore, *does* the Saviour *say*? "Give them *something* to eat, you *yourselves*," he says. But he says this, not as being ignorant of how great a poverty the apostles had — perish the thought![^3] — but in order that, when those *men* had said, "We do not have," he might seem to come to the working of the miracle out of necessity, and not out of vainglory,[^4]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in the previous lemma at «Ὁ δὲ» ("But he—"); the full clause is «Ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Οὐ χρείαν ἔχουσιν ἀπελθεῖν· δότε αὐτοῖς ὑμεῖς φαγεῖν». The emphatic *ὑμεῖς* ("you *yourselves*") is retained; the object of "give…to eat" is unexpressed in Greek and supplied as "*something*."
[^2]: Greek «Φιλάνθρωποι ἦσαν οἱ μαθηταί» (OCR «Φιλάνθοωπος εἰσαν»). *φιλάνθρωπος* is the standard term for benevolent love of humankind; rendered "lovers of humankind" to keep the word visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἄπαγε* — an idiomatic exclamation of dismissal, "away *with the thought*! / by no means! / God forbid!" Here disavowing the notion that Christ was actually unaware of the apostles' poverty.
[^4]: The comment breaks off at «οὐ κατὰ φιλοδοξίαν» ("and not out of vainglory / love of display"), completed in the following lemma. *φιλοδοξία* ("vainglory, ambition for reputation") is the opposite motive from which Christ is shown to act: he elicits the disciples' admission of want so that the miracle appears as a response to real need, not a bid for glory.

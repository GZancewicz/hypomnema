# Matthew 14 (ΙΔʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 14:33 (citing Ps. 76:20 LXX)

> **In the sea *are* your ways, and your paths in many waters.[^1]**

But one must lead the account up *to a higher sense*:[^2] the vessel[^3] *is* the earth; the waves, *this present* life[^4] troubled by the evil spirits; night, ignorance.[^5] And in the fourth watch — namely, near the end of the ages — Christ stood by *them*.[^6] For the first watch, on the one hand, *is* the covenant with Abraham; the second, the law of Moses; the third, the prophets; the fourth, the coming[^7] of the Lord. For he himself saved those who were being tossed by the waves,[^8] having entered *the ship* and having become with us, so that we, having recognized him, might worship him as God. But see this also, how the things that befell him in the sea foreshadowed the denial of Peter, then *his* turning-back and repentance; for just as there he says, being emboldened:[^9]

[^1]: The lemma quotes Ps. 76:20 (LXX; = Ps. 77:19 Heb./Eng.): «Ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ αἱ ὁδοί σου, καὶ αἱ τρίβοι σου ἐν ὕδασι πολλοῖς» (OCR *σουκαὶ αὶ* = *σου, καὶ αἱ*). The Septuagint reads the singular *ἡ ὁδός σου* ("your way"); Theophylact's text here has the plural *αἱ ὁδοί σου* ("your ways"), which is followed. Cited by him as David's testimony that walking on the sea belongs to God alone.
[^2]: Greek *Ἀνακτέον δὲ τὸν λόγον* (OCR *Ἀνακτίον*) — the verbal adjective of *ἀνάγω*, "one must lead/refer *the account* up," i.e., interpret it in the higher, anagogical sense (*ἀναγωγή*). Rendered "lead the account up *to a higher sense*," the supplied words making the allegorical turn explicit.
[^3]: Greek *σκάφος*, the hull or skiff — the boat's hollow body; here glossed as the earth.
[^4]: Greek *ὁ βίος*, "life / this present life, mode of living" (as distinct from *ζωή*), rendered "*this present* life."
[^5]: Greek *ἀγνωσία*, "want of knowledge, ignorance."
[^6]: Greek *ἐπέστη ὁ Χριστός*, "Christ stood by / came upon *them*" (from *ἐφίστημι*, of a sudden arrival). Object supplied.
[^7]: Greek *παρουσία*, "coming, arrival, presence" — the Lord's advent; rendered "coming."
[^8]: Greek *τοὺς κλυδωνιζομένους*, "those being tossed / tempest-tossed by the surge" (from *κλυδωνίζομαι*, of billowing waves).
[^9]: The sentence breaks off; it is completed in the next lemma, which supplies Peter's boast «I will by no means deny you» (cf. Matt. 26:35) and draws the parallel with «Command me to come upon the waters». Greek *θρασυνόμενος*, "being bold / emboldened / speaking rashly."

# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 15:32

> **But Jesus, having summoned his disciples, said: I have compassion upon the crowd, because already three days they remain with me, and they do not have what they may eat, and to send them away fasting I do not wish, lest they faint on the way.**[^1]

The crowds, on the one hand, did not dare to ask for loaves, inasmuch as they had come for healing. But he himself, being loving-toward-humankind,[^2] takes thought; and in order that no one might be able to say that "They have other provisions," he says, "Even if they had *them*, they would have been used up; for it is already three days." And he shows that they had also come from afar, in saying, "Lest they faint on the way." And these things he says to the disciples, wishing to rouse them toward saying to him that "You are able to feed them, as also the five thousand"; but those *men* are still senseless.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς προσκαλεσάμενος τοὺς μαθητὰς αὐτοῦ, εἶπε· Σπλαγχνίζομαι ἐπὶ τὸν ὄχλον, ὅτι ἤδη ἡμέρας τρεῖς προσμένουσί μοι, καὶ οὐκ ἔχουσι τί φάγωσι, καὶ ἀπολῦσαι αὐτοὺς νήστεις οὐ θέλω, μήποτε ἐκλυθῶσιν ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ» (Matt. 15:32). *Σπλαγχνίζομαι*, "I have compassion" (lit. "I am moved in the inward parts"), rendered by the conventional "I have compassion." *ἐκλυθῶσιν*, "they may faint / give out / be exhausted."
[^2]: Greek *φιλάνθρωπος*, "loving toward humankind / benevolent"; rendered with the calque "loving-toward-humankind" to keep the theological term visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἀνοηταίνουσιν*, "they are being senseless / act foolishly / lack understanding" (cf. *ἀνόητος*). The disciples' failure to infer from the earlier feeding sets up the wonder of their later wisdom (developed in Lemma 30).

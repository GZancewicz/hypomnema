# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 38 · Matt. 15:39

> **And having dismissed the crowds, he embarked into the boat, and came into the borders of Magdala.**[^1] (Matt. 15:39)

Jesus withdraws, since no other sign made more *people* follow him as *did* that of the loaves. So that they were even about to make him *a* king, as John says.[^2] In order, therefore, that he might flee the suspicion of tyranny,[^3] he departs.

[^1]: Theophylact's text reads «εἰς τὰ ὅρια Μαγδαλά» ("into the borders of Magdala"), the Byzantine/Textus-Receptus reading; many critical texts read Μαγαδάν ("Magadan"). Translated as it stands. *ἐνέβη εἰς τὸ πλοῖον* — *ἐνέβη* (aorist of *ἐμβαίνω*, "step in, go aboard, embark"); "embarked into the boat." *ἀπολύσας* ("having dismissed / released") is kept as a participle. The supplied "*a*" before "king" (below) and here nothing needs supplying beyond the anarthrous phrase noted.
[^2]: Cf. John 6:15, "they were about to come and seize him, that they might make him king." *βασιλέα* is anarthrous; the supplied article "*a*" is italicized.
[^3]: Greek *τὴν τῆς τυραννίδος ὑποψίαν*. *τυραννίς* here does not mean cruel rule but the seizing of royal power by popular acclamation — a usurped or illegitimate kingship; "the suspicion of tyranny" carries the sense "the suspicion of *making a bid for* usurped rule." Rendered "tyranny" to keep the single word, with this sense noted.

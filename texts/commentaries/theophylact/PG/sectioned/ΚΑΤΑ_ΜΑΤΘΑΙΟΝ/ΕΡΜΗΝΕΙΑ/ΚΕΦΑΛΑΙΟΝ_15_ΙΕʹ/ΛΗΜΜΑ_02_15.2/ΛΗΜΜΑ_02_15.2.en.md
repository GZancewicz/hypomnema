# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 15:2

> **…thy disciples transgress the tradition of the elders? For they wash not their hands whenever they eat bread."**[^1] (Matt. 15:2)

Even if all the regions had Scribes and Pharisees, yet the more honored *ones* were in Jerusalem; wherefore these also envied most, as being more fond of glory.[^2] And the Jews had a custom, from ancient tradition, not to eat with unwashed hands. Therefore, seeing the disciples despising this tradition, they thought that they were setting the elders at naught.[^3] What then *does* the Savior *do*?[^4] On the one hand he defends nothing against this; but on the other hand he brings a counter-charge against them,[^5]

[^1]: Greek «μαθηταί σου παραβαίνουσι τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν πρεσβυτέρων; οὐ γὰρ νίπτονται τὰς χεῖρας αὐτῶν, ὅταν ἄρτον ἐσθίωσιν.» This completes the sentence begun in Lemma 1 ("Why *do* thy disciples…"). *ὅταν* rendered "whenever"; *ἄρτον ἐσθίωσιν* literally "eat bread."
[^2]: Greek *ἐντιμότεροι* ("more honored / more esteemed") and *φιλοδοξότεροι* ("more fond of glory / more vainglorious"), both comparatives; kept as comparatives.
[^3]: Greek *ἐξουθενεῖν τοὺς πρεσβυτέρους* — "to set the elders at naught / to despise the elders." The OCR *ἐἔουθενεῖν* is securely *ἐξουθενεῖν*.
[^4]: Greek *Τί οὖν ὁ Σωτήρ;* — an elliptical question with no expressed verb ("What then *does* the Savior?"); the supplied "*does… do*" is italicized.
[^5]: The sentence breaks off at *ἀντεγκαλεῖ δὲ αὐτοῖς,* ("but he brings a counter-charge against them,"); it is completed in the next lemma, which supplies Christ's answer. The verb *ἀντεγκαλεῖ* is "to bring a counter-accusation / charge in return." The μὲν … δὲ antithesis ("on the one hand … on the other hand") is preserved.

# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 15:22

> **«...and he hid himself.»[^1]**

Besides, since he saw the Pharisees not having received the discourse concerning foods,[^2] he passes over to the Gentiles. And she says "Have mercy"—not on my daughter, for she is without perception, but on me, the *one* suffering the dreadful things and perceiving *them*. And she does not say, "Come and heal," but "Have mercy." But he does not answer her a word, not as despising *her*, but in order that he might show that he came primarily for the sake of the Jews; and might block up their false accusations,[^3] they not having *it in their power* to say later that he benefits the Gentiles; and at the same time, that he might also display the persevering faith of the woman.

[^1]: This lemma opens with the tail of the Marcan citation begun in the previous lemma («ἐπεὶ ὡς ὁ Μᾶρκος φησί», "since, as Mark says"). «καὶ ἔκρυψεν ἑαυτόν» ("and he hid himself") is Theophylact's paraphrase of Mark 7:24, «καὶ εἰσελθὼν εἰς οἰκίαν οὐδένα ἤθελε γνῶναι, καὶ οὐκ ἠδυνήθη λαθεῖν» ("and having entered a house he wished no one to know *it*, and he could not be hid").
[^2]: «τὸν περὶ βρωμάτων λόγον» — the immediately preceding discourse on what defiles a person and on eating with unwashed hands (Matt. 15:1–20). OCR «ταρισαίσυς» = *Φαρισαίους* ("Pharisees").
[^3]: Greek *ἐμφράξῃ τὰς συκοφαντίας* — literally "wall up / stop the mouth of their slanders (false accusations)." The image is of stopping up an opening; rendered "block up their false accusations."

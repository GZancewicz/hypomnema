# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 15:11

> **…*that which* enters into the mouth defiles the man, but that which proceeds out of the mouth, this defiles the man.**[^1]

No longer does he converse with the Pharisees, as *being* incurable, but with the crowd. And by the summoning *of them* he seems to honour them, so that they may accept his word; and he says, "Hear and understand," rousing them to attention. But when the Pharisees were blaming the disciples as eating with unwashed hands, the Lord speaks concerning foods, *saying* that no food defiles the man — that is, pollutes *him*. And if the food does not pollute, much more *does* the eating with unwashed hands *not do so*. For what defiles the inner man is the saying of things which one ought not.[^2] And through this he hints at the Pharisees, *as* being polluted by the speaking of envious words.[^3] But behold *his* wisdom, how he neither openly legislates the eating unwashed, nor dissuades *from it*; but he teaches something else — the not casting forth wicked words from the heart.

[^1]: Greek «…εἰσερχόμενον εἰς τὸ στόμα κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον, ἀλλὰ τὸ ἐκπορευόμενον ἐκ τοῦ στόματος, τοῦτο κοινοῖ τὸν ἄνθρωπον» (OCR *εἰς τὴν στόμα* = *εἰς τὸ στόμα*, neuter). The verb *κοινοῖ* means literally "makes common," i.e. renders ritually unclean; Theophylact himself glosses it just below with *τουτέστι μολύνει* ("that is, pollutes"). Rendered "defiles" for the plain sense, with his own gloss "pollutes" retained in the comment.
[^2]: Greek «Μολύνει γὰρ τὸν ἐντὸς ἄνθρωπον, τὸ λέγειν ἃ μὴ δεῖ» — the subject is *τὸ λέγειν ἃ μὴ δεῖ* ("the saying of things which one ought not *to say*"), the object *τὸν ἐντὸς ἄνθρωπον* ("the inner man"). OCR *ἃ μὴ εῖ* is securely *ἃ μὴ δεῖ*.
[^3]: Greek «μολυνομένους ἐκ τοῦ λέγειν φθονούντων ῥήματα». The genitive participle *φθονούντων* ("of *those* envying / envious *ones*") is syntactically loose: either "words of envious *men*" or, taken adjectivally with *ῥήματα*, "envious words." Rendered "envious words"; the point is that the Pharisees are themselves defiled by the spiteful, envious speech they utter.

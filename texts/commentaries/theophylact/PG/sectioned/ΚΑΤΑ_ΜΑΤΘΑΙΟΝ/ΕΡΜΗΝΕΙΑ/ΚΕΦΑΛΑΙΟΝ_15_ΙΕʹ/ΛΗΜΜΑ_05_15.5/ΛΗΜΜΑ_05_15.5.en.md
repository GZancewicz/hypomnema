# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 15:5-6

> **But ye say: 'Whosoever shall say to *his* father or *his* mother, "*It is* a gift, whatever thou mightest have been profited by from me" — and he shall in no wise honor his father or his mother'; and ye have annulled the commandment of God on account of your tradition."**[^1] (Matt. 15:5-6)

The Pharisees, on the one hand, were accusing the disciples, as *men* transgressing a commandment of the elders; but Christ, on the other hand, shows them *to be* transgressing the law of God. For they were teaching the children to give nothing to their parents, but to dedicate whatever they have to the treasury of the temple. For there was in the temple a treasury, into which whoever wished would cast *offerings*, and this was called *gaza*;[^2] and the treasure was distributed to the poor. The Pharisees, then, persuading the children to give nothing to their parents, but to dedicate these things to the treasury of the temple, taught them to say that "It is a gift" — that is, "It is a thing dedicated to God, O father, *even* that which thou seekest to be profited by from me." And thus they themselves divided the money with the children, and the parents were left untended in *their* old age.[^3]

And the lenders also used to do this. For if someone lent money to someone, then the debtor was troublesome and did not pay back what was owed, the lender would say to this ungrateful debtor: "*It is* korban, that which thou owest me"[^4] — that is, "a gift dedicated to God." The debtor, therefore, as owing *it* thenceforth[^5] to God, would pay it down even unwillingly.[^6] This the Pharisees taught the children to do.

[^1]: Greek «Ὑμεῖς δὲ λέγετε· Ὃς ἂν εἴπῃ τῷ πατρὶ ἢ τῇ μητρί· Δῶρον ὃ ἐὰν ἐξ ἐμοῦ ὠφεληθῇς, καὶ οὐ μὴ τιμήσῃ τὸν πατέρα αὐτοῦ ἢ τὴν μητέρα· καὶ ἠκυρώσατε τὴν ἐντολὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ διὰ τὴν παράδοσιν ὑμῶν.» The citation is elliptical: *Δῶρον ὃ ἐὰν ἐξ ἐμοῦ ὠφεληθῇς* has no verb ("*It is* a gift, whatever thou mightest be profited by from me") — "*It is*" is supplied. *οὐ μὴ τιμήσῃ* is the emphatic double negative with the subjunctive, "he shall in no wise honor / by no means honor." The span runs from Matt. 15:5 into 15:6 (the verse-division "…honor his father" / "and ye have annulled…" is the traditional 15:5–6 seam); folder metadata "15.5" is refined to 15:5-6.
[^2]: Greek *γάζα*, a Persian loan-word for "treasure / treasury," here retained transliterated because Theophylact names it as the word (whence *γαζοφυλάκιον*, "treasury," in the sentence just above). The OCR *ἔβαλλεν* has no expressed object; "*offerings*" is supplied.
[^3]: Greek *κατελιμπάνοντο ἀγηροκόμητοι* — "were left un-cared-for-in-old-age." The adjective *ἀγηροκόμητος* (privative + *γηροκομέω*, "to tend the aged") means "not tended in old age"; rendered "left untended in *their* old age."
[^4]: Greek *Κορβάν ἐστιν ὃ ὀφείλεις μοι* — "Korban is that which thou owest me." *Κορβάν* (Hebrew/Aramaic *qorbān*, "offering") is a gift dedicated to God; cf. Mark 7:11. Theophylact himself glosses it in the next words.
[^5]: Greek OCR *ἔκτοτι*, securely *ἔκτοτε* ("from then on / thenceforth").
[^6]: Greek OCR *καὶ ἄνιν κατέβαλλε*; *ἄνιν* is corrupt and is best read as *ἄκων* ("unwilling / against his will"), giving "would pay it down even unwillingly." [?] Flagged because the reading, though highly probable in context (the debtor pays reluctantly, coerced by the vow), rests on emending the garbled OCR.

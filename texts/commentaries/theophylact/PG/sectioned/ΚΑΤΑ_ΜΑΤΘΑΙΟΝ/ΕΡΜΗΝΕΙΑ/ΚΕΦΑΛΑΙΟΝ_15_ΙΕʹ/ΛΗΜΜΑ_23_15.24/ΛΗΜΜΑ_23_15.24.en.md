# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 15:25–27

> **«But she, having come, was worshipping him, saying, Lord, help me. But he, answering, said: It is not good to take the children's bread and to throw *it* to the little dogs. But she said: Yes, Lord; for indeed the little dogs eat from the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters.»[^1]**

Since the woman saw her advocates, the apostles, having failed, she again approaches fervently, and calls *him* Lord. And since Christ calls her a dog—because the Gentiles have an unclean life and busy themselves about the blood of things sacrificed to idols—but *calls* the Jews children, she answers understandingly and very wisely, *saying* that: Even if I am a dog and not worthy to receive bread—that is, some power and a great sign—yet nevertheless give this, which *is* small as compared to your power, but to me great; just as also *with* the crumbs, those who eat the loaves hold them *to be* nothing great, but the dogs *hold them* great, and from these they are fed.[^2]

[^1]: Matt. 15:25–27. «κυνάρια» is a diminutive, "little dogs / puppies" (rendered "little dogs" to keep the diminutive against «κύνες», the plain "dogs," in the exposition); «ψιχία» is likewise a diminutive, "little crumbs." OCR «κυναρκα»/«πυνάρκα» = *κυνάρια*; «ψιχίων» = crumbs. The lemma agrees with the common text.
[^2]: Theophylact's paraphrase turns on the woman granting Christ's terms while re-weighting them: the "bread" she is refused he glosses as «δύναμίν τινα καὶ μέγα σημεῖον» ("some power and a great sign"), and her request as a mere "crumb"—small relative to his power, great relative to her need. The two «μὲν ... δὲ» pairs are rendered with a plain contrastive ("small ... but ... great"; "the loaf-eaters hold them nothing great ... but the dogs *hold them* great"). In «οἱ μὲν τοὺς ἄρτους ἐσθίοντες οὐ μέγα τι ἔχουσιν, οἱ δὲ κύνες μέγα», *τὰς ψίχας* is the object held ("regarded"): the loaf-eaters reckon the crumbs nothing great, the dogs reckon them great, and are fed by them.

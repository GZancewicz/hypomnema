# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 15:12

> **Then his disciples, having come to *him*, said to him: Knowest thou that the Pharisees, having heard the saying, were made to stumble?**[^1]

The disciples, on the one hand, say *it* on account of the Pharisees, that they were made to stumble; but nevertheless they themselves also were thrown into confusion — and this *is* clear from Peter's coming forward and asking about this.[^2] At any rate Jesus, having heard that the Pharisees were made to stumble, said:

[^1]: Greek «Τότε προσελθόντες οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ, εἶπον αὐτῷ· Οἶδας ὅτι οἱ Φαρισαῖοι ἀκούσαντες τὸν λόγον, ἐσκανδαλίσθησαν;» (OCR *Ἀσανλαλίσθησαν* = *ἐσκανδαλίσθησαν*). The verb *ἐσκανδαλίσθησαν* (from *σκάνδαλον*, a snare/stumbling-block) is the theologically loaded "were made to stumble / were scandalized / took offence"; rendered "were made to stumble" throughout to keep the term visible. *τὸν λόγον* = "the word / the saying."
[^2]: *ἐθορυβήθησαν* — "were thrown into confusion / were agitated / troubled." The *μὲν* ("on the one hand") of the disciples' speaking on the Pharisees' behalf is answered by *Πλὴν ἀλλὰ* ("but nevertheless"): the disciples voiced the objection as though it were the Pharisees', yet were themselves disturbed. The lemma breaks off at *εἶπε* ("said:"), completed by lemma 12.

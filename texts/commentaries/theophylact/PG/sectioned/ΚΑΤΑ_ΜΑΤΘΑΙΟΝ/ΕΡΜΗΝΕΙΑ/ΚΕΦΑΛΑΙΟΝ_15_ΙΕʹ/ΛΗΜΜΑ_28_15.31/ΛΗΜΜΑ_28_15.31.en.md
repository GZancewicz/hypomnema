# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 15:31

> **crowds to marvel, seeing *the* mute speaking, *the* maimed whole, *the* lame walking, *the* blind seeing; and they glorified the God of Israel.**[^1]

He does not continually reside in Judea, but in Galilee, on account of the great unbelief of the Jews; for the Galileans were more believing than those *others*. See, therefore, their faith — how even up into the mountain they go up, being lame and blind, and they do not shrink back, but even fling themselves before the feet of Jesus, considering him *to be* above a human being, whence indeed they obtained their cure.[^2]

And you, therefore, go up into the mountain of the commandments, where the Lord sits; even if you are blind, not being able of yourself to see the good;[^3] even if lame — seeing, on the one hand, the good, but not being able to come to it; even if mute — neither hearing another *who* exhorts, nor being able to exhort another; even if maimed — that is, not being able to stretch out your hand for almsgiving; even if you are sick with some other thing — having fallen beside the feet of Jesus and having touched the footprints of that life *of his*, you will be healed.[^4]

[^1]: Greek «…ὄχλους θαυμάσαι βλέποντας, κωφοὺς λαλοῦντας, κυλλοὺς ὑγιεῖς, χωλοὺς περιπατοῦντας, τυφλοὺς βλέποντας· καὶ ἐδόξασαν τὸν Θεὸν Ἰσραήλ» (Matt. 15:31). This lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 26 (Matt. 15:29): "…so that the crowds marveled…" The disease-nouns are anarthrous; supplied "*the*" italicized. *κυλλοὺς ὑγιεῖς* is elliptical ("*the* maimed whole"), with the participle "*being*/*made*" understood from the parallel clauses.
[^2]: Greek *ῥίπτονται πρὸς τοὺς πόδας* ("they fling/cast themselves before the feet") echoes *ἔῤῥιψαν* of the lemma (Lemma 27); rendered "fling" to keep the link. *ὡς ὑπὲρ ἄνθρωπον δοκοῦντες αὐτόν*, "considering him *to be* above a human being" (i.e. more than merely human).
[^3]: *τὸ καλόν*, "the good" — also "the beautiful/noble"; kept as "the good" consistently through the fourfold parallel that follows.
[^4]: Theophylact allegorizes the four infirmities of the lemma into spiritual conditions: blindness (cannot see the good of oneself), lameness (sees but cannot approach), muteness (can neither receive nor give exhortation), and being *κυλλός*/maimed in the hand (cannot stretch it out for almsgiving). *τῶν ἰχνῶν τοῦ βίου ἐκείνου ἀψάμενος* — "having touched the footprints [tracks] of that life *of his*," i.e. imitating the manner of Christ's life.

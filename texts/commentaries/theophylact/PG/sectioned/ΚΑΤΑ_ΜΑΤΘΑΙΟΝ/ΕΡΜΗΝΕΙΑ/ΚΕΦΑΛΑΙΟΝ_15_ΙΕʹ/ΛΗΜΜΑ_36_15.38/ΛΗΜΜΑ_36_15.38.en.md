# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 36 · Matt. 15:38 (proof-text: Isa. 40:6 / 1 Pet. 1:24)

> **For all flesh *is* grass, and all glory of man *is* as *the* flower of grass.**[^1]

But the seven baskets are here the surplus,[^2] because the things which they were not able to eat were spiritual and more perfect; those things, therefore, were left over which seven baskets hold — that is, the things which only the Holy Spirit knows.[^3]

[^1]: A scriptural proof-text woven into the exposition of Matt. 15:38, not a Gospel lemma. Greek «Πᾶσα γὰρ σὰρξ, χόρτος· καὶ πᾶσα ἀνθρώπου δόξα, ὡς ἄνθος χόρτου» — Isa. 40:6 (LXX), taken up at 1 Pet. 1:24. The *γὰρ* ("for") is Theophylact's connective, drawing the citation back to the "grass" of the previous lemma; it is rendered roman as a present particle. His order *ἀνθρώπου δόξα* ("of man glory") is followed literally as "glory of man" (LXX has *δόξα ἀνθρώπου*); the two forms of "to be," absent in the Greek, are supplied in italics.
[^2]: Greek *τὰ περιττὰ* — "the leftover / superfluous things," i.e. the surplus of loaves that the crowd could not consume, read allegorically.
[^3]: Greek «ἃ μόνον τὸ ἅγιον Πνεῦμα οἶδε» ("which only the Holy Spirit knows"). The thought runs straight into the next lemma's proof-text, «Τὸ γὰρ Πνεῦμα πάντα ἐρευνᾷ, καὶ τὰ βάθη τοῦ Θεοῦ» ("For the Spirit searches all things, even the depths of God," 1 Cor. 2:10). OCR «σπλ.ρίδες» = «σπυρίδες»; «ούν» = «οὖν».

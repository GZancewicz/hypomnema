# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 15:18–20

> **… *the things* going out from the mouth come out from the heart, and those defile the man; for out of the heart come forth evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false testimonies, blasphemies. These are the things that defile the man. But to eat with unwashed hands does not defile the man. (Matt. 15:18–20)**[^1]

The Savior rebukes the disciples, and reproaches *their* folly — either as *ones* having been scandalized, or as *ones* having been ignorant of the plain *thing*. He says, therefore, that: "This which is understood and evident to all — do you not understand *it*? that the foods, on the one hand, do not remain within, but go out below, defiling the man in no way as to *his* soul, for they do not remain[^2] inside. But the thoughts,[^3] on the other hand, are both begotten from within and remain *there*,[^4] and going out — that is, advancing into work and into deed — defile the man; for the thought of fornication, both remaining within, defiles, and going out into work and being performed, utterly stains *him*.[^5]"

[^1]: The lemma opens mid-sentence, completing the "But the things …" (*Τὰ δὲ*) of the previous lemma, and covers Matt. 15:18–20. "*the things*" is supplied for the bare participle *ἐκπορευόμενα*.
[^2]: OCR *λένουσιν*, securely *μένουσιν* ("remain"): the foods "do not remain inside," picking up *οὐ μένει ἔσω* just above.
[^3]: OCR *Οἱ ἃ λογισμοὶ*, securely *οἱ δὲ λογισμοί* ("but the thoughts / reasonings"), answering *τὰ μὲν βρώματα* ("the foods, on the one hand") — hence the "on the one hand … on the other hand" here.
[^4]: OCR *μένουσιν ἐκ ἵ* is corrupt; the sense is that the thoughts, once begotten within, "remain" there (something like *μένουσιν ἐν αὐτῇ / ἔνδον*). Rendered "remain *there*," with the corruption flagged; the exact wording cannot be recovered from the OCR.
[^5]: Theophylact plays on a pair of staining-verbs: *μιαίνει* ("defiles / pollutes") of the thought while it remains within, and the intensive compound *κατασπιλοῖ* (from *σπιλόω*, "to spot / stain") of the thought once carried out into act — "stains *him* utterly / through and through."

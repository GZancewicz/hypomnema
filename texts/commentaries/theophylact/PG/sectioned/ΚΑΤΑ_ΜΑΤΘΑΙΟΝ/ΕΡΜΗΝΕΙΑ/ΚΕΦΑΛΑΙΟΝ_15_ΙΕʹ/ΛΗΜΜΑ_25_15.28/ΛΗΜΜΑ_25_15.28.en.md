# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 15:28

> **Great *is* your faith. (Matt. 15:28)**[^1]

Indeed, by saying "Let it be to you as you wish,"[^2] he showed that if she had not had faith, she would not have obtained her request; so that, if we too wish, *there is* nothing hindering *us from* obtaining the things we wish. But note that even *if* saints ask on our behalf — as the apostles *did* on behalf of that *woman* — nevertheless we, asking on behalf of ourselves, accomplish more. And the Canaanite woman *is* also a symbol of the Church *that is* from the nations.[^3] For indeed the nations too, having been thrust away before, were afterward raised up into the rank of sons, and were deemed worthy of the bread — I mean, of the body of the Lord. But the Jews became dogs, seeming to be fed from the crumbs — that is, from the small and worthless things of the letter.[^4] And Tyre signifies "constraint"; and Sidon, "those hunting"; and "Canaanite," "*one* prepared for lowliness."[^5] Accordingly, the Gentiles *who are* held fast by wickedness, among whom were the demons hunting souls — these too are prepared for lowliness; for the righteous were prepared for the height of the kingdom of God.[^6]

[^1]: The lemma is anarthrous with no copula in the Greek — «Μεγάλη σου ἡ πίστις» ("Great your faith"); the supplied "*is*" is italicized. This is the latter half of Matt. 15:28, the word of Jesus to the Canaanite woman.
[^2]: Greek «Γενηθήτω σοι ὡς θέλεις» — the immediately following clause of Matt. 15:28, which Theophylact quotes back within his comment.
[^3]: Greek *τῆς ἐξ ἐθνῶν Ἐκκλησίας*, "the Church *drawn* from the nations/Gentiles" — the standard patristic phrase for the Gentile Church. *ἔθνη* is rendered "nations" here and "Gentiles" just below, following Theophylact's own alternation of sense.
[^4]: Greek *τοῦ γράμματος*, "of the letter" — i.e. the merely literal (as opposed to spiritual) reading of Scripture; the "small and worthless" (*μικρῶν καὶ εὐτελῶν*) crumbs fed to the "dogs."
[^5]: Theophylact gives Hebrew-onomastic etymologies of the place-names of the episode (the woman came from the region of Tyre and Sidon, Matt. 15:21): *Τύρος → συνοχήν* ("constraint/confinement/distress"), *Σιδὼν → θηρῶντες* ("hunters / those hunting"), *Χαναναία → ἡτοιμασμένη τῇ ταπεινώσει* ("prepared for lowliness/abasement"). These are traditional glosses from the onomastic lists, not derivations Theophylact argues for; rendered so as to keep the roots visible, since the allegory that follows turns on each one.
[^6]: The sentence plays on the three etymologies just given: the Gentiles are *συνεχόμενοι* ("held fast/constrained," picking up *συνοχή* = Tyre), beset by *θηρῶντες* demons ("hunting," = Sidon), and *ἡτοιμασμένοι τῇ ταπεινώσει* ("prepared for lowliness," = Canaanite). Greek *ταπείνωσις* ("lowliness / abasement / humiliation") is here the negative pole, set against *τὸ ὕψος τῆς βασιλείας τοῦ Θεοῦ* ("the height of the kingdom of God") to which the righteous are prepared; rendered "lowliness" to hold both the etymology and the contrast with "height."

# Matthew 15 (ΙΕʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 15:38

> **…*those* eating were four thousand men, besides women and children. (Matt. 15:38)**[^1]

Teaching frugality, he reclines *them* upon the ground; but teaching us to give thanks before food, he himself also gives thanks.

But you ask how *it is* that there, on the one hand — although the loaves were five and the fed *were* five thousand — *there were* twelve baskets;[^2] but here, on the other hand — the loaves being more and the fed fewer — seven baskets[^3] were left over? It is possible, then, to say either that the baskets were larger than the *former* baskets, or that *it was so* in order that the equality of the miracle might not cast them into forgetfulness.[^4] For if now also twelve baskets had been left over, they would have forgotten, from the sameness, that he had worked a miracle a second time upon loaves.

But you, know this too: that the four thousand — that is, those having the four virtues[^5] perfectly — these are fed with seven loaves, that is, with more spiritual and more perfect words. For the number seven *is* a symbol of the seven spiritual gifts.[^6] And they recline upon the ground, placing beneath themselves all earthly things and trampling them — just as the five thousand also reclined upon the grass, that is, they set the flesh and the glory beneath themselves.[^7]

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 34: «Οἱ δὲ ἐσθίοντες ἦσαν τετρακισχίλιοι ἄνδρες, χωρὶς γυναικῶν καὶ παιδίων.» The participle *ἐσθίοντες* ("*those* eating"), left hanging at the end of Lemma 34, supplies the subject; the demonstrative is italicized as supplied. OCR «τετρακισχλιοι» securely = **«τετρακισχίλιοι»** ("four thousand").
[^2]: The reference is to the feeding of the five thousand (Matt. 14:15–21), where the leftover fragments filled *δώδεκα κόφινοι* ("twelve baskets"). Theophylact contrasts the two miracles: fewer loaves and more people there yielded more baskets; more loaves and fewer people here yielded fewer.
[^3]: Theophylact's contrast turns on a genuine lexical distinction in the Gospel text that English blurs. The five-thousand account uses *κόφινος* (the smaller wicker basket), the four-thousand account *σπυρίς* (a large hamper or provision-basket). His first answer — «ἢ αἱ σπυρίδες μείζους ἦσαν τῶν κοφίνων» ("either the *σπυρίδες* were larger than the *κόφινοι*") — depends on it. Both are rendered "baskets"; "the *former* baskets" marks the *κόφινοι*.
[^4]: Greek «ἵνα μὴ ἡ ἰσότης τοῦ θαύματος εἰς λήθην αὐτοὺς ἐμβάλῃ». *ἰσότης* = "equality, sameness"; the point is that an identical tally of leftover baskets would have let the two distinct miracles merge in memory. OCR «οὕνι. εἰπεῖν» = «οὖν εἰπεῖν»; «μβάλῃ» = «ἐμβάλῃ».
[^5]: The four cardinal virtues (prudence, justice, courage, temperance); Theophylact reads the *four* thousand allegorically as those who possess them "perfectly" (*τελείως*).
[^6]: The seven gifts of the Spirit (cf. Isa. 11:2). OCR «πνευματιχωτέροις» = «πνευματικωτέροις» ("more spiritual").
[^7]: Greek «ἐπὶ τοὺς χόρτους ἀνέπεσον» — "reclined upon the grass" (*χόρτος*). The word sets up the proof-text quoted in the next lemma, «πᾶσα σὰρξ χόρτος» ("all flesh is grass"): reclining on the *grass* figures the trampling-down of *flesh* and worldly *glory*. OCR «ὑπονάτιω» = «ὑποκάτω» ("beneath").

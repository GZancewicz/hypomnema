# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 16:28[^1]

> **Amen I say to you, there are some of those standing here, who shall by no means taste of death, until they see the Son of man coming in his kingdom. (Matt. 16:28)**

He said that the Son of man is about to come in his glory. In order, therefore, that those *men* might not disbelieve, he says that there are some here who, so far as *is* admissible,[^2] shall see in the transfiguration the glory of the second coming; and at the same time he also shows in what sort of glory those who have suffered for his sake shall be. For as his flesh flashed like lightning at that time, so, according to *a like* proportion, the saints then shall shine forth. And he hints here[^3] at Peter and James and John, whom he took along up into the mountain and showed them his kingdom — that is, the state that is to come, in which both he himself shall come and the righteous shall shine forth. He says, accordingly, that: "Some of those here shall by no means die, until they see me[^4] being transfigured." But observe that *it is* those who stand *firm* in the good and *are* steadfast[^5] — they *are the ones who* behold Jesus being transfigured into *what is* more radiant, and they advance ever in faith and in the commandments.

[^1]: The folder metadata assigns this lemma to 16.26, but the Greek lemma is «Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, εἰσί τινες τῶν ὧδε ἑστώτων …», which is Matt. 16:28. Header and reference corrected accordingly.
[^2]: Greek *κατὰ τὸ ἐγχωροῦν* — "so far as is possible / admissible," i.e. to the degree they were capable of receiving it. The disciples glimpse the glory of the Second Coming only in the measure allowed.
[^3]: Greek *αἰνίττεται* — "he hints / speaks in riddling fashion / alludes obliquely." Theophylact reads the "some standing here" as a veiled reference to the three witnesses of the Transfiguration.
[^4]: Greek OCR *Γδωσιμε]*, with the bracket already marking corruption in the source; securely = *ἴδωσί με* ("they see me"), matching the transfiguration reading. Reconstruction certain, noted for consistency.
[^5]: Greek *οἱ ἑστῶτες ἐν τῷ καλῷ καὶ βέβαιοι*. The participle *ἑστῶτες* ("standing *firm*") deliberately echoes *τῶν ὧδε ἑστώτων* ("those standing here") of the lemma: only those who "stand" steadfast in the good truly "see" the transfigured Christ. The wordplay is lost in a purely literal English rendering, hence this note.

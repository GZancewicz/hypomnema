# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 16:17

> **And Jesus, answering, said to him: Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal *it* to you, but my Father who is in the heavens. (Matt. 16:17)**

He calls Peter blessed as *one* having received the knowledge from divine grace; and, accepting him, he shows henceforth *that* the suppositions of the other people *are* false. And he said to him "Bar-Jonah," that is, "son of Jonah,"[^1] all but saying this: that "Just as you *are* the child of Jonah, so I *am the child* of my Father who *is* in heaven, being consubstantial[^2] with him." And he calls the knowledge a "revelation," inasmuch as the hidden and unknown things *were* made manifest by the Father.

[^1]: Greek *Βὰρ Ἰωνᾶ*, glossed by Theophylact himself as *υἱὲ τοῦ Ἰωνᾶ* ("son of Jonah"): the Aramaic *bar* = "son." He builds an analogy on the patronymic — as Peter is son of his father Jonah, so Christ is Son of the Father.
[^2]: Greek *ὁμοούσιος* — the technical Nicene term, "of the same essence/substance" (as the Father). Rendered "consubstantial." Theophylact reads the father–son analogy as an assertion of Christ's essential, not adoptive, sonship.

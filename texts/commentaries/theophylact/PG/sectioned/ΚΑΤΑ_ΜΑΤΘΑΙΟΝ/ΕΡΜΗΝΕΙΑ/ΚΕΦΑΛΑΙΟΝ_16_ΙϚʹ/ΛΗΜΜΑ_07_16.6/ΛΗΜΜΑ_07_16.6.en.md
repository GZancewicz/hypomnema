# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 16:6

> **—Jesus said to them: See and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees. (Matt. 16:6)**[^1]

Just as leaven is sour[^2] and old, so also the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees was biting the souls, being sour, and bringing in old traditions of the elders; and just as leaven is a mixture of water and flour, so also the teaching of the Pharisees *is* mixed out of both word and corrupted life. But he did not say to them plainly, "Beware of the teaching of the Pharisees," in order that he might remind them of the signs of the loaves.[^3]

> **But they were reasoning among themselves, saying that, We took no loaves. (Matt. 16:7)**[^4]

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence broken off at the end of the previous lemma («…Ὁ δὲ», "But he—"), the subject «Ἰησοῦς» ("Jesus") standing at the head of this file; the supplied dash marks the join. Restored: «Ὁρᾶτε καὶ προσέχετε ἀπὸ τῆς ζύμης τῶν Φαρισαίων καὶ Σαδδουκαίων». The construction *προσέχετε ἀπὸ* + genitive = "beware / take heed of." The OCR garbles the proper nouns as «δαρισαίων» / «ΣαΝδουκαίων», securely = Φαρισαίων / Σαδδουκαίων.
[^2]: Greek *ὀξώδης* ("sour, acidic, sharp"), used twice of the leaven and, by transfer, of the Pharisees' teaching that "was biting (*ἔδακνε*) the souls." Rendered "sour" to keep the fermented-leaven image; "acidic/sharp" is equally defensible and better preserves the "biting" pun.
[^3]: I.e. the miraculous multiplications of the loaves (Matt. 14 and 15); Greek *τῶν σημείων τῶν ἄρτων* ("the signs of the loaves"). By speaking of "leaven" rather than plainly of "teaching," the veiled word prompts the disciples to recall those miracles.
[^4]: This verse (16:7) carries over at the foot of the lemma file with no exposition attached; the imperfect *διελογίζοντο* is "were reasoning / were deliberating." Its comment is deferred to a later lemma.

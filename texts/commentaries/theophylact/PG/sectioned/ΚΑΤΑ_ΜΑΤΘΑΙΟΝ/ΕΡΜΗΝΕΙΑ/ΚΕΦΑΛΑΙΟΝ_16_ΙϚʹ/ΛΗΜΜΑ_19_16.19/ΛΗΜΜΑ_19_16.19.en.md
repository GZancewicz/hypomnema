# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 16:20

> **Then *he* charged his disciples that they should tell no one that he is the Christ. (Matt. 16:20)**

Christ was wishing, before the cross, that the glory[^1] concerning him be overshadowed. For if people had heard, before the passion, that he is God, and then were seeing him suffering, how would they not have been scandalized? For this reason, therefore, he hides himself from the many, so that after the resurrection he might be made known without scandal,[^2] the Spirit smoothing out all things through the wonder-workings.

[^1]: Greek *τὴν περὶ αὐτοῦ δόξαν*. *δόξα* here spans "glory" and "reputation / the opinion held about him"; the sense is that the high estimate of his person should be kept veiled until after the cross. Rendered "glory" to keep the theological weight, but "reputation / opinion about him" is equally defensible.
[^2]: Greek *ἀσκανδαλίστως* ("without scandal / without giving offense"), an adverb picking up *ἐσκανδαλίσθησαν* just above; the whole passage turns on the σκάνδαλον word-group (stumbling / being made to fall).

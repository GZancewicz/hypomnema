# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 16:3

> **Today *there will be* a storm, for the heaven is red *and* lowering; hypocrites, the face of the heaven, on the one hand, you know how to discern, but the signs of the times you cannot? (Matt. 16:3)**[^1]

He refutes their testing inquiry, naming them hypocrites, and he says that: Just as of the things appearing in *the* heaven one *is*, on the one hand, a sign of storm, and another of fair weather — and no one, having seen the sign of storm, would look for calm,[^2] nor, *having seen* that of calm, *would look for* storm — so also concerning me it is necessary to reckon. For this *is* one season of the coming,[^3] and another the *one* to come; now *there is* need of the signs upon earth. But the *signs* in the heaven are stored up for that season, when the sun shall be quenched, the moon shall be hidden, *and* the heaven shall be changed.

[^1]: The verse continues the saying begun in the previous lemma. "*and* lowering" renders the participle *στυγνάζων* ("being gloomy/overcast, wearing a sullen face"), agreeing with "heaven"; supplied "*and*" is italicized. Theophylact's *μὲν … δὲ* ("on the one hand … but") is preserved in the rendering of the two clauses about discerning the sky versus the times.
[^2]: "Calm" renders *γαλήνη* (properly the stillness of a calm sea), which Theophylact here substitutes for the verse's *εὐδία* ("fair weather," previous lemma) as its opposite to *χειμών* ("storm"): Greek *οὐκ ἄν τις τὸ σημεῖον τοῦ χειμῶνος ἰδών, γαλήνην ζητήσειεν*. The two words are near-synonyms for good weather; the shift is his, not the Gospel's, and is left visible here.
[^3]: Greek *παρουσία* — the technical term for Christ's "presence/coming," here set in antithesis: *ἄλλος … οὗτος τῆς παρουσίας ὁ καιρός, καὶ ἄλλος ὁ μέλλων* ("this *is* one season of the παρουσία, and another the *one* to come"). The word does double duty — his present earthly *presence* now, versus the future *coming* (the Second Coming) at the end — so a single English word cannot capture both poles cleanly; "coming" is chosen, with the sense flagged here.

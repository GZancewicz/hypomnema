# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 16:19

> **… keys of the kingdom of the heavens, and whatever you should bind upon the earth shall be bound in the heavens; and whatever you should loose upon the earth shall be loosed in the heavens. (Matt. 16:19)**

As God he says the "I will give to you," authoritatively; for as the Father has given you the revelation, so I *give* the keys. And by "keys" you will understand *those things* which bind and loose — the remissions or the censures[^1] of *men's* offenses; for they have authority to remit and to bind, those who according to Peter have been deemed worthy of the episcopal grace. For even if the "I will give to you" has been said to Peter alone, yet it has been given also to all the apostles. When? When he said, "Of whomever you remit the sins, they are remitted."[^2] For indeed the "I will give" signifies future time — that is, the *time* after the resurrection.

And the virtues, too, are called "heavens"; and *the* keys of these *are* the works.[^3] For through working we enter, as through certain keys that open *the way*, into each of the virtues. But if I do not work, but only know the good, I have, on the one hand, only the key of knowledge, but, on the other hand, I remain outside; and he who does not walk in them is bound in the heavens — that is, in the virtues; for the diligent *man* is loosed in these. Let us therefore not have sins, lest with cords[^4] …

[^1]: Greek «τὰς δεσμούσας καὶ λυούσας τὰς τῶν σφαλμάτων ἢ συγχωρήσεις ἢ ἐπιτιμήσεις». The syntax is compressed: the "keys" are the *remissions* (*συγχωρήσεις*, "pardons") or *censures/penances* (*ἐπιτιμήσεις*) of offenses, which respectively loose and bind. Rendered so as to keep both the binding/loosing pair and the pardon/censure pair visible.
[^2]: Greek «Ἄν τινων ἀφῆτε τὰς ἁμαρτίας, ἀφίενται» — John 20:23, cited as the moment the loosing-and-binding authority was extended to all the apostles after the resurrection.
[^3]: Greek *ἐργασίαι* — "workings, labors, practices, deeds." The keys to the "heavens" (= virtues) are the active *doing* of them; hence the following play on *ἐργάζεσθαι* ("to work"). Rendered "works," then "working," to keep the root visible.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at «ἵνα μὴ σειραῖς …» ("lest with cords …"). The thought is completed in the source by the image of being bound fast by the cords of one's own sins (cf. Prov. 5:22 LXX, «σειραῖς… τῶν ἑαυτοῦ ἁμαρτιῶν σφίγγεται», "he is bound tight with the cords of his own sins"); only the legible fragment is translated here.

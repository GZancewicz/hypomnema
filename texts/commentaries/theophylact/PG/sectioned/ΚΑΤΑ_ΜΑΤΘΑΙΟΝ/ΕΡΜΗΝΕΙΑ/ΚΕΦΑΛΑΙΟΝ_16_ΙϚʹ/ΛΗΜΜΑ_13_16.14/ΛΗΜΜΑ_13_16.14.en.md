# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 16:14–16

> **But they said: Some, on the one hand, *say* John the Baptist, but others Elijah; and others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. (Matt. 16:14)**

Those, on the one hand, who named him John, were like Herod, thinking that John after the resurrection had received also this gift, that of miracle-working; but those *who named him* Elijah, on account of *his* reproving *character*, and on account of *its* being expected that he would come; but those *who named him* Jeremiah, on account of the self-grown *nature* of *his* wisdom and *its being* without instruction; for Jeremiah, *being but a child*,[^1] was appointed to prophecy.

> **He says to them: But you, whom do you say me to be? And Simon Peter, answering, said: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God. (Matt. 16:15–16)**

And again, as *being* fervent,[^2] Peter leaps forward, and confesses him truly to be the Son of God. For he did not say, "You are Christ, *a* Son of God," without the article ὁ ("the"), but with the article, "the Son" — that is, that very one, the one and only, not a Son by grace, but the one begotten out of the very substance of the Father; since there were also many "christs,"[^3] all the priests and the kings, but "the Christ," *taken* with the article,[^4] is one.

[^1]: The OCR reads «κἷς ὢν», which is corrupt; the sense demanded by the context ("self-grown wisdom, without instruction") is best recovered as **«παῖς ὤν»** ("being *but* a child / a boy"), alluding to Jeremiah's protest at his call, "I am *too* young / a child" (Jer. 1:6–7 LXX, «νεώτερος… παιδάριον»). The point is that Jeremiah prophesied while still a youth, untaught. Reconstruction flagged because the Greek word is illegible.
[^2]: Greek *θερμός* — "warm, hot, fervent, ardent." A recurring characterization of Peter in this commentary (impetuous, quick to leap forward); rendered "fervent" to keep the epithet consistent.
[^3]: Greek *Χριστοὶ πολλοί* — literally "many anointed ones." Theophylact's argument turns on the etymology of *Χριστός* ("anointed"): priests and kings were all "anointed," hence "christs" in the general sense; only Jesus is *ὁ Χριστός*, "the Anointed," with the definite article. Retained as "christs" to preserve the word-play the sentence depends on.
[^4]: The OCR reads «κκτὰ τοῦ ἄρθρου»; read as **«μετὰ τοῦ ἄρθρου»** ("with the article"), matching the identical phrase used just above of "the Son." The whole passage hangs on the presence or absence of the Greek article ὁ.

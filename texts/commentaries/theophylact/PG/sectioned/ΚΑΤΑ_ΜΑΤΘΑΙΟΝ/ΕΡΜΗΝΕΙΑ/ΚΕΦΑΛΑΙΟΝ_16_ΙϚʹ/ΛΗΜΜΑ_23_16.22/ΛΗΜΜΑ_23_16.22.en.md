# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 16:24[^1]

> **Then Jesus said to his disciples: If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and let him take up his cross, and let him follow me. (Matt. 16:24)**

"Then" — when? When he rebuked Peter. For, wishing to show that Peter erred in hindering him from suffering, he says, in effect: "You hinder me. But I say to you that not only is my not-suffering harmful to you, but neither will you be able to be saved, unless you yourself also die — but *this holds* also for anyone else, whether man, whether woman, whether poor, whether rich." And he said "wishes,"[^2] in order to show that virtue is a matter of free choice and not compelled. And he follows after Jesus not *merely* the one who confesses him Son of God, but the one who also goes through all the terrible *things* and undergoes the same *things* *as he*.

"Let him deny" [?]signifies *a matter* of purpose.[^3] That is: let him have nothing common with the body, but let him despise himself — just as we are accustomed to say, "So-and-so denied so-and-so," instead of *saying* "He has *him* neither *as* friend nor *as* acquaintance." So, therefore, everyone too ought to have no friendship toward the body; so as even to "take up the cross," that is, to choose death, and with zeal to seek out even the reproachful death — for such was the cross among the ancients. But, "And let him follow me," he says. For many robbers and thieves are crucified, yet those are not my disciples; "let him follow," therefore — that is, let him display also all the rest of virtue. And he also denies himself who yesterday, on the one hand, was licentious, but today, on the other hand, is self-controlled. Such was Paul, having denied himself in saying,[^4]

[^1]: The lemma folder is labelled 16.22, but the Greek quoted here (*Εἴ τις θέλει ὀπίσω μου ἐλθεῖν …*) is Matt. 16:24; the header and reference are corrected accordingly.
[^2]: Greek *Θέλει δὲ εἶπεν* — "and he said *wishes*," i.e. the conditional "if anyone *wishes*" (*εἴ τις θέλει*) of the lemma. The word is retained as the lemma-word being expounded: the voluntariness of "wishing" proves virtue is *αὐτεξούσιον*, "self-determined / a matter of free will."
[^3]: Greek here is defective: the manuscript gives «Ἀπαρνησάσθω προθέσεως σημαίνων», with an evident gap between the lemma-word *Ἀπαρνησάσθω* ("let him deny *himself*") and *προθέσεως σημαίνων* ("signifying *a matter* of purpose / resolve"). Something has dropped out; the legible words are translated as they stand and the lacuna flagged with [?]. The point that follows ("let him have nothing common with the body, but despise himself") supplies the intended sense of self-renunciation.
[^4]: The sentence breaks off mid-clause; it is completed in the next lemma, which supplies the Pauline words «Ζῶ δὲ οὐκέτι ἐγώ, ζῇ δὲ ἐν ἐμοὶ Χριστός» (Gal. 2:20).

# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 16:22

> **And Peter, having taken him aside, began to rebuke him, saying: *May God be* merciful to you,[^1] Lord; this shall not be to you. (Matt. 16:22)**

On the one hand, that which was revealed *to him* Peter confessed rightly; but on the other hand, that which was not revealed *to him*, in this he erred — so that we may learn that without God he would not have uttered even that great *saying*. Therefore Peter, not wishing Christ to suffer, and being ignorant of the mystery of the resurrection, says: "*May God be* merciful to you, Lord; this shall not be to you."

[^1]: Greek *ἵλεώς σοι, Κύριε* — literally "*may God be* gracious / propitious to you, Lord," a Septuagintal idiom of averting an evil omen; hence the familiar force "far be it from you." The words "*may God be*" have no counterpart in the Greek (which is a bare adjective, ἵλεως) and are supplied. Rendered here as the deprecatory formula it is, without conforming to any English-Bible wording.

# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 16:21

> **From that time Jesus began to show his disciples that it is necessary for him to go away into Jerusalem, and to suffer many things from the elders and chief priests and scribes, and to be killed, and on the third day to be raised. (Matt. 16:21)**

He foretells to them the passion, so that, when it came unexpectedly, they might not be scandalized, having supposed that he had suffered as *one* unwilling and not knowing.[^1] For since they had heard, through Peter's confession, that he is the Son of God, then he reveals to them also the things of the passion. But after the grievous things, he adds also the joyful thing — the being raised on the third day.

[^1]: Greek *ὡς ἄκων καὶ ἀγνοῶν πέπονθεν* — "that he suffered as *one* unwilling and ignorant (not-knowing)," i.e. as though the passion had overtaken him against his will and without foreknowledge. The two participles *ἄκων* / *ἀγνοῶν* are contrasted with the point Theophylact is making: he suffered willingly and with full foreknowledge, having foretold it.

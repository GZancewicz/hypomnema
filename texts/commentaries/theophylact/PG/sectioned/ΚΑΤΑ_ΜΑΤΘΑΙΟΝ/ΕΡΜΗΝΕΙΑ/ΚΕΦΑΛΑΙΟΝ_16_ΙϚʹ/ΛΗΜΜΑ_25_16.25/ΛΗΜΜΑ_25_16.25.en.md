# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 16:25

> **For whoever should wish to save his soul[^1] shall lose it; but whoever should lose his soul for my sake shall find it. (Matt. 16:25)**

Toward martyrdom[^2] he urges us on. On the one hand, then, he finds his soul for the present — that is, saves *it* — the one who denies *Christ*, who also loses *it* afterward; but *the other* loses his soul, yet for the sake of Christ, *he* who shall bear witness on his behalf.[^3] *He it is* who also shall find it in incorruption and in eternal life.

[^1]: Greek *ψυχή* means both "soul" and "life"; the saying turns on the single word carrying both senses (to save one's earthly *life* is to lose one's *soul*). Rendered uniformly "soul" throughout to keep Theophylact's one-word play visible, but "life" is equally defensible at each occurrence.
[^2]: Greek *μαρτύριον* — "witness / testimony," and by the martyr-tradition "martyrdom." Theophylact reads the verse as an exhortation to martyrdom, so "martyrdom" is chosen; the primary sense "witness" underlies it.
[^3]: Greek *ὃς μαρτυρήσει ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ*. The verb *μαρτυρέω* ("bear witness / be a martyr") echoes *μαρτύριον* above; *ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ* = "on his [Christ's] behalf." The one who loses his soul for Christ is precisely the one who "bears witness / is martyred" for him.

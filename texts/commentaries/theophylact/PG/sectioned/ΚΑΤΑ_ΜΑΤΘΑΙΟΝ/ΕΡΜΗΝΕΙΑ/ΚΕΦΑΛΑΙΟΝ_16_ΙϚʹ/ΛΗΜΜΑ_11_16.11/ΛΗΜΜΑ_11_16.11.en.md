# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 16:11

> **…understand that I did not speak to you concerning bread, Take heed of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees?**[^1]

Then they understood that he did not say to take heed of the leaven of the bread, but of the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees. For they had supposed that this is what he was saying to them, namely, to guard against the defilement[^2] *coming* from the Jewish foods; wherefore also they were reasoning that they took no loaves. Therefore he reproaches[^3] them as senseless and of little faith. For, on the one hand, in respect of the fact that they did not remember with how many loaves he fed how many, *they were* senseless; but, on the other hand, in respect of the fact that they disbelieved — that, even if they had not bought loaves from *the* Jews, he himself was able to bring *them* forth[^4] — in respect of this *they were* of little faith. But when he had rebuked them more vehemently — for gentleness *is* not everywhere a good thing — immediately they understood that he had called the teaching "leaven." So powerful, everywhere, is well-reasoned rebuke.[^5]

[^1]: The lemma completes "How *do* you not…" of Lemma 10 with *νοεῖτε* ("understand"). Restored: «οὐ περὶ ἄρτου εἶπον ὑμῖν, Προσέχετε ἀπὸ τῆς ζύμης τῶν Φαρισαίων καὶ Σαδδουκαίων;» The clause "Take heed of the leaven…" is the words quoted back from 16:6.
[^2]: Greek *μολυσμόν* ("defilement, pollution"), i.e. ritual defilement contracted through non-kosher food; *βρωμάτων* = "foods, viands."
[^3]: The OCR reads «ἀναιδίζει», which as it stands would mean "he behaves shamelessly toward"; the sense securely required is *ὀνειδίζει*, "he reproaches / reviles / upbraids," parallel to *ἐπετίμησεν* ("he rebuked") just below. Translated as *ὀνειδίζει*; flagged because the two verbs are not identical in force.
[^4]: Greek *παραγαγεῖν* ("to bring forth, produce, provide"), the aorist infinitive of *παράγω*; here of Christ's power to supply bread. The supplied object "*them*" is not in the Greek.
[^5]: Greek *Τοσοῦτον ἰσχύει πανταχοῦ ἡ εὔλογος ἐπιτίμησις* — "So much does well-reasoned (*εὔλογος*, 'reasonable, well-grounded') rebuke avail everywhere."

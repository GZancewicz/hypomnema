# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 16:18 (with Ps. 9:14 LXX) → 16:19

> **"The one who lifts me up out of the gates of death."[^1]**

Of what kind of gates, O David? Of the two: of murder and of adultery.[^2]

> **And I will give to you the …[^3]**

[^1]: Greek «Ὁ ὑψῶν με ἐκ τῶν πυλῶν τοῦ θανάτου» — Ps. 9:14 (LXX; Heb. 9:13). The quotation is spliced into the ongoing comment on the "gates of Hades" (Matt. 16:18); the words "David said" that introduce it stand at the end of Lemma 16.
[^2]: Theophylact takes David's "gates of death" as David's own two great sins, murder (of Uriah) and adultery (with Bathsheba, 2 Sam. 11) — the gates out of which he was "lifted up" by repentance.
[^3]: Greek «Καὶ δώσω σοι τὰς …» — the opening of Matt. 16:19, breaking off before «κλεῖς» ("keys"). The scripture quotation and its exposition continue in Lemma 18.

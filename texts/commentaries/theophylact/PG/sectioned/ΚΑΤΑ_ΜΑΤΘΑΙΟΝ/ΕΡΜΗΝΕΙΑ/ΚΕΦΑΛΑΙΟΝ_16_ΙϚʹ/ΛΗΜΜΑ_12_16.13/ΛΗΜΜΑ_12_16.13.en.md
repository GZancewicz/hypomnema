# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 16:13

> **But Jesus, having come into the parts of Caesarea of Philip,[^1] was asking his disciples, saying: Whom do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?**[^2]

He made mention of the one who founded the city,[^3] since there is also another Caesarea, that of Strato,[^4] and he asks them not in that one, but in this one. For he leads the disciples far away from the Jews, so that, fearing no one, they might speak with boldness.[^5] And first he asks the supposition[^6] of the multitude, in order that they might be brought up to a greater conception, and might not fall into the same lowliness as the many; but he does not ask, "Whom do the Pharisees say that I am?" but, "Men" — speaking concerning the guileless[^7] multitude.

[^1]: Greek *Καισαρείας τῆς Φιλίππου* (OCR «σιλίππου»), "Caesarea of Philip," i.e. Caesarea Philippi. *τὰ μέρη* (OCR «μίρη») = "the parts / the districts."
[^2]: *ἡρώτα* is imperfect, "was asking / kept asking." The word order of the question is kept as the Greek has it: *Τίνα με λέγουσιν οἱ ἄνθρωποι εἶναι, τὸν Υἱὸν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου;* — literally "Whom do men say me to be, the Son of Man?"
[^3]: i.e. Philip the tetrarch, who built up the city and gave it his name; hence the Evangelist's designation "Caesarea of Philip."
[^4]: Greek *ἡ Στράτωνος* — "that of Strato," Caesarea on the sea (Caesarea Maritima), anciently "Strato's Tower." Theophylact notes the qualifier "of Philip" distinguishes this inland Caesarea from that coastal one.
[^5]: Greek *μετὰ παῤῥησίας* ("with boldness / with frankness of speech"); *ὡς ἂν … εἴπωσι* is a purpose clause, "so that they might speak."
[^6]: Greek *ὑπόληψιν* ("opinion, assumption, supposition"). *ἀνενεχθῶσιν* ("might be brought up / raised up"), aorist passive subjunctive of *ἀναφέρω*.
[^7]: Greek *ἀδόλου* ("guileless, without deceit, sincere"; OCR «ἀδόλοι… κλήθους» for *ἀδόλου πλήθους*), contrasting the plain, undeceiving common people with the scheming Pharisees.

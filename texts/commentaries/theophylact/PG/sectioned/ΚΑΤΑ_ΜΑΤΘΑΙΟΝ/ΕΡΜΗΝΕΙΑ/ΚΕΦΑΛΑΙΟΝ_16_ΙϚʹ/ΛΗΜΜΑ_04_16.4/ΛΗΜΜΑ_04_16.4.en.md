# Matthew 16 (ΙϚʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 16:4

> **An evil and adulterous generation seeks after a sign, and a sign shall not be given to it, except the sign of Jonah the prophet; and having left them he went away. (Matt. 16:4)**

An evil generation, as *those who are* tempting; adulterous, as *those who are* departing from God and cleaving to the devil. But *while* they seek a sign from heaven, he does not give *one*, except the sign of Jonah — that is, that having been for three days in the belly of the great sea-monster of death,[^1] he shall rise. But this sign, you might say, is even from heaven; for in his death the sun was darkened,[^2] and all creation was altered. But observe the wording:[^3]

[^1]: Greek *τριήμερος ἐν τῇ κοιλίᾳ τοῦ μεγάλου κήτους τοῦ θανάτου γενόμενος* — "having become, for three days, in the belly of the great sea-monster of death." *κῆτος* is the "sea-monster / great fish" that swallowed Jonah (Jonah 2:1 LXX, cited at Matt. 12:40); Theophylact allegorizes it, the appended genitive *τοῦ θανάτου* ("of death") equating the monster with death itself — i.e. "the great sea-monster *which is* death." Rendered "sea-monster of death" to keep both the Jonah image and the gloss.
[^2]: Cf. Matt. 27:45. Greek *ἐσκοτίσθη* ("was darkened").
[^3]: The exposition breaks off at *Ὅρα δὲ τὴν λέξιν* ("But observe the wording:"); the point about the wording is carried out in the next lemma, which re-quotes «Σημεῖον οὐ δοθήσεται αὐτῇ…» and turns on the difference between "to it" (the generation) and "to them."

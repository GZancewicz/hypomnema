# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 8:22

> **Jesus said to him: Follow me, and leave the dead to bury their own dead.[^1] (Matt. 8:22)**

After the point at which[^2] one shall have given himself to God, he ought not to turn back to the affairs of life. For one must, on the one hand, honor even one's parents, but *one must* prefer God. But here the parent was also an unbeliever, and *this is* plain from the saying: "Leave the dead" — that is, the unbelievers — "to bury their own dead." But if that man was not permitted even to bury *his* father, woe to those who, after having taken up the monastic life,[^3] turn back to *worldly* affairs.

[^1]: The sentence begins in the previous lemma (18), which carries «Ὁ δὲ» ("But…"); this file resumes at «Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν αὐτῷ». Greek *ἄφες τοὺς νεκροὺς θάψαι τοὺς ἑαυτῶν νεκρούς*: *ἄφες* = "leave / permit"; *τοὺς ἑαυτῶν νεκρούς* = "their own dead." Rendered from Theophylact's Greek, not conformed to a received English wording.
[^2]: The OCR reads «κεθ´ ὃ», securely = **«μεθ᾿ ὅ»** ("after which / after that"); i.e., "from the point at which." A low-stakes, certain correction, noted for consistency.
[^3]: Greek *μετὰ τὸ μονάσαι* — literally "after the to-live-as-a-monk," i.e., "after entering the monastic (solitary) life." The verb *μονάζειν* is the technical Byzantine term for embracing monasticism; kept explicit rather than generalized to "having withdrawn."

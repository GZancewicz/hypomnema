# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 8:17

> **there might be fulfilled that which was spoken through Isaiah the prophet, saying: He himself took our infirmities, and bore *our* diseases.**[^1]

In *the* evening and out of season[^2] they were bringing the sick, but he, being benevolent,[^3] healed all. Then, so that you might not disbelieve how so many diseases were being healed in a short *time*, he brings forward Isaiah *as* bearing witness. And yet[^4] the prophet says this concerning sins, whereas Matthew has set it down concerning diseases — for indeed the greater number[^5] *of them* come to be out of sins.

[^1]: This completes the quotation begun in Lemma 13 (see there); the whole is Matt. 8:16–17. *Αὐτὸς τὰς ἀσθενείας ἡμῶν ἔλαβε* — *ἀσθένεια* is "weakness / infirmity"; *νόσος* below is "disease / sickness." Rendered "infirmities" and "diseases" to keep the pair distinct. "*our*" before "diseases" is supplied (the Greek carries only «τὰς νόσους», "the diseases").
[^2]: Greek *Ὁψίας καὶ παρὰ καιρὸν* — *ὁψίας* here as a genitive of time ("at evening / late"); *παρὰ καιρὸν* = "contrary to *the* fitting time, unseasonably." The point: they brought the sick late in the day, past the proper hour.
[^3]: Greek *φιλάνθρωπος ὢν*, "being a lover of mankind / benevolent." OCR *ὡν* → *ὢν* ("being"); *κθεράπευσς* → *ἐθεράπευσε* ("healed"), both secure.
[^4]: Greek *Καίτοιγε* ("and yet / although indeed"), marking the tension: Isaiah spoke of sins, Matthew applies the words to bodily diseases.
[^5]: Greek *τὰ πλείω* ("the more / the majority"), i.e. most diseases arise from sins. OCR *τοσκῆτα* → *τοσαῦτα* ("so many") and *Ετα* → *Εἶτα* ("then") above, both secure.

# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 8:12

> **…*of the* kingdom shall be cast out into the outer darkness; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of the teeth. (Matt. 8:12)**[^1]

He did not say that many Gentiles will recline, so that he might not strike the Jews, but he spoke in a veiled way, "from *the* East and West." And he made mention of Abraham, showing that he is not opposed to the Old *Covenant*; and having said "outer darkness," he made clear that there is also an inner *one*, which is also milder.[^2] For there are degrees even in punishment. And "sons of the kingdom" he calls the Jews, for to them *belong* the promises. For "a son," he says, "my firstborn is Israel" (Exod. 4:22).

[^1]: This lemma begins mid-sentence, continuing the previous lemma («οἱ δὲ υἱοὶ τῆς βασιλείας…», "but the sons of the kingdom…"). The genitive «βασιλείας» belongs to that broken-off phrase; supplied "*of the*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek «ἐξώτερον δὲ σκότος εἰπὼν, ἐδήλωσεν ὅτι ἐστὶ καὶ ἐνδότερον, ὃ καὶ ἐλαφρότερον». Theophylact reads "*outer* darkness" as implying a contrasting "*inner*" darkness that is «ἐλαφρότερον» — literally "lighter," here rendered "milder" to avoid collision with the darkness imagery; the sense is a less severe punishment — hence his gloss "there are degrees («βαθμοί») even in punishment." The comparative could also be rendered "milder/easier."

# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 8:15

> **…he touched her hand,[^1] and the fever left her. And she arose and was serving them. (Matt. 8:15)**

He entered into Peter's house in order to eat. And having touched the hand, he not only quenched the fever, but also gave back pure health to the woman, so that both her strength returned and she was strong enough to serve. And yet we know that a long time is extended out for the sick unto recovery. And whereas the other evangelists said that they besought him, and thus he healed her (cf. Mark 1:30; Luke 4:38), Matthew did not say this, having taken thought for brevity. For I told you also at the beginning, that what this *one* omitted, another says; and learn also that marriage in no way hinders virtue, for the chief[^2] of the apostles had a mother-in-law—[^3]

[^1]: This lemma resumes the sentence begun in the previous lemma, which broke off at «Καὶ»; here it continues «ἥψατο τῆς χειρὸς αὐτῆς» ("he touched her hand").
[^2]: Greek «ὁ κορυφαῖος τῶν ἀποστόλων» — "the coryphaeus (foremost/chief) of the apostles," i.e. Peter.
[^3]: The sentence trails off at «πενθερὰν εἶχεν» ("had a mother-in-law"), the point being that Peter, chief of the apostles, was married and yet no less virtuous. The clause is complete in sense; the exposition continues into the following lemma.

# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 8:20

> **Jesus says to him: The foxes have dens, and the birds of heaven *have* nests;[^1] but the Son of man has not where he may lay *his* head.**[^2]

He calls "a scribe" the one who knows the letter of the law.[^3] This *man*, therefore, having seen the many signs, expected that Jesus was gathering money from these *things*; wherefore also he is eager to follow him, so that he too might gather. But Christ, meeting his thought, all but[^4] says this: You expect, *by* following me, to gather money; do you not see that I am poor?[^5] Such also ought the one who follows me to be. And these things he was saying as if *hoping* he might persuade him, *once* changed, to follow; but he withdraws.

But some say that the foxes and the birds are the demons.[^6] He says, therefore, that: The demons rest in you, and I henceforth have no rest[^7] in your soul.

[^1]: Greek *τὰ πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ κατασκηνώσεις*; *κατασκηνώσεις* = "nests, roosts, dwelling-places." The verb "*have*" is supplied by parallel from the previous clause (the Greek repeats no verb).
[^2]: "*his*" before "head" is supplied; the Greek has only «τὴν κεφαλὴν» ("the head"). *κλίνῃ* is subjunctive, "may lay / incline." This completes the quotation begun in Lemma 16 (Matt. 8:19–20).
[^3]: A word-play impossible to keep in English: *γραμματέα* ("scribe") is glossed by *τὸ γράμμα τοῦ νόμου* ("the letter of the law") — the scribe (*γραμματεύς*) is one versed in the *γράμμα* (letter/writing) of the law.
[^4]: Greek *μονονουχί*, "all but, well-nigh, as good as" — Christ does not say it in so many words but virtually so.
[^5]: Greek OCR *ρικάε εἰμι* is corrupt; securely = **«πτωχός εἰμι»** ("I am poor"), demanded by the sense (the scribe hoped to grow rich by following him). OCR *προσεδό-κκησεν* → *προσεδόκησεν* ("expected"), *αὐτοῆ* → *αὐτοῦ* ("his"), *ὀωείλει* → *ὀφείλει* ("ought"), all secure.
[^6]: An alternative allegorical reading: the "foxes" and "birds" are taken to be demons, which lodge in the unbelieving scribe's soul, leaving Christ no place to rest there.
[^7]: A root-play on *ἀναπαύονται* ("rest, repose," of the demons) and *ἀνάπαυσιν* ("rest, repose," which Christ lacks): where the demons find their rest, Christ finds none. *λοιπὸν* rendered "henceforth."

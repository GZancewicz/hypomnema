# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 8:24

> **a great tempest[^1] arose in the sea, so that the boat was covered by the waves; but he himself was sleeping.[^2] (Matt. 8:24)**

He kept the disciples alone *with him*, so that they might see the miracle. But he allows them to be tossed by the waves, in order that they might be trained against temptations, and in order that, through this sign, they might believe the more.[^3] And he sleeps, so that, having been made afraid, they might recognize their own weakness and might call upon him; wherefore he says: And—[^4]

[^1]: Greek *σεισμὸς μέγας* — literally "a great shaking / commotion" (the ordinary word for "earthquake"). Matthew alone uses *σεισμός* of this storm (Mark and Luke have *λαῖλαψ*); Theophylact keeps it. Rendered "tempest" for the sense of a violent upheaval of the sea, but the earthquake-word is the one on the page.
[^2]: The sentence continues from lemma 20, which broke off at «καὶ ἰδοὺ» ("and behold"); this file resumes at «σεισμὸς μέγας ἐγένετο…».
[^3]: The OCR reads «πιαταύσωσι», which is corrupt; from the context («ἐπὶ πλεῖον» = "the more") it is best restored as **«πιστεύσωσι»** ("they might believe") — hence "believe the more." An alternative restoration is *πιστωθῶσι* ("be confirmed / made sure"); either yields substantially the same sense. Flagged because the verb form is not securely legible.
[^4]: Greek *διό φησιν· Καὶ* — "wherefore he says: And—," breaking off to introduce the next lemma (22), which supplies «προσελθόντες οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ…» ("his disciples, having come to him…"). The subject of *φησιν* ("he says") is left unexpressed — the evangelist, or impersonally "*Scripture* says."

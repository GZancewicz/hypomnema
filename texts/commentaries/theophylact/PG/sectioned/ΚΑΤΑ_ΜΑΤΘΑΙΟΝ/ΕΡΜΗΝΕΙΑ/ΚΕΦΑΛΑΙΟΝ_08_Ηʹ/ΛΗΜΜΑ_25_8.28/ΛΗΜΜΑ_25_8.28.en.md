# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 8:28

> **And to him, having come to the other side, into the country of the Gergesenes, there met him two demoniacs.[^1] (Matt. 8:28)**

While those in the boat were doubting what sort *of man* this is[^2] — that both the winds and the sea obey him — the demons come *as* heralds. But if, on the other hand, Mark and Luke speak of one *man*, the *one* having the legion, understand that that *man* was one of these two, the one perhaps more notable; and *Christ* came to them of his own accord,[^3] since — they being fearsome — no one dared to bring them *to him*.

[^1]: Greek *δύο δαιμονιζόμενοι* ("two demoniacs / two *men* being demon-possessed"). Matthew's "two" against the single demoniac of Mark 5:2 and Luke 8:27 is exactly the harmonizing difficulty Theophylact takes up in the exposition below. Place-name: Theophylact's text reads *Γεργεσηνῶν* ("Gergesenes"); the manuscript tradition of Matt. 8:28 varies among Gergesenes / Gadarenes / Gerasenes. Translated as it stands on the page.
[^2]: Greek *ποταπός ἐστιν οὗτος* — *ποταπός* is "of what sort / of what kind" (with an undertone of "how great, how remarkable"); the present tense *ἐστιν* ("is") is retained, reflecting the disciples' still-open question.
[^3]: Greek *ἀφ᾿ ἑαυτοῦ δὲ ἦλθε πρὸς αὐτοὺς* — the verb is singular with an unexpressed subject; the sense requires Christ (not the demons) as subject, so *Christ* is supplied. *ἀφ᾿ ἑαυτοῦ* = "of his own accord / on his own initiative," the point being that since no one dared lead the fearsome demoniacs to him, he went to them himself.

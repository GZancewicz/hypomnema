# Matthew 8 (Ηʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 8:28–29

> **Coming out of the tombs, exceedingly fierce, so that no one was able[^1] to pass by through that road.**

They were spending *their* time in the tombs, the demons wishing to instill a *false* teaching: that the souls of the dead become demons. Which let no one even think; for the soul, having gone out, does not wander in the world. For the *souls* of the righteous, on the one hand, *are* in *the* hand of God; but the *souls* of sinners, on the other hand, these too are led away from here, as *was the soul* of the rich man.[^2]

> **And behold, they cried out, saying: What *is there* to us and to you,[^3] Jesus, Son of God? Have you come here before *the* time to torment us? (Matt. 8:29)**

Behold, they proclaim *him* Son of God, having first confessed their enmity. But they consider *it* a torment not to be permitted to wrong men; but *as for the phrase* "before the time," understand that they thought Christ — not enduring the excess of their wickedness — would not wait for the time of *their* punishment; which is not *so*. For until the consummation[^4] they are permitted to wrestle with us.

[^1]: OCR corruption in the source: «Ἐατε μὴ ἰσχύειν» is securely **«ὥστε μὴ ἰσχύειν»** ("so that … was not able"), the standard result-clause construction; and «χαλεποὶλίαν» = **«χαλεποὶ λίαν»** ("exceedingly fierce"). Both reconstructions are certain and low-stakes.
[^2]: The reference is to the rich man of Luke 16:22–23, whose soul, at death, is "led away from here" to torment — Theophylact's proof that the souls of sinners do not linger in the world as roving demons.
[^3]: Greek *Τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί* — literally "What to us and to you?", a Semitic idiom of dissociation ("What have we to do with you?" / "What *is there* between us and you?"). Rendered word-for-word with the copula supplied; the idiom is preserved rather than smoothed.
[^4]: Greek *συντέλεια* — the "consummation / completion" of the age (cf. Matt. 24:3), i.e. the end of the world, the appointed time of the demons' punishment. *παλαίειν ἡμῖν* is literally "to wrestle with us."

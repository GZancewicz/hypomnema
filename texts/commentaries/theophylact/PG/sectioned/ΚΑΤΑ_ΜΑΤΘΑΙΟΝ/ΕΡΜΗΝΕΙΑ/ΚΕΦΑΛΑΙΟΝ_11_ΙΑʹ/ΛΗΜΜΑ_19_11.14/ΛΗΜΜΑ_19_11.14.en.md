# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 11:14–15

> **…are willing to receive *it*, he himself is Elias who is about to come. (Matt. 11:14)**[^1]

If ye are willing, he says, to receive *this* — that is,[^2] if you judge fairly, and not enviously — he himself is *the one* whom the prophet Malachi said would be Elias, the one about to come.[^3] For indeed both the Forerunner and Elias have the same ministry: the one having run before the first coming, but Elias being about to run before the coming *one*.[^4] Then, showing that it is a riddle[^5] that John is Elias, and that there is need of understanding in order to comprehend *it*, he says that: "He who has ears to hear, let him hear,"[^6] leading them on to ask and to learn.

[^1]: The lemma runs on from the previous one: «Καὶ εἰ» ("And if") closed Lemma 18, and this file opens mid-quote at «θέλετε δέξασθαι…». The whole verse (Matt. 11:14) in Theophylact's text: «Καὶ εἰ θέλετε δέξασθαι, αὐτός ἐστιν Ἡλίας ὁ μέλλων ἔρχεσθαι» — "And if ye are willing to receive *it*, he himself is Elias who is about to come." The object of "receive" is unexpressed in the Greek; supplied "*it*" is italicized.
[^2]: OCR «του· ἔστιν» is securely *τουτέστιν* ("that is").
[^3]: The reference is to Malachi (Mal. 4:5, LXX Mal. 3:22–23), where the coming of Elias is foretold. Greek: «αὐτός ἐστιν ὃν εἶπεν ὁ προφήτης Μαλαχίας Ἡλίαν μέλλοντα» — "he is *the one* whom the prophet Malachi said *would be* Elias *who is* to come."
[^4]: A wordplay on one root: *προδραμών* ("having run before") of the Forerunner (*Πρόδρομος*) at the first coming, and *προδραμούμενος* ("being about to run before") of Elias at the second. Greek: «Ὁ μὲν τῆς πρώτης παρουσίας προδραμών, ὁ δὲ Ἡλίας τῆς μελλούσης προδραμούμενος» (OCR *προδραιολμενος*). "The coming *one*" / "the future *one*" (*τῆς μελλούσης*, sc. *παρουσίας*) = the Second Coming.
[^5]: Greek *αἴνιγμα* — "riddle, dark saying, enigma"; i.e. the identification of John with Elias is not literal but requires spiritual discernment.
[^6]: Matt. 11:15, quoted by Theophylact within his own sentence: «Ὁ ἔχων ὦτα ἀκούειν, ἀκουέτω.»

# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 11:7

> **But as these were going, Jesus began to say to the crowds about John: What did you go out into the wilderness to behold? A reed shaken by *the* wind? (Matt. 11:7)**

Perhaps the crowds, having heard the question of John, would have been offended, *supposing* that perhaps John too is in doubt about Christ, and has thus so easily turned round in *his* judgment[^1] — and that though he had testified about Christ before. Therefore, doing away with this thought, Christ says: John is not a reed, that is, easily-turned.[^2] For if he were such, how then did you go out to him into the wilderness? For surely you would not have gone out to a reed, that is, an easily-swayed man,[^3] but you went out as to a great and steadfast man. Accordingly, he remains such as you yourselves supposed *him to be*.

[^1]: Greek *μετεστράφη τὴν γνώμην* — aorist passive, "was turned about," with *τὴν γνώμην* ("in judgment / opinion / resolve") as accusative of respect. *μήποτε* here is not "lest" of fearing but introduces the content of the crowds' misgiving: "*thinking* that perhaps…"
[^2]: Greek *εὐμετάτρεπτος* (OCR *εὐμετάορεπτος*), "easily turned about, changeable, fickle" — the moral quality Christ denies of John by the image of the wind-shaken reed.
[^3]: Greek *εὐρίπιστον ἄνθρωπον* — literally "a man easily blown about"; the adjective *εὐρίπιστος* evokes the *Εὔριπος*, the strait whose currents notoriously shifted back and forth, hence "unstable, veering, tossed to and fro." *ἤτοι* = "that is / or rather," introducing Theophylact's gloss on *κάλαμος* ("reed").

# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 11:26

> **Yea, Father, for thus there came to be good pleasure before thee. (Matt. 11:26)**[^1]

He shows here the love-for-mankind of the Father, that not being urged by another did he reveal *these things* to the infants,[^2] but thus it pleased him from the beginning. For *good pleasure* is the will and the being-well-pleased[^3]

[^1]: The lemma has ὁ Πατήρ in the nominative form (with article) doing duty for a vocative, "O Father" — a common Greek idiom; rendered simply "Father." Greek «ναὶ» = "yea, even so." «ὅτι» is here causal, "for/because." «οὕτως ἐγένετο εὐδοκία ἔμπροσθέν σου» literally "thus there came to be good pleasure before thee"; *εὐδοκία* is the "good pleasure / gracious will / satisfaction" that Theophylact glosses at the end of the comment. Not conformed to the KJV "Even so, Father: for so it seemed good in thy sight."
[^2]: "these things" (τὰ μυστήρια, "the mysteries") is supplied from the preceding lemma (11:25), where the Father is said to have hidden them from the wise and revealed them to the infants (νήπιοι, "babes"); rendered "infants" for consistency with that verse.
[^3]: The comment breaks off mid-definition: Greek «Εὐδοκία γὰρ, ἡ θέλησις καὶ ἡ ἀρέσκεια» — "For *εὐδοκία* [is] the will and the good-pleasure/gratification (*ἀρέσκεια*)." Theophylact is defining the word *εὐδοκία* as it stands in the lemma; the sentence has no verb ("is" is supplied) and its continuation is not carried into the next lemma, which opens a new verse. Translated as far as the Greek runs.

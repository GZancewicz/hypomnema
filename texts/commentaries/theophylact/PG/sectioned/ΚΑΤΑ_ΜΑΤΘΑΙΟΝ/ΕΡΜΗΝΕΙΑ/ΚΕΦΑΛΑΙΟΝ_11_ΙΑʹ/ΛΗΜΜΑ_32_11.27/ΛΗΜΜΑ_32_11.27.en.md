# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 11:27

> **All things were delivered to me by my Father. (Matt. 11:27)**[^1]

He said above to the Father, that "Thou hast revealed, O Father";[^2] therefore, in order that thou mayest not suppose that he himself does nothing, but that all things are the Father's, he says that "All things were delivered to me," and there is one authority, mine and his.[^3] But hearing "were delivered," do not think that they were delivered *to him* as to a slave and *to one* inferior, but as to a son. For in respect of that whereby he was begotten from the Father, in respect of this it was delivered to him; for if he had not been begotten, and yet were of the same essence with the Father, they would not have been delivered.[^4] For see what he says:

[^1]: Greek «Πάντα μοι παρεδόθη ὑπὸ τοῦ Πατρός μου» — "All things were delivered/handed over (*παρεδόθη*, aorist passive of *παραδίδωμι*) to me by my Father."
[^2]: Referring back to the lemma at 11:25 («ἀπεκάλυψας… ὦ Πάτερ»), "Thou hast revealed… O Father."
[^3]: Greek «καὶ μία ἐξουσία ἐμοῦ κἀκείνου» — literally "and one authority (*ἐξουσία*) of me and of him," i.e. the Son's and the Father's authority is one and the same.
[^4]: Greek «εἰ γὰρ μὴ ἐγεννήθη, καὶ τῆς αὐτῆς οὐσίας ἦν τῷ Πατρὶ, οὐκ ἂν παρεδόθησαν». The negative *μή* governs only the first verb, *ἐγεννήθη* ("if he had not been begotten"); the second clause is positive — "and yet were of the same essence (*τῆς αὐτῆς οὐσίας*) with the Father." The argument follows the preceding sentence, that the delivering corresponds to the begetting ("in respect of that whereby he was begotten… it was delivered to him"): a hypothetically *un*begotten Son, even one consubstantial with the Father, would receive no "delivery" at all; the very language of "were delivered" therefore attests begotten sonship, and marks the Son as heir rather than as a servant receiving a grant.

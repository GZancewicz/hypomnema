# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 11:27

> **All things were delivered to me by my Father. (Matt. 11:27)**[^1]

He said above to the Father, that "Thou hast revealed, O Father";[^2] therefore, in order that thou mayest not suppose that he himself does nothing, but *that* all things are the Father's, he says that "All things were delivered to me," and *that there is* one authority, mine and his.[^3] But hearing "were delivered," do not think that they were delivered *to him* as to a slave and *to one* inferior, but as to a son. For in respect of that whereby he was begotten from the Father, in respect of this it was delivered to him; for if he had not been begotten and *had not* been of the same essence with the Father, they would not have been delivered.[^4] For see what he says:

[^1]: Greek «Πάντα μοι παρεδόθη ὑπὸ τοῦ Πατρός μου» — "All things were delivered/handed over (*παρεδόθη*, aorist passive of *παραδίδωμι*) to me by my Father."
[^2]: Referring back to the lemma at 11:25 («ἀπεκάλυψας… ὦ Πάτερ»), "Thou hast revealed… O Father."
[^3]: Greek «καὶ μία ἐξουσία ἐμοῦ κἀκείνου» — literally "and one authority (*ἐξουσία*) of me and of him," i.e. the Son's and the Father's authority is one and the same.
[^4]: Greek «εἰ γὰρ μὴ ἐγεννήθη, καὶ τῆς αὐτῆς οὐσίας ἦν τῷ Πατρὶ, οὐκ ἂν παρεδόθησαν». The single negative *μή* is here taken to govern both verbs ("if he had **not** been begotten and [not] been of the same essence… they would not have been delivered"), which the argument requires: precisely *because* the Son is begotten and consubstantial (*τῆς αὐτῆς οὐσίας*, "of the same essence") does "all things" belong to him as to an heir, not as a grant to a servant. Read with strict grammar the second clause is positive ("and was of the same essence"), which would yield the opposite sense; the theological logic of the passage settles it for the negative. The point turns on the antithesis son/heir vs. slave.

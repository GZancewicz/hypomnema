# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 34 · Matt. 11:27

> **by my Father; (Matt. 11:27)**[^1]

just as, for the sake of an example,[^2] if a comely child be born from a comely father, and it should say that "The beauty was given to me from my father."[^3]

[^1]: Greek «ὑπὸ τοῦ Πατρός μου» — "by my Father." This completes the sentence begun in the previous lemma: "not by *a* master, but «by my Father»."
[^2]: Greek «ὥσπερ λόγου χάριν» — "just as, for example / by way of illustration."
[^3]: Greek «Ἐδόθη μοι τὸ κάλλος ἐκ τοῦ πατρός μου» — "The beauty (*κάλλος*) was given to me from my father." The analogy: as a beautiful child derives its beauty from a beautiful father by generation, so the Son's having "all things delivered" to him bespeaks natural, begotten possession, not a servant's endowment.

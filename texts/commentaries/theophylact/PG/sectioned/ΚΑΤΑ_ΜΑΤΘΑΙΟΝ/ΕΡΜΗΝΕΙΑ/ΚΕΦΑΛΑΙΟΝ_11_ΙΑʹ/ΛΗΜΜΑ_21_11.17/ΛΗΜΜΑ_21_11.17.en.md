# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 11:17

> **…saying: We piped to you, and ye did not dance. We wailed[^1] to you, and ye did not beat *your breasts*.[^2] (Matt. 11:17)**

He hints[^3] here at the peevishness[^4] of the Jews. For being perverse *of temper*, they were pleased neither with the austere discipline[^5] of John, nor with the [ease] of [Christ] [?][^6] — on account of *its* levity;[^7] to whom, even if one weeps,

[^1]: *Ἐθρηνήσαμεν* (from *θρηνέω*) — "to sing a dirge, wail, lament for the dead." Rendered "wailed" to keep it distinct from the mourning-response *κόπτομαι* below; the common English text (KJV "we have mourned") flattens the pair.
[^2]: *οὐκ ἐκόψασθε* (OCR «σύν ἐκόόασθε») — *κόπτομαι* is "to beat oneself (the breast) in mourning." Supplied "*your breasts*" is italicized; the Greek carries the object in the verb alone.
[^3]: *αἰνίττεται* — "hints, speaks in riddling fashion" (cognate with *αἴνιγμα*, the "riddle" of Lemma 19).
[^4]: *τὸ δυσάρεστον* — "the hard-to-please / peevish *quality*"; the neuter adjective used as an abstract noun.
[^5]: *σκληραγωγία* (OCR «σκληβαγωγίᾳ») — "hard/austere manner of life, severe self-discipline."
[^6]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupt: «οὔτε τῇ τοῦ στουμένοις διὰ κουφότητα». Legible are «οὔτε τῇ τοῦ …» ("nor with the … of …") and «διὰ κουφότητα» ("because of levity"). By the antithesis with John's austerity, the sense is that they were displeased with the *ease/gentleness* of Christ, faulting it as frivolity; the corrupt «στουμένοις» cannot be securely recovered and is left bracketed rather than reconstructed.
[^7]: *κουφότης* — "lightness, levity, frivolity." The sentence breaks off at «οἷς κἂν κλαίῃ τις,» ("to whom, even if one weeps,") and is completed in Lemma 22.

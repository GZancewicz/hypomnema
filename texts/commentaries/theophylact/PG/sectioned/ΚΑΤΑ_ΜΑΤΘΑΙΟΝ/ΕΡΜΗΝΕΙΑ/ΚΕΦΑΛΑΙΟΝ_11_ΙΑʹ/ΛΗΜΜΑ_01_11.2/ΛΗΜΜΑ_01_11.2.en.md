# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 11:1

> **And it came to pass, when Jesus finished commanding his twelve disciples, he departed thence to teach and to preach in their cities.**[^1]

Having sent forth the disciples, he himself henceforth keeps quiet, working no miracle, but only teaching in the synagogues. For if he himself were healing while present, no one would come to the disciples; therefore, in order that those men might have room for healing, he himself departs. But[^2]

[^1]: The folder-name assigns this lemma to 11:2, but the Greek quoted is Matt. 11:1: «Καὶ ἐγένετο ὅτε ἐτέλεσεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς διατάσσων τοῖς δώδεκα μαθηταῖς αὐτοῦ, μετέβη ἐκεῖθεν τοῦ διδάσκειν καὶ κηρύσσειν ἐν ταῖς πόλεσιν αὐτῶν.» The OCR shows «ἐγὸνιτο» for *ἐγένετο* ("it came to pass") and «κηρόσσειν» for *κηρύσσειν* ("to preach"); both are secure. The genitive articular infinitives τοῦ διδάσκειν καὶ κηρύσσειν express purpose ("to teach and to preach"); the supplied "*to*" is left roman here as an ordinary infinitive marker.
[^2]: The exposition breaks off at «Ὁ δὲ» ("But *the*…"), which is the opening of the next scripture citation (Matt. 11:2, «Ὁ δὲ Ἰωάννης ἀκούσας…»). It is carried over into Lemma 2. The particle δὲ ("but") is a present word and so is roman.

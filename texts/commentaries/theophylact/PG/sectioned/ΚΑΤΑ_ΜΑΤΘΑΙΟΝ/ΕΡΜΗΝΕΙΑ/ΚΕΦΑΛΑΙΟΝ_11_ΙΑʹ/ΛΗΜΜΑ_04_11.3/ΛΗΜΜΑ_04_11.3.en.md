# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 11:3

> **«Behold the Lamb of God»?**[^1]

But since his disciples envied Christ, in order that he might give them full assurance,[^2] for this reason he sends them, so that, having seen the miracles, they might be persuaded that Christ is greater than John. For this reason also he himself feigns to ask,[^3] "Art thou he that cometh?" — that is, the one expected in the Scriptures to come with flesh. But some say that as to the "he that cometh," he was asking concerning the descent into Hades, as though John were ignorant, as if saying: "Art thou he that cometh also into Hades, or do we look for another?" But this is senseless. For John, being more than prophets,[^4] how would he be ignorant of the crucifixion of Christ and of the descent into Hades — and this after having named him a lamb, on account of his being about to be slain for us? John knew, therefore, that the Lord would come also into Hades with the soul, that even there he might save — as Gregory the Theologian says[^5] — those, as many as were about to believe if in their days he had become incarnate; and he asks not as being ignorant, but as wishing to give his disciples full assurance concerning Christ, through the working of the miracles. For behold what Christ also says in the face of such a question.[^6]

[^1]: The lemma opens with a scripture phrase, OCR-garbled as «Ἰε ἃ Ἀμνὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ;», securely = «Ἴδε ὁ Ἀμνὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ» ("Behold the Lamb of God," John 1:29). It is not a fresh Gospel lemma but the completion of the sentence broken off in Lemma 3: "concerning him of whom he testified that 'Behold the Lamb of God' — how could he be ignorant?" Left as a bold blockquote because Theophylact's text presents it as a quotation.
[^2]: Greek «πληροφορήσῃ» — "might fully assure / fully persuade / satisfy the mind of." The verb recurs below («πληροφορῆσαι») and is rendered consistently "give full assurance."
[^3]: Greek «σχηματίζεται αὐτὸς ἐρωτᾷν» — "he himself assumes the manner/pose of asking," i.e. he stages the question rather than asks from real ignorance. The verb *σχηματίζομαι* can carry the sense "feign, pretend, make a show of"; here the disciples of John, not Christ, are its concern — John frames the question for their sake. Rendered "feigns to ask" to keep the note of deliberate staging; an alternative is "he shapes *his question* so as to ask."
[^4]: Greek «προφητῶν περισσότερος ὤν» — "being more/greater than prophets," echoing Christ's own verdict below (Matt. 11:9, «περισσότερον προφήτου», "more than a prophet"). *περισσότερος* is literally "more abundant, exceeding"; rendered "more than prophets."
[^5]: A reference to Gregory the Theologian (Gregory of Nazianzus), on Christ's descent into Hades to save those who would have believed had they lived in the days of his incarnation. Greek «ὥς φησιν ὁ Θεολόγος Γρηγόριος».
[^6]: The exposition ends «Ὅρα γὰρ καὶ ὁ Χριστὸς τί λέγει πρὸς τὴν τοιαύτην ἐρώτησιν» — literally "For see also what Christ says to such a question," pointing forward to the answer quoted in the following lemmata (Matt. 11:4–6). «πρὸς τὴν τοιαύτην ἐρώτησιν» rendered "in the face of such a question."

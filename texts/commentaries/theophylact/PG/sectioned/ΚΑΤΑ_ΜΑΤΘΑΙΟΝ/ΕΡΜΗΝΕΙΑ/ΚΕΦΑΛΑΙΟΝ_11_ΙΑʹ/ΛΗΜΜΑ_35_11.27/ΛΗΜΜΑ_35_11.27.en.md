# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 11:27

> **And no one fully knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone fully know the Father except the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son may will to reveal *him*. (Matt. 11:27)**[^1]

He says the greater thing: that "*It is* nothing wonderful if I am Master of all, seeing that I have also another, greater thing — to know the Father, and so to know him as even to reveal him to others."[^2] But observe: above he said that the Father revealed the mysteries to the infants;[^3] but here, that the Son reveals the Father. Dost thou see, therefore, one power of Father and of Son, seeing that both the Father reveals and the Son *reveals*?

[^1]: Greek «οὐδεὶς ἐπιγινώσκει… εἰ μὴ…». The verb *ἐπιγινώσκω* (with the intensive *ἐπι-* prefix) means "to know fully, recognize, know accurately," a stronger word than plain *γινώσκω*; rendered "fully knows" to keep the force. The final clause «καὶ ᾧ ἐὰν βούληται ὁ Υἱὸς ἀποκαλύψαι» is elliptical — "and [he] to whomsoever the Son may will to reveal [him knows the Father]"; the object "*him*" is supplied.
[^2]: The Greek is a compressed argument: «Οὐδὲν θαυμαστὸν εἰ πάντων εἰμὶ Δεσπότης, ὅπουγε καὶ ἕτερον μεῖζον ἔχω, τὸ εἰδέναι τὸν Πατέρα» — "*It is* nothing wonderful if I am Master (*Δεσπότης*) of all, seeing that (*ὅπουγε*) I have also another, greater *thing*, namely to know the Father." The point: to be lord of all creation is less than the greater privilege of *knowing* the Father — a knowledge so complete as to be communicable to others.
[^3]: Referring to the lemma at 11:25 (the Father revealed the mysteries to the "infants/babes," *νήπιοι*). The symmetry — Father reveals, Son reveals — is Theophylact's proof of the "one power" (*μία δύναμις*) common to both.

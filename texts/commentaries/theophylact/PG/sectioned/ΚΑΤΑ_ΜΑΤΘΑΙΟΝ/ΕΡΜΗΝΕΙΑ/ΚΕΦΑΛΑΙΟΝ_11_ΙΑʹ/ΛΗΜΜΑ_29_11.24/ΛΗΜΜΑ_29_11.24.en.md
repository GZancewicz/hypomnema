# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 11:23–24

> **...of Sodom it will be more tolerable in *the* day of judgment than for you. (Matt. 11:24)**[^1]

Capernaum was exalted because of its being the city of Jesus, for it was glorified as, forsooth, his fatherland;[^2] but it was in nothing profited, because of its not believing. For, on the contrary, even for this reason it has been condemned the more, unto Hades — that, having such an *One* for inhabitant, it was in nothing profited from him.

And since Capernaum is interpreted "a place of consolation,"[^3] behold that even if someone be deemed worthy to become a place of the Paraclete — that is to say, of the Holy Spirit — and then be high-minded, and be lifted up as far as heaven, he falls down thereafter because of his high-mindedness. Shudder, therefore, O man.

[^1]: This lemma opens with the tail of the Gospel citation begun in the previous folder: *Σοδόμων ἀνεκτότερον ἔσται ἐν ἡμέρᾳ κρίσεως, ἢ σοί* — "for *the land* of Sodom it will be more tolerable in *the* day of judgment than for you." The supplied "*the*" (day of judgment) is italicized.
[^2]: Greek *ὡς δῆθεν πατρὶς αὐτοῦ* — "as, supposedly / as though *it were*, his fatherland." *δῆθεν* carries a note of ostensibility ("purportedly, forsooth"): Capernaum's reputation as Christ's home town was a matter of appearance, and it did not profit from it. *πατρίς* rendered "fatherland" (native city / home town).
[^3]: A play on the name lost in English: Theophylact derives *Καπερναούμ* as *χωρίον παρακλήσεως* ("place/field of consolation"), then links *παράκλησις* ("consolation, comfort, exhortation") to *Παράκλητος* ("the Paraclete, the Comforter"), the Holy Spirit. The point turns on the shared root: to be a "place of consolation" is to be a place of *the Consoler/Paraclete*. Greek: «Καπερναοὺμ ἑρμηνεύεται χωρίον παρακλήσεως… χωρίον τοῦ Παρακλήτου». *ὑψηλοφροσύνη* ("high-mindedness, haughtiness of thought") echoes, by antithesis, the "exalted unto heaven / brought down to Hades" of the lemma.

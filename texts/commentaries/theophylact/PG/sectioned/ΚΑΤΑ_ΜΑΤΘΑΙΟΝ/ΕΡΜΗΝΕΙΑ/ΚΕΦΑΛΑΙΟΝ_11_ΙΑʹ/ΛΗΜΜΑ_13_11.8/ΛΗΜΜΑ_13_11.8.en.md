# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 11:10

> **For this is he concerning whom it is written: Behold, I send my messenger before your face, who shall prepare your way before you. (Matt. 11:10)**

He was called a messenger, both on account of the angelic and almost immaterial life,[^1] and because he announced and proclaimed Christ. And he prepared the way of Christ, both as bearing witness concerning him and as baptizing unto repentance; for after repentance comes forgiveness of sins — which forgiveness Christ gives. But after [John's disciples had departed], Christ [said these things],[^2] so that he might not seem to flatter that man. And the aforesaid prophecy is Malachi's (Mal. 3:1).

[^1]: The single Greek word *ἄγγελος* means both "messenger" and "angel," and the comment turns on this double sense. In the lemma it renders the "messenger" who goes before Christ; but Theophylact immediately explains the name by John's *ἀγγελικὴ ζωή* ("angelic life") as well as by his announcing (*ἤγγειλε*, from the same root) of Christ — a word-play impossible to preserve in one English word. "Messenger" is kept in the lemma and the etymological gloss ("angelic life") is retained in the comment so the pun remains visible.
[^2]: The clause is corrupt/truncated in the OCR: «Μετὰ δὲ τὸ Χριστὸς, ἵνα μὴ δόξῃ κολακεύειν ἐκεῖνον». Only *Χριστὸς* ("Christ") and "so that he might not seem to flatter that man" (*ἐκεῖνον* = John) are securely legible; the material governed by the neuter article *τὸ* has dropped out (the bare nominative *Χριστὸς* cannot stand after it). The reconstruction "after [John's disciples had departed], Christ [said these things]" follows the Gospel narrative and the standard patristic point (cf. Chrysostom): Christ delayed praising John until John's messengers had gone (Matt. 11:7, "as they departed, Jesus began to say concerning John"), lest his praise appear to be flattery of John. The supplied words are bracketed.

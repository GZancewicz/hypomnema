# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 11:10

> **For this is he concerning whom it is written: Behold, I send my messenger[^1] before your face, who shall prepare your way before you. (Matt. 11:10)**

He was called *a* messenger, both on account of the angelic and almost immaterial life,[^1] and because he announced and proclaimed Christ. And he prepared the way of Christ, both as bearing witness concerning him and as baptizing unto repentance; for after repentance *comes* forgiveness of sins — which forgiveness Christ gives. But after the [...] Christ,[^2] so that he might not seem to flatter that man. And the aforesaid prophecy is Malachi's.[^3]

[^1]: The single Greek word *ἄγγελος* means both "messenger" and "angel," and the comment turns on this double sense. In the lemma it renders the "messenger" who goes before Christ; but Theophylact immediately explains the name by John's *ἀγγελικὴ ζωή* ("angelic life") as well as by his announcing (*ἤγγειλε*, from the same root) of Christ — a word-play impossible to preserve in one English word. "Messenger" is kept in the lemma and the etymological gloss ("angelic life") is retained in the comment so the pun remains visible.
[^2]: The clause is corrupt/truncated in the OCR: «Μετὰ δὲ τὸ Χριστὸς, ἵνα μὴ δόξῃ κολακεύειν ἐκεῖνον». Only "so that he might not seem to flatter that man" is securely legible; the head of the sentence is broken (the bare nominative *Χριστὸς* after the neuter article *τὸ* cannot stand). The probable sense, and the standard patristic point here (cf. Chrysostom), is that John first bore witness to Christ, and only afterward did Christ bear witness to John — "so that he might not seem to flatter *John*" (i.e. lest it look like reciprocal flattery). Translated only as far as the Greek is recoverable; the head is left bracketed rather than invented.
[^3]: The citation "Behold, I send my messenger…" is from Mal. 3:1.

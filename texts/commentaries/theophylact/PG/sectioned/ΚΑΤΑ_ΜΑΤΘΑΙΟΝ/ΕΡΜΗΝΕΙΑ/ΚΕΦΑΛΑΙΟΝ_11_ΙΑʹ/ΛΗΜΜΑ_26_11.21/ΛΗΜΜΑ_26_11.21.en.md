# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 11:21

> **Woe to you, Chorazin, woe to you, Bethsaida. (Matt. 11:21)**

In order that you might understand that those who did not believe were not evil out of nature, but out of *deliberate* choice,[^1] he makes mention of Bethsaida, from which were Andrew, and Peter, and Philip, and the *sons* of Zebedee;[^2] so that the wickedness *is* not out of nature, but out of *deliberate* choice. For if *it were* out of nature, those *men* also would have been evil,[^3]

[^1]: Greek *προαίρεσις* — "deliberate choice, purpose, moral preference, free election of the will." A key term of patristic moral psychology, standing in fixed antithesis here to *φύσις* ("nature"): men are wicked not by their constitution but by the free bent of their will. Rendered "*deliberate* choice" to keep the volitional weight; the supplied "*deliberate*" is italicized as an interpretive amplification not separately present as a word.
[^2]: OCR corruptions, all securely recoverable: *Βηθααῖδὰ* = **Βηθσαϊδά** (Bethsaida); *φίλιππος* = **Φίλιππος** (Philip); *οἱ τοῦ Τεβεδαίου* = **οἱ τοῦ Ζεβεδαίου** ("the *sons* of Zebedee," i.e. James and John). "*sons*" is supplied; the Greek has only the article + genitive, the idiom for "the *sons/people* of Zebedee."
[^3]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at *ἦσαν ἂν κἀκεῖνοι πονηροί* ("those *men* also would have been evil,"); the thought — that the apostles native to Bethsaida would themselves have been wicked, had wickedness sprung from nature — is completed in the following lemma.

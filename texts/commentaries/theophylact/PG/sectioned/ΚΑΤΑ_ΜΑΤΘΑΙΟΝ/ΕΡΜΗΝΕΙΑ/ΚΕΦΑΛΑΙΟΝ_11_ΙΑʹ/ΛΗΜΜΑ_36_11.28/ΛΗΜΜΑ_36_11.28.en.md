# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 36 · Matt. 11:28

> **Come to me, all ye that labour and are burdened, and I will give you rest. (Matt. 11:28)**[^1]

He calls all, not only Jews but also Gentiles. For thou mightest understand as "labouring," on the one hand, the Jews, as going through the heavy legal observances[^2] and labouring in the working of the commandments of the law; but as "burdened," on the other hand, the Gentiles, who were being pressed down by the weight of *their* sins. All these, therefore, Christ gives rest to. For to believe, and to confess, and to be baptized — what kind of labour *is it*? And how *is it* not rest? — when[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Δεῦτε πρός με πάντες οἱ κοπιῶντες καὶ πεφορτισμένοι, κἀγὼ ἀναπαύσω ὑμᾶς». *οἱ κοπιῶντες* = "those toiling/labouring"; *πεφορτισμένοι* = "those laden/burdened" (perfect passive of *φορτίζω*); *ἀναπαύσω* = "I will give rest / cause to rest." Not conformed to the KJV "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
[^2]: Greek «παρατηρήματα νομικὰ βαρέα» — "heavy legal observances," the ritual scruples and prescriptions of the Mosaic law that Theophylact casts as the Jews' "labour."
[^3]: The comment breaks off at «ὅταν» ("when"), the last words of the chapter's exposition in this file; the sentence — evidently continuing to say *when* the reward is so great, or the like — is not completed here.

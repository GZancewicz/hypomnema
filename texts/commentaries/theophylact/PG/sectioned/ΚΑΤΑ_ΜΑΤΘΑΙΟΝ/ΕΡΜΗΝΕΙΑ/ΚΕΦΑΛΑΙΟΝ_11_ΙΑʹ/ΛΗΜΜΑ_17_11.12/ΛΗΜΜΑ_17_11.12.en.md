# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 11:12

*This lemma continues the exposition of Matt. 11:12 begun in the previous lemma; it carries no scripture quotation of its own.*[^1]

This seems to be incoherent,[^2] but it is not. Consider, then: for indeed Christ, having said concerning himself that he is greater than John, impels them toward believing in him — showing that many seize the kingdom of the heavens, that is, faith in him, for which *there is* need of much force. For to leave father and mother, and to despise one's very soul[^3] — of how much force is *this*?[^4]

[^1]: The source file opens with a stray guillemet («) but no scripture text; the words are Theophylact's comment on "the kingdom of the heavens is taken by force, and *the* violent seize it" (Matt. 11:12), quoted in Lemma 16.
[^2]: Greek *ἀνακόλουθον* — "not following, inconsequent, a non-sequitur." The apparent difficulty: how the saying about violence connects with what precedes.
[^3]: Greek *τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτῆς καταφρονῆσαι* — *ψυχή* is "soul" and also "life"; "to despise one's very soul/life itself" (cf. the Gospel demand to hate one's own life). Rendered "one's very soul," with "life" as the alternative.
[^4]: The closing words are OCR-garbled as «πόσης βίας δ ιστίς ι»; they are securely recoverable as **«πόσης βίας ἐστί;»** ("of how much force is *it*? / how great a force does this require?"), a rhetorical question. Rendered accordingly.

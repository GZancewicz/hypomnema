# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 11:12

This seems to be incoherent,[^1] but it is not. Consider, then: for indeed Christ, having said concerning himself that he is greater than John, impels them toward believing in him — showing that many seize the kingdom of the heavens, that is, faith in him, for which there is need of much force. For to leave father and mother, and to despise one's very soul[^2] — of how much force is this?[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἀνακόλουθον* — "not following, inconsequent, a non-sequitur." The apparent difficulty: how the saying about violence connects with what precedes.
[^2]: Greek *τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτῆς καταφρονῆσαι* — *ψυχή* is "soul" and also "life"; "to despise one's very soul/life itself" (cf. the Gospel demand to hate one's own life). Rendered "one's very soul," with "life" as the alternative.
[^3]: The closing words are OCR-garbled as «πόσης βίας δ ιστίς ι»; they are securely recoverable as **«πόσης βίας ἐστί;»** ("of how much force is *it*? / how great a force does this require?"), a rhetorical question. Rendered accordingly.

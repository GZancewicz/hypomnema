# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 11:19

> **The Son of man came eating and drinking, and they say, Aha![^1] a man *who is* a glutton and a wine-drinker, a friend of tax-collectors and sinners. (Matt. 11:19)**

To a dirge, on the one hand, he likens the manner of life[^2] of John; for indeed John displayed much harshness both in words and in deeds. But *that* of Christ, on the other hand, *he likens* to a pipe;[^3] for indeed the Lord, being most gracious,[^4] condescending to all in order that he might gain all these, and proclaiming *the* kingdom as good news, and *displaying* nothing so gloomy[^5] as John *did*.

> **And wisdom was justified from her children. (Matt. 11:19)**[^6]

This he says, that: "Henceforth,[^7] since neither the life of John nor my own pleases you, but you spit upon[^8] all the ways of salvation, I — *the* Wisdom — appear righteous.[^6] For there will no longer be *any* excuse for you, but you will be condemned altogether. For indeed I, on the one hand, have fulfilled all things; but you, on the other hand, having disobeyed, show me *to be* righteous, as *one who has* left out nothing"…[^9]

[^1]: *Ἰού* — an exclamation of surprise or (here) derision: "Aha! / Lo! / Look!" Not the KJV's neutral "Behold"; Theophylact's text preserves the mocking cry of the crowd. *φάγος* = "glutton"; *οἰνοπότης* = "wine-drinker / wine-bibber"; *τελωνῶν* = "tax-collectors / publicans."
[^2]: *διαγωγή* (OCR «ἂιαγωγήν») — "manner/conduct of life."
[^3]: The two similes answer the parable of the children (11:16–17): John's severe way = the dirge (over which they would not "beat their breasts"); Christ's gracious way = the pipe (to which they would not "dance").
[^4]: *χαριέστατος* (superlative of *χαρίεις*) — "most gracious / most winning / full of charm." Greek OCR *Σν* = *ὢν* ("being"); *συγκαταβαίνων* (OCR «συγκαταθαίνων») = "condescending, coming down to *the level of*."
[^5]: *σκυθρωπόν* — "gloomy, sullen, of stern countenance."
[^6]: The lemma «Καὶ ἐδικαιώθη ἡ σοφία ἀπὸ τῶν τέκνων αὐτῆς» (Matt. 11:19b) turns on the root *δικ-*: the verb *ἐδικαιώθη* ("was justified/vindicated") and the adjective *δίκαιος* ("righteous/just") in Theophylact's gloss «δίκαιος φαίνομαι ἐγώ ἡ σοφία» are cognate — Christ, *as* Wisdom, is "justified" / "shown righteous." The phrase *ἀπὸ τῶν τέκνων αὐτῆς* ("from/by her children") is ambiguous (agent "by," or source "from"); Theophylact does not gloss "children" directly but reads the whole as Wisdom's vindication. Rendered "from her children," preserving the ambiguity.
[^7]: *Λοιπόν* (OCR «Αοιπὸν») — "henceforth, for the rest, from now on."
[^8]: *διαπτύετε* — "you spit upon / spurn / reject with contempt."
[^9]: The sentence trails off at a comma («δεικνύετε,») and the following lemma opens a fresh quotation (Matt. 11:20, «Τότε ἤρξατο ὁ Ἰησοῦς ὀνειδίζειν…»). The thought is essentially complete: "you show me *to be* righteous, as *one who has* omitted nothing."

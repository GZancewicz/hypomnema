# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 11:3

> **said to him: Art thou he that cometh, or do we look for another?**[^1]

Not as himself being ignorant of Christ does John ask.[^2] For how, concerning him of whom he was bearing witness that[^3]

[^1]: Greek «εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Σὺ εἶ ὁ ἐρχόμενος, ἢ ἕτερον προσδοκῶμεν;» (Matt. 11:3). This finite verb «εἶπεν» ("said") completes the sentence begun in Lemma 2. «ὁ ἐρχόμενος» is literally "the coming *one*," the expected Messiah; rendered "he that cometh." «προσδοκῶμεν» may be read as present indicative ("do we look for") or subjunctive ("are we to look for"); the present is given here.
[^2]: Greek «Οὐχ ὡς αὐτὸς ἀγνοῶν τὸν Χριστὸν ὁ Ἰωάννης ἐρωτᾷ» — "Not as *one* himself being ignorant of Christ does John ask," i.e. John does not ask because he himself is in doubt. The point is developed at length in the next lemma.
[^3]: The exposition breaks off mid-sentence at «Πῶς γὰρ, ὑπὲρ οὗ ἐμαρτύρει, ὅτι…» ("For how, concerning him of whom he testified, that…"). The sentence is completed at the head of Lemma 4 with the words John bore witness of: «Ἴδε ὁ Ἀμνὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ» ("Behold the Lamb of God," John 1:29) — i.e. "how could he be ignorant of the one of whom he testified 'Behold the Lamb of God'?"

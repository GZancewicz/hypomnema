# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 08 · Matt. 11:5

> **"The things which you see, report to John,"[^1] (cf. Matt. 11:4)**

and assuredly that one, seizing the occasion,[^2] will testify more about me to you. But the poor being evangelized you will understand to be either those who preach the Gospel, that is, the apostles — for they were poor, as fishermen and despised on account of their being unlettered[^3] — or those who give ear to the Gospel and hear about the eternal good things.[^4] And showing to John's disciples that the things which they are pondering do not escape his notice, he says that—[^5]

[^1]: The OCR *Ἀβλέπετε* is securely *Ἃ βλέπετε* ("the things which you see"); this completes the *φησὶν, ὅτι* ("he says that—") left hanging at the close of the previous lemma. Theophylact's clause paraphrases the Lord's command of Matt. 11:4 (*ἃ ἀκούετε καὶ βλέπετε*, "what you hear and see").
[^2]: Greek *ἀφορμῆς δραξόμενος* (OCR *φφορμῆς*), a future-middle participle of *δράσσομαι*, "being about to lay hold of / seizing *the* occasion." *ἀφορμή* = "starting-point, occasion, pretext, resource"; the sense is that John, taking the miracles as his cue, will bear yet fuller witness to Christ before the crowds.
[^3]: Greek *διὰ ἰδιωτείαν* (OCR *ἰδιωπείαν*): *ἰδιωτεία* is the condition of the *ἰδιώτης* — the private, untrained, unlettered man; "want of education, plainness, lack of skill." Rendered "being unlettered"; it denotes their reputation for lacking learning, not moral fault.
[^4]: Theophylact offers two readings of *πτωχοὶ εὐαγγελίζονται*: (a) taking *πτωχοί* as subject-preachers — "*the* poor evangelize [others]," i.e. the apostles; or (b) as recipients — "*the* poor have the Gospel preached to them / give ear to it." The Greek participle *εὐαγγελιζομένους* is here read actively in sense (a) and passively in sense (b); the ambiguity is his very point.
[^5]: The sentence again breaks off at *φησὶν, ὅτι* ("he says that—"); it is completed by the opening of the next lemma, «Μακάριός ἐστιν, ὃς ἐὰν μὴ σκανδαλισθῇ ἐν ἐμοί» (Matt. 11:6).

# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 11:8

> **But what did you go out to see? A man clothed in soft garments? Behold, those who wear the soft things are in the houses of kings. (Matt. 11:8)**

For lest they should be able to say that, having been enslaved to luxury, he afterwards became slack,[^1] he says, No. For his robe, being of hair, shows that he is an enemy of luxury. For if indeed he had been wearing soft things, he would also have been in the houses of kings — but not in the prison — had he wished to live luxuriously. Learn, therefore, that the exact[^2] Christian must not wear soft things.

[^1]: Greek *τρυφῇ δουλεύσας ὕστερον χαῦνος γέγονε* — "having been a slave to luxury he afterwards became *χαῦνος*." *χαῦνος* is literally "porous, spongy, loose," and figuratively "slack, flabby, enervated, softened up"; the sense pairs with *τρυφή* ("soft living, luxury, self-indulgence"). Rendered "slack."
[^2]: Greek *τὸν ἀκριβῆ Χριστιανόν* — *ἀκριβής* = "exact, precise, strict, scrupulous"; i.e. the Christian who holds to the rule with precision. *Μάθει* is imperative, "learn."

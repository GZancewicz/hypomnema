# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 11:25

> **In that time Jesus, answering, said: I acknowledge to you, Father, Lord of heaven and of the earth, that you hid these things from wise and understanding, and revealed them to babes. (Matt. 11:25)**[^1]

What he says is of this sort: "I acknowledge," instead of, "I give thanks to you, O Father," that the Jews who seem to be wise and experienced in the Scriptures disbelieved, but the unlearned and *those who are as* babes[^2] believed and knew the mysteries. And God hid the mysteries from those seeming to be wise, not as begrudging *them* or as being cause of *their* ignorance, but because of their not being worthy — from this very thing, *namely* their thinking themselves to be wise; for he who thinks himself wise, and is confident in his own knowledge, does not even call upon God. And thereafter God, as *one* not being called upon, neither helps him, nor reveals *anything*. And besides, God, rather because of *his* love-of-mankind,[^3] does not reveal the mysteries to the many, in order that they might not be punished the more, as after the[^4]

[^1]: Greek *Ἐξομολογοῦμαί σοι* — Theophylact himself glosses the word: *Ἐξομολογοῦμαι, ἀντὶ τοῦ, Εὐχαριστῶ σοι* ("'I acknowledge,' instead of, 'I give thanks to you'"). The verb *ἐξομολογέομαι* spans "confess, acknowledge openly, give thanks, praise" — hence the gloss is needed, since its more usual sense ("confess") is not what is meant here. Rendered "I acknowledge" in the lemma to preserve the very word the comment must explain, with the "give thanks" sense supplied by Theophylact's own paraphrase. *συνετῶν* ("understanding, intelligent, discerning"); *νηπίοις* ("to babes, infants").
[^2]: Greek *οἱ… ἁμαθεῖς καὶ νήπιοι* — "the unlearned and infantile / *those who are as* babes," picking up the *νηπίοις* of the lemma. *ἁμαθής* is "unlearned, untaught, ignorant." "*those who are as*" supplied to smooth the substantive.
[^3]: Greek *φιλανθρωπία* — "love of mankind, benevolence, kindness," a standing term for the divine philanthropy; rendered literally "love-of-mankind" to keep it visible as the fixed term.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at *ἵνα μὴ πλεῖον κολασθῶσιν, ὡς μετὰ τὸ* — "...in order that they might not be punished the more, as after the—". The clause is incomplete in this folder: the sense being that fuller knowledge, spurned, would only aggravate their punishment (to whom more is given, more is required). The following lemma (31) opens instead with a fresh Gospel citation («Ναὶ, ὁ Πατήρ…»), so the broken thought is not continued there; only what is legible is rendered.

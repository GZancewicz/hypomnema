# Matthew 11 (ΙΑʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 11:21–22

> **For if in Tyre and Sidon had been done the mighty works which were done in you, long ago would they have repented in sackcloth and ashes. Nevertheless I say to you, for Tyre and Sidon it will be more tolerable in *the* day of judgment than for you. (Matt. 11:21–22)**[^1]

He calls the Jews worse than the Tyrians and Sidonians, inasmuch as the Tyrians transgressed the natural law, but the Jews *transgressed* also the Mosaic. And those, indeed, did not see miracles; but these, even having seen *them*, slandered *them*;[^2] and the sackcloth is a symbol of repentance, and to sprinkle ashes and dust upon the head we behold among those who mourn.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἀνεκτότερον ἔσται* ("it will be more tolerable / more endurable"); *πλήν* rendered "nevertheless." The supplied "*the*" before "day of judgment" is italicized (the Greek *ἐν ἡμέρᾳ κρίσεως* is anarthrous).
[^2]: Greek *διέβαλλον*, imperfect of *διαβάλλω* — "to slander, misrepresent, accuse falsely, traduce." The sense is not merely "reject" but that, having seen the miracles, they spoke against them and put a false construction on them (cf. attributing them to Beelzebul). The imperfect ("kept slandering") is rendered with the simple past for English; "*them*" (the miracles) is supplied as object.
[^3]: Greek *τὸ σποδὸν καὶ κόνιν πάττειν κατὰ τῆς κεφαλῆς* — "to strew/sprinkle ashes and dust down upon the head." *πάττειν* is "to sprinkle, strew"; *σποδός* "ashes," *κόνις* "dust." Rendered "to sprinkle ashes and dust upon the head." *ἐπὶ τῶν πενθούντων βλέπομεν*: "we behold *this practice* in the case of those who mourn."

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 21:20

> **the disciples, having seen *it*, marveled, saying: "How did the fig tree wither immediately?"**[^1]

Since the Lord did frequent wonder-workings, all *of them* tending toward beneficence,[^2] but displayed no punitive wonder-working, lest some should think that he is not able to punish, he shows here this power of his also — not having used it against men, but against a tree, as *one who is* benevolent; just as also before this, against the swine. Therefore he withers the tree, in order that he might bring men to their senses. But the disciples marvel, reasonably. For this tree is *the more* moist,[^3] and the more displays the wonder, in that it dried up quickly. And the fig tree signifies also the Synagogue of the Jews, having leaves only, that is, the apparent letter, not the fruit of the spirit. But also every man who has given himself over to the sweetness of the present life is likened to a fig tree; who has no spiritual fruit, that he might give *it* to Jesus when he hungers, but leaves only — the temporary and down-flowing appearance.[^4] This one, therefore, hears *himself* cursed. For "Go," he says, "you cursed, into the fire."[^5] But he is also withered; for as he is fried[^6] in the flame, his tongue is dried up, as also *was* that of the rich man.[^7]

[^1]: Greek «… ἰδόντες οἱ μαθηταὶ ἐθαύμασαν, λέγοντες· Πῶς παραχρῆμα ἐξηράνθη ἡ συκῆ;» This closes the scripture quotation begun in Lemmata 19–20 (Matt. 21:18–20). *παραχρῆμα* = "immediately, at once."
[^2]: Greek OCR *πάσας ρώσας*; read here as *πάσας φερούσας* ("all bringing / tending [toward beneficence]"). The reconstruction is not certain — the participle is corrupt — but the sense ("all his miracles aimed at doing good") is secure from the antithesis with the "punitive" (*κολαστικήν*) miracle that follows.
[^3]: Greek *ὑγρότερόν ἐστι* — "is rather moist / more full of sap." The comparative heightens the marvel: a sap-filled, living tree withering *quickly* is the more astonishing.
[^4]: Greek *τὴν πρόσκαιρον φαντασίαν καὶ καταῤῥέουσαν* — "the temporary appearance, and *one* streaming/flowing away (down)." *φαντασία* here is outward "show / semblance"; *καταῤῥέουσαν* pictures it as running off like water.
[^5]: Cf. Matt. 25:41 — Greek *Πορεύεσθε … οἱ κατηραμένοι, εἰς τὸ πῦρ* ("Go, you cursed, into the fire"). Theophylact paraphrases/abbreviates the saying, tying the "cursed" (*κατηραμένος*) fruitless man to the cursed fig tree.
[^6]: Greek *ἀποτηγανιζομένου* — literally "being fried in a frying-pan (*τήγανον*)," a vivid verb for the torment of the flame.
[^7]: Cf. Luke 16:24 (the rich man of the parable), whose tongue is tormented in the flame.

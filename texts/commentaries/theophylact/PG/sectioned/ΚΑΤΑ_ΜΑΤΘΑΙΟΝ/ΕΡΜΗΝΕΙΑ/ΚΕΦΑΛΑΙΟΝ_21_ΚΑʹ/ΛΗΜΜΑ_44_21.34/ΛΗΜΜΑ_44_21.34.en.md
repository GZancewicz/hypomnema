# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 21:46

> **…seeking to seize him, they feared the crowds, since they held him as a prophet. (Matt. 21:46)**[^1]

Again observe that the crowd, *being* guileless and unfeigned,[^2] follows the truth, while the teachers of the law work evil.[^3] But even until now the Hebrews seek to seize Jesus, yet they do not seize him, nor do they understand *him*. For they shall seize the Antichrist, and shall bow down to that one; but Christ shall not be taken — that is, apprehended — by them.[^4]

[^1]: This lemma opens by completing the sentence begun in lemma 43 (Matt. 21:45); the participle «ζητοῦντες» ("seeking") depends on the «καὶ» there. The folder metadata assigns Matt. 21:34, but the quotation is securely Matt. 21:46 («ζητοῦντες αὐτὸν κρατῆσαι, ἐφοβήθησαν τοὺς ὄχλους, ἐπειδὴ ὡς προφήτην αὐτὸν εἶχον»); the ref is corrected accordingly. The final clause is literally "since they held him as a prophet" (εἶχον, imperfect: "were holding / regarded").
[^2]: Greek *ἄδολος* ("without guile, unadulterated") and *ἄπλαστος* ("unmoulded, unfeigned, artless"). The pair is rendered "guileless and unfeigned"; "*being*" is supplied.
[^3]: Greek *κακουργοῦσιν*, literally "do evil / act as evildoers," here of the scribes' malice; rendered "work evil."
[^4]: The passage turns on a double word-play the English cannot fully carry. First, *κρατῆσαι / κρατοῦσιν* ("to seize / they seize") is used both of physically laying hold of Jesus and, by extension, of grasping him with the mind — hence the pairing with *νοοῦσι* ("they understand"). Second, in the closing clause *οὐ ληφθήσεται, ἤτοι νοηθήσεται* ("shall not be taken, that is, apprehended"): *ληφθήσεται* ("be caught, seized, taken") is glossed by Theophylact himself with *νοηθήσεται* ("be understood, mentally apprehended"). "Apprehended" is chosen for the second verb to keep the seize/comprehend ambiguity visible, since English "apprehend" carries both senses; a flatter rendering would be "shall not be caught, that is, understood."

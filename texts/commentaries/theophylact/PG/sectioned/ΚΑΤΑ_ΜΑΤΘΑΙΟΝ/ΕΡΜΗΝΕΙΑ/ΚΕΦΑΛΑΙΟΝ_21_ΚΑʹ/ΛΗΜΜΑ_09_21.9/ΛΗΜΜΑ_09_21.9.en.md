# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 21:9

> **"For I am the way," he says.**[^1]

For if someone does not strew his own flesh — that is, humble *it* — in the road, that is, remaining in Christ and not being a heretic, the Lord does not ride upon him. But the *word* Hosanna — some, on the one hand, say that it signifies a hymn or a psalm; but others, on the other hand, *say what is* also more accurate: "Save now."[^2] And the Lord is called "he who comes," as *one* expected by the Hebrews to come; for thus also John says:[^3]

[^1]: The lemma is not from Matthew but a citation of Christ's words at John 14:6, «Ἐγὼ γάρ εἰμι ἡ ὁδός» ("For I am the way"), brought in to interpret "they strewed *their garments* in the road" (Matt. 21:8) from the previous lemma: the "road" is Christ.
[^2]: Theophylact reports two explanations of *Ὡσαννά*: that it means a hymn/psalm, or — "more accurately" — that it renders Hebrew *hôšîʿâ-nnâ*, "Save now / Save, we pray" (cf. Ps. 118:25). Greek «Σῶσον δή» ("Save now / Save, indeed").
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at «οὕτω γὰρ καὶ ὁ Ἰωάννης λέγει» ("for thus also John says"); the quotation it introduces — John *the Baptist's* question «Σὺ εἶ ὁ ἐρχόμενος;» ("Are you he who comes?", Matt. 11:3) — stands at the head of the next lemma.

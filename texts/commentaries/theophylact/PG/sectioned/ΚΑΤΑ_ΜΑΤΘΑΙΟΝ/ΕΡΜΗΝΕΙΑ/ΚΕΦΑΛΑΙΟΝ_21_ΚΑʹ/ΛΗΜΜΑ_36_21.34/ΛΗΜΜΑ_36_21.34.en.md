# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 36 · Matt. 21:38-39

> **This is the heir; come, let us kill him. (Matt. 21:38)**

For indeed the Jews, those who *were* saying "This is the Christ," those very men crucified him. And they cast him out of the vineyard, for the Lord was killed outside the city.[^1] But also, since we were saying the vineyard is [?…][^2] the people, *they killed him* outside the vineyard — that is, outside the mind of the guileless people;[^3] *thus* the Pharisees, the wicked husbandmen, killed him.

[^1]: Cf. Heb. 13:12; the Lord suffered "outside the gate." Greek *ἔξω γὰρ τῆς πόλεως ἀπεκτάνθη ὁ Κύριος* ("for the Lord was killed outside the city").
[^2]: The Greek here is badly OCR-corrupted: after «Ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐπεὶ ἀμπελῶνα ἐλέγομεν εἶναι» ("But also, since we were saying the vineyard is…") the source reads an unrecoverable string, «ἐαλ, οοοῖνρτω ο ΘΙοηΙεαὶ, ΠεΙ ξηίοΟΙα. -ρν. πθος π. ρπΝΙΕΕ», before resuming at «τὸν λαόν» ("the people"). The legible frame gives the sense — the vineyard signifies the people — but the intervening clause cannot be reconstructed; it is marked [?] rather than invented.
[^3]: Greek *ἔξω τῆς τοῦ ἀδόλου λαοῦ γνώμης* — "outside the mind/opinion of the guileless people," i.e., apart from the disposition of the simple, uncorrupted folk. Theophylact reads "outside the vineyard" a second time allegorically: the leaders killed Christ away from the true mind of the honest people.

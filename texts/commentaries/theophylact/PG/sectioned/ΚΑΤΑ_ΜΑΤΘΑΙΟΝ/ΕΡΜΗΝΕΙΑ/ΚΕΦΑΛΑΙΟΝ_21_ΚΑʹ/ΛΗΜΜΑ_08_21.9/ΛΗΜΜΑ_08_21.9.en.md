# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 21:9

> **crowds, the ones going before and the ones following, were crying out, saying: Hosanna to the son of David; blessed *is* he who comes in *the* name of *the* Lord; hosanna in the highest. (Matt. 21:9)**[^1]

According to the history, on the one hand, the strewing-under of the garments shows much honour, and the cutting of the branches *is* an indication of festal celebration; but according to the anagogical *sense*, on the other hand, learn that after the apostles have laid down their garments — that is, their virtues — then the Lord sits *upon them*. For if the soul be not adorned with the apostolic virtues, God does not ride upon it. But the ones going before are the prophets *who lived* before the incarnation of Christ; and the ones following *are* the martyrs and teachers *who came* after these things, who strew their garments for Christ — that is, they subject the flesh to the spirit; for the body *is* a garment and covering of the soul. They strewed *them*, therefore, in the road — that is, in Christ.[^2]

[^1]: This lemma opens with the words the section carries; the sentence began at the tail of the previous lemma with «οἱ δὲ» ("And the..."), so the full clause reads "And the crowds, the ones going before and the ones following…" Greek Δαυΐδ is the conventional "David." *Ὡσαννὰ* is left transliterated (as in the Greek) and is explained in the following lemma.
[^2]: Theophylact reads the garment-strewing anagogically: the road is Christ himself (drawn out in the next lemma from John 14:6, "I am the way"), and to "strew garments in the road" is to lay one's virtues, and one's subjected flesh, upon/within Christ. Greek *ἀναγωγή* ("anagogical / spiritual elevation *sense*") is the standard patristic term for the higher, mystical reading opposed to *ἱστορία* ("the history," the literal narrative).

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 21:14

> **And *the* lame and *the* blind came to him in the temple, and he healed them.** (Matt. 21:14)

From the *fact* that he heals the sick[^1] he shows that he is God, and that he does well in casting out with authority, from his house, the unworthy. And this too is made plain: that after the Jews were cast out — those attending to the law and to the animal-slaughterings[^2] — the blind and lame from the nations were taken in, being healed by him.

[^1]: Greek OCR of this sentence is corrupt but securely recoverable: «θεραπεύοι τοί- ἀσθενεῖς» = **θεραπεύει τοὺς ἀσθενεῖς** ("he heals the sick / weak"); «καλῶς ποιεῖς» = **καλῶς ποιεῖ** ("he does well"); «Ανβάλλων μετ᾽ ἐξρυσίᾳς» = **ἐκβάλλων μετ᾽ ἐξουσίας** ("casting out with authority"); «τοὺς ἀναίους» = **τοὺς ἀναξίους** ("the unworthy"). None of these affects the sense.
[^2]: Greek *ζωοσφαγίαις* ("animal-slaughterings / slayings of living creatures"), corresponding to the *ζωοθυσίαι* ("animal-sacrifices") of the previous lemma; the blind and lame "from the nations" (*ἐξ ἐθνῶν*, the Gentiles) received in their place typify the Church replacing the Jews who clung to those sacrifices.

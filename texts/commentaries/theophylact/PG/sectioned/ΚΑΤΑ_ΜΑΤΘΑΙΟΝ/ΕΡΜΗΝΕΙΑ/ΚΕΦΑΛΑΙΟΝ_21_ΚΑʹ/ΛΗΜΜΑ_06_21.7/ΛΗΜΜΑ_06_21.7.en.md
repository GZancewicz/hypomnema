# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 21:7

> **they brought the ass and the colt, and placed upon them their garments, and he sat upon them.** (Matt. 21:7)[^1]

Luke, on the one hand, and Mark said *there was* a beast of burden only; but Matthew, on the other hand, *said* an ass and a colt — not saying things contrary *to each other*; for as the colt was being led,[^2] the mother also followed along *with it*. And he sat upon them — not upon the two beasts of burden, but upon the garments; or *rather*, first, on the one hand, he sat upon the ass, then also upon the colt.[^3] Since he also first rested upon the synagogue of the Jews, then upon the people from the nations.[^4]

[^1]: The Greek: «ἤγαγον τὴν ὄνον καὶ τὸν πῶλον, καὶ ἐπέθηκαν ἐπάνω αὐτῶν τὰ ἱμάτια αὐτῶν, καὶ ἐκάθισεν ἐπάνω αὐτῶν». The final *ἐπάνω αὐτῶν* ("upon them") is grammatically ambiguous — of the animals or of the garments — an ambiguity Theophylact himself takes up and resolves in the exposition below.
[^2]: OCR «ἀνο μένῳ γὰρ τῷ πώλῳ συνηκολούθει καὶ ἡ μήτηρ»; «ἀνομένῳ» is read as *ἀγομένῳ* ("being led"), yielding "for as the colt was being led, the mother also followed along." An alternative restoration would take the participle of the mother rather than the colt, but the sense is unaffected: the two animals travel together, so the two evangelists do not contradict.
[^3]: The disjunction *ἢ … μὲν … εἶτα …* ("or, first … then …") offers a second explanation of the plural "upon them": either it refers to the garments, or Christ sat successively on each animal. "or *rather*" supplies the force of the corrective *ἤ*.
[^4]: *τῷ ἐξ ἐθνῶν λαῷ* — "the people from the nations," i.e. the Gentiles, set against *ἡ τῶν Ἰουδαίων συναγωγή* ("the synagogue of the Jews"); the two seatings figure Christ's resting first on Israel, then on the Gentile Church.

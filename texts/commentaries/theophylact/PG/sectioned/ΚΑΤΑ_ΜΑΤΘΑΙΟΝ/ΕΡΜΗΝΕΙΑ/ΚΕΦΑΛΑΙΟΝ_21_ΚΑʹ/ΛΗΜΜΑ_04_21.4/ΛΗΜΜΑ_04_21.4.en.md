# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 21:4–5

> **Now this whole *thing* came to pass that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophet, saying: Say to the daughter of Sion: Behold, thy king comes to thee meek, and mounted upon an ass and a colt the foal of a beast of burden.** (Matt. 21:4–5)[^1]

He sat upon the ass, there being no other need, except that of fulfilling the prophecy, and that he might show you how one ought to ride in a lowly manner; for not upon a horse, but upon a lowly little ass was he carried. He fulfills, then, the prophecy both according to *the* history and according to *the* anagogical *sense*: according to the history, on the one hand, as having sat upon an ass — the thing seen; but according to the anagogical *sense*, on the other hand, because he also sat upon the colt, *that is*, upon the new and untamed people, the insubordinate *one*.[^2]

And both the ass and the colt were bound with the cords of their own sins. And two *disciples* were sent to loose *them*: Paul, on the one hand, to the nations, but Peter to the circumcision, that is, the Jews.[^3] And even until now, two loose us from *our* sins: the Apostle and the Gospel.[^4] And Christ comes meek; for he came not to judge the world in the first coming,[^5] but to save. But also the other kings of the Hebrews, on the one hand, were rapacious and unjust, whereas Christ, on the other hand, *is* a meek king.

[^1]: The prophetic citation (Zech. 9:9, blended with Isa. 62:11 "Say to the daughter of Sion") appears in the OCR with corruptions: «ἔρχεταί σοι πραθς» for «ἔρχεταί σοι πραΰς» ("comes to thee meek"), and «πραθε» in the manuscript line likewise = *πραΰς*. Greek *πραΰς* ("meek, gentle, mild") is the pivot-word of the whole comment and is rendered "meek" throughout for consistency. *υἱὸν ὑποζυγίου* is literally "son of a beast of burden," here "the foal of a beast of burden."
[^2]: Theophylact reads the two animals on two levels: *καθ᾽ ἱστορίαν* ("according to the history / the literal narrative") and *κατὰ ἀναγωγήν* ("according to the anagogical [upward, spiritual] sense"). The pair is kept visible with "history" / "anagogical sense." The colt (*πῶλος*), unbroken and never ridden, figures the Gentile people, «τοῦ νέου καὶ ἀδαμάστου λαοῦ, τοῦ ἀνυποτάκτου» ("the new and untamed people, the insubordinate one").
[^3]: OCR «Δύο δὲ ἀαεστάλησαν λύσοντες» = «Δύο δὲ ἀπεστάλησαν λύσοντες» ("And two were sent to loose *them*"); the future participle *λύσοντες* expresses purpose. The supplied "*disciples*" renders the sense of the masculine plural (the two sent in v. 1).
[^4]: «ὁ Ἀπόστολος καὶ τὸ Εὐαγγέλιον» — in Byzantine liturgical usage *ὁ Ἀπόστολος* ("the Apostle") denotes the appointed Epistle reading, paired with *τὸ Εὐαγγέλιον* ("the Gospel"). Rendered literally "the Apostle"; the sense is the Epistle-and-Gospel that "loose us from our sins," answering the two animals loosed above.
[^5]: Greek *ἐν τῇ πρώτῃ παρουσίᾳ* ("in the first *parousia*/coming/advent"), set against the implied second coming for judgment. Rendered "in the first coming."

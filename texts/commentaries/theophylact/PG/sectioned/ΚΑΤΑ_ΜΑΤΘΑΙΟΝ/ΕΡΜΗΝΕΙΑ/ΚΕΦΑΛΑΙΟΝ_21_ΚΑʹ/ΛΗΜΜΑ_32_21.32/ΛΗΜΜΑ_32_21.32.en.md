# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 21:32

> **John came in *the* way of righteousness. (Matt. 21:32)**[^1]

That is, in a blameless life; for you cannot say that his life was blameworthy, but nevertheless the harlots obeyed, but you did not; wherefore also they go before you — that is, they enter into the kingdom earlier than you. So that strive you also, that you too may at least enter after those women, having yourselves also believed; but if you should not believe, you shall not enter at all.

And many, even until now, on the one hand promise to God and Father — to be perhaps monks or priests — but after the promise they grow slack. But others, on the other hand, did not undertake a monastic or priestly manner of life, yet live in a monastic and priestly way; so that these are obedient children, who do *it* even though they promised nothing.[^2]

[^1]: Greek «Ἦλθεν Ἰωάννης ἐν ὁδῷ δικαιοσύνης». These words open the lemma but are grammatically the completion of the preceding lemma's *ἐπήγαγεν ὅτι* ("he added that…"), being Christ's own words at Matt. 21:32. The verse-reference is supplied in the blockquote as a locator. *ἐν ὁδῷ δικαιοσύνης* is rendered literally "in *the* way of righteousness"; the article is supplied.
[^2]: Greek *οἱ πράττοντες κἂν μηδὲν ὑπέσχοντο* — literally "the ones doing *it*, even if they promised nothing." The object of "doing" (the father's will / the good) is unexpressed in the Greek and supplied here as "*it*."

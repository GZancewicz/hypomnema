# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 38 · Matt. 21:41

> **They say to him: Wicked *men*, wickedly will he destroy them, and will let out the vineyard to other farmers, who will render to him the fruits in their seasons. (Matt. 21:41)**[^1]

"When he comes" — when? Is it, then, at the second coming? This too, on the one hand, seems to be understood *thus*; but it is better to be understood in this way: the lord of the vineyard *is* God and Father, who sent his Son, the very one who was also murdered by them. When therefore this *one* comes — that is, when he looks upon[^2] the lawlessness which the Jews committed — then he will wickedly destroy the wicked, having sent upon *them* the Roman armies. And his vineyard — that is, the people — he will let out to other farmers, that is, to apostles and teachers.

But understand *as* the vineyard also the divine Scriptures, in which, on the one hand, the hedge is the letter; but the winepress that was dug *is* the depth of the spirit; and the tower *is* theology, being lifted up and most lofty.[^3] These Scriptures, therefore, on the one hand, wicked farmers had formerly, the Pharisees; but God let these out to us, who cultivate them well. For those *men* also killed the Lord outside the vineyard, that is, outside *of* the things which the Scriptures said.

[^1]: The saying turns on a paronomasia impossible to reproduce cleanly in English: **«κακοὺς κακῶς ἀπολέσει αὐτούς»** — accusative adjective *κακούς* ("wicked *men*," the object) chiming with the adverb *κακῶς* ("wickedly / badly"), literally "*the* wicked, wickedly will he destroy them." The resumptive *αὐτούς* ("them") doubles the object. Rendered to keep the wicked/wickedly echo visible.
[^2]: OCR *ἐπιλέψῃ* is securely *ἐπιβλέψῃ* ("look upon / regard"); reconstruction certain, noted for consistency with the other OCR corrections in this passage (e.g. *Τομαῖκά* = *Ῥωμαϊκά*, "Roman"; *ποεύματος* = *πνεύματος*, "spirit").
[^3]: The three allegorized features are those of the parable's vineyard (Matt. 21:33, after Isa. 5:1–2): *φραγμός* the "hedge / fence" = the letter (*τὸ γράμμα*); *ληνὸς ὀρυχθείς* the "winepress dug out" = the depth of the spirit (*τὸ βάθος τοῦ πνεύματος*); *πύργος* the "tower" = *ἡ θεολογία*, the doctrine of God, called *διῃρμένη καὶ ὑψηλοτάτη* ("raised up and most lofty"). Theophylact reads the vineyard doubly: first as Israel/the people, then as the Scriptures themselves.

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 21:22

> **all things, whatsoever you ask in prayer, believing, you shall receive.**[^1]

Great *is* the promise of Christ to the disciples — the being able to move mountains, if we do not doubt, that is, hesitate.[^2] For whatsoever things we ask, believing without hesitation in the power of God, we shall receive. "Yes," someone says, "even if I should ask something unprofitable,[^3] but believe foolishly that God will give me this, shall I then receive that unprofitable *thing* also? And how *is* God benevolent, if indeed he fulfills my unprofitable request?" Hear, therefore. First,[^4] on the one hand, that when you hear "faith," you ought to understand not the foolish *faith*, but the real *one*; and *to understand* that prayer, the one asking for the profitable things, as the Lord handed *it* down, saying:

[^1]: Greek «… πάντα ὅσα ἂν αἰτήσητε ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ, πιστεύοντες, λήψεσθε.» This closes the saying begun in Lemma 22 (Matt. 21:22).
[^2]: Greek *ἐὰν μὴ διακριθῶμεν, τουτέστι, διστάσωμεν* — "if we do not *διακριθῶμεν*, that is, hesitate/waver (*διστάσωμεν*)." Theophylact glosses the ambiguous *διακρίνομαι* (see Lemma 22) with the plainer *διστάζω* ("hesitate, doubt"); in Lemma 24 he turns instead to its "be separated" sense.
[^3]: Greek *ἀσύμφορον* — "disadvantageous, unprofitable, not conducive *to one's good*"; recurring throughout the passage as the opposite of *τὰ συμφέροντα* ("the profitable / beneficial things").
[^4]: Greek *Πρῶτον μὲν* — "First, on the one hand"; the *μὲν* sets up a first point, its answering member left implicit as the argument continues into Lemma 24.

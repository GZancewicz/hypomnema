# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 21:13

> **…he says to them: It is written: My house shall be called a house of prayer; but you have made it a den of robbers.**[^1] (Matt. 21:13)

As master of the house — which is the temple — he cast out those selling, showing that the things of the Father are also his.[^2] And this he did, at once, on the one hand, caring for the good order of the temple, and, on the other hand, at the same time signifying also the transferal of the sacrifices;[^3] for by the very acts through which he cast out the oxen and the doves, he foreannounced that there is no longer need of animal-sacrifices or slaughter-victims, but of prayer. "For my house," he says, "*is* a house of prayer, but you made it a den of robbers"; for in the dens of robbers occur slaughters and sheddings of blood. Or *else* it was also on account of those trafficking, and buying and selling, that he called the temple a den of robbers; for the love of gain is a robber-like passion.

And "money-changers" (*κολλυβισταί*) are those called among us "table-men" (*τραπεζῖται*, bankers);[^4] for a *κόλλυβος* is a kind of paltry coin, just as we, perhaps, have the obols or the small silver-pieces. And doves are sold also by those who sell the ranks in the Churches; for the grace of the Spirit, which is a dove, these *men* sell.[^5] Whence they are cast out from the temple above and *from* the *temple* below; for they are unworthy to exercise the priesthood.

But see you also, lest ever you make the temple of God — that is, your own understanding — a den of robbers, that is, of the demons. And it will become a den, if we have thoughts that are matter-loving,[^6] selling and buying and money-loving, gathering together even the very *κόλλυβα*, that is, the paltry coins; and if we also sell and buy up the doves — that is, if there be in us any spiritual doctrine and any such thought *of that kind* — we have made ourselves a den of robbers.

[^1]: The Gospel words continue the sentence begun in the preceding lemma (Matt. 21:12). *σπήλαιον λῃστῶν* rendered "den of robbers"; *λῃστής* is properly a brigand/plunderer rather than a mere thief (*κλέπτης*), and the distinction is exactly what Theophylact draws on below ("the love of gain is a robber-like passion," and the bloodshed done in robbers' dens).
[^2]: Greek here OCR-garbled as «Ὡς δεσπότης τοῦ οἴκου ἄ ἐστι τοῦ ἱεροῦ»; read as **ὃς / ὅ ἐστι τοῦ ἱεροῦ** ("*the house* which is the temple"). The point is that Jesus acts as owner of the house, the temple being the Father's, and so demonstrates that what is the Father's is also his own.
[^3]: Greek *μετάθεσιν τῶν θυσιῶν* — the "transposition / removal / change" of the sacrifices, i.e. the abolition of the Old-Covenant blood-offerings in favor of prayer. The doubled *ἅμα μὲν … ἅμα δὲ* ("at once … and at the same time") is kept explicit.
[^4]: Theophylact glosses the Gospel's rarer word by the everyday one of his own day: *κολλυβισταί* = *τραπεζῖται*. Both are retained in Greek since he is discussing them precisely as words.
[^5]: The dove-sellers are read allegorically as those guilty of simony — selling ecclesiastical office (*βαθμούς*, "ranks/degrees"), and thereby "selling" the grace of the Holy Spirit, whose emblem is the dove (cf. Matt. 3:16).
[^6]: Greek OCR «φιλοθλους», read as **φιλοΰλους** (*φιλόϋλος*, "matter-loving / fond of *material* things"); the reconstruction fits the surrounding chain "buying and selling and money-loving (*φιλοχρημάτους*)." The pun on *κόλλυβα* (the "paltry coins" of the money-changers, now the petty material calculations of the soul) is carried over into the moral application, so the Greek word is kept.

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 21:9

> **"Are you he who comes?"[^1]**

*This is* instead of, "the one expected to come." And in another way also he is called "he who comes," inasmuch as his second coming is expected day by day; it is necessary, therefore, that each one always expect the consummation, and *expect* the Lord *as* ever coming, and prepare himself. And...[^2]

[^1]: The lemma continues from the previous section's «οὕτω γὰρ καὶ ὁ Ἰωάννης λέγει» ("for thus also John says"): the quoted words «Σὺ εἶ ὁ ἐρχόμενος;» are John *the Baptist's* question to Jesus (Matt. 11:3 / Luke 7:19), "Are you he who comes?" — i.e. the awaited Messiah. The Greek is unpunctuated and could also be read as a statement, "You are he who comes."
[^2]: The section closes with a bare «Καὶ» ("And"), which begins the next scripture lemma, «Καὶ εἰσελθόντος αὐτοῦ εἰς Ἱεροσόλυμα…» ("And when he had entered into Jerusalem…", Matt. 21:10).

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 33 · Matt. 21:33-39

> **Hear another parable. There was a man, a householder, who planted a vineyard, and put a fence around it, and dug a winepress in it, and built a tower, and let it out to husbandmen, and went abroad. (Matt. 21:33)**

He brings another parable upon them, showing that even *when* deemed worthy of countless *acts of* care, they were not made better. For the man, the householder, *is* the Lord, who on account of his love-of-mankind is addressed as "man." And the vineyard *is* the people of the Jews, planted by God in the land of the promise; for, it says, "Having brought *them* in, plant them in your holy mountain."[^1] And the fence *is* the law, not permitting them to mingle with the nations; or *else* the holy angels who were guarding Israel. The winepress, the altar. The tower, the temple. And the husbandmen *are* the teachers of the people, Pharisees and Scribes. And the householder God went abroad when he no longer spoke to them in a pillar of cloud; or *else* the going-abroad of God *is* his long-suffering. For God seems both to slumber and to go abroad in *his* being long-suffering, and in not demanding the reckonings for wrongdoings hard upon *their* heels.[^2]

> **But when the season of the fruits drew near, he sent his servants to the husbandmen, to receive his fruits. And the husbandmen, having taken his servants, one they beat, but another they stoned.[^3] Again he sent other servants more than the first, and they did to them likewise. But afterward he sent to them his son, saying, They will respect my son. But the husbandmen, having seen the son, said among themselves, This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and let us seize his inheritance. And having taken him, they cast *him* out of the vineyard, and killed *him*. (Matt. 21:34-39)**

[^1]: Greek «Εἰσαγαγὼν καταφύτευσον αὐτοὺς εἰς ὄρος ἁγιόν σου» — cf. the Song at the Sea, Exod. 15:17 LXX. Rendered from Theophylact's wording as a locator, not conformed to an English Bible.
[^2]: Greek *μὴ παρὰ πόδας τῶν ἀδικημάτων ἀπαιτεῖν τὰς εὐθύνας*, literally "not to demand the reckonings alongside the feet of the wrongdoings" — i.e., not exacting the account immediately, on the heels of the offenses. "Hard upon *their* heels" renders the idiom *παρὰ πόδας*.
[^3]: The OCR of the lemma gives only two verbs, «ὃν μὲν ἔδειραν, ὃν δὲ ἐλιθοβόλησαν» ("one they beat, but another they stoned"), whereas the common Gospel text (and Theophylact's own exposition in the next lemma) has three: «ὃν μὲν ἔδειραν, ὃν δὲ ἀπέκτειναν, ὃν δὲ ἐλιθοβόλησαν» ("one they beat, another they killed, another they stoned," Matt. 21:35). The middle clause has evidently dropped out in this witness; translated here as it stands on the page, per the rule of following the text.

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 21:22

> **"Do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one,"**[^1]

and whatever words *there are* of such a prayer. Then consider also the wording of the "should you doubt."[^2] For the one *who is* united to God, and *who is* one toward him, and *who is* not divided and separated, how would he ask for the unprofitable? So that, if we are inseparable and undivided from God, then we shall ask for the profitable things and shall receive *them*.

[^1]: Greek «Μὴ εἰσενέγκῃς ἡμᾶς εἰς πειρασμὸν, ἀλλὰ ῥῦσαι ἡμᾶς ἀπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ» — a clause of the Lord's Prayer (Matt. 6:13), cited here by Theophylact as his example of "the prayer asking for the profitable things" (from Lemma 23). *ἀπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ* is translated "from the evil one" (masculine/personal), though it may also be read as neuter, "from evil."
[^2]: Greek *ὅρα καὶ τὴν λέξιν τοῦ, Διακριθῆτε* — "consider also the word/wording of *διακριθῆτε*" (from the saying in Lemma 22). Here Theophylact draws out the sense of *διακρίνομαι* he had left aside earlier: not "doubt" (so Lemma 23) but "be divided / separated." His argument turns on the pun — *ὁ διακριθεὶς καὶ χωρισθείς* is "the one divided and separated" *from God* — which no single English word preserves; hence "doubt" in the lemma but "divided/separated" in the exposition.

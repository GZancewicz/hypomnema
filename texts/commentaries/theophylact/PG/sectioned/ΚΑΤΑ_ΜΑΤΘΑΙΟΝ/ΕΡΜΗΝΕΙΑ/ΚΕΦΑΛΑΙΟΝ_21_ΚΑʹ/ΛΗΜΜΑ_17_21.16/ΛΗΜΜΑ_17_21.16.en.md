# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 21:16

> **…said to him: Do you hear what these are saying? But Jesus says to them: Yes. Have you never read, that Out of the mouth of infants and nursing ones you have prepared praise?**[^1] (Matt. 21:16)

The Pharisees, having seen the children offering to Christ the hymn of David — which the prophet seems to dedicate to God[^2] — are cut through with envy, and they revile him as tolerating that the things of God be said about him; but he, rather confirming *it*, says: "Yes." "For so far," he says, "am I from silencing those saying these things to me, that I even bring forward the prophet as witness, and prove you *to be* either ignorant or envious. For have you not read, that *Out of the mouth of infants and nursing ones you have prepared praise*?"

And "you have prepared" (*κατηρτίσω*) signifies, in place of *this*: "I displayed a complete and perfect hymn," even though those *children* seem imperfect in age.[^3] For the things which they said were not their own,[^4] but, having given only their mouth to the Spirit, they were instruments of that *Spirit*. For this reason, therefore, he says "Out of the mouth of infants," hinting that the words were not of their own understanding, but of the mouth alone, moved by divine grace. And this signified that he would be blessed also from the nations — the infantile and foolish. And for the apostles too this was a consolation: that a word would be given also to them, even though they be simple.[^5] And you too, if you are an infant in wickedness, being guileless, and suck the spiritual milk — the divine oracles — then you will become worthy to hymn God.

[^1]: The Gospel words continue the sentence begun in the preceding lemma (Matt. 21:15). The quoted testimony is Ps. 8:2 (LXX 8:3): *ἐκ στόματος νηπίων καὶ θηλαζόντων κατηρτίσω αἶνον*. Theophylact quotes the Psalm as it stands in Matthew's citation ("praise," *αἶνον*), where the Hebrew has "strength"; translated per his text.
[^2]: I.e. the psalm (Ps. 8) which the prophet-*psalmist* David appears to compose in honor of God is now being applied by the children to Christ — and Christ accepts it, thereby claiming to be God. Greek OCR «ὃν δοκεῖ ὁ προφήτης Θεῷ ἀναθεῖναι»; supplied *τῷ* before Θεῷ.
[^3]: Greek *κατηρτίσω* (aorist of *καταρτίζω*, "to make complete, fit out, perfect"); Theophylact glosses it as *ἄρτιον καὶ τέλειον ἐδείξω ὕμνον* — "I showed forth a hymn complete and perfect." The point turns on the word: the praise God "perfected" out of infant mouths is itself perfect, however imperfect (*ἀτελεῖς*) the children's age. The wordplay *ἄρτιον / κατηρτίσω* is kept visible via the retained Greek.
[^4]: Greek OCR here is corrupt: «Οὐ γὰρ ἐκεῖ ναι Γλανον ἃ ἕλεγον». Read as **οὐ γὰρ ἐκείνων ἦν ἃ ἔλεγον** ("for the things which they said were not theirs / not their own"), which the following clause requires — the children merely lent their mouths to the Spirit and were its instruments. The reconstruction is offered for sense; the corrupt word «Γλανον» is not securely recoverable, but the drift of the sentence is not in doubt.
[^5]: Greek *ἀφελεῖς* ("plain, simple, unlearned, guileless"); the apostles, like the children, were unsophisticated, yet a *word* (utterance, discourse) would be granted them by the same grace. Greek OCR «θήσεται» read as **δοθήσεται** ("shall be given").

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 21:11

> **crowds said: This is Jesus the prophet, the one from Nazareth of Galilee. (Matt. 21:11)**[^1]

The crowds, being guileless and sincere,[^2] neither envied Christ, nor again did they have the fitting opinion concerning him. Wherefore also now they call him a prophet. But perhaps, since they say *it* with the article — "the prophet" — it can be understood that this is the expected prophet whom Moses spoke of.[^3] For they did not say, "This is a prophet," but, "The prophet" — that is, that hoped-for one.

[^1]: The lemma continues from the previous section's «Οἱ δὲ» ("And the..."); the full clause reads "And the crowds said: This is Jesus the prophet, the one from Nazareth of Galilee."
[^2]: Greek OCR «ἄδολοι ὄντες καὶ καλαστοι»; *ἄδολοι* = "guileless / without deceit" is secure. The corrupt «καλαστοι» is best read as **ἄπλαστοι** ("unaffected / sincere / artless"), the customary pairing with *ἄδολοι*; rendered "sincere" and flagged as a reconstruction.
[^3]: The OCR here is garbled: «ὃν εἰπε Μωγὰρ εἶπον, Οὗτός ἐστι προφήτης». It is reconstructed as «ὃν εἶπε Μωϋσῆς. Οὐ γὰρ εἶπον, Οὗτός ἐστι προφήτης» — "whom Moses spoke of. For they did not say, 'This is a prophet.'" The reference is to the prophet like Moses of Deut. 18:15; the negative *οὐ* ("not") has dropped out of the OCR but is required by the sense and by the antithesis that follows ("but, 'The prophet'"). Greek *ὁ προσδοκώμενος / ὁ ἐλπιζόμενος* = "the expected one / the hoped-for one."

# Matthew 21 (ΚΑʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 21:42–44

> **You are a Samaritan and have a demon.**[^1]

But he himself, having risen from *the* dead, was set *as the* head of *the* corner — that is, he became *the* head of the Church, joining the Jews and the Gentiles into one faith. For just as the stone that makes the corner in the building holds together the wall on *this* side and *that* side, so also Christ bound all together into one faith. But this corner is marvelous, and came about from *the* Lord. For the Church that holds us together and unites *us* in the faith came about from *the* Lord, and is worthy of much wonder, on account of *its* being built well. And in another way too it is marvelous, because by the miracles[^2] the word of Christ was confirmed and made strong; so that the constitution of the Church is marvelous.

The kingdom of God, therefore, was taken away from the Jews — that is, the intimacy toward God[^3] — and was given to those who believed. For those who stumble against the stone and are made to stumble at Christ will, on the one hand, be shattered also at the second coming; but even from here already they have been winnowed by him — that is, they were scattered everywhere over the earth, just as we now see the wretched Jews. This, therefore, is *the meaning of* "it will winnow": that is, he will scatter them,[^4]

[^1]: The builders' words, supplying the *λέγοντες* ("saying:") of the previous lemma; but the quotation is not from Matthew — it is **John 8:48**, «Σαμαρείτης εἶ καὶ δαιμόνιον ἔχεις» ("You are a Samaritan and have a demon"), the Jews' insult to Jesus, which Theophylact adduces as a sample of how "the builders" rejected the stone as useless.
[^2]: Greek *θαύμασιν* — "wonders / miracles"; the same root as *θαυμαστή* ("marvelous"), so the sentence plays on the Church being "marvelous" (*θαυμαστή*) partly because it was confirmed by "marvels" (*θαύματα*). The wordplay is only partly reproducible in English.
[^3]: Greek *ἡ οἰκειότης ἡ πρὸς Θεόν* — "the closeness / kinship / familiarity toward God," the condition of being God's own household (*οἰκεῖοι*); rendered "intimacy toward God."
[^4]: The exposition breaks off at *διασπερεῖ αὐτούς,* ("he will scatter them,"); it continues into the next lemma, which opens the following pericope («Καὶ ἀκούσαντες οἱ ἀρχιερεῖς…», Matt. 21:45). Theophylact's *διασπερεῖ* ("scatter") glosses the *λικμήσει* ("winnow") of Matt. 21:44 (see the note on the previous lemma).

# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 20:34

> **And Jesus, having been moved with compassion, touched their eyes, and immediately their eyes recovered sight, and they followed him. (Matt. 20:34)**

The blind men, having come to know the Lord from the report,[^1] seized the opportunity, on the one hand having heard that he was passing along the road; but on the other hand having believed that Jesus, the one from the seed of David according to the flesh, was able to heal them; and, as *being* exceedingly ardent regarding the faith, they did not keep silent even *when* being silenced,[^2] but cried out even more. Whence he does not even ask them whether they have faith, but what they wish, so that no one might suppose that to *those* wishing *for* one thing he gives another.[^3] Therefore he shows[^4] that they were crying out not for the sake of receiving money, but *for the sake* of being healed.

And having touched *them* he heals, so that we might learn that every member of his holy flesh was life-giving and a member of God. But if Luke and Mark speak of one blind man, this is no discrepancy;[^5] for those *evangelists* made mention of the more conspicuous one. And besides, Luke says that before going away into Jericho he healed a blind man, but Mark, after going out from Jericho; Matthew therefore, being fond of brevity,[^6] comprehended the two together in the same *place*.

But understand *as* the blind men those from the nations, who were healed in passing; for Christ did not come primarily[^7] for the sake of the Gentiles, but for the sake of those from Israel. And just as the blind men learned about Jesus from hearing, so also the Gentiles *came to believe* from the hearing of faith.[^8] And those silencing the blind men, that they should not cry out the name of Jesus, are the persecuting tyrants, who attempted to silence the Church; but she confessed the name of Christ the more — wherefore she was both healed and sees the light of the truth more distinctly, and follows Christ, imitating him according to *her* manner of life.

[^1]: Greek «Ἀπὸ τῆς φήμης» — "from the report / rumor / fame," the account of Jesus that had spread abroad.
[^2]: Greek «οὐδὲ ἐπιστομιζόμενοι ἐσιώπων»; the verb «ἐπιστομίζω» is literally "to put *something* on the mouth," hence "to muzzle, to stop the mouth, to silence." The same verb recurs below for the persecutors who «ἐπειρῶντο ἐπιστομίζειν τὴν Ἐκκλησίαν» ("attempted to silence the Church"); rendered "silence" throughout to keep the link visible.
[^3]: Greek «ἵνα μή τις νομίσῃ ὅτι ἄλλα βουλομένοις ἄλλα δίδωσι» — literally "that no one might think that to *those* wishing other things he gives other things," i.e. that he grants people something different from what they actually wanted. He asks them to name their desire so that his gift is seen to match their request.
[^4]: OCR «Δείανιυσιν», securely = **«Δείκνυσιν»** ("he shows / demonstrates"). Likewise in the same sentence OCR «ἱαθῆναι» = **«ἰαθῆναι»** ("to be healed").
[^5]: Greek «οὐδὲν τοῦτο διαφωνεῖ» — "this is in no way discordant / in no way disagrees." The reference is to Luke 18:35–43 and Mark 10:46–52, which each name a single blind man (Mark names Bartimaeus), whereas Matthew reports two.
[^6]: Greek «φιλοσύντομος ὢν» — "being fond of conciseness / a lover of brevity."
[^7]: Greek «προηγουμένως» — "primarily / principally / in the first instance"; the sense is that the Gentiles were not the first object of Christ's mission, which was directed first to Israel (cf. Matt. 15:24).
[^8]: Greek «ἐξ ἀκοῆς πίστεως», an echo of Rom. 10:17 and Gal. 3:2, 5; the phrase «ἀκοὴ πίστεως» is itself ambiguous ("the hearing of faith" / "the message that is heard, *which is* faith"). The clause is elliptical, with the verb supplied from the parallel «ἔμαθον» ("learned"); I supply "*came to believe*" to match «οἱ ἐθνικοὶ» ("the Gentiles") with the preceding «οἱ τυφλοὶ … ἔμαθον».

# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 20:22

> **But Jesus answering said: You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup which I am about to drink? or to be baptized *with* the baptism *with* which I am baptized? They say to him: We are able. (Matt. 20:22)**

Leaving the mother aside, he converses with the sons, so that he might show that he was not ignorant that it was they *who* put the mother forward, and he says to them: You do not know what you are asking; how great it is and *how it* astounds even the powers of the angels. Then, leading them away from such reasonings, he rather urges *them* on toward dangers. And he asks, not being ignorant, but that he might compel *them* to lay bare the sore[^1] through the answer, and that they might be zealous to fulfill the promise. For this is what he says: Since no one is able to become a partaker of my kingdom, unless he becomes also a partaker of my sufferings, tell me, if you are able to suffer the like *things*.

For by "cup" he means martyrdom and *his* own death, showing at once, on the one hand, that it is easy, like drinking a cup, and that we too henceforth must run to this as to something easy; and, on the other hand, making clear that he himself also gladly welcomes death. And just as the one who drinks a cup immediately grows drowsy, being weighed down, so also the one who drinks the cup of martyrdom is carried down into the sleep of death. And he names his death also "baptism," as being cleansing of us all; but they promised, not knowing what they were saying, but only, for the sake of attaining what they wished, promising everything readily.

[^1]: Greek *φανερῶσαι τὸ ἕλκος* — "to make manifest the sore / ulcer / wound." The medical metaphor: the disciples' ambition is a hidden ulcer, and the Lord's question forces it into the open so it can be healed.

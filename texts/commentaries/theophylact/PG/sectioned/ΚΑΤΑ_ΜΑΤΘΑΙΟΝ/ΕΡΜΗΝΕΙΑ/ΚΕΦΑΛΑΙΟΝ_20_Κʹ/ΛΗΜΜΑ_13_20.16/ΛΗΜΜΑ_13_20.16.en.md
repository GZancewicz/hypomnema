# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 20:16

> **Thus shall the last be first, and the first last. For many are called, but few chosen. (Matt. 20:16)**

Evening[^1] *is* the consummation; at all events, in the consummation all receive a denarius apiece, which is the grace of the Holy Spirit, refashioning the man into the *state* according to *the* image, and making *him* a partaker of *the* divine nature.[^2] But those before the sojourning of Christ laboured more, inasmuch as[^3] death had not yet then been abolished, nor had the devil been crushed, but sin was still alive; but we, having been justified by the grace of Christ through baptism, receive power to conquer our adversary, *who has* already been cast down and put to death by Christ. But also, according to the former interpretation, the younger *men*, having believed, have more toil than those who came *to Him* in old age; for the young *man*, on the one hand, bears the weight of anger and the burning heat of desire, warring against the passions; but the old *man*, on the other hand, comes to be in calm. But nevertheless all are deemed worthy of one gift of the Holy Spirit. Therefore the parable teaches us that even in old age it is possible to repent and to attain the kingdom; for this is the eleventh hour.

But will not the saints envy those who received equal *things*? Away *with the thought*! but this is shown from this, that so many and so great are the good *things* given to the righteous, that they are not even able to stir up [?envy].[^4]

[^1]: Greek *ὀψία* ("evening, late *hour*"), picking up the parable's "when evening had come" (Matt. 20:8); Theophylact allegorizes it as *ἡ συντέλεια*, "the consummation *of the age*."
[^2]: The clause fuses Gen. 1:26 (*κατ᾽ εἰκόνα*, "according to the image") with 2 Pet. 1:4 (*θείας κοινωνοὶ φύσεως*, "partakers of *the* divine nature"). Greek *ἀναμορφοῦσα … εἰς τὸ κατ᾽ εἰκόνα* — literally "re-forming *him* into the *that-which-is* according to the image"; the supplied "*state*" renders the bare neuter article τὸ.
[^3]: Greek OCR *καθ᾽ Α*, read as *καθὸ* / *καθ᾽ ὅ* ("inasmuch as, in that"); low-stakes reconstruction from context.
[^4]: The clause is OCR-garbled as «ῶατε ναὶ οθένον κινῇσαι δύνανται»; read as **«ὥστε οὐδὲ φθόνον κινῆσαι δύνανται»** ("so that they are not even able to stir up envy") — i.e. the goods given to the righteous are so great that they leave no room for envy, which directly answers the preceding question "will not the saints envy?". The reading of *φθόνον* ("envy") is probable but not certain; flagged accordingly.

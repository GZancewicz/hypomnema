# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 20:18–21

> **we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man shall be delivered up to the chief priests and Scribes, and they shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him up to the Gentiles to mock, and to scourge, and to crucify, and on the third day he shall rise again. Then there came to him the mother of the sons of Zebedee with her sons, worshipping and asking something from him. And he said to her: What do you wish? She says to him: Say that these my two sons may sit, one at your right hand and one at your left, in your kingdom. (Matt. 20:18–21)**

The *sons* of Zebedee supposed that, if the Lord went off to Jerusalem, he would reign *with* a perceptible kingdom;[^1] for they heard him continually saying, "We are going up to Jerusalem"; therefore they experienced something human, and they persuade their mother to approach, they themselves being wary of approaching openly; for they too approached secretly, as Mark says; for he states that James and John come forward to him — instead of the[^2]

[^1]: Greek *βασιλεύσει αἰσθητὴν βασιλείαν* — a cognate accusative, "he will reign a perceptible kingdom," i.e. rule a kingdom perceptible to the senses (an earthly, political one). The adjective *αἰσθητός* ("perceptible, sensible") stands in the commentary's habitual contrast with *νοητός* ("intelligible") / spiritual.
[^2]: The lemma breaks off at *ἀντὶ τοῦ* ("instead of the"). The point is that in Mark's account (Mark 10:35) James and John themselves make the request, whereas Matthew has the mother make it — Theophylact is about to say that Matthew names the mother "instead of" naming the two sons directly. The sentence is not completed in the source before the next scripture lemma begins.

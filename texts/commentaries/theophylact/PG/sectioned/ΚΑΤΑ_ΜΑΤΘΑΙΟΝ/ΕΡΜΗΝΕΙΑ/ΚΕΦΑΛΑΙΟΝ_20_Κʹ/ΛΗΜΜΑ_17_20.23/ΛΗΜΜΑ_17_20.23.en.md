# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 20:23

> **And he says to them: My cup on the one hand you shall drink, and *with* the baptism *with* which I am baptized you shall be baptized; but to sit at my right hand and at my left, is not mine to give, but *is for those* for whom it has been prepared by my Father. (Matt. 20:23)**

You shall indeed bear witness[^1] — I know this, and it altogether came to pass. For Herod, on the one hand, killed James, but Trajan, on the other hand, condemned John *while he was* bearing witness to the Word of the truth.[^2] But "to sit is not mine to give, but *is for those* for whom it has been prepared" — that is, if anyone be found who, together with being martyred, also has all the rest of virtue, that one shall receive the gift. For it has been prepared for that one who has laboured, just as also the crowns are prepared for those who contend. Just as, therefore, when a foot-race is set forth, and the king is presiding over the games, if someone should come forward who has in no way contended, and should say: Give me the crown, O president of the games — to him who has not contended that one would say: It is not mine to give the crown by favour, but *to him* for whom it has been prepared, that is, to the one who has run and conquered — so indeed here also Christ says that: I am not able to give you the seat at my right hand by favour; for it belongs to others, to those who have laboured, and for them it has been prepared.

You will ask, therefore: Are then some *people* going to sit *there* after all? But you will learn that no one will sit there. For this belongs to the divine essence alone. For to which of the angels did he say:[^3]

[^1]: Greek *μαρτυρήσετε*, from *μαρτυρέω* — both "to bear witness / testify" and, in the developed Christian sense, "to be martyred." Theophylact plays on both senses: the disciples will testify to the truth and will in fact die for it. Rendered "bear witness" to keep the primary sense, but the martyrdoms named next are the point.
[^2]: On James, cf. Acts 12:2 (killed by Herod Agrippa). The tradition that John was condemned under Trajan reflects the account of John's long life and exile; Theophylact treats John's confession under persecution as a "witness" (martyrdom) even though he was not put to death.
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at "did he say:"; it is completed at the head of Lemma 18 with the citation «Sit at my right hand?» (Ps. 110:1), the point being that such session belongs to the Son alone, said of no angel.

# Matthew 20 (Κʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 20:27-28

> **to be first, shall be your slave; just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life *as* a ransom in exchange for[^1] many. (Matt. 20:27-28)**

When the ten saw the two rebuked by Christ, then they too are indignant, and showed that they also had been vexed[^2] at the honor of these *two*. For they were still imperfect; for the ones, on the one hand, were rising up against the ten,[^3] but the others, on the other hand, envied the two. Since therefore the ten, having heard, were thrown into confusion, he calls *them* to *him*, soothing them by the calling before the words. For indeed the *sons* of Zebedee, having broken away from the others, were speaking with him *privately*; therefore he speaks with all *of them* in common. For, knowing that the love of being first *is* tyrannical and *stands* in need of a more vehement blow, he reaches them more deeply, casting them off into the portion of the heathen and of the unbelievers, if they should wish to be lovers of glory. And thus he shames them, saying:

Other men, on the one hand, because they rule, are made illustrious, and the loving of the first places is a passion of the heathen; but my disciples, on the other hand, are honored from humility — so that even the one wishing to be greater is obliged to serve the more inferior *ones*, which belongs to the utmost humility. And this I show in myself, who, being ruler and king of the *things* in the heavens, humbled myself, *being about* to serve you unto salvation;[^4] and *being about* to serve you so greatly that *I* even give *my* life *as* a ransom in exchange for many — that is, for all; for the "all" *are* the "many."[^5]

[^1]: Greek *ἀντὶ πολλῶν*, "in exchange for / instead of / in place of many." The preposition *ἀντί* carries the sense of substitution (one *in place of* many), which Theophylact does not press here; rendered "in exchange for" to keep the exchange-idea visible. See n. 5 for his own gloss on "many."
[^2]: The OCR reads «προήχθοντο», which is not a standard form. The sense demanded by the context — the ten "showed that they too" resented the honor shown to the two — points to a verb of being pained or aggrieved (*ἤχθοντο*, from *ἄχθομαι*, "to be vexed, grieved"), or possibly *προσώχθιζον*. Rendered "had been vexed"; the reading is insecure and flagged accordingly. An alternative, taking the base verb as *προάγω*, would give "had been provoked / led on" at the honor of these two.
[^3]: Greek *οἱ μὲν … κατεξανίσταντο τῶν δέκα· οἱ δὲ ἐφθόνουν τοῖς δύο*. The referents cross: "the ones" (*οἱ μέν*) are the two sons of Zebedee, who *rose up against* (*κατεξανίσταντο*, set themselves over) the ten; "the others" (*οἱ δέ*) are the ten, who *envied* the two. So both parties were still at fault — hence "they were still imperfect."
[^4]: Greek *διακονήσων … δουλεύσων*, future participles expressing purpose ("*being about* to serve / to be a slave to you"). The pair moves from *διακονέω* ("serve, minister to") to the stronger *δουλεύω* ("be a slave to"), matching the lemma's own escalation from *διάκονος* ("servant") to *δοῦλος* ("slave"); the supplied "*being about*" marks the future/purpose force.
[^5]: Greek *οἱ γὰρ πάντες, πολλοί* — "for all *people are* many." Theophylact glosses the Gospel's "ransom in exchange for **many**" (*ἀντὶ πολλῶν*) as meaning, in effect, "for **all**" (*ἀντὶ πάντων*): the "many" is not a limiting term but simply the whole multitude, since "all" and "the many" coincide.

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 33 · Matt. 13:50

> **…they shall cast them into the furnace of fire; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.[^1] (Matt. 13:50)**

Fearful *is* this parable; for it shows that even if we should believe, but should not have a good life, we shall be cast into the fire. For the net,[^2] on the one hand, *is* the teaching of the fishermen apostles, which was woven[^3] from both signs and prophetic testimonies. For the things which the apostles taught, they confirmed both by the miracles and by the voices of the prophets. This net, accordingly, gathered from every kind:[^4] Barbarians, Greeks, Jews, harlots, tax-collectors, robbers. But when it was filled — that is, when the world shall be consummated — then those in the net are separated. For even if we have believed, but should be found rotten, we are cast out; but those *who are* not of such a kind are cast into vessels — I mean indeed the eternal tabernacles.[^5]

And every deed, whether good or wicked, is said to be food of the soul. For the soul also has intelligible teeth.[^6] Then, therefore, the soul gnashes these — that is, being crushed *as to* its practical powers, because it did such things…[^7]

[^1]: The lemma completes Matt 13:50. The OCR is corrupt at two points: source «κάμινον τοῦ πυρότ» securely = *κάμινον τοῦ πυρός* ("furnace of fire"), and source «βρυγμὸς τῶν δλόντων» securely = *βρυγμὸς τῶν ὀδόντων* ("gnashing of teeth") — the latter reconstruction is confirmed by the exposition below, which turns on *ὀδόντες* ("teeth").
[^2]: Greek *σαγήνη*, a large dragnet or seine — the same word as in the lemma («ὁμοία… σαγήνῃ»); rendered "net" consistently throughout so the allegory (net = apostolic teaching) stays visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἐπλάκη* (aorist passive of *πλέκω*, "plait, weave, braid"), continuing the net-imagery: the net "was woven" from signs and prophetic testimonies.
[^4]: Greek *ἐκ παντὸς γένους*, echoing the lemma's *ἐκ παντὸς γένους συναγαγούσῃ*. *γένος* here shades from "kind" (of fish, in the parable) to "race/nation" (of people, in the interpretation); rendered "kind" to preserve the link to the lemma, though "race" fits the list of peoples that follows.
[^5]: Greek *τὰς αἰωνίους σκηνάς* (source «σπηνάς», an OCR slip for *σκηνάς*), "the eternal tabernacles/dwellings" — an allusion to Luke 16:9 (*τὰς αἰωνίους σκηνάς*). Here glossing the parable's "vessels" (*ἀγγεῖα*) as the eternal dwellings of the saved.
[^6]: Greek *ὀδόντας νοητούς* — "intelligible / noetic teeth," teeth perceptible to the mind rather than the senses. Theophylact allegorizes the "gnashing of teeth" (*βρυγμὸς τῶν ὀδόντων*) of the lemma: the soul has *νοητοί* teeth which it "gnashes" (*βρύχει*) in remorse. The pun on *ὀδόντες / βρυγμός / βρύχει* is intrinsic to the passage and largely survives in English "teeth / gnashing / gnashes."
[^7]: The exposition breaks off here without closing punctuation in the source (*…διότι τοιαῦτα ἔπραξεν*, "because it did such things"). Greek *τὰς πρακτικὰς αὐτῆς δυνάμεις συντριβομένη*: "being crushed/ground down *as to* its practical (active) powers" — an accusative of respect; the soul's faculties of action are what is ground, matching the teeth-imagery.

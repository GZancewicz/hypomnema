# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 13:46

> **…having found one pearl of great price, having gone away he sold all that he had, and bought it. (Matt. 13:46)**[^1]

The sea *is* the present life.[^2] The merchants *are* those who pass through it and seek to find some knowledge. Many things, on the one hand, therefore, seem to be pearls — the opinions of the many wise *men*; but one, on the other hand, *is* the precious one. For the truth is one, which is Christ. Just as, therefore, the pearl is reported to be generated within an oyster — into which, when it opens its valves,[^3] a flash of lightning flashes in; then again, when it closes these, the pearl is conceived out of the lightning and the dew, and for this reason it is most white — so also Christ was conceived in the Virgin out of the lightning from above, *namely* the Holy Spirit. And just as he who has a pearl, often holding it in his hand, himself, on the one hand, knows how much wealth he has, but others, on the other hand, are ignorant *of it* — so also the preaching is hidden among the obscure and lowly.[^4] One must therefore acquire this pearl, giving all *that one has*.

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence begun in the preceding lemma (Matt. 13:45), whose relative pronoun «ὅς» ("who") governs this clause.
[^2]: Greek *Θάλασσα, ὁ παρὼν βίος* ("The sea *is* the present life"). The pearl-parable itself does not name a sea; Theophylact supplies it from the maritime imagery of the pearl-merchant (and by anticipation of the net cast into the sea, 13:47), then allegorizes each term. The linking verb "is" is supplied throughout these equations, which in the Greek are bare appositions.
[^3]: Greek *ᾧπερ ἀνοίγοντι τὰς πτύχας, ἐναστράπτει ἀστραπή* ("to which, opening its folds, a lightning-flash flashes in"). *πτύχας* ("folds") = the valves/shells of the oyster; the verb, OCR-corrupted as *ἐνακθπτει*, is securely *ἐναστράπτει* ("flashes in"). The natural-history legend that pearls are conceived from lightning and dew entering the opened oyster is a commonplace of ancient and Byzantine lore, here made a type of the virginal conception.
[^4]: Greek *ἐν τοῖς ἀφανέσι καὶ εὐτελέσι κρύπτεται* ("is hidden among the obscure and lowly"). The two adjectives (*ἀφανής* "unseen/obscure," *εὐτελής* "cheap/of little worth") are neuter/masculine plural and may be read as "lowly things" or "lowly people"; the pearl-in-oyster image favors "things/persons of no outward account."

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 13:58

> **…he did not do there many powers,[^1] because of their unbelief. (Matt. 13:58)**

See Christ, how he does not insult them, but gently says that "A prophet is not without honor," and the rest. For we humans are accustomed always to despise the things in *our* familiarity,[^2] but to welcome the strange things.[^3] And he added the *phrase* "in his *own* house" because his brothers too, those from the same house, were envying him. And he did not do there many powers, because of their unbelief, sparing them — in order that they might not, remaining unbelieving even after the signs, be punished the more. Many *works*, on the one hand, therefore, he did not do; but a few *he did*,[^4] in order that they might not have *it* to say that "If he had done *any* at all, we would have believed."

But you, understand *it* also thus: that even until this day Jesus is dishonored in his native place — that is, among the Jews; but we, the strangers, honor him.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *δυνάμεις πολλάς* — literally "many powers," i.e. many mighty works / miracles. Rendered "powers" for consistency with the gloss of *δύναμις* elsewhere in this passage (cf. Lemma 38); the meaning is deeds of power. The lemma continues the Gospel quotation begun in the previous section, opening mid-sentence at *ἐποίησεν* ("he did").
[^2]: Greek *τῶν ἐν συνηθείᾳ* ("the things in customary familiarity / that we are used to"); set against *τὰ ξένα* just below. The proverb: we scorn the familiar and prize the foreign.
[^3]: Greek plays on one root: *τὰ ξένα* ("the strange / foreign things") which we welcome, and, at the close, *οἱ ξένοι* ("the strangers / foreigners") — namely we the Gentiles — who honor Christ, whereas his own *πατρίς* (the Jews) dishonors him. The wordplay ("the foreign" welcomed / "we foreigners" honoring) is preserved by rendering both with "strange"/"strangers."
[^4]: The Greek is elliptical — *ὀλίγα δ᾽ ἄν* ("but a few *he would/did do*"), the verb *ἐποίησεν* supplied from the preceding clause; "*he did*" is italicized as supplied. He worked a few miracles, not none, so as to leave them no excuse of "had he done any we would have believed."

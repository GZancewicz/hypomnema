# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 13:36b

> **And his disciples came to him, saying: Explain to us the parable of the tares of the field. (Matt. 13:36b)**

Concerning this one alone they ask, as the others *being* considered clearer by them. And tares[^1] are all the destructive things that grow up among the wheat — darnel, bitter-vetch, wild oat,[^2] and the rest.

[^1]: Greek *ζιζάνια* — "tares, darnel." Here Theophylact takes the word broadly, as a general name for all noxious weeds that spring up in a grainfield, and then lists particular species.
[^2]: Greek *αἷρα, ὄροβος, βρώμιον* — respectively "darnel" (*αἷρα*, itself a synonym for tares), "bitter-vetch" (*ὄροβος*), and *βρώμιον*, an uncertain form, most likely for *βρόμος* ("wild oat, oat-grass"). The three names denote common field weeds; the last is rendered by its probable sense.

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 13:13

> **For this reason I speak to them in parables, because seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand. (Matt. 13:13)**

Take heed. For from here is resolved the question of those who say that by nature and from God some *people* are evil. For Christ too said, they say, that "To you it has been given to know the mysteries, but to the Jews it has not been given." We say, therefore, to those who say such things, with God *helping*, that God makes all *people* to understand what is fitting, naturally.[^1] For he enlightens every man coming into the world;[^2] but our free choice[^3] darkens us. This, then, is what is shown here also. For he says that "seeing" *by* nature — that is, having been fashioned by God to understand — "they do not see" *by* choice; and "hearing" — that is, having been fashioned by God, naturally, so as to hear and to understand — "they do not hear, nor do they understand," *this* from choice. For tell me: did they not see the miracles of Christ? Yes, but they made themselves blind, and accused him. This, then, is the *meaning of* "seeing they do not see." Accordingly, he brings forward the prophet also as a witness.


[^1]: Theophylact turns the whole passage on a fixed antithesis of two adverbs: *φυσικῶς* ("naturally / by nature") and *προαιρετικῶς* ("by choice / by *free* choice"). God grants sight and hearing *by nature* (as a created endowment); a man loses them *by choice*. Rendered "naturally" / "by choice" throughout to keep the pair visible.
[^2]: An echo of John 1:9 («ὃ φωτίζει πάντα ἄνθρωπον ἐρχόμενον εἰς τὸν κόσμον»), "which enlightens every man coming into the world."
[^3]: Greek *προαίρεσις*, the deliberate choice or moral purpose of the will; a key term in this lemma, kept consistently as "free choice" / "choice" (cf. *προαιρετικῶς* above).

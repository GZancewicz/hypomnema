# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 13:8

> **But others fell upon the good ground; and it yielded fruit, one, on the one hand, a hundred; another, on the other hand, sixty; and another, thirty. (Matt. 13:8)**

The three parts of the seed perished, and the fourth alone was saved; for few are those who are being saved. But later he speaks concerning the good ground, giving us hope of repentance; for it is possible, even if one be stony, even if *he be* beside the road, even if thorn-bearing, that he become good ground. Not indeed do all who have received the word bear fruit equally; but one, on the one hand, *bears* a hundred — perhaps the one having perfect possessionlessness[^1] and the utmost asceticism; another, on the other hand, sixty — perhaps the cenobitic monk and *one* still in the active *life*;[^2] and another thirty — the one having chosen honorable marriage, and pursuing the virtues diligently as far as is possible.

[^1]: Greek *ἀκτημοσύνη* ("possessionlessness"), the monastic renunciation of private property; kept literal rather than rendered "poverty," which in Greek would be *πενία / πτωχεία*.
[^2]: Greek *πρακτικός*, a technical ascetic term for the "practical/active" life of moral discipline, contrasted with the *θεωρητικός* ("contemplative") life; "still" (*ἔτι*) marks it as a lower rung than the perfect ascetic of the hundredfold. Rendered "in the active *life*"; alternatively "still *given to* practice."
[^3]: The possessor-word before *χρηστότητα* ("goodness / kindness") is OCR-corrupt in the source as «λεοῆ»; from context it is almost certainly **Θεοῦ** ("of God") or **Κυρίου** ("of the Lord"). Supplied "*of God*"; flagged since the exact word is unrecoverable from the garble.

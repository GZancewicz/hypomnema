# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 13:24-30

> **Another parable he set before them, saying: The kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man who had sown good seed in his field; but while the men were sleeping, there came his enemy and sowed tares in the midst of the wheat, and went away. But when the grass sprouted and produced fruit, then appeared the tares also. And the servants of the householder, having come, said to him: Lord, did you not sow good seed in your field? Whence then has it the tares? And he said to them: An enemy, a man, did this. And the servants said to him: Do you wish, then, that we go off and gather them up? But he said: No; lest, gathering up the tares, you root up the wheat together with them. Let both therefore grow together until the harvest, and at the time of the harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather up first the tares, and bind them into bundles for the burning of them, but the wheat gather into my[^1] storehouse. (Matt. 13:24-30)**

In the former parable he said that the fourth part of the seed fell into the good earth; but in the present *one* he shows that not even that very *portion* which fell into the good earth did the enemy leave uncorrupted, on account of our sleeping and being slothful. The field, therefore, *is* the world, or the soul of each *person*. The sower, Christ. The good seed, the good men, or *good* thoughts. The tares, the heresies, or the evil thoughts. And he who sowed these, the devil.[^2] [The sleeping *gives place*][^3] to the heretics and to the evil thoughts.

And the servants *are* the angels, who are indignant that there are heresies, or wickednesses in the soul, and wish to pull up and to cut out of life both the heretics and those who devise evil things. God does not permit the heretics to be destroyed through wars, so that the righteous also may not suffer together and be destroyed together *with them*. Likewise neither, on account of the evil thoughts, does God wish to cut off the man, so that the wheat also may not be corrupted together *with them*. For instance, if Matthew, being a tare, had been cut off out of life, there would have been cut off together with him also the wheat of the word[^4] that was afterward destined to sprout from him; likewise also Paul, and the robber; for these, being tares, were not cut off, but were permitted to live, so that their virtue thereafter might grow.

He says, accordingly, to the angels that: In the consummation, then, you shall gather up the tares, that is, the heretics. But how? Into bundles — that is, having bound their hands and feet; for no longer is anyone able to work then, but every active power is bound. But the wheat, that is, the saints, are gathered by the reaper angels into the heavenly storehouses. Likewise, therefore, the evil thoughts also which Paul had when he was persecuting *the Church* were burned by the fire of Christ — which he came to cast upon the earth[^5] — but the wheat, that is, the good thoughts, were gathered into the storehouses of the Church.

[^1]: Source OCR reads *εἰς τὴν ἀποθήκην ιλου*; the garbled word is securely the possessive *μου* ("my"), as in the Gospel text (Matt. 13:30, *εἰς τὴν ἀποθήκην μου*).
[^2]: Theophylact varies the term for the adversary: in the lemma the sower of tares is *ὁ ἐχθρός* ("the enemy," rendered "An enemy, a man" for *ἐχθρὸς ἄνθρωπος*), while here in the exposition he names him *ὁ διάβολος* ("the devil").
[^3]: Source is corrupt here: *Καθτις τοῖς αἱρετικοῖς καὶ τοῖς πονηροῖς λογισμοῖς.* The datives "to the heretics and to the evil thoughts" are legible, but the governing word (OCR *Καθτις*) is lost. Given the preceding "on account of our sleeping and being slothful," it almost certainly said that this sleeping/sloth *gives occasion (place)* to the heretics and evil thoughts (something like *καιρὸν/χώραν δίδωσι*). The supplied "gives place" is a best-guess reconstruction, bracketed; the exact verb cannot be recovered from the text on the page.
[^4]: Source OCR *τοῦ λόγου ἵτος*; *ἵτος* is securely *σῖτος* ("wheat, grain") — "the wheat of the word," i.e., the fruitful yield of the gospel that Matthew would later produce, in keeping with the parable's imagery.
[^5]: Cf. Luke 12:49, "I came to cast fire upon the earth."

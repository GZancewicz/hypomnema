# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 13:33[^1]

> **Another parable he spoke to them: The kingdom of the heavens is like leaven, which a woman, having taken, hid in three measures of meal, until the whole was leavened. (Matt. 13:33)**

By "leaven" he means the apostles, just as also *by* "grain of mustard." As, therefore, the leaven, being small, transforms the whole *lump* of meal into itself, so also you will transform the whole world, even though you are few. And a *saton*[^2] was a certain measure among the Jews, just as we too have, for example, *choenixes*[^3] or ten-litra *measures*. But some understand the leaven *to be* the preaching; and the three measures, the three powers of the soul — the rational, the spirited, and the appetitive;[^4] and the woman, the soul, which hid the preaching in all its powers, and was mingled together *with it*, and [was … ][^5], and was wholly sanctified by it; for we must be wholly leavened and transformed toward the more divine. For he says, "until the whole was leavened."

[^1]: The folder name records the draws-from verse as 13.24, but the Greek here is the parable of the leaven, Matt. 13:33; the header verse is corrected accordingly.
[^2]: Greek *σάτον* (Hebrew *seʾah*), a dry measure; Theophylact retains the Gospel's term and glosses it as "a certain measure among the Jews." Left transliterated rather than converted, since he is explaining the unit itself.
[^3]: Greek *χοίνικας ἢ δεκάλιτρα* — "choenixes or ten-litra *measures*," everyday Byzantine measures cited as familiar equivalents. A *choenix* is a dry measure (roughly a day's grain ration); *δεκάλιτρον* is a "ten-*litra*" measure (the *litra* being the Roman *libra*/pound).
[^4]: The classic tripartite soul: *λογικόν* (rational), *θυμικόν* (spirited/irascible), *ἐπιθυμητικόν* (appetitive/desiring) — the three "powers" (*δυνάμεις*) leavened by the preaching.
[^5]: The source is corrupt at this point, in a series of feminine participles describing the soul: *καὶ συνανακραθεῖσαν, καὶ ἴυιλωεῖσαν, καὶ ἁγιασθεῖσαν ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ ὅλην* — "and mingled together, and [?], and wholly sanctified by it." The middle participle (OCR *ἴυιλωεῖσαν*) is unrecoverable with certainty; from the leaven imagery it was plausibly *ζυμωθεῖσαν* ("leavened") or *τελειωθεῖσαν* ("perfected"). Left bracketed rather than invented.

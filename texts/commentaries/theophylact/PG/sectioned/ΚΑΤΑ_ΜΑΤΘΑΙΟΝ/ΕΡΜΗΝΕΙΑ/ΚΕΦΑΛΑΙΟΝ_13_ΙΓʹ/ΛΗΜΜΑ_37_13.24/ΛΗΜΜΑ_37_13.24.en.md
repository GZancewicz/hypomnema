# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 13:54

> **…having come into his native place,[^1] he taught them in their synagogue. (Matt. 13:54)**[^2]

He said "these parables,"[^3] since he is also about to speak others after a little *while*. But he moves on,[^4] in order that he might benefit others also by his presence. And by "his native place" understand Nazareth; for in it he was reared. And he teaches in the synagogue, speaking in public and with boldness,[^5] in order that they might not have *grounds* to say afterward that he was teaching certain unlawful things.

[^1]: The lemma is a continuation: the source .txt opens mid-sentence at *ἐλθὼν εἰς τὴν πατρίδα αὐτοῦ* ("having come into his native place"), the second half of Matt. 13:54, the first half of which («καὶ ἐλθὼν…» following 13:53) was carried by the preceding lemma. Kept as a participle ("having come") per the Greek.
[^2]: Greek *πατρίς* ("native place, homeland, one's own country/town"); Theophylact glosses it explicitly below as Nazareth. Rendered "native place" throughout rather than "country," since the referent is a town.
[^3]: Greek *Ταύτας εἶπε τὰς παραβολάς* — Theophylact is explaining the demonstrative in the Gospel's «when Jesus had finished **these** parables» (13:53): the text says "these" because yet others were about to be spoken. The subject of *εἶπε* is left ambiguous in the Greek and is rendered "he said."
[^4]: Greek *Μεταβαίνει* ("he changes place / moves on / departs"), present tense retained.
[^5]: Greek *παῤῥησίᾳ* ("boldness, openness, outspokenness, frankness of speech"); paired here with *ἐν κοινῷ* ("in public / in common"). The point is that he taught openly and before all, so that no charge of secret unlawful teaching could later be laid.

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 13:3

> **Behold, the sower went out to sow his seed. (Matt. 13:3)**

He calls himself the sower, and the seed the word. And he went out not in place — for he was everywhere — but according to that whereby he drew near to us with flesh, according to this he is said to go out, from the fatherly bosoms,[^1] namely. He went out, therefore, to us, since we were not able to come to him. And to do what did he go out? Was it, then, to set the earth aflame on account of the many thorns? Was it to take vengeance? No, but to sow. And he said "his seed," because the prophets too were sowing. But not their own seed, rather that of God. But this one, being God, sowed his own seed, *the seed* proper to him;[^2] for he was not made wise by divine grace — away with *the thought*![^3] — but he himself was the wisdom of God.

[^1]: Greek *τῶν κόλπων τῶν πατρικῶν* — literally "the fatherly bosoms," the plural *κόλπων* as often for the singular "bosom"; i.e. from the bosom of the Father (cf. John 1:18). Kept plural to mirror the Greek.
[^2]: Greek *τὸν σπόρον αὐτοῦ τὸν ἴδιον* — a doubled possessive, "his seed, his own *proper* seed." The emphatic *ἴδιον* ("one's own, particular") is rendered redundantly to preserve the stress on the seed being his own and not another's.
[^3]: Greek *ἄπαγε!* — an exclamation of rejection, "away *with it*! / far from it! / God forbid!" The supplied object is italicized.

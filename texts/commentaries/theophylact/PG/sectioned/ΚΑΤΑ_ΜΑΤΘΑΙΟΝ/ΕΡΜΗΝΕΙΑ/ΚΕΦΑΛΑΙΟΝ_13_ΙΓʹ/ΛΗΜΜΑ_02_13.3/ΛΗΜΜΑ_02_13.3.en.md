# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 13:3

> **And he spoke to them many things in parables, saying: (Matt. 13:3)**

To the artless[^1] crowds, on the one hand, upon the mountain he speaks without parables; but here, on the other hand, the deceitful[^2] Pharisees being present, he speaks in parables — so that even if as *men* not understanding, they might ask and might learn. Besides, they being unworthy, it was not fitting that the teachings should be set before them bare.[^3] For one must not throw the pearls before the swine.[^4] And *as* the first parable he speaks the one that makes the hearer more attentive; for listen:

[^1]: Greek *ἀπλάστοις* — "unmoulded, unfeigned, simple, guileless." Theophylact sets it in antithesis to *δολερῶν* ("deceitful") just below: the plain, unfeigning crowds versus the crafty Pharisees. Rendered "artless" to keep the moral simplicity in view; "guileless" or "simple" would serve equally.
[^2]: Greek *δολερῶν* — "treacherous, wily, full of guile"; deliberately opposed to *ἀπλάστοις* above.
[^3]: Greek *γυμνὰς* ("naked, bare, unveiled") — the teachings should not be set before them stripped of the parable's covering.
[^4]: Cf. Matt. 7:6, *μὴ βάλητε τοὺς μαργαρίτας ὑμῶν ἔμπροσθεν τῶν χοίρων*.

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 13:23

> **But he who was sown upon the good earth, this is he who hears the word and understands *it*, who indeed bears fruit and produces, one a hundredfold, another sixty, another thirty. (Matt. 13:23)**

Various are the forms of virtue, various also are those who make progress. And see that there is an order in the parable. For we must, first, on the one hand, hear the word and understand *it*, so that we may not be as those by the wayside; then, hold fast firmly that which has been heard; then, not be lovers of money. For what benefit *is it* if I hear and hold fast, but through love of money I choke[^1]

[^1]: The lemma-comment breaks off here mid-sentence in the source: *ὑπὸ δὲ φιλοχρηματίας συμπνίξω…* ("but through love of money I choke [the word]…"). The verb *συμπνίξω* is the same "choke together" (*συμπνίγω*) used of the thorns in the previous lemma (13:22); the object "the word" is implied. The thought — that hearing and retaining profit nothing if avarice then chokes the word — is left grammatically unfinished on the page.

# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 13:52

> **…said to them: For this reason every scribe *who has been* discipled unto the kingdom of heaven is like unto a man *who is* a householder, who brings forth out of his treasure new and old *things*. (Matt. 13:52)**[^1]

Did you see how the parables made them more discerning?[^2] Behold, at any rate, those *who were* otherwise foolish and unlearned understood the things spoken with difficulty. Whence the Savior, praising them, says that "Every scribe," and the rest. For he calls them scribes, as *being* learned in the law; but even if they were learned in the law, they did not remain in the law, but were discipled unto the kingdom — that is, unto the knowledge of Christ — and are able to bring forth the things both of the new and of the old.[^3]

The householder, therefore, *is* Christ, as *being* rich; for in him *are* the treasures of wisdom.[^4] *He it is* who, teaching the new *things*, then thus brought testimonies from the old: as when he said that even for an idle word you shall be called to account[^5] — this *is* the new; then he adduced a testimony: "For out of your words you shall be justified, and out of your words you shall be condemned"[^6] — this *is* the old. Like to this one, at any rate, *are* the apostles, such as Paul, saying: "Be imitators of me, as I also *am* of Christ."[^7]

[^1]: The lemma completes Matt 13:52. Greek *εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Διὰ τοῦτο πᾶς γραμματεὺς μαθητευθεὶς εἰς τὴν βασιλείαν τῶν οὐρανῶν, ὅμοιός ἐστιν ἀνθρώπῳ οἰκοδεσπότῃ, ὅστις ἐκβάλλει ἐκ τοῦ θησαυροῦ αὐτοῦ καινὰ καὶ παλαιά*. *μαθητευθείς* is aorist passive, "having been made a disciple / discipled" (supplied "*who has been*" italic). *ἐκβάλλει* is literally "casts out"; rendered "brings forth." *καινὰ καὶ παλαιά* ("new and old") is anarthrous and neuter plural; the supplied noun "*things*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek OCR «παατ εκτικωτέρους»; reconstructed as *πρακτικωτέρους* (comparative of *πρακτικός*, "practical, effective, apt in action") — hence more nearly "more effective / more capable." The reading is not fully secure; rendered "more discerning" to fit the immediate point (that even the dull now grasped hard sayings), with the Greek flagged here for the reader to check. An alternative is "more practical / more apt."
[^3]: *τά τε τῆς καινῆς καὶ τῆς παλαιᾶς*, "the things both of the new and of the old" — i.e. of the New and the Old *Covenant/Testament*; the feminine *καινῆς / παλαιᾶς* implies *διαθήκης* ("covenant"). This picks up the lemma's neuter *καινὰ καὶ παλαιά* ("new and old *things*") and reads it covenantally.
[^4]: An allusion to Col 2:3, *ἐν ᾧ εἰσιν πάντες οἱ θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας* ("in whom are all the treasures of wisdom"), glossing the parable's "treasure" (*θησαυρός*).
[^5]: An allusion to Matt 12:36, *παντὸς ῥήματος ἀργοῦ… ἀποδώσουσιν λόγον* ("they shall give account for every idle word"). Greek here *ὑπὲρ ἀργοῦ λόγου εὐθυνθήσῃ*, "for an idle word you shall be called to account / examined" (*εὐθύνω*, "to call to account, audit"); the second-person singular "you shall be" is retained.
[^6]: Matt 12:37, *ἐκ γὰρ τῶν λόγων σου δικαιωθήσῃ, καὶ ἐκ τῶν λόγων σου καταδικασθήσῃ*. Theophylact abbreviates the second clause; his "καὶ κατακριθήσῃ" is rendered "and… you shall be condemned." He labels this saying "the old" (*τὸ παλαιόν*) — i.e. the confirming proof-text set beside the "new" teaching — even though, as it stands, both come from Matt 12; the point is the pattern of new teaching sealed by an authoritative testimony, not a literal Old-Testament citation.
[^7]: 1 Cor 11:1, *μιμηταί μου γίνεσθε, καθὼς κἀγὼ Χριστοῦ* ("Be imitators of me, as I also *am* of Christ"). The supplied "*am*" is italicized, the verb being unexpressed in the Greek.

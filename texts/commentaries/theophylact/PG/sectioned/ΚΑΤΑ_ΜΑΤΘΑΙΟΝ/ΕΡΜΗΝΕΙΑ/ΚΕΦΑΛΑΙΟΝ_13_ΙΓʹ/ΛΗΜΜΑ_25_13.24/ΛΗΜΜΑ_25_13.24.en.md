# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 13:42

> **They shall cast them into the furnace of the fire; there shall be the wailing and the gnashing of the teeth. (Matt. 13:42)**[^1]

The things that needed to be said have been said above. For we said that here he is treating of the heresies, which are permitted to exist until the consummation. For if we should kill the heretics and cut *them* off, there will be factions and battles, and perhaps also many of the faithful will be destroyed in the factions. But indeed both Paul and the robber[^2] had tares *in themselves* before they believed;[^3] therefore they were not cut off at that time, on account of the wheat that was going to sprout in them — for afterward they bore fruit to God; but the tares, on the other hand, they burned up with the fire of the Spirit and *its* fervor.[^4]

[^1]: The lemma continues the sentence begun in the preceding lemma (Matt. 13:41), where "the Son of Man shall send forth his angels, and they shall gather… and the doers of lawlessness"; the subject of *βαλοῦσιν* ("they shall cast") is those angels. Rendered as a continuation, per the rule of translating the words the file actually carries.
[^2]: Greek *ὁ λῃστής* — the "robber" or "brigand," i.e. the penitent thief crucified with Christ (Luke 23:40–43); kept as "robber," the standard sense of the word.
[^3]: Greek *ζιζάνια εἶχον πρὶν ἢ πιστεῦσαι* ("had tares before they believed"). The verb is *εἶχον* ("had, held"), not "were"; the sense is that Paul and the robber had within them the condition of tares (weeds) before their conversion. Rendered literally with a supplied "*in themselves*" for the sense; a looser paraphrase would be "were themselves tares."
[^4]: Greek *τῇ ζέσει* ("with the seething / boiling / fervor"), paired here with *τῷ πυρὶ τοῦ Πνεύματος* ("the fire of the Spirit"). The unexpressed subject of *κατέκαυσαν* ("they burned up") is Paul and the robber, who burned up their own tares. The source then carries a string of OCR gibberish (*οοωροβΟΟΘίς*), evidently a scan artifact / catchword and not text; left untranslated.

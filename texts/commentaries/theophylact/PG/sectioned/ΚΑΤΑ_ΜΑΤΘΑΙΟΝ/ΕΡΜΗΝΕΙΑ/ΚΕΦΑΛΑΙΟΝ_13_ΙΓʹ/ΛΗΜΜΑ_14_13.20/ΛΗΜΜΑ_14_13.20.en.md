# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 13:20-22

> **But he who was sown upon the rocky places, this is he who hears the word and immediately with joy receives it, but has no root in himself, but is *only* for a season. And when tribulation or persecution arises on account of the word, immediately he is caused to stumble. (Matt. 13:20-21)**

He said "tribulation," since many, being afflicted by parents or by other calamities, immediately blaspheme; and "persecution," on account of those who fall away under the tyrants.[^1]

> **But he who was sown among the thorns, this is he who hears the word, and the care of this age and the deceit of wealth chokes the word, and he becomes unfruitful. (Matt. 13:22)**

He did not say that "this age chokes," but "the care of the age"; nor "wealth," but "the deceit of wealth." For wealth, when it is scattered abroad,[^2] does not choke, but increases the word; and the thorns are the cares and the indulgences,[^3] because they kindle both the fire of desire and the fire of Gehenna.[^4] And just as the thorn, being sharp, sinks into the body and is with difficulty cast out, so also indulgence, if it takes hold of a soul, sinks in and is with difficulty rooted out.

[^1]: Greek *διὰ τοὺς ἐπὶ τῶν τυράννων παραπίπτοντας* — "on account of those who fall away in the time of / under the tyrants" (i.e., those who apostatize when persecuted by pagan rulers). *παραπίπτω* here is the technical "fall away, lapse, apostatize."
[^2]: Greek *ὅταν σκορπισθῇ* ("when it is scattered / dispersed"), i.e., distributed to the poor as alms — the opposite of hoarding. The verb is that of Ps. 111(112):9 / 2 Cor. 9:9 ("he scattered abroad, he gave to the poor").
[^3]: Greek *αἱ τρυφαί* ("luxuries, indulgences, soft living"), rendered "indulgences" here and "indulgence" (singular *ἡ τρυφή*) below to keep the term consistent.
[^4]: Greek *τὸ πῦρ καὶ τὸ τῆς ἐπιθυμίας καὶ τὸ τῆς γεέννης ἀνάπτουσι* — literally "they kindle the fire, both that of desire and that of Gehenna." The single word "fire" (*τὸ πῦρ*) governs both genitives: the cares and indulgences light both the present fire of lust and the coming fire of hell. In the source the following sentence is trailed by a stray catchword letter "Κ" (a printer's cue for the next section), not part of the text.

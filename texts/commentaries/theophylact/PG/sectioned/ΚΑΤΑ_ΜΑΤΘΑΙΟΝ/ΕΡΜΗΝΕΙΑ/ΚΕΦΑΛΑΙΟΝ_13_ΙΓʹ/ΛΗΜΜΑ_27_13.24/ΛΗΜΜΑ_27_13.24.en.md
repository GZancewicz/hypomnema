# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 13:44

> **Again, the kingdom of the heavens is like unto treasure hidden in a field, which a man, having found, hid; and from his joy he goes, sells all that he has, and buys that field. (Matt. 13:44)**

The field *is* the world, the treasure *is* the preaching and the knowledge of Christ. And it is hidden in the world. For "we preach," says Paul, "wisdom that has been hidden";[^1] and he who seeks the knowledge of God finds it. And all that he has — whether Hellenic doctrines,[^2] or wicked habits, or money — he casts away at once, and buys the field, that is, the world; for he has the world as his own possession, *namely* he who has the knowledge of Christ. For, having nothing, he possesses all things, and he has the elements[^3] as slaves, commanding them as Jesus *did*, as Moses *did*.

[^1]: 1 Cor. 2:7, *σοφίαν… τὴν ἀποκεκρυμμένην* ("wisdom… that has been hidden"). Theophylact abbreviates Paul's clause.
[^2]: Greek *δόγματα ἑλληνικά* ("Hellenic doctrines"), i.e. pagan Greek teachings; *Ἕλλην* ("Greek/Hellene") in Byzantine usage regularly means "pagan." Rendered "Hellenic doctrines" to keep the word visible rather than flattening to "pagan."
[^3]: Greek *τὰ στοιχεῖα* ("the elements"), here the elements of the natural world, which the one who knows Christ commands as a master commands slaves — as Jesus commanded wind and sea and as Moses commanded the elements at the Exodus. The word can elsewhere mean "rudiments" or "elemental spirits" (cf. Gal. 4:3), but the following clause ("commanding them as Jesus, as Moses") fixes the sense as the physical elements.

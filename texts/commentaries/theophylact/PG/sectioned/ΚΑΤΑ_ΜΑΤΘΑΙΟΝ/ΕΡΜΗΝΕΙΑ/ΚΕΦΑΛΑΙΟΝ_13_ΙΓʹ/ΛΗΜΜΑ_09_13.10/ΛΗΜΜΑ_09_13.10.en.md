# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 13:10–12

> **And the disciples, having come *to him*, said to him: Why do you speak to them in parables? But he, having answered, said to them, that: To you it has been given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of the heavens, but to those it has not been given. For whoever has, it shall be given to him, and he shall be given in abundance; but whoever has not, even what he has shall be taken away from him. (Matt. 13:10–12)**

The disciples, seeing much obscurity in the things spoken by Christ, as new guardians[^1] of the multitude, approach asking; but he says: "To you it has been given to know the mysteries" — that is, since you have eagerness and diligence, it is given to you; but to those, not having diligence, it is not given. For that one receives, who seeks. "For seek," he says, "and it shall be given to you."[^2] Behold, then, how here the Lord spoke the parable, but only the disciples, having sought, received *it*. So that we have said well, that to the one having diligence, to that one is given the knowledge, and it is made to abound. But to the one not having diligence and a worthy disposition, even what he seems to have shall be taken away from him; that is, even if he has some small spark of the good, he extinguishes even this, not fanning it into flame through the Spirit and through spiritual works, and so kindling *it*.


[^1]: Greek *καινοὶ κηδεμόνες* ("new guardians / caretakers"): the disciples, newly *become* protectors of the crowd, ask on its behalf. The adjective could also be read *κοινοὶ* ("common / shared") — i.e. guardians belonging to the whole people — but the text reads *καινοί* ("new"), which is followed here.
[^2]: An echo of Matt. 7:7 / Luke 11:9 («ζητεῖτε καὶ δοθήσεται ὑμῖν»), adduced by Theophylact as proof that receiving follows seeking.

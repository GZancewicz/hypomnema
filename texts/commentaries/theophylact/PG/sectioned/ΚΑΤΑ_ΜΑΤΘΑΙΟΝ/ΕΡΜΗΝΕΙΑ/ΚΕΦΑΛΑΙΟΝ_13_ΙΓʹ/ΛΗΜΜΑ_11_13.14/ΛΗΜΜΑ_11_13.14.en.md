# Matthew 13 (ΙΓʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 13:14–15

> **And in them is fulfilled the prophecy of Isaiah, which says: With hearing you shall hear, and shall in no wise understand; and seeing you shall see, and shall in no wise perceive. For the heart of this people was thickened, and with the ears they heard heavily, and their eyes they closed; lest ever they should see with the eyes, and hear with the ears, and understand with the heart, and turn back, and I should heal them. (Matt. 13:14–15)**

Do you see what the prophecy says? That it is not for this reason that you will not understand, namely because I created your heart thick, but because it was thickened[^1] — being, that is to say, thin before; for that which is being thickened was thin before. And this *heart* having been thickened, thus they closed their eyes. But he did not say that God closed their eyes, but that they *did so* out of *their own* choice. And this they did, he says, "so that they might not turn back, and I heal them"; for of deliberate malice[^2] they made it their eager aim to remain unhealed and unturned.


[^1]: Theophylact's whole point turns on the verb *παχύνω* ("to thicken, make dense/dull/fat") and its opposite *λεπτός* ("thin, fine"). The heart was not *created* thick (*παχεῖα*) but *became* thickened (*ἐπαχύνθη*), having been *λεπτή* ("thin, subtle") before — the aorist passive marks an acquired, not a created, condition, and so exonerates the Creator. "Thickened" is kept literal to preserve the thin-vs-thick antithesis.
[^2]: Greek *ἐθελοκάκως*, an adverb from *ἐθελόκακος* ("willfully bad, evil of set purpose"): they were dull not by compulsion but by a deliberately chosen malice. Rendered "of deliberate malice"; alternatively "being wickedly willing" or "of their own bad will."

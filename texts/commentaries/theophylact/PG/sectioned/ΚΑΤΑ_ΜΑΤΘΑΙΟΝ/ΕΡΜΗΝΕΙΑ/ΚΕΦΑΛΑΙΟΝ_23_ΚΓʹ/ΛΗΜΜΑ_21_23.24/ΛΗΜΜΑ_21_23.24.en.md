# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 23:24

> **Blind guides, who strain out the gnat, but swallow the camel. (Matt. 23:24)**

Again he reproaches them as senseless, because, despising the greater commandments, they sought exactness in the small *ones*; not even overlooking the tithing of the cummin, but tithing even that; and if anyone blamed them as petty, they alleged that the law says this. But it was better and more pleasing to God, if they demanded judgment and mercy and faith from the people. And what is judgment? *It is* to do nothing unjust and irrational, but all things with discernment and with reason.[^1] For immediately mercy also follows upon judgment; for he who does all things with discernment knows also whom it is necessary to have mercy on. And upon mercy follows faith; for he who shows mercy believes assuredly that he will lose nothing, but will receive all *back*. And besides, it is necessary *not only* to show mercy, but also to believe in *the* true God. For many of the Greeks,[^2] on the one hand, had mercy, but, not believing in the living God, they did not have faith following upon *their* mercy.

Therefore each of the teachers must tithe from his people — that is, exact from the ten senses, the five bodily and the five *of the* soul, judgment and mercy and faith.[^3] But the *saying*, "These it was necessary to do," the Lord spoke not urging *them* on to tithe the vegetables, but so that he might not seem, for the present, to legislate against Moses. And he names them blind guides, because, boasting to teach and to know all things, they benefited no one, but rather even ruined all, casting *them* into *the* pit of unbelief. And he names these *men* strainers-out of the gnat, as guarding against the small sins, on the one hand, but, on the other hand, swallowing and overlooking the camel, that is, the great *ones*.

[^1]: Greek *κεκριμένως* — "with discernment / judiciously / with discrimination"; cognate with *κρίσις* ("judgment"), the very word being defined. The play (judgment = to act *judgingly*) is hard to keep in English; "with discernment" is used for the adverb and "judgment" for the noun.
[^2]: Greek *τῶν Ἑλλήνων*, "of the Greeks" — in patristic usage regularly "the pagans / the Gentiles" as opposed to believers. Rendered literally "Greeks."
[^3]: Theophylact puns on tithing: *ἀποδεκατοῦν* ("to take a tenth / tithe"), *δεκάτωσις* ("tithing"), and *τῶν δέκα αἰσθήσεων* ("the ten senses"). The teacher is to exact a "tithe" from the *ten* senses — five bodily, five of the soul. The etymological play (δεκάτη / δέκα) cannot be reproduced in English.

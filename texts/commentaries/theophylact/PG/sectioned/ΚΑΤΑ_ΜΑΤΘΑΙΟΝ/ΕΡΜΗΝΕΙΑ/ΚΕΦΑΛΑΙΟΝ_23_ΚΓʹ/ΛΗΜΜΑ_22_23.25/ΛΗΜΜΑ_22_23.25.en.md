# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 22 · Matt. 23:25–26

> **Woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you cleanse the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full from robbery and injustice. Blind Pharisee, cleanse first the inside of the cup and of the platter, so that the outside of them also may become clean. (Matt. 23:25–26)**[^1]

Holding fast the traditions of the elders, they washed the cups and the platters — that is, the dishes on which the cooked foods are set — but they drank wine *gotten* from robbery, and ate viands, *and thereby* rather defiled them.[^2] "Do not, therefore," he says, "acquire wine from injustice, and thereupon the inside of the cup becomes clean"; or *rather*, he speaks not concerning cups and platters, but concerning the bodily and outward condition, and the inward and spiritual *one*. For you, he says, on the one hand, shape the outside of the cup — that is, your outward condition — into the more dignified *form*; but, on the other hand, your inside is full of filth, for you rob and act unjustly. But it is necessary to wash the inside, that is, the soul, so that together with the purity of the soul the outward dignity also may shine forth.

[^1]: Greek *γέμουσιν ἐξ ἀρπαγῆς καὶ ἀδικίας* — "they are full from robbery and injustice" (KJV "full of extortion and excess"); Theophylact's text reads *ἀδικίας*, "injustice," which his comment turns on ("you rob and act unjustly"). The subject of the plural *γέμουσιν* ("they are full") is the cup and platter together.
[^2]: The Greek is slightly OCR-disturbed here: «καὶ ἐδέσματα ἤσθιον, ἄ ἐμόλυνον μᾶλλον αὐτά». The stray *ἄ* is best read as the relative/connective *ἃ* ("*things* which") or simply as "and *so*"; the sense is that the stolen wine and food defiled the vessels the more, outweighing the ritual washing. Rendered "*and thereby* rather defiled them."

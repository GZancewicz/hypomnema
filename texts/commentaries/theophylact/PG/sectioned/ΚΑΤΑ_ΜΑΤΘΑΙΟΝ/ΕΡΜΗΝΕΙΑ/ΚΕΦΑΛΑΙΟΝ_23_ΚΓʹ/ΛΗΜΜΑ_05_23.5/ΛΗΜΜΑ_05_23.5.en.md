# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 23:5

> **but all their works they do in order to be seen by men; and they broaden their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments.**[^1] (Matt. 23:5)

Heavy burdens the Pharisees laid on, compelling men to fulfill the commandments of the law, *which are* minute[^2] and hard to keep; or *else* they also weighed *them* down with *things* more than the commandments of the law, handing down certain traditions beyond the law, while they themselves did not move *them* with their finger — that is, themselves doing nothing, nor daring even to draw near to such burdens. For when the teacher not only teaches, but also does *it*, he seems to help bear *the burden* and to work together with those being taught; but when, on the one hand, he loads *the burden* upon me, but he himself, on the other hand, does nothing, he rather weighs me down — showing, from the things which he himself neglects, that it is impossible for the things which he says to be accomplished. Accordingly, the Lord accuses the Pharisees, as not willing to help bear the weights of the commandments and to labor together *in them*.

And not only do they do nothing good, but they also fashion themselves *to look* as *though* doing *it*; and yet, even if they were doing *good* — but were doing *it* in order to be seen — the gain would be withdrawn from them;[^8] but as it is, of how great a condemnation are they worthy, when they neither do *it* and *yet* wish to be glorified as *though* doing *it*! And what do they do? They broaden their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments. And this was of such a kind: in the law it was laid down: "Fasten *them* upon your hand, and let them be unmoved before your eyes."[^3] They wrote, therefore, upon two small skins — that is, little papers — the ten commandments of the law, and they placed the one, on the one hand, upon their forehead, but the other they hung upon the right *hand*;[^4] and the borders they made upon the edges of their garments, certain hyacinth-blue or scarlet threads, like tassels.[^5] And this too they found laid down in the law, so that, seeing them, they might not forget the commandments of God. But God did not will this; rather, the having of the phylactery upon the hand signified that one must work the commandments; and the borders' being scarlet made clear that we must be marked[^6] with the blood of Christ. But those *men* made both the phylacteries large and the borders, in order that they might seem to be guardians of the law[^7] to those who saw these things.

[^1]: Greek «πάντα δὲ τὰ ἔργα αὐτῶν ποιοῦσι πρὸς τὸ θεαθῆναι τοῖς ἀνθρώποις· πλατύνουσι δὲ τὰ φυλακτήρια αὐτῶν, καὶ μεγαλύνουσι τὰ κράσπεδα τῶν ἱματίων αὐτῶν». *φυλακτήρια* = "phylacteries" (from *φυλάσσω*, "to guard/keep"), the little cases holding scripture texts; *κράσπεδα* = "borders, hems, fringes / tassels." Both are treated as loaded terms in the exposition below.
[^2]: Greek *λεπτὰς οὔσας*, from *λεπτός* ("fine, thin, minute, subtle"); of commandments it means minute / minutely detailed / fine-drawn, hence hard to keep (*δυσφυλάκτους*). Rendered "minute"; the literal image is "fine-drawn," and "subtle" is also possible.
[^3]: A conflated recollection of the Law's precept on binding the commandments: cf. Deut. 6:8 / Exod. 13:9, 16 («καὶ ἔσται ἀσάλευτον πρὸ ὀφθαλμῶν σου» and «δήσεις … ἐπὶ τῆς χειρός σου»). Greek here: «Ἐφάψατε ἐπὶ τῆς χειρὸς ὑμῶν, καὶ ἔστω ἀσάλευτα πρὸ ὀφθαλμῶν ὑμῶν» (OCR "Κστοι" resolved as «ἔστω», "let it/them be"). Translated as Theophylact quotes it, not conformed to any English Bible.
[^4]: Greek *ἐπὶ τῆς δεξιᾶς*, "upon the right *hand*" (feminine, the noun *χειρός* understood); some texts of the practice place it on the arm. The supplied "*hand*" follows the parallel «ἐπὶ τῆς χειρός» in the quoted precept.
[^5]: Greek *ὑακίνθινά τινα ἢ κόκκινα κλώσματα οἷον κροσσούς*: "certain hyacinth-blue or scarlet threads (*κλώσματα*), like tassels (*κροσσούς*)."
[^6]: Greek *σημειοῦσθαι*, "to be marked / signed / sealed *with a sign*"; here of being marked with Christ's blood.
[^7]: Greek *νομοφύλακες*, literally "law-guardians / keepers of the law" — a deliberate echo of *φυλακτήρια* (the "guarding" cases) just discussed; rendered "guardians of the law" to keep the wordplay visible.
[^8]: Greek *ὑφῃρεῖτο ἂν αὐτοῖς τὸ κέρδος*, imperfect passive of *ὑφαιρέω* ("take away from under, withdraw, subtract"), with no agent expressed: the reward would be forfeited/withdrawn of itself, not stolen by another. Rendered "withdrawn" rather than a term implying an external thief.

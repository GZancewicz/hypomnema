# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 07 · Matt. 23:7

> **…*the* greetings in the marketplaces, and to be called by men, Rabbi, Rabbi. (Matt. 23:7)**[^1]

Babai![^2] What a thing he says! For because they love *these things*, on account of this they are condemned. But if the one *who* merely loves the chief place *is* blamed,[^3] the one who even does all things for the sake of it — of what is he worthy?

[^1]: The lemma is a continuation: it carries the tail of the sentence begun in the previous lemma («φιλοῦσι δὲ τὴν πρωτοκλισίαν… καὶ τοὺς ἀσπασμοὺς ἐν ταῖς ἀγοραῖς», "they love the chief place… and the greetings in the marketplaces"). The article *τούς* stands in the previous lemma; the supplied "*the*" restores it here. Greek *Ῥαββί* is kept as the loan-word "Rabbi" (glossed below in Lemma 08 as *διδάσκαλος*, "teacher").
[^2]: Greek *Βαβαί* — an interjection of astonishment ("Ah!", "Good heavens!"), left untranslated by transliteration since it renders no propositional content; it registers Theophylact's amazement at Christ's words.
[^3]: The OCR reads «ὡέγεται»; securely *ψέγεται* ("is blamed / censured"). The argument is *a fortiori*: if merely loving the chief place is blameworthy, how much more the one who does everything to obtain it.

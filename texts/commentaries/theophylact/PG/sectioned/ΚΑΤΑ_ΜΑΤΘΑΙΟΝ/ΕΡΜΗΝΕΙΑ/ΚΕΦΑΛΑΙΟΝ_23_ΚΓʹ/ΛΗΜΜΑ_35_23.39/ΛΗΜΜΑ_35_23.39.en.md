# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 23:39

> **…see *me* from now on, until ye shall say: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord. (Matt. 23:39)**

He doubles the name of Jerusalem, pitying her and calling her back with compassion. For just as *one* makes his defense[^1] toward a certain *woman* ardently loved, on the one hand, but despising, on the other hand, the one who loves *her* — being about to bring on the chastisement — for he charges her as murderous, and *charges* that, though he often wished to have mercy on her, she herself would not, but, persuaded by the devil who scatters her and leads *her* away from the truth (which is a thing of one single kind),[^2] received not the Lord who gathers together. For nothing so scatters *one* from God as does sin, just as, in turn, a good conscience gathers *one* toward God. And, displaying his tender affection, he set down the example of the bird. "Wherefore, since ye will not, I leave the temple desolate." And from hence let us learn that *it is* on account of us *that* God inhabits even the temples; but whenever we are *given up as* reprobate, *he abandons us.*[^3] … *The word* "until ye shall say, Blessed…" *he speaks with a view to* the second coming;[^4] for then even against their will shall they worship him, and shall say the *saying*:

[^1]: «ἀπολογεῖται» — "he makes his defense / pleads his case." The image is of a spurned lover who justifies himself before inflicting punishment on the beloved who scorned him; Christ is the implied subject of the analogy, applied to himself and Jerusalem.
[^2]: «ἥτις ἑνοειδές τι χρῆμά ἐστι» — "which is a thing of single/unitary form." The relative «ἥτις» most naturally refers to «ἀληθείας» ("the truth"): truth is one and unifying (it gathers to unity), whereas sin scatters into multiplicity. The main clause «τὸν συνάγοντα Κύριον οὐκ ἐδέχετο» ("she received not the Lord who gathers together") has Jerusalem as its subject.
[^3]: The apodosis is absent in the Greek; the sense, supplied from «ἀφίημι τὸν ναὸν ἔρημον» above, is that God then abandons *us* (leaves the temple desolate). Greek: «ὅταν δὲ ἡμεῖς ἀπεγνωσμένοι ὧμεν…».
[^4]: The connective is defective in the OCR: between «ἀπεγνωσμένοι ὧμεν» and the dangling genitive «τῆς δευτέρας παρουσίας» text appears to have dropped. The reconstructed sense: the promise "ye shall not see me… until ye shall say, Blessed…" points to the Second Coming. Greek: «…τῆς δευτέρας παρουσίας. Τότε γὰρ καὶ ἄκοντες αὐτὸν προσκυνήσουσι, καὶ ἐροῦσι τό·». The citation «Blessed is he that cometh» resumes in Lemma 36.

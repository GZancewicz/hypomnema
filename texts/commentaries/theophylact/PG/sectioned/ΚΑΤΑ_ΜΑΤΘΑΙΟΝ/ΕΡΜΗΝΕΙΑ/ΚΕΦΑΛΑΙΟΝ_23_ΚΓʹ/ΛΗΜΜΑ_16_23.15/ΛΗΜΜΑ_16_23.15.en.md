# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 23:16–17

> **Woe to you, blind guides, who say: "Whoever swears by the temple, it is nothing; but whoever swears by the gold of the temple, he is bound."[^1] Fools and blind! For [which is greater, the][^2] gold? And whoever…** (Matt. 23:16–17)

*This lemma carries only the Gospel text, without exposition; Theophylact defers his comment on the whole passage about swearing to a later lemma, where he says "He names them blind…" and expounds vv. 16–22 together.*[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ὀφείλει* — "he owes, he is a debtor, he is under obligation" (i.e. the oath is binding on him). Rendered "he is bound" to keep the legal/debt force of the verb, on which the Pharisees' distinction between binding and non-binding oaths turns.
[^2]: The source is OCR-truncated here to *«Μωροὶ καὶ τυφλοί, τί γὰρ χρυσόν;»*, a collapse of the full v. 17, *«μωροὶ καὶ τυφλοί· τίς γὰρ μείζων ἐστίν, ὁ χρυσὸς ἢ ὁ ναὸς ὁ ἁγιάζων τὸν χρυσόν;»* ("Fools and blind! For which is greater, the gold, or the temple that sanctifies the gold?"). The bracketed words restore the sense; only the legible "gold" is left unbracketed.
[^3]: The exposition proper begins at lemma 19 (Matt. 23:22), with *«Τυφλοὺς αὐτοὺς ὀνομάζει…»* ("He names them blind, as *those* unwilling to teach what is fitting…"). Lemmata 16–18 present the Gospel text of the passage on oaths broken into successive fragments; the trailing "And whoever…" here runs on into the next lemma (Matt. 23:18).

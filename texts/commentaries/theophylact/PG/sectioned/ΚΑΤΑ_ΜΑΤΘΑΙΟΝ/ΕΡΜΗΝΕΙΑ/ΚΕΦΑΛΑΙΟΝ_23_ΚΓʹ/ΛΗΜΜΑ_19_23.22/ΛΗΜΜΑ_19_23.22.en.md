# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 23:22

> **…*and he who* has sworn by heaven swears by the throne of God, and by *him who* sits upon it. (Matt. 23:22)**[^1]

He names them blind, as not willing to teach the things that are fitting, and, on the one hand, honoring the more trifling things, but, on the other hand, deeming the things worthy of honor of secondary account. For indeed the gold that *was* in the temple, and the Cherubim and the golden jar,[^2] they preferred above the temple itself. Wherefore also they taught the many that it is nothing to swear by the temple, but *rather* to swear by the gold laid around the temple, which for this reason was altogether venerable, because it was laid around the temple. And the gifts, moreover, that *were* placed upon the altar, they said were more honorable than the altar itself; so that thus also the Pharisees dogmatized, that if someone swore by the gold[^3] and then perjured himself, he was condemned to pay back the equal amount. For they preferred the gift of the altar because of the gains from the sacrifices. But if, having sworn by the temple, he perjured himself, since he was not able to build *anything* like the temple, for this reason he was released *from liability*; and thereafter the oath by the temple seemed to be lighter, on account of the love of gain of the Pharisees.

Therefore, on the one hand, under the old *covenant* Christ does not concede that the gift is greater than the altar; but on the other hand, in *the case of* us, the altar is rather sanctified by the gifts. For the loaves are changed by divine grace into that very Lordly body; wherefore also the altar is sanctified by these.

Woe to you,[^4]

[^1]: This is the tail of the Gospel citation that was begun in the previous lemma (which broke off at «καὶ ὁ»); the sentence resumes here with *ὀμόσας ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ, ὀμνύει ἐν τῷ θρόνῳ τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ ἐν τῷ καθημένῳ ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ*. The supplied "*and he who*" renders the connective and the substantival participle carried over from the previous lemma.
[^2]: Greek *τὴν στάμνον τὴν χρυσῆν* — the golden jar (of manna), cf. Heb. 9:4.
[^3]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupt: «ἐάν τις ὥμοσε κατὰ τοῦ χρυσοῦ τος, εἶτα ἐπιώρκησε». The stray *τος* is unrecoverable (perhaps a fragment of a demonstrative or of *χρυσίου*); the sense is secure from the parallel clauses — swearing "by the gold." Translated as "by the gold," with the corrupt syllable left aside.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off with the opening words of the next Gospel citation (Matt. 23:23), «Οὐαὶ ὑμῖν,» which is completed in the following lemma.

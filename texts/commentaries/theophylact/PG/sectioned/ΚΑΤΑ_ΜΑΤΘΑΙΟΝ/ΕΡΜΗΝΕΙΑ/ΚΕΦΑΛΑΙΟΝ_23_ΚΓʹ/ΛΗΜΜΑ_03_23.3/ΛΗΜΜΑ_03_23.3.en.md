# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 23:3

> **all things, therefore, whatsoever they tell you to keep, keep and do; but according to their works do not do. For they say and do not do.**[^1] (Matt. 23:3)

When he had stopped their mouths[^2] and had shown *them* to be sick with an incurable sickness, then he speaks about them; and he discourses about *their* manner of life and conduct,[^3] setting right the hearers, so that they might not despise the teachers, even if they be of corrupt life. And at the same time he shows that he is not opposed to the law either, but rather wills that the things of the law be done, even if those who teach these things be unworthy. For whatsoever the teachers say, he says, consider that Moses says these things, or rather God.

Therefore, then, must one do all things whatsoever they say, even if they be evil? We say, therefore, first, on the one hand, that a teacher would never even dare to urge anyone toward evil. Then, even if we grant this — that there will be someone urging *another* toward an evil life — such a one does not speak from the seat of Moses, nor from the law. But the Lord speaks about those seated in the seat of Moses, that is, about those who teach the things of the law. One must, therefore, listen to *those* teaching something from the divine law,[^4] even if they themselves do not do *it*.

[^1]: Greek «πάντα οὖν ὅσα ἂν εἴπωσιν ὑμῖν τηρεῖν, τηρεῖτε καὶ ποιεῖτε, κατὰ δὲ τὰ ἔργα αὐτῶν μὴ ποιεῖτε. Λέγουσι γὰρ καὶ οὐ ποιοῦσιν». (OCR "πάντα ον" = «πάντα οὖν».) The particle *οὖν* is rendered "therefore," *δὲ* "but," *γὰρ* "for." Theophylact's text has «ὅσα ἂν εἴπωσιν ὑμῖν τηρεῖν» ("whatsoever they tell you to keep"); the common text of the verse reads «ὅσα ἐὰν εἴπωσιν ὑμῖν», without «τηρεῖν» — his added infinitive is kept.
[^2]: Greek *ἐπεστόμισε* (from *ἐπιστομίζω*), literally "to put *something* upon the mouth," hence "to muzzle, to silence, to stop the mouth"; rendered "stopped their mouths" to keep the concrete image.
[^3]: Greek *περὶ βίου δὲ καὶ πολιτείας διαλέγεται*. The pair *βίος* / *πολιτεία* denotes "manner of life" and "conduct / way of living as a member of a community" (*πολιτεία* also = "citizenship, polity"); rendered "manner of life and conduct." (OCR "βίοι" = *βίου*.)
[^4]: OCR "ἀπὸ τοῦ θείοι όου" is securely «ἀπὸ τοῦ θείου νόμου» ("from the divine law"), confirmed by the parallel with «ἀπὸ τοῦ νόμου» just above.

# Matthew 23 (ΚΓʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 23:31

> **…you testify to yourselves that you are sons of those who murdered the prophets. (Matt. 23:31)**[^1]

He pronounces them wretched[^2] not because they build the tombs of the prophets — for this *is* pleasing to God — but because, feigning these things and, as it were, condemning their fathers, they were doing worse *deeds* than those *men*, and outstripped[^3] them in wickedness, lying openly, *in that they say* that "If we were in the days of our fathers, we would not have murdered the prophets"; for they themselves were raging[^4] to murder the Lord of the prophets. Wherefore also he says:

[^1]: Greek «μαρτυρεῖτε ἑαυτοῖς ὅτι υἱοί ἐστε τῶν φονευσάντων τοὺς προφήτας». The sentence's governing conjunction *ὥστε* ("so that / wherefore") stands at the end of the previous lemma; hence the opening ellipsis. *μαρτυρεῖτε ἑαυτοῖς*, literally "you bear witness to yourselves," i.e. against yourselves.
[^2]: Greek *ταλανίζει αὐτούς*, from *ταλανίζω* ("to call wretched, pronounce miserable"), picking up the "Woe" (*οὐαί*) of the preceding lemmata; rendered "pronounces them wretched."
[^3]: Greek *ὑπερηκόντισαν* (from *ὑπερακοντίζω*, "to shoot/throw beyond," hence "to surpass, outstrip"); rendered "outstripped."
[^4]: Greek *ἐλύττων* (imperfect of *λυττάω*, "to rage, be rabid/mad"); "they were raging to murder," conveying a frenzied eagerness.

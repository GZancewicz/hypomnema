# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 6:18

> **…that you may not appear to men fasting, but to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you. (Matt. 6:18)**[^1]

The ancients held as a sign of joy the being anointed with oil after the bath; and you, therefore, appear thus rejoicing.[^2] And oil is understood also as almsgiving. And our head is Christ, whom one must anoint with alms;[^3] and the face — that is, the senses — *one must* wash with tears.

[^1]: This lemma continues the sentence broken off in Lemma 22 («…ὅπως μὴ»); it opens here with «φανῇς» ("that you may appear"). The particle *ἀλλά* ("but") is present and rendered roman. The construction is "so that you may not appear to men fasting, but *to your Father*": the fasting is displayed not to men but to the Father who is in secret.
[^2]: Greek *χαίρων φαίνου* — an imperative ("show yourself / appear rejoicing"), echoing the *φανῇς* ("appear") of the lemma but now in a good sense: outward joy, not affected gloom.
[^3]: Theophylact reads the verse allegorically: *ἔλαιον* ("oil") = *ἐλεημοσύνη* ("almsgiving, mercy") — a play on the near-homophony of *ἔλαιον* and *ἔλεος* ("mercy"). Christ is the "head" to be anointed with alms; the "face," i.e. the *αἰσθήσεις* ("senses"), is washed with tears.

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 40 · Matt. 6:32

> **For all these things the nations seek after.[^1] (Matt. 6:32)**

The eating, on the one hand, he does not forbid; but the saying, "What shall we eat?", on the other hand, he forbids — which the rich say from the evening: "What shall we eat tomorrow?" You see that[^2] he forbids the luxury and the wanton excess.[^3]

[^1]: The word is OCR-garbled as «Κθνη»; securely = **«ἔθνη»**, "the nations" (also "the Gentiles / the heathen"). Rendered "the nations." The lemma continues the thought begun in the previous lemma («Do not therefore be anxious, saying: What shall we eat…»).
[^2]: The OCR reads «ὁρᾷς τι… κωλύει»; read as **«ὁρᾷς ὅτι… κωλύει»** ("you see that… he forbids"), which the syntax requires.
[^3]: Greek «ὕβρις» — "insolence, wanton excess, arrogance, outrage"; here paired with «τρυφή» ("luxury, self-indulgence") and denoting the overreaching excess of the rich. Rendered "wanton excess"; "insolence" or "arrogance" are equally defensible.

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 34 · Matt. 6:26

> **Look upon the birds of the heaven, that they do not sow, nor reap, nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them. Do you not much more excel them? (Matt. 6:26)**[^1]

[?Though able *to instruct us otherwise*,][^2] he makes mention of the birds, so that he may shame us — *seeing* that we are more irrational even than these. And God feeds them, having implanted[^3] in them a natural knowledge for the gathering of food. And which of you[^4]

[^1]: Greek *διαφέρετε* (from *διαφέρω*) means "differ from, surpass, be of more value than"; hence "do you not much more excel them?" (the familiar English "are ye not much better than they?" carries the same sense of surpassing worth). Kept literal as "excel." *πετεινὰ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ* = "the birds of the heaven / of the sky."
[^2]: The clause opening this comment is OCR-corrupted as «ΔυνάἸωάννην,» — the string «Ἰωάννην» ("John") is intrusive and cannot belong here. Only the participial stem *Δυνά-* (i.e. *δυνάμενος*, "being able / though able") is securely legible; what he was "able" to do is lost. The bracketed "*to instruct us otherwise*" is a conjectural filler, not translation, supplied only so the sentence reads — the underlying words are unrecoverable and must not be trusted. The certain point (from what survives) is that Christ *chose* to cite the birds in order to shame us.
[^3]: OCR «ἀνθεὶς»; read as *ἐνθείς* (aorist participle of *ἐντίθημι*, "having placed in, implanted"), which the sense requires — God placed in the birds a natural instinct for collecting food.
[^4]: The sentence breaks off at «Τίς δὲ ἐξ ὑμῶν» ("And which of you"); it is completed at the head of the next lemma with «μεριμνῶν, δύναται προσθεῖναι ἐπὶ τὴν ἡλικίαν αὐτοῦ πῆχυν ἕνα;» ("…by being anxious, is able to add to his stature one cubit?" — Matt. 6:27).

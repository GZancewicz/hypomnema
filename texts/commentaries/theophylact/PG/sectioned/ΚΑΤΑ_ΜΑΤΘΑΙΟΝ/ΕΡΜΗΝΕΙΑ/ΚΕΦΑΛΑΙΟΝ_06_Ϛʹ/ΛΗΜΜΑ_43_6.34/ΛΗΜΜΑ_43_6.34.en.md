# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 43 · Matt. 6:34

> **Therefore do not be anxious for the morrow; for the morrow will be anxious *for* its own things; sufficient *for* the day *is* its evil. (Matt. 6:34)**[^1]

By the "evil" of the day he means the affliction and the distress;[^2] as if *to say*: *It is* sufficient *for* you that you are afflicted[^3] on behalf of today; but if you will be anxious also on behalf of the morrow, when will you have leisure *for* God, *while* always afflicting yourself on behalf of the bodily *things*?

[^1]: Greek «Μὴ οὖν μεριμνήσητε εἰς τὴν αὔριον· ἡ γὰρ αὔριον μεριμνήσει τὰ ἑαυτῆς· ἀρκετὸν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ἡ κακία αὐτῆς.» The verb *μεριμνάω* is rendered consistently "be anxious" (also "care for, be concerned about"). *ἡ κακία αὐτῆς* is literally "the evil/badness of it (the day)"; Theophylact glosses this "evil" in the sense of trouble/affliction, not moral wickedness — see next note. Both clauses "for the morrow will be anxious" and "sufficient is its own evil" personify the day.
[^2]: Greek *κακία* ("evil, badness, malice") is here explained by two near-synonyms: *συντριβή* ("crushing, breaking, affliction" — literally a being-shattered) and *κάκωσις* ("ill-treatment, distress, affliction"). The gloss turns on the fact that *κακία* need not mean moral evil but can mean hardship/trouble; the pun between *κακία* and *κάκωσις* (both from the *κακ-* root) is lost in English.
[^3]: Greek *συντρίβῃ* / *συντρίβων* (present passive/active of *συντρίβω*, "to crush, shatter, wear down"). Rendered "afflicted" / "afflicting" to keep the connection with *συντριβή* in the preceding sentence; more literally "crushed" / "wearing yourself down."

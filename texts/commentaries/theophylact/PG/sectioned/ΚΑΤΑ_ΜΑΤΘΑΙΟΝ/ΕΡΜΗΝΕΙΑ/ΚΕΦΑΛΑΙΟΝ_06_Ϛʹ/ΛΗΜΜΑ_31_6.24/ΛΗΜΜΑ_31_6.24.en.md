# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 6:24

> **For either he will hate the one, and will love the other; or he will hold to the one, and will despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon. (Matt. 6:24)**[^1]

Do you see that it is not possible for the rich and unjust *man* to serve God? For the love of money casts him out, away from God,[^2]

[^1]: The lemma opens in the OCR as «Ἡ γὰρ τὸν ἕνα μισήσει…»; the initial «Ἡ» is the disjunctive «ἢ» ("either"), correlating with the following «ἢ» ("or"): «ἢ γὰρ… ἢ…» ("for either… or…"). Rendered directly from Theophylact's Greek, not conformed to the familiar English wording. «μαμμωνᾷ» is the Aramaic loan-word *mammon* (wealth/riches personified), kept as "mammon."
[^2]: The sentence breaks off with a comma at «ἀπὸ Θεοῦ» ("from God"); the thought is resumed at the head of the next lemma, whose exposition begins «Διὰ τοῦτο, ποῖον; Διὰ τὸ ἀπὸ Θεοῦ ἐκβάλλεσθαι ὑπὸ τῶν χρημάτων» ("'For this reason' — what sort? Because of being cast out from God by riches"). Greek *φιλοχρηματία* = "love of money."

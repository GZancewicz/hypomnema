# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 6:27

> **…*by* being anxious, is able to add to his stature one cubit? (Matt. 6:27)**[^1]

This he says: that even if you are anxious, you accomplish nothing when God does not will *it*.[^2] Why then do you needlessly cut yourself to pieces?[^3]

[^1]: This lemma-file opens mid-quotation; the verse begins in the previous lemma with «Τίς δὲ ἐξ ὑμῶν» ("And which of you"), so the whole is: «Τίς δὲ ἐξ ὑμῶν μεριμνῶν δύναται προσθεῖναι ἐπὶ τὴν ἡλικίαν αὐτοῦ πῆχυν ἕνα;» *ἡλικία* is ambiguous — it means either "stature" (bodily height) or "age / span of life." A "cubit" (*πῆχυς*) is properly a measure of length, which favors "stature," yet adding a cubit of *height* is odd, so many read "span of life." Rendered "stature" here, following the length-measure; the alternative "age / lifetime" is equally defensible. *μεριμνῶν* is a present participle, "being anxious / taking thought."
[^2]: OCR «γοῦτό φησιν, Ἐτι Κἂν μεριμνᾷς»; read as «τοῦτό φησιν· ὅτι κἂν μεριμνᾷς» ("this he says: that even if you are anxious"). The corrections «γοῦτό»→«τοῦτό» and «Ἐτι»→«ὅτι» are routine and low-stakes.
[^3]: Greek *κατακόπτεις σεαυτόν* — literally "cut yourself down / cut yourself to pieces," i.e. wear yourself out, torment yourself. *περιττῶς* = "needlessly, superfluously."

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 6:1

> **Take heed that you do not do your alms[^1] before men, in order to be seen by them; but if not, you have no reward with your Father who is in the heavens. (Matt. 6:1)**

Having led *them* up to the greatest of the virtues, love, now he does away also with vainglory, which follows upon good deeds. But see what he says: "Take heed" — as if he were speaking about a terrible beast: "take heed lest it tear you to pieces." And even if you give alms before men, but you do not do this in order to be seen, you are not condemned. But if you have vainglory as your aim, even if you should do it in your chamber, you are condemned. For God either punishes or crowns the intention.[^2]

[^1]: The lemma reads «τὴν ἐλεημοσύνην ὑμῶν» ("your alms / almsgiving"), whereas the modern critical text has «τὴν δικαιοσύνην ὑμῶν» ("your righteousness"). Theophylact follows the Byzantine/Received text, and his entire comment turns on almsgiving (ἐλεημοσύνη); the variant is preserved and rendered "alms" rather than "corrected" to "righteousness."
[^2]: Greek *γνώμη* — the inner disposition, intent, or settled judgment of the will (not a mere passing thought). Rendered "intention"; the point is that God rewards or punishes the *purpose* of the deed, not its outward form.

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 6:21

> **…is your treasure, there will your heart also be.[^1] (Matt. 6:21)**

Since he had cast out the disease of vainglory,[^2] he speaks henceforth about possessionlessness.[^3] For men acquire the greater part *of their goods* on account of vainglory. And he shows the unprofitableness of the treasure upon earth: that moth, on the one hand, and corrosion destroy foods and garments, but thieves, on the other hand, *destroy* gold and silver. Then, so that someone might not say to him that "But not all *people* are robbed," he says: Even if none of these things be *so*, yet at any rate this very thing — your being nailed[^4] to the anxiety about wealth — how *is it* not wretched? Wherefore he says:

[^1]: The lemma folder opens with the words *ἐστιν ὁ θησαυρὸς ὑμῶν, ἐκεῖ ἔσται καὶ ἡ καρδία ὑμῶν*; the governing *ὅπου γάρ* ("for where") was carried over at the end of the preceding lemma, so the whole reads: "for where is your treasure, there will your heart also be."
[^2]: Greek *κενοδοξία* ("vainglory, empty glory / conceit"); *νόσημα* is "disease, sickness." The reference is back to the preceding section on almsgiving, prayer, and fasting done to be seen by men.
[^3]: Greek *ἀκτημοσύνη*, literally "the state of having no possessions," a standard ascetic/monastic virtue ("possessionlessness / freedom from possessions"). Rendered literally rather than as a looser "poverty," since Theophylact's point is the deliberate renunciation of acquiring, not destitution.
[^4]: Greek *προσηλῶσθαι*, perfect passive of *προσηλόω*, "to nail fast to, fix as with nails." The vivid image is kept; it recurs in Lemma 29 (*προσηλώσῃς τὸν νοῦν*, "nail your mind"). The OCR reads *πᾶς οὐκ ἄθλιον*; securely *πῶς οὐκ ἄθλιον* ("how *is it* not wretched?"), a rhetorical question.

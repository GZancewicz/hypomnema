# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 38 · Matt. 6:30

> **But if the grass of the field, being today and tomorrow cast into an oven, God so clothes,[^1] *will he* not much more *clothe* you, *O* you of little faith? (Matt. 6:30)**

We learn from this, that one must not take thought for adornment.[^2] For this is *a thing* of the transient flowers. So that everyone who adorns himself is grass. But he said "you" — the rational *ones*, for whom he fashioned a soul and a body; and of little faith *are* all who are anxious. For if they had perfect faith in God, they would not be anxious so unremittingly.[^3]

[^1]: The lemma verb is OCR-garbled as «ἀμφιννυσιν»; securely = **«ἀμφιέννυσιν»** ("clothes"). In the apodosis «οὐ πολλῷ μᾶλλον ὑμᾶς» the verb is carried over by ellipsis; the supplied "*will he… clothe*" is italicized. The vocative «ὀλιγόπιστοι» ("you of little faith") has no address-particle in the Greek, so the supplied "*O*" is italicized.
[^2]: The opening word is OCR-garbled as «Αανθάνομεν»; securely = **«Μανθάνομεν»** ("we learn"). A low-stakes reconstruction, noted for consistency.
[^3]: The Greek adverb is OCR'd «ἐνενδότως», which is not a standard form; read here as **«ἀνενδότως»** ("unremittingly, without giving in, relentlessly"), which fits the sense — perfect faith would not fret so relentlessly. The reading is not certain; an alternative would take a form of ἐνδίδωμι ("yieldingly, slackly"). Flagged rather than silently resolved.

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 6:25

> **For this reason I say to you, do not be anxious for your soul what you shall eat and what you shall drink, nor for your body what you shall put on. (Matt. 6:25)**[^1]

"For this reason" — what sort *of reason*? Because of being cast out from God by riches.[^2] But the soul does not eat — for it is incorporeal — but he said this according to the common usage. For the soul seems not to endure being in the body, unless the flesh be nourished. But he does not abolish[^3] the working, but the giving of ourselves wholly to *worldly* cares, and the neglecting of God — this he forbids. Since one must both till the ground, but also take thought for the soul.

[^1]: Greek *ψυχή* here spans "soul" and "life"; Theophylact's whole comment turns on the literal sense "soul," since he immediately objects that "the soul does not eat, for it is incorporeal." Rendered "soul" throughout to keep his argument visible, though the ordinary force of «μὴ μεριμνᾶτε τῇ ψυχῇ» is "do not be anxious for your life." *μεριμνάω* = "be anxious / take thought / be careful for."
[^2]: This resumes the sentence broken off at the end of the previous lemma ("…the love of money casts him out, away from God"). Greek «Διὰ τοῦτο, ποῖον;» literally "'For this reason' — what kind *of one*?", i.e. he asks which cause is meant.
[^3]: OCR «Οὐκ Ἀνβάλλει δὲ τὸ ἐργάζεσθαι»; the verb is corrupt. The sense required (paired with «τοῦτο κωλύει», "this he forbids," just below) is "he does not do away with / abolish working," best recovered as *ἀναιρεῖ* ("abolishes, does away with"). Translated by that sense; the garbled form is flagged rather than trusted.

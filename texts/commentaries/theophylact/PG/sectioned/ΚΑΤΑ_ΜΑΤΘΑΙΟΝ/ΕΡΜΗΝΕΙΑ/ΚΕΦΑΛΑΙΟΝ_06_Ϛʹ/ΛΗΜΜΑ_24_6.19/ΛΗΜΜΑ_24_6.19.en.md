# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 6:19

> **Do not treasure up for yourselves treasures upon the earth, where moth and rust[^1] destroy, and where thieves break through[^2] and steal. (Matt. 6:19)**[^3]

[^1]: Greek *βρῶσις*, literally "an eating / consuming, corrosion" — the wearing-away that consumes stored goods (whence "rust" in the common English rendering). Rendered "rust" for the plain sense; the underlying word is the act of "eating away." The verb *ἀφανίζει* ("makes to vanish, destroys") is the same *ἀφαν-* root played on in Lemma 21, and stands in the singular though it governs both "moth" and "rust."
[^2]: Greek *διορύσσουσι* ("dig through, break through") — of thieves tunnelling through a wall. The OCR «διορύσοουσι» is a slip for **«διορύσσουσι»**.
[^3]: The OCR «δποῦ» is a slip for **«ὅπου»** ("where"), and «κλέπταε» for **«κλέπται»** ("thieves"). The lemma's closing quotation mark is absent in the source; the scripture runs on unbroken into the next lemma.

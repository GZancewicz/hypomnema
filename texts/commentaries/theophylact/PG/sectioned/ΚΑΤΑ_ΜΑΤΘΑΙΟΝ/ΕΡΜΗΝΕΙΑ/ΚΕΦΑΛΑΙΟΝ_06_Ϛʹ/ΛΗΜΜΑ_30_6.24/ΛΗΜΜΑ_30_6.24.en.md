# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 6:24

> **No one is able to serve two masters. (Matt. 6:24)**

He calls "two masters" those who enjoin contrary things, such as God and Mammon.[^1] But do we ourselves make the devil our master?[^2] — just as *we make* the belly *our* god.[^3] But by nature and truly Lord *is* God; and Mammon is unrighteousness.[^4]

[^1]: Greek *Μαμμωνᾶς* (Mammon), the Aramaic word for "wealth, riches," left untranslated by the Evangelist and here by Theophylact; kept as "Mammon." The OCR *Δύς κυρίους* is securely *Δύο κυρίους* ("two masters").
[^2]: Greek *Ἡμεῖς δὲ ποιοῦμεν κύριον ἡμῶν τὸν διάβολον;* — punctuated as a question in the source. The sense is reproachful: it is we ourselves who set up the devil as lord over us. An alternative reading takes it as a statement ("But we make the devil our lord"), but the interrogative mark is retained.
[^3]: An allusion to Phil. 3:19, "whose god *is their* belly" (*ὧν ὁ θεὸς ἡ κοιλία*). "*we make*" and "*our*" are supplied; the Greek has only *ὥσπερ καὶ τὴν κοιλίαν θεόν* ("as also the belly a god").
[^4]: Greek *ἡ ἀδικία*, "unrighteousness, injustice"; cf. the "mammon of unrighteousness" (*μαμωνᾶς τῆς ἀδικίας*, Luke 16:9). Theophylact defines Mammon precisely as unrighteousness itself.

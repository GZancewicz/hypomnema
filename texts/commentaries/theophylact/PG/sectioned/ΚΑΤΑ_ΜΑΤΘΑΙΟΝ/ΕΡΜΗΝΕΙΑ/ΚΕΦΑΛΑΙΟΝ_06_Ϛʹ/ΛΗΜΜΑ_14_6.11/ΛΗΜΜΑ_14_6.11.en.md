# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 6:11

> **Our bread, the *epiousion*, give us today. (Matt. 6:11)**[^1]

"*Epiousion*," that is, the *bread* sufficient for our substance and constitution, he says; and he takes away the anxiety about tomorrow. And the body of Christ, too, is bread *epiousion*[^2] — of which we pray to partake without condemnation.

[^1]: Greek «Τὸν ἄρτον ἡμῶν τὸν ἐπιούσιον δὸς ἡμῖν σήμερον» (Matt. 6:11). The adjective *ἐπιούσιος* is famously untranslatable — it occurs only in the Lord's Prayer and its meaning was already disputed in antiquity ("for the coming day," "necessary for existence," "supersubstantial"). It is left transliterated here precisely because Theophylact treats it *as a word* and expounds it by etymology; see next note. The article-plus-adjective construction «τὸν ἄρτον … τὸν ἐπιούσιον» is rendered "the bread, the *epiousion*."
[^2]: Theophylact glosses the word by splitting it as «ἐπὶ τῇ οὐσίᾳ» — "*upon / with respect to* the substance": «τὸν ἐπὶ τῇ οὐσίᾳ καὶ συστάσει ἡμῶν αὐτάρκη» ("*the bread* self-sufficient for our substance and constitution/composition"). This derivation (from οὐσία, "being/substance," rather than from ἐπιέναι, "to come upon") is why the same word can then be applied to the eucharistic body of Christ as the true "substantial" bread. «αὐτάρκη» = "sufficient, self-adequate"; «σύστασις» = "constitution, composition, holding-together." The wordplay on οὐσία is lost in English and is preserved by keeping *epiousion* transliterated in the body.

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 6:3

> **But when you do alms, let not your left *hand* know what your right *hand* does. (Matt. 6:3)**

He said this hyperbolically: that, if *it were* possible, escape the notice even of yourself.[^1] Or also otherwise:[^2] the left *is* vainglory, the right *is* almsgiving; therefore let not vainglory know the almsgiving.

[^1]: Greek *καὶ σεαυτὸν λάνθανε* — literally "escape even your own notice," i.e. let the deed be hidden even from yourself. The verb *λανθάνω* ("to be hidden, escape notice") has no idiomatic single-word English equivalent in the active imperative; rendered "escape the notice even of yourself."
[^2]: The OCR here is corrupt: the source prints *ϟἩ καὶ ἄλλως* for **«Ἢ καὶ ἄλλως»** ("Or also otherwise"), and the following clause is scrambled as *ἀριστερᾷ, ἡ κενοδοξία δεξιὰ, ἡ ἐλεημοσύνη*, securely **«ἡ ἀριστερὰ, ἡ κενοδοξία· ἡ δεξιὰ, ἡ ἐλεημοσύνη»** ("the left, vainglory; the right, almsgiving"). The reconstruction is secure from the sense.

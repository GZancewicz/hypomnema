# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 6:13a

> **And bring us not into temptation. (Matt. 6:13)**[^1]

We humans are weak; therefore one must not cast oneself into temptations, but, having fallen into them, one must pray not to be swallowed up by the temptation. For the *man* who has been swallowed up and defeated, that *man* was brought into the pit[^2] of temptation — but not the *man* who has indeed fallen in, yet conquered.

[^1]: Greek «Καὶ μὴ εἰσενέγκῃς ἡμᾶς εἰς πειρασμόν» (Matt. 6:13). *πειρασμός* means both "temptation" (enticement to sin) and "trial, testing" (affliction); Theophylact's exposition — one should not throw oneself in, but pray not to be *overcome* — plays on both senses. Rendered consistently "temptation" to keep the single Greek word visible, but the "trial/test" overtone is active throughout.
[^2]: Greek «βόθρον», "pit, hole, trench" (a trap-pit into which one is swallowed down); it answers to «καταποθῆναι / καταποθεὶς» ("to be swallowed up / gulped down") earlier in the sentence. The whole contrast: «ὁ ἐμπεσὼν μὲν, νικήσας δέ» — "the one who has on the one hand fallen in, but on the other hand conquered" — is here compressed to "has indeed fallen in, yet conquered" to keep the μὲν … δέ antithesis without over-weighting a single clause.

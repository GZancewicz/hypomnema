# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 6:14

> **For if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will forgive you also. (Matt. 6:14)**[^1]

Again he teaches us to be free from the remembrance of wrongs.[^2] And he reminds us of the Father, so that we may reverence him, and may not become savage,[^3] being children of such a one.

[^1]: The OCR reads «Ἔὴν γὰρ ἀφῆτε τοῖς ἀνθρώποις τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν, ἀφήσει καὶ ὑμῖν ὁ Πατήρ ὑμῶν ὁ οὐράνιος»; «Ἔὴν» is a plain OCR slip for **«Ἐὰν»** ("if"). The particle *γάρ* ("for") is present in the text and is rendered roman. The word order places «καὶ ὑμῖν» ("you also") before the verb's subject; rendered "will forgive you also."
[^2]: Greek *ἀμνησικάκους* (from *ἀμνησίκακος*, "not-remembering-evil"): bearing no grudge, not holding wrongs in memory. Rendered "free from the remembrance of wrongs" to keep the compound's force visible; a smoother gloss would be "forgiving / unresentful."
[^3]: Greek *μὴ ἐκθηριώμεθα* (from *ἐκθηριόομαι*, "to be made savage / turned into a wild beast"): "may not become brutish / grow beast-like." The verb is built on *θηρίον* ("wild beast").

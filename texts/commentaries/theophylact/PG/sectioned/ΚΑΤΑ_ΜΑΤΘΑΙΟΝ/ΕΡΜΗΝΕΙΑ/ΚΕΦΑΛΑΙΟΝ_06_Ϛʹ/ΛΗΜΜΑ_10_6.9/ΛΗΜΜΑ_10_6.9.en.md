# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 6:9

> **Thus, therefore, pray you: Our Father, who *is* in the heavens.**

A vow[^1] is one thing, and a prayer another. For a vow, on the one hand, is a promise *made* to God — as when someone promises to abstain from wine or from something else; but prayer, on the other hand, is a request for good things. And having said "Father," he shows you of what good things you have been deemed worthy, having become a son of God; and in saying "in the heavens," he showed you your fatherland and the paternal house. For if you wish to have God as Father, look toward the heavens, and not toward earth. And you do not say "My Father," but "Our Father," so that you may have all *men* as brothers of one Father.

[^1]: Theophylact turns on a lexical distinction between two near-synonyms: *εὐχή* and *προσευχή*, both ordinarily "prayer." He fixes *εὐχή* in its older sense of "vow" (*ὑπόσχεσις πρὸς Θεόν*, "a promise to God," e.g. a vow of abstinence), and reserves *προσευχή* for "prayer" proper (*ἀγαθῶν αἴτησις*, "a request for good things"). English "prayer" for both would erase the point; *εὐχή* is rendered "vow" and *προσευχή* "prayer."

# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 6:29

> **Solomon in all his glory arrayed himself as one of these. (Matt. 6:29)**[^1]

Not only from the irrational winged *creatures* does he shame us, but also from the lilies that wither. For if God so adorned those, although there being no need *of it*, how much more will he fully supply the need of our garments? And he shows that, even if you should be anxious much, you will not be able to be adorned as the lilies; seeing indeed that Solomon, the most prudent and most given to luxury, throughout all his kingdom did not have the strength to array himself *with* anything of such a kind.

[^1]: Matt. 6:29. The negation governing this clause — «οὐδέ» ("not even") — stands at the end of the previous lemma («Λέγω δὲ ὑμῖν, ὅτι οὐδὲ…», "But I say to you, that not even…"), so the sense running across the break is "…that not even Solomon in all his glory arrayed himself as one of these." The verb περιεβάλετο is middle ("put around himself, arrayed himself"); rendered "arrayed himself." Translated from Theophylact's Greek, not conformed to any English Bible.

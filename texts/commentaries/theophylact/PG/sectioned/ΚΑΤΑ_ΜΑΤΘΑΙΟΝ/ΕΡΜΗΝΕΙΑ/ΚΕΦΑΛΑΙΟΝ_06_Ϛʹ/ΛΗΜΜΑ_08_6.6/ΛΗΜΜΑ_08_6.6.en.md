# Matthew 6 (Ϛʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 6:7

> **And when praying, do not babble,[^1] as the Gentiles do. (Matt. 6:7)**

Babbling[^1] is idle chatter — such as to ask for something of earthly things: glory, wealth, victory. But stammering[^2] is inarticulate utterance, like that of little children. You, accordingly, do not be a chatterer. For they think that in their much-speaking they will be heard. One must not make long prayers, but short ones, yet frequent, and, uttering few words, persevere in prayer.

[^1]: Greek *μὴ βαττολογήσητε* (verb), glossed by Theophylact with the noun *βαττολογία* — "battology," empty, repetitive, garrulous speech. He plays on the word's sound and etymology, which cannot be reproduced in English; rendered "babble"/"babbling." He immediately distinguishes it from a related-sounding term (see next note).
[^2]: Greek *βατταρισμός* — "stammering, stuttering," the *inarticulate* (*ἄναρθρος*) speech of small children. Theophylact separates the two near-homonyms: *βαττολογία* is meaningful but idle/excessive talk (asking God for worldly things), whereas *βατταρισμός* is mere unformed sound. The paronomasia *βαττολογία / βατταρισμός* is lost in translation.

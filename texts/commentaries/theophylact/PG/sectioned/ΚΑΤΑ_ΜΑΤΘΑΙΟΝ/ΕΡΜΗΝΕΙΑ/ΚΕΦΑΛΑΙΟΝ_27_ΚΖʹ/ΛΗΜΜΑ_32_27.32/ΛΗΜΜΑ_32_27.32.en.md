# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 27:32

> **But as they were going out they found a man, a Cyrenian, by name Simon; this *man* they pressed into service,[^1] that he might carry his cross.**

The three, on the one hand, say that Simon bore the cross of Jesus; but John, on the other hand, says that the Lord himself bore *it*. It is likely, therefore, that both happened. For at the beginning, on the one hand, Jesus bore the cross, no one being willing to bear *it*; but on the way, having found Simon, they laid the cross upon him.

But do you learn this also, that "Simon" is interpreted "obedience";[^2] therefore he who has obedience, that one takes up the cross of Christ. But also Cyrene, being of the Pentapolis,[^3] signifies the five senses, *which are* compelled to take up the cross.

And[^4]

[^1]: Greek *ἠγγάρευσαν* (from *ἀγγαρεύω*, a loan-word of Persian origin) — to requisition, to press into forced service the way a courier or an occupying soldier could commandeer a passer-by; hence "pressed into service" / "compelled."
[^2]: Theophylact's etymology: *Σίμων ἑρμηνεύεται ὑπακοή* ("Simon is interpreted 'obedience'"). The Hebrew name Shimʿon is linked to the root *šāmaʿ*, "to hear," and so to hearkening/obedience (*ὑπακοή*). Offered as a spiritual reading, not a lexical claim.
[^3]: Cyrene was the chief city of the Libyan Pentapolis ("five-city" region); Theophylact takes the "five" as figuring the five bodily senses.
[^4]: The Gospel text breaks off; "And" (*Καὶ*, OCR *ιΚαὶ*) begins the quotation continued in the next lemma (27:33).

# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 43 · Matt. 27:40

> **"If thou art *the* Son of God, come down from the cross,"[^1] (Matt. 27:40)**

— so that, having been provoked, he might come down, and the salvation of all through the cross might be overturned. Wherefore Christ both is *the* Son of God and was not persuaded by the enemy, so that you too might learn that one must not yield to the contrivances of the devil, but *must* do the good, even if men are going to hold an evil opinion *of one*.[^2]

[^1]: The lemma quotes Matt. 27:40, «Εἰ υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ θεοῦ, κατάβηθι ἀπὸ τοῦ σταυροῦ» ("If thou art *the* Son of God, come down from the cross"), the mockery of the passers-by (echoed also in v. 42). The folder metadata assigns this lemma to 27:43, but the words expounded are those of v. 40. *Υἱός* is anarthrous (no article); the supplied "*the*" is italicized. The quotation continues directly from the previous lemma, which ends "and the devil suggested those who said:" — so this is the content of that suggestion.
[^2]: Greek *πονηρὰν ὑπόληψιν ... ἔχειν*: *ὑπόληψις* is "opinion, notion, supposition"; *τεχνάσματα* ("contrivances, artful devices") of the devil. The verb *πείθεσθαι* ("be persuaded / yield / obey") is repeated: Christ "was not persuaded" by the enemy, and "one must not be persuaded" by his devices.

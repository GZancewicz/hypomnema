# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 48 · Matt. 27:46

> **"Why hast thou forsaken me?"[^1] (Matt. 27:46)**

he said, so that he might show that he truly became man, and not according to appearance;[^2] for indeed man has a natural appetite for life, being a lover of life. Therefore, just as he agonized and was distressed[^3] before the cross, showing the timidity[^4] that belongs to us from nature, so also now he says:[^5]

[^1]: Greek *ἵνα τί με ἐγκατέλιπες* — see Lemma 44, n. 3. The lemma folder assigns this to 27:49, but the words expounded are Jesus' cry of 27:46; in the Greek the sentence opens *Τὸ δέ* ("And the *saying*…"), picking up the phrase left hanging at the end of Lemma 47.
[^2]: Greek *οὐ κατὰ φαντασίαν* ("not according to appearance / not in phantasm") — an anti-docetic point: Christ's dread was real, not a mere semblance of humanity.
[^3]: Greek *ἠγωνίασε καὶ ἠδημόνησε* — the verbs of Gethsemane (cf. *ἀδημονεῖν*, Matt. 26:37); "was in agony and was sore troubled / distressed."
[^4]: Greek *δειλία*, "cowardice, timidity, shrinking fear" — here the natural human shrinking from death, which Christ took on together with our nature.
[^5]: The exposition breaks off; it continues in the next lemma, which repeats «Why hast thou forsaken me?» and adds "displaying our natural love of life."

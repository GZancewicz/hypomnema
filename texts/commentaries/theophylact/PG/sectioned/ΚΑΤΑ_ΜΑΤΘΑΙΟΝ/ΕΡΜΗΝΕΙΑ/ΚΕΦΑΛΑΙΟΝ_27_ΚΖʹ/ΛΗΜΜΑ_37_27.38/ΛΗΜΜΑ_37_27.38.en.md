# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 27:38

> **Then are crucified together with him two robbers,[^1] one on *the* right, and one on *the* left;[^2] but those…** *(Matt. 27:38)*

This lemma carries only Theophylact's scripture quotation, and it breaks off mid-sentence (οἱ δὲ…, "but those…," continued by the participle in the next lemma). He defers his exposition of the whole crucifixion passage (Matt. 27:38–44) to Lemma 42, where the comment opens «Πρὸς διαβολὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ συσταυροῦσιν αὐτῷ δύο λῃστάς…».

[^1]: Greek *λῃσταί* — "robbers, bandits, brigands"; not merely "thieves" (*κλέπται*), but violent malefactors. Retained as "robbers" throughout for consistency, since Theophylact's exposition turns on their being *παράνομοι* ("lawless *men*").
[^2]: Greek *ἐξ εὐωνύμων* — literally "from *the* well-named / left-hand *side*." The word *εὐώνυμος* ("of good name, auspicious") is a euphemism for the left, the ill-omened side, avoided by direct naming; here it simply means "on the left," paired with *ἐκ δεξιῶν*, "on the right." The supplied "*the*" renders the anarthrous plural substantives.

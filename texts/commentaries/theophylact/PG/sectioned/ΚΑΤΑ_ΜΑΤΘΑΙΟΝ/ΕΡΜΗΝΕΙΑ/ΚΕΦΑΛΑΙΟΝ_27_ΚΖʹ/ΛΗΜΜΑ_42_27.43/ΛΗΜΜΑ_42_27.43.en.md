# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 27:43–44

> **He has trusted in God, let him deliver him now, if he wants him;[^1] for he said that, I am God's Son.[^2] And the same thing also the robbers who had been crucified together with him were reproaching against him.** *(Matt. 27:43–44)*

For *the* slandering[^3] of Christ they crucify together with him two robbers, so that he too might be reckoned such a lawless *man* as those. And these were a type of the two peoples, both the Jewish and the Gentile; for both *were* lawless, and reproaching Christ; just as also these robbers formerly were reproaching *him*, both of them; but afterwards the one, having recognized him, confessed *him* king — wherefore also he said: "Remember me, Lord, [when you come][^4] in your kingdom" — so therefore also the Gentile people confessed Christ. But the other robber, the Jewish people, was blaspheming; and the devil[^3] put forward those saying:[^5]

[^1]: Greek *εἰ θέλει αὐτόν* — literally "if he wants him / desires him." The clause echoes Ps. 21[22]:9 LXX, *σωσάτω αὐτόν, ὅτι θέλει αὐτόν* ("let him save him, since he delights in him"), where *θέλει* with the accusative carries the Semitic sense "takes pleasure in, delights in." Rendered literally "if he wants him"; the Psalm sense is "if he delights in him."
[^2]: Greek *Θεοῦ εἰμι Υἱός* — word order "of God I am Son," with *Θεοῦ* fronted for emphasis and *Υἱός* anarthrous. Rendered "I am God's Son" to keep the emphasis without supplying an article the Greek lacks.
[^3]: Theophylact frames the sentence with a deliberate wordplay: it opens *Πρὸς διαβολὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ* ("for the *slandering* of Christ," *διαβολή*) and closes with *ὁ διάβολος* ("the *devil*"). Both derive from *διαβάλλω*, "to slander, throw *accusation* across"; the devil is the arch-slanderer whose work the crucifiers unwittingly perform. Rendered "slandering" / "devil" to keep both English words visibly cognate to the same Greek root.
[^4]: The quotation is Luke 23:42. The OCR is corrupt here, reading «Μνήσθητί μου, Κύριε, Μν τῇ βασιλείᾳ σου», where the stray *Μν* before *τῇ βασιλείᾳ σου* is a garbling. The securely legible words are *Μνήσθητί μου, Κύριε … τῇ βασιλείᾳ σου* ("Remember me, Lord … your kingdom"); the bracketed "[when you come]" restores the expected *ὅταν ἔλθῃς* (with *ἐν* before "your kingdom"), the standard form of the verse, but is not itself recoverable from the OCR.
[^5]: The exposition breaks off leading into the next lemma: *ὑπέβαλε τοὺς λέγοντας·* ("put forward / prompted those saying:"), whose words are supplied in Lemma 43, «Εἰ Υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ, κατάβηθι ἀπὸ τοῦ σταυροῦ» ("If you are Son of God, come down from the cross"). Greek *ὑπέβαλε* (from *ὑποβάλλω*, "throw under, suggest, prompt, instigate, put words in the mouth of"): here the devil is the prompter behind the mockers' words; rendered "put forward," with the sense "prompted / incited."

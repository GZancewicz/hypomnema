# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 70 · Matt. 27:66

> **[*But they,*] having gone, made the tomb secure, having sealed the stone, together with the guard. (Matt. 27:66)**

He does not name the Sabbath "Sabbath," because it was not even a Sabbath — so far as depended on the wickedness of the Jews.[^1] For, the law directing that on the day of the Sabbath no one should move from his place,[^2] the lawless Jews are gathered together unto the foreign-born Pilate,[^3] instead of the customary assembly.[^4] But they, on the one hand, are moved by their own wickedness[^5] to approach Pilate and to make the tomb secure; but this, on the other hand, is God's dispensation,[^6] in order that the resurrection might come to pass with the enemies for witnesses,[^7] and with the tomb sealed and guarded.

But it is worthwhile to inquire whence the Jews, having learned *it*, said that on the third day he would be raised; for surely the Lord did not say this plainly and nakedly.[^8] It is possible, therefore, to say that from the example of Jonah they knew this. For Christ said that[^9]

[^1]: Greek *ὅσον ἐπὶ τῇ κακίᾳ τῶν Ἰουδαίων* ("so far as concerned / as it depended on the wickedness of the Jews"). The OCR prints *ἄσον* for *ὅσον*, a secure correction. The idiom *ὅσον ἐπί* + dative means "as far as X is concerned, so far as it rests with X"; the sense is that, on their side, by their conduct, they had turned the day into no Sabbath at all.
[^2]: Greek *μηδένα κινεῖσθαι ἐκ τοῦ τόπου αὐτοῦ* ("that no one should move from his place"). The OCR prints *κικεῖσθαι* for *κινεῖσθαι*. The allusion is to the sabbath ordinance of Exod. 16:29 (LXX, *μὴ ἐκπορευέσθω ἕκαστος ἐκ τοῦ τόπου αὐτοῦ*).
[^3]: Greek *τὸν ἀλλόφυλον Πιλᾶτον* ("the foreign / of-another-tribe / Gentile Pilate"). *ἀλλόφυλος* is the LXX word for a foreigner or Gentile (esp. the Philistines); rendered "foreign-born" to mark that Pilate is outside the covenant people, the point of Theophylact's contrast.
[^4]: Greek *ἀντὶ τῆς νενομισμένης συνάξεως* ("instead of the customary/lawful assembly"). *νενομισμένη* (perfect passive participle of *νομίζω*) means "prescribed by custom or law, habitual"; *σύναξις* is the appointed sabbath gathering (later the technical word for the liturgical synaxis). The barb: on the sabbath they hold, not their lawful assembly, but an unlawful one before a Gentile.
[^5]: Greek *ὑπὸ τῆς ἑαυτῶν κινοῦνται πονηρίας* ("are moved by their own wickedness"). The OCR prints *Ἀαυτῶν* for *ἑαυτῶν*. Note the deliberate echo of *κινεῖσθαι* above (the law forbade them to "move" from their place, yet their own malice "moves" them to Pilate).
[^6]: Greek *Θεοῦ… οἰκονομία* ("God's dispensation / economy / providential arrangement"). *οἰκονομία* is a loaded term for God's ordering of salvation; "dispensation" is retained as the conventional English rendering, with "providential arrangement" as its plain sense.
[^7]: Greek *ὑπὸ μάρτυσι τοῖς ἐχθροῖς* ("with the enemies as witnesses / under the enemies for witnesses"). The point is evidentiary: the very foes who sealed and watched the tomb become unimpeachable witnesses to the resurrection.
[^8]: Greek *σαφῶς καὶ γυμνῶς* ("plainly and nakedly / openly and undisguisedly"). The pairing means "in clear, unveiled terms"; *γυμνῶς* ("nakedly, without covering") contrasts with the veiled, figurative speech (the sign of Jonah, the temple) that Theophylact adduces next.
[^9]: The sentence breaks off at *ὅτι* ("that"); it is completed in Lemma 71, which supplies Christ's words about Jonah («just as Jonah was three days in the belly of the sea-monster…»).

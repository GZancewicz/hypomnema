# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 27:2

> **…having bound him they led *him* away, and delivered *him* to Pontius Pilate the governor. (Matt. 27:2)**

See the devil, how he held them all fast, having persuaded *them* to murder on such days — on which there was need of many sacrifices and offerings, for the other sins, and of purity and purification;[^1] but they both bind *him* and lead *him* away to the governor Pilate, who on the one hand was from Pontus, but on the other, being subject as a tributary to the Romans, was sent *as* governor of Judea.[^2] And they delivered the Lord to Pilate as though *he were* seditious, and *as one who* had plotted against the king.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «ἐν αἷς πολλῶν ἔδει θυσιῶν καὶ προσφορῶν, ὑπὲρ τῶν ἄλλων ἁμαρτιῶν, καὶ ἁγνείας καὶ καθάρσεως» — "on which there was need of many sacrifices and offerings, for the other [i.e. one's remaining] sins, and of purity and purification." The point: the Jewish leaders committed murder precisely during the festival days that called for ritual purity. OCR: *Ἵε* at the head is securely *Ἴδε* ("See"); *κν* is *ἐν* ("on/in").
[^2]: Greek «ὃς ἀπὸ Πόντου μὲν ἦν, Ῥωμαίοις δὲ ὑποτελῶν, ἀπεστάλη ἡγεμὼν τῆς Ἰουδαίας». Theophylact glosses the name *Ποντίῳ* (Pontius) by deriving it from *Πόντος* (Pontus). *ὑποτελῶν*, "paying tribute / being subject/tributary" — Pilate, though from Pontus, held his post as a Roman subordinate. OCR *Πλάτον* is *Πιλάτον* (Pilate); *Ἰουδαίαςι* is *Ἰουδαίας*.
[^3]: Greek «ὡς δῆθεν στασιώδη, καὶ κατὰ τοῦ βασιλέως μελετήσαντα». *δῆθεν*, "as though, ostensibly, supposedly" (with an ironic edge — the charge was a pretext). *βασιλεύς* here = the (Roman) emperor / Caesar, against whom sedition would be charged; rendered "king" to keep Theophylact's word. *μελετήσαντα*, "having practised/plotted/schemed."

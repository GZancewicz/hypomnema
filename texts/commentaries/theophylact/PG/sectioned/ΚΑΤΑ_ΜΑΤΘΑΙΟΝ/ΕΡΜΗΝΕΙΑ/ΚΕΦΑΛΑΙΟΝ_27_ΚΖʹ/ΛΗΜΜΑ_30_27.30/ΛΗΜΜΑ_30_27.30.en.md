# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 27:30 (Ps. 117:16 LXX)

> **The right hand of the Lord exalted me.[^1]**

And through the ears having received the revilings,[^2] he healed the whispering of the serpent that had entered in through the ears of Eve.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Δεξιὰ Κυρίου ὕψωσέ με» — Ps. 117:16 LXX (Ps. 118:16 Heb.). The citation completes the sentence begun at Lemma 29 ("our flesh … which the Lord took up, just as David also says"). The connection turns on *δεξιά*, "right hand": the reed was set in Christ's *right hand* (Lemma 27, *ἐπὶ τὴν δεξιὰν αὐτοῦ*), and the reed signifies the weak flesh which the Lord assumed and, so assuming, exalted — "the right hand of the Lord exalted me." The word-play is native to the Greek and largely lost in English.
[^2]: Greek *διὰ … τῶν ὤτων δεξάμενος τὰς δυσφημίας* — "having received the revilings (*δυσφημίας*: evil-speakings, blasphemies) through the ears." Note the assonance *δεξιά … δεξάμενος* ("right hand … having received," both from the root *δεχ-/δεξ-*), continuing the play of the previous clause. "The ears" resumes the last member of the catalogue of outrages at Lemma 29 (the ears insulted by the mocking words).
[^3]: Greek *ἰάσατο τὸν διὰ τῶν ὤτων τῆς Εὔας ἐπεισελθόντα τοῦ ὄφεως ψιθυρισμόν* — "he healed the serpent's whispering (*ψιθυρισμόν*) that had entered in through the ears of Eve." A recapitulation motif: the wound came in through Eve's *ears* by the serpent's whisper, and is healed by Christ's *ears* receiving the soldiers' reviling.

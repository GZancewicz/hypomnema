# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 27:5

> **…having thrown the pieces of silver in the temple, he withdrew, and having gone away he hanged himself. (Matt. 27:5)**

*See*, Judas comes to reflect too late,[^1] and he repents, on the one hand, but not well; for to condemn oneself, on the one hand, *is* good, but to hang oneself *is* devilish. For, not bearing the reproaches *that would come* afterward, he removes himself from life, *when it was* necessary rather to weep and to propitiate the *one who had been* betrayed.[^2]

But some say that Judas, being avaricious, supposed that he himself would both gain the pieces of silver by betraying Christ, and *that* Christ would not be killed, but would escape the Jews, as he had many times escaped; but then, having seen him condemned and already sentenced to die, he repented, since the matter had turned out contrary to what he had supposed. Wherefore also he hanged himself, so that he might get before Jesus into Hades, and, having supplicated *him*, might obtain salvation.[^3]

Nevertheless, know that he put, on the one hand, his neck into the noose, having hanged himself from a certain tree; but the tree having bent down, he survived, God willing either to preserve him unto repentance, or unto *his becoming* an example and a shame.[^4] For they say that he fell into a dropsical disease, so that where a wagon easily passes through, he *himself* was not able to pass through. Then, having fallen headlong, he burst asunder[^5] — that is to say, he was torn apart, as Luke says in the Acts.

[^1]: Greek «Ἐι ὑστεροβουλίᾳ γίνεται ὁ Ἰούδας». The opening word *Ἐι* is OCR-corrupt; from context it is an imperative/interjection ("See," *Ἴδε* — as at the head of Lemma 2 — or "Behold"), taken so here. *ὑστεροβουλία* is literally "after-counsel, wisdom-too-late, second thoughts that come only after the deed" (*ὕστερος* "later" + *βουλή* "counsel"); rendered "comes to reflect too late." The whole clause: Judas arrives at belated regret.
[^2]: Greek «δέον δακρῦσαι καὶ ἐξιλεώσασθαι τὸν προδοθέντα» — "*it being* necessary to weep and to propitiate the one *who had been* betrayed [i.e. Christ]." *ἐξιλεώσασθαι*, "to propitiate, appease, make atonement to." *δαιμονιῶδες* above, "demonic / devilish" (of a demon), balanced against *καλόν* ("good"): self-condemnation is good, self-slaughter is of the demon.
[^3]: Greek «ἵνα προλάβῃ τὸν Ἰησοῦν ἐν τῷ ᾅδῃ, καὶ ἱκετεύσας σωτηρίας τεύξηται». *προλάβῃ*, "might get before / anticipate / reach first" — the notion that Judas hanged himself to arrive in Hades ahead of the descending Christ and there entreat him. Reported as others' opinion (*Τινὲς δὲ λέγουσιν*), not endorsed.
[^4]: Greek «τοῦ δὲ δένδρου κλιθέντος, ἐπέζησε, τοῦ Θεοῦ θέλοντος αὐτὸν ἢ εἰς μετάνοιαν συντηρῆσαι, ἢ εἰς παραδειγματισμὸν καὶ αἰσχύνην». *κλιθέντος*, "having bent/leaned/inclined down" (the branch gave way). *παραδειγματισμός*, "a making-into-a-public-example, exposure to shame." OCR *Κθηκε* is *ἔθηκε* ("he put/placed"). This harmonizes Matthew's hanging (27:5) with the bursting of Acts 1:18.
[^5]: Greek *ἐλάκησεν* (from *λάσκω*), "burst with a crack, split open" — the very verb of Acts 1:18 («πρηνὴς γενόμενος ἐλάκησεν μέσος»). Theophylact glosses it *ἀντὶ τοῦ, διεῤῥάγη* ("that is to say, he was torn apart / burst asunder"), which is retained in the body. *πρηνὴς πεσών*, "having fallen headlong / face-forward."

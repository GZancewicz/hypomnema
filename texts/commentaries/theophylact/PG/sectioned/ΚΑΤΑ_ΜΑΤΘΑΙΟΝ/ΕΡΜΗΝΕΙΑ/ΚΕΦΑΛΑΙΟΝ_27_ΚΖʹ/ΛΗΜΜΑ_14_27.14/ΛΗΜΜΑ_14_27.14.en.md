# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 27:14

> **he answered him not even one word; so that the governor marveled greatly.**[^1]

As one being accused of public crimes he is led away to Pilate, whence *the latter* also asks him whether he had set his hand to sedition, and had undertaken to reign over the Jews.[^2] But Jesus said to him: "You say *it*"[^3] — giving him a most wise answer; for neither did he say "I am not," nor again "I am," but somewhat in the middle he said, "You say *it*." For this can be understood also thus, that "I am, as you say"; and *also* thus, that "I on the one hand do not say this, but you on the other hand say *it*." Nothing else, however, did he answer; for he knew that the tribunal was not proceeding according to reason.[^4]

Pilate therefore marveled at the Lord — at once, on the one hand, as *one* despising death, and, at the same time, on the other hand, *at* how, being eloquent and having countless things to put forward for *his* defense, he answered nothing, but disregarded those *who were* accusing *him*. And let us also be taught from this, whenever we have *to do* before a corrupt tribunal, to say nothing, so that we may neither stir up more tumult, nor be causes of greater condemnation *for ourselves* with those who do not listen to our defenses.[^5]

[^1]: Greek *ἀπεκρίθη αὐτῷ πρὸς οὐδὲ ἓν ῥῆμα* — literally "he answered him with respect to not even one word," rendered "not even one word." The OCR splits *οὐδὲ | ἓν* across lines (*οὐδὲ / δν*); securely *οὐδὲ ἓν*. The closing «…» falls after *λίαν* ("greatly"), where the exposition begins.
[^2]: Greek *εἰ ἐπεβάλετο ἀνταρσίᾳ* — *ἐπιβάλλομαι* here "to set one's hand to / attempt," with *ἀνταρσία* "rebellion, insurrection, sedition." The subject of *ἐρωτᾷ* ("asks") is Pilate; supplied "*the latter*" for clarity.
[^3]: Greek *Σὺ λέγεις* — literally "You say," i.e. "You say *it* / You are saying *so*." Theophylact makes the reply's calculated ambiguity the whole point of his comment, so the bare "You say" is kept and the object supplied in italics.
[^4]: Greek *ᾔδει γὰρ τὸ κριτήριον μὴ κατὰ λόγον προβαῖνον* — "for he knew the tribunal *to be* not proceeding according to reason"; *κατὰ λόγον* "according to reason / rationally / fairly."
[^5]: The final clause is grammatically loose in the OCR (*κατακρίσεως πλείονος ὦμεν αἴτιοι ; τοῖς μὴ τῶν ἀπολογιῶν ἡμῶν ἀκούουσι*, with a stray raised point). Rendered as one sentence: the corrupt judges "who do not listen to our defenses" (*τοῖς μὴ τῶν ἀπολογιῶν ἡμῶν ἀκούουσι*) are the ones before whom silence spares us greater condemnation.

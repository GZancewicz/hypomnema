# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 27:30 (Ps. 38:9 LXX)

> **A reproach to *the* fool thou hast given me.[^1]**

For the soldiers, being without understanding,[^2] were doing things worthy of themselves; for on the one hand they clothed *him* with the cloak instead of purple, as a king; but the reed, on the other hand, they gave *him* instead of a scepter, and the crown instead of a diadem. And they did obeisance *while* jesting; for this is what the falling on the knees signifies — the doing of obeisance. But observe how they went through every form of insolence: outraging on the one hand the face by means of the spittings, but the head by means of the crown, and the hand by means of the reed, and the rest of the body by means of the cloak, *and* the ears by means of the mocking words.[^3]

Yet even if those *men* were doing in jest the things which they were doing, nevertheless do you understand these things to have been accomplished by Jesus in a more mystical *sense* as well.[^4] For on the one hand the scarlet cloak signified our nature, which — *being* bloody and murderous[^5] — he took up, and having clothed himself *with it*, he sanctified *it*. But the crown of thorns, on the other hand, *signifies* the sins that have come about through the cares of life,[^6] which Christ consumes by his own divinity; for this is what his head hints at enigmatically. But the reed is a symbol of our flesh, decayed and weak, which the Lord took up, just as David also says:[^7]

[^1]: Greek «Ὄνειδος ἄφρονι ἔδωκάς με» — Ps. 38:9 LXX (Ps. 39:8 Heb.). Word-for-word: "*A* reproach (*ὄνειδος*, accusative predicate) to *a* senseless one (*ἄφρονι*, dative) thou gavest (*ἔδωκάς*) me" — i.e. "thou hast made me a reproach to the fool." Rendered literally, keeping the predicate-accusative construction visible rather than smoothing to "made me." *ἄφρων* ("senseless, mindless") is chosen to link with *ἀνόητοι* ("without understanding") applied to the soldiers just below.
[^2]: Greek *ἀνόητοι ὄντες* ("being mindless / without understanding"), deliberately echoing the *ἄφρονι* ("fool") of the Psalm citation: the soldiers act the part of the "fool" to whom the Psalm makes the sufferer a reproach.
[^3]: The participle *ὑβρίζοντες* ("outraging, insulting") stands last in the Greek and governs the whole *μὲν … δὲ … δὲ …* chain of accusatives (face, head, hand, body, ears); it is brought forward in English so the string of members hangs from it. The final member, "the ears … by the mocking words" (*τὰ ὧτα διὰ τῶν χλευαστικῶν λόγων*), has no *δὲ* in the Greek and prepares the reference to the ears at Lemma 30.
[^4]: Greek *ὅμως σὺ νόει ταῦτα καὶ μυστικώτερον ὑπὸ τοῦ Ἰησοῦ τελεσθῆναι*. The emphatic *σύ* ("do *you* / for your part understand") is imperative; *μυστικώτερον* is a comparative adverb, "in a more mystical way," here "in a deeper/mystical sense as well" (the *καὶ* = "also"). The point: the soldiers' jest was, at another level, something truly brought to fulfillment by Jesus.
[^5]: Greek *αἱματώδη … καὶ φονικήν* — "bloody / blood-like" and "murderous, prone to slaughter"; predicated of our human *φύσις* ("nature"), the antecedent of *ἥν* ("which"). The scarlet (blood-colored) cloak is read as this bloodstained, murderous nature that Christ assumes and hallows.
[^6]: Greek *αἱ ὑπὸ μεριμνῶν τοῦ βίου συμβᾶσαι ἁμαρτίαι* — "the sins that have come about under/through the anxieties of life," evoking the thorns that choke the seed among the "cares (*μέριμναι*) of this life" (cf. Matt. 13:22; Luke 8:14). The verb *ἀναλίσκει* ("consumes, destroys, does away with"; OCR *ἀναλίσνει*) and *αἰνίττεται* ("hints at, intimates enigmatically") carry the allegory: the thorn-crown on the *head* signifies these sins undone by Christ's divinity.
[^7]: The sentence runs on into the next lemma, which supplies the citation «Δεξιὰ Κυρίου ὕψωσέ με» ("the right hand of the Lord exalted me," Ps. 117:16 LXX) — the reed having been placed in Christ's *right hand* (Lemma 27).

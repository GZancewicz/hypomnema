# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 27:18

> **envy they had delivered him up.**[^1]

Pilate was eager to release the Christ, even if *in a manner* softer than *was* fitting; for he ought to have stood firm on behalf of the truth. First, therefore, he asked the Lord: "Do you not hear what these testify against you?"[^2] And he asked so that, when Christ had made a defense, he might have an occasion for releasing him; but since the Lord did not make a defense — knowing altogether that even if he should make a defense he would not be released — Pilate turns to another way, and takes refuge henceforth in the custom, all but saying this: "If you do not release Jesus as innocent, at least grant him to the feast as a condemned man."[^3] For how[^4] would Pilate have supposed that they would ask *that* Jesus, being innocent, be crucified, and would release the guilty robber? Knowing therefore that he is not guilty, but is envied, for this reason he asks them; whence it is shown that he was *too* soft; for he ought to have hazarded himself beforehand for the good *man*. For this reason, therefore, he is also worthy of condemnation, as having covered up the truth.

And Barabbas is interpreted "son of the father"; for *bar* *means* the son, and *abbas* the father.[^5] The Jews, accordingly, asked for the son of their father, the devil, but crucified Jesus; and even until now they attach themselves, on the one hand, to the son of their father, the Antichrist, but reject, on the other hand, the Christ.

[^1]: Greek *φθόνον παρέδωκαν αὐτόν* — completing the quotation from the previous lemma (ΛΗΜΜΑ_15), *διὰ φθόνον* "because of envy." *παρέδωκαν* "they delivered up / handed over / betrayed."
[^2]: Here Theophylact recalls the words of Matt. 27:13 (*Οὐκ ἀκούεις τί οὗτοί σου καταμαρτυροῦσιν;*), now with *τί* ("what") for the earlier *πόσα* ("how many things"), applying Pilate's question to his argument.
[^3]: Greek *χαρίσασθε τοῦτον τῇ ἑορτῇ* — *χαρίζομαι* "to grant as a favor"; the sense is "make a gift of him to the feast," i.e. release him as the festal favor even while treating him as condemned.
[^4]: Greek *Ποῦ γὰρ ἂν ὑπέλαβεν* — literally "For where would he have supposed," an idiom for "how / by no means would he have supposed"; the whole is a rhetorical question expecting the answer "nowhere / he could never have."
[^5]: The etymology is treated as words *qua* words: Aramaic *bar* (*βὰρ*) = "son," *abba* (*ἀββᾶς*) = "father," so *Βαραββᾶς* = "son of the father." Theophylact turns this into a typology: Barabbas the "son of the father" (the devil / Antichrist) chosen over Jesus the Son of the true Father.

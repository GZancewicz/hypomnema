# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 27:26

> **—he released to them Barabbas, but Jesus, having scourged *him*, he delivered up that he might be crucified. (Matt. 27:26)**[^1]

O the wonder! While being judged by Pilate, he was terrifying this man's wife;[^2] but it is not he himself *who* sees the dream, but the wife — either because he himself was unworthy, or because he would not have been believed, but would have seemed to be speaking to curry favour;[^3] and perhaps he would even have kept silent, if he had known this, seeing that he was a judge. But the dream *was* a work of Providence, having come about not so that Christ might be released on account of it, but so that the wife might be saved.

How, then, did he not release *him*? Because it was not safe for him to let him go, inasmuch as *he was* being accused of *aiming at* tyranny; but it was necessary to seek proofs — whether he was enlisting soldiers, whether he was forging weapons; but as it is, being *a man* light and soft, he is swept along.[^4] Wherefore neither *is he* free of blame; for when those *men* asked for the one notorious in wickedness, he gave *him*; but concerning Christ he kept asking, "What then shall I do *with* Jesus?" — making those *men* lords of the judgment; and yet, being governor, he was able to snatch him away, just as the tribune *snatched* Paul.[^5]

But they kept saying, "Let him be crucified," wishing not only to kill him, but also to clothe *him* with an evil charge; for the cross was reserved as punishment for the wicked. And he washes *himself*, showing himself, forsooth, clean of the abomination[^6] — thinking wrongly *in this*; for though naming Jesus righteous, he nevertheless betrayed *him* to the murderers. But they take upon themselves the punishment for his murder, which indeed also overtook them, when the Romans wiped out both them and their children; but the Hebrews too, up to the present, being children of those who murdered the Lord, have his blood upon themselves; for on account of *their* unbelief toward the Lord they are persecuted by all, and there is no boldness[^7] for these who are persecuted for this very thing.

And he scourged Jesus[^8] — that is, he flogged *him* — either as a favour to them, or showing that he himself also had condemned him, and *that* they are about to crucify not an innocent man, but one *already* dishonoured. Therefore was fulfilled also the *word*:[^9]

[^1]: The lemma continues the sentence begun at *Τότε* ("Then—") in the previous lemma, so the English opens with the dash. "Having scourged *him*" renders *φραγελλώσας* (OCR *φραγγελώσσας*), an aorist participle from the Latin loan-word *flagellare* ("to flog with the *flagellum*"); Theophylact glosses it below with the Greek *ἐμάστιξεν*. "That he might be crucified" renders the purpose clause *ἵνα σταυρωθῇ*.
[^2]: Greek *κρινόμενος ὑπὸ Πιλάτου, ἐξεφόβει τὴν τούτου γυναῖκα* — "being judged by Pilate, he was terrifying this man's wife" (imperfect *ἐξεφόβει*, "kept frightening"), i.e. the dream sent to Pilate's wife (Matt. 27:19). "This man's" (*τούτου*) refers to Pilate.
[^3]: Greek *ἔδοξε πρὸς χάριν λέγειν* — "he would have seemed to speak *πρὸς χάριν*," i.e. ingratiatingly, to gain favour; the sense is that had Pilate himself reported the dream, he would have been suspected of inventing it to please *someone* (to excuse releasing Jesus). The whole clause is counterfactual, governed by *οὐκ ἂν ἐπιστεύθη* ("he would not have been believed").
[^4]: Greek *νῦν δὲ ὡς κοῦφος καὶ μαλακὸς, παρασύρεται* — *κοῦφος* ("light, frivolous") and *μαλακὸς* ("soft, weak, pliant") describe Pilate's want of firmness; *παρασύρεται* ("is dragged / swept along," present passive) pictures him carried off by the crowd's pressure. *νῦν δὲ* here is the logical "but as it is / but in fact," not temporal "now."
[^5]: Greek *ὥσπερ τὸν Παῦλον ὁ χιλίαρχος* — the reference is to Acts 23:10, where the *χιλίαρχος* (Roman tribune, commander of a thousand) had his soldiers snatch Paul out of the mob by force. The verb "snatched" is supplied from the preceding *ἁρπάσαι* ("to snatch away").
[^6]: Greek *τοῦ μύσους* — *μύσος* is "defilement, pollution, an abominable / foul deed"; here the blood-guilt Pilate would disown by washing. *δῆθεν* ("forsooth, supposedly") marks the gesture as a mere pretence.
[^7]: Greek *οὐδεμία παῤῥησία τούτοις* — *παρρησία* means "boldness, freedom / confidence of speech, open assurance"; here of a persecuted, dispersed people it denotes their want of any bold or confident public standing. Rendered "boldness"; alternatives: "confidence," "no confident standing." "For this very thing" renders *δι᾽ αὐτὸ τοῦτο*, referring back to their unbelief toward the Lord (*τὴν εἰς τὸν Κύριον ἀπιστίαν*), not to the words spoken to Pilate.
[^8]: Greek *Ἐφραγγέλλωσε δὲ τὸν Ἰησοῦν, τουτέστιν, ἐμάστιξεν* — Theophylact glosses the Latinism *ἐφραγέλλωσε* ("he scourged," from *flagellare*) with the ordinary Greek *ἐμάστιξεν* ("he flogged / whipped").
[^9]: The sentence breaks off at *Ἐπληρώθη οὖν καὶ τὸ,* ("Therefore was fulfilled also the *word*:"); the prophetic citation that completes it stands in the next lemma — «Τὸν νῶτόν μου ἔδωκα εἰς μάστιγας» (Isa. 50:6).

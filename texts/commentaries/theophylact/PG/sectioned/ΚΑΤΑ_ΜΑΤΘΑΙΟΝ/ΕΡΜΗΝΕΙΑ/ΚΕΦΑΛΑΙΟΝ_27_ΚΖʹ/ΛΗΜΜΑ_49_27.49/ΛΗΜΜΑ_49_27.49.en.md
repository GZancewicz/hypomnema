# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 49 · Matt. 27:46

> **"For what reason did you forsake me?"[^1]**

— displaying our natural love of life. For indeed he was truly man, and in all things like us, apart from sin. But some understood this thus: that the Savior,[^2] taking upon *himself* the person[^3] of the Jews, says: "For what reason did you forsake the Jewish people, O Father, so that it committed so great a sin, and was delivered over to destruction?" [^1]: The folder-name verse is 27.49, but the Greek «Ἵνα τί με ἐγκατέλιπες;» expounds **Matt. 27:46** — the second clause of the cry from the cross, «Ἠλὶ ἠλὶ λιμὰ σαβαχθανί; τοῦτ᾽ ἔστι· Θεέ μου, Θεέ μου, ἱνατί με ἐγκατέλιπες;» ("My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"). This lemma continues directly from Lemma 48, which ended «οὕτω καὶ νῦν φησιν·» ("so also now he says:"); the exposition here opens with the participle *ἐμφαίνων* ("displaying"), which hangs on that verb. *Ἵνα τί* rendered "For what reason" (lit. "to what end") consistently throughout.
[^2]: The OCR reads «Χωτὴρ», securely = **«Σωτὴρ»** ("Savior"). The preceding «σὸ» in «ὅτι σὸ τῶν Ἰουδαίων πρόσωπον ἀναλαμβάνων ὁ Σωτὴρ φησίν» is corrupt; the sense requires an article or nothing («ὅτι ὁ … ὁ Σωτὴρ φησίν», "that the Savior … says"). Translated per the reconstructed sense.
[^3]: Greek *πρόσωπον* — "face," but here "person / persona / dramatic role." This is a prosopopoeia: Christ speaks *in the character of* the Jewish people, addressing the Father. The same figure governs Lemma 50.
[^4]: The sentence breaks off at the bare neuter article «τὸ». This article introduces the quoted phrase that opens Lemma 50: «…ὁ Χριστὸς, τὸ «Ἐγκατέλιπές με,» φησίν, ἀντὶ τοῦ…» ("…Christ says the *'You forsook me,'* instead of…"). The clause is completed there.

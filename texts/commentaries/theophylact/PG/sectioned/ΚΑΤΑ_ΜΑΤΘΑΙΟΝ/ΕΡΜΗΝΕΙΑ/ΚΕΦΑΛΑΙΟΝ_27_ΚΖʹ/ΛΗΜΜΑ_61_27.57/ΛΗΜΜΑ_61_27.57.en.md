# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 61 · Matt. 27:57

> **But evening having come, there came a rich man from Arimathea, *named* Joseph, who also himself had become a disciple to Jesus. (Matt. 27:57)**

This lemma carries only the Gospel text, with no exposition attached to it; Theophylact defers his comment on the whole burial narrative (27:57–61) and expounds it collectively at Lemma 65 below.[^1]

[^1]: In the source, Lemmata 61–64 present the successive verses of the burial account (27:57–60) as a sequence of scripture citations without intervening commentary — indeed 62–64 run on as a single unbroken sentence, each ending in *καὶ* ("and"). Theophylact then gives his exposition of the entire block at Lemma 65 (27:61), where he returns to Joseph, Pilate, the new tomb, and the two Marys. The word *ἐμαθήτευσε* is here intransitive-middle in force ("became a disciple, was discipled to"), matching the Gospel's own usage of the same verb.

# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 22 · Matt. 27:24–25

> **—Pilate, *seeing* that it availed nothing, but rather that a tumult was arising, having taken water washed off his hands before the crowd, saying: "I am innocent of the blood of this righteous man; you shall see *to it*." And answering, all the people said: "His blood *be* upon us and upon our children." Then—**[^1]

[^1]: A scripture-quotation fragment with no exposition of its own; the comment on it stands in Lemma 23. Though the folder assigns it to 27:24, the quotation runs through v. 25. It opens with "Pilate," completing the clause broken off in the previous lemma (*Ἰδὼν δὲ ὁ Πιλάτος* = "But Pilate, having seen…"); here that participle is carried by the supplied "*seeing*." "Availed nothing… a tumult was arising" renders the present-tense *οὐδὲν ὠφελεῖ … θόρυβος γίνεται*. "Washed off his hands" keeps the imperfect middle *ἀπενίπτετο*. "You shall see *to it*" renders *ὑμεῖς ὄψεσθε* (future middle, lit. "you yourselves shall see"), an idiom of disclaiming responsibility; "*to it*" is supplied. In "His blood *be* upon us" the verb is unexpressed in the Greek (*Τὸ αἷμα αὐτοῦ ἐφ᾽ ἡμᾶς*), so "*be*" is supplied. The fragment breaks off at *Τότε* ("Then—"), completed by the next lemma.

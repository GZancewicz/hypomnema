# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 65 · Matt. 27:61

> **Mary the Magdalene, and the other Mary, sitting opposite the sepulchre. (Matt. 27:61)**

Joseph, on the one hand, was formerly hiding himself, but now he dares a great deed, laying down his own soul[^1] for the teacher's[^2] body, and taking upon himself so great an enmity toward all the Jews. And *it is* as a great gift *that* Pilate gives him the body. For since Christ had been put to death as an insurrectionist,[^3] reasonably his body was about to be cast out unburied; but Joseph, being rich, *it is* likely that he even gave Pilate gold. And having taken it he honors it, laying it down in a new tomb, in which no one had yet been laid; and this was of God's dispensation,[^4] so that, the Lord having risen, no one might be able to say that another *man*, laid there dead beforehand, *was the one who* rose. For this reason, therefore, the tomb *was* new.

But Mary the Magdalene, and the other Mary — that is, the Theotokos,[^5] whom above he called the mother of Joses and James — these two are sitting opposite the tomb, keeping watch, so that, if the madness[^6] should cease, they might go away and cling to the body[^7] and anoint it with myrrh. Concerning such women Isaiah was speaking: "Women coming from a sight, come hither; for it is not a people having understanding."[^8] For clearly he says concerning the Jewish people, who crucified the Lord, that they have no understanding. He calls upon these *women*, therefore, to leave, on the one hand, the senseless people, but to come to the apostles and to announce to them the good news of the resurrection.

[^1]: Greek *τιθεὶς τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ* — *ψυχή* is "soul" and also "life"; the phrase deliberately echoes the Gospel's «τίθημι τὴν ψυχήν» ("to lay down one's life," John 10:11, 15). Rendered "laying down his own soul" to keep the verbal echo visible; "risking his own life" is the plainer sense.
[^2]: Greek *τοῦ διδασκαλικοῦ σώματος*, lit. "the teacherly / master's body" — i.e. the body of *the* Teacher (Christ). The adjective *διδασκαλικός* is used substantively for "belonging to the Teacher."
[^3]: Greek *ὡς … στασιαστοῦ θανατωθέντος τοῦ Χριστοῦ* ("Christ having been put to death as a *στασιαστής*"). *στασιαστής* = one who stirs up sedition, a rebel/insurrectionist; the point is that the Romans executed him on a political charge, so his corpse would ordinarily be denied burial. (The OCR «κὰρ» = *γὰρ*, "for.")
[^4]: Greek *οἰκονομίας Θεοῦ* — *οἰκονομία*, the divine "economy / dispensation / providential arrangement," a standard patristic term; rendered "dispensation." (The OCR «ταοεὶς» in the clause that follows = *τεθεὶς*, "having been laid.")
[^5]: *Θεοτόκος* ("God-bearer / Mother of God") retained as a technical title. Theophylact identifies "the other Mary" with the Virgin, cross-referring to his comment on 27:56 (Lemma 60), where he explained that James and Joses were Joseph's sons by an earlier wife, so the Theotokos was called their (step-)mother.
[^6]: Greek *ἡ μανία* — the "madness / frenzy," i.e. the raging hostility of the Jews; they wait for it to subside before approaching the body.
[^7]: Greek *περιχυθῆναι τῷ σώματι*, lit. "to be poured around the body" — a vivid verb for embracing/enfolding it; rendered "cling to the body."
[^8]: A loose citation of Isa. 27:11 LXX: «γυναῖκες ἐρχόμεναι ἀπὸ θέας, δεῦτε· οὐ γὰρ λαός ἐστιν ἔχων σύνεσιν» ("women who come from a spectacle, come; for it is not a people that has understanding"). *θέα* = "sight / viewing / spectacle." Theophylact reads the "women coming from the sight [of the crucifixion]" typologically of the Marys, and the "people without understanding" of the Jews.

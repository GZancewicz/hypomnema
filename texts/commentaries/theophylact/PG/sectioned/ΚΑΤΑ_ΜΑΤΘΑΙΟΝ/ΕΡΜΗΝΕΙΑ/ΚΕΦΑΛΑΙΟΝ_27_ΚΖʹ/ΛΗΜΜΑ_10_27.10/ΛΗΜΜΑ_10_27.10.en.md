# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 27:10

> **they gave them for the field of the potter, according as *the* Lord appointed me.[^1]**

They used to call "corban"[^2] the treasury in the temple, into which they cast the gifts offered to God. But see how God brings them to nought,[^3] so that their murderous[^4] intent is noised abroad; for until this day, he says, that field is called Field of blood, so that all may be reminded that they murdered the Lord. But learn this also, that even among the Jews hospitality was so earnestly cultivated, that even for strangers a certain plot was bought for burial. Let us therefore be ashamed, we who on the one hand seem to be of a more perfect life, but on the other hand overlook strangers. And "the price of him who was priced," he says, means clearly *the price* of Christ; for on the one hand he was beyond price,[^5] but on the other hand he was priced by *the* sons of Israel — that is, the sons of Israel set his price, having agreed with Judas to give thirty pieces of silver.

[^1]: Greek *καθὰ συνέταξέ μοι Κύριος*, "according as the Lord directed/appointed me." OCR-garbled in the source as «κἰς τλν ἀγρὸν… καθὰ»; securely recoverable to *εἰς τὸν ἀγρὸν τοῦ κεραμέως*, "for the field of the potter." This closes the quotation begun at Lemma 7.
[^2]: Greek *κορβανᾶν* (accusative *τὸν κορβανᾶν*), a Hebrew/Aramaic loanword (*qorbān*, "offering") used for the temple treasury into which sacred gifts were placed; kept transliterated because Theophylact is glossing it as a term.
[^3]: Greek *ματαιοῖ αὐτούς* — "makes them vain / frustrates / brings them to nought." The verb *ματαιόω* denotes rendering futile or exposing as empty; the point is that God turns their scruple to their own public shame.
[^4]: Greek *μιαιφόνον*, literally "defiled-with-slaughter, blood-stained"; here of their *γνώμη* ("purpose, resolve, mind"), rendered "murderous intent."
[^5]: The OCR reads «τίμητος μὲν γὰρ ἦν», but the *μὲν … δὲ* antithesis with the following *ἐτιμήθη* ("yet he was priced") requires the privative **«ἀτίμητος»** ("beyond price, not to be valued"): "for he was beyond price, yet he was priced by the sons of Israel." An initial *ἀ-* has almost certainly dropped in the OCR. Rendered "beyond price"; without the privative the clause would read "for he was of value, and he was valued," which yields no real contrast.

# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 60 · Matt. 27:56

> **…Mary the Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James and Joses, and the mother of the sons of Zebedee. (Matt. 27:56)**

On the one hand the gentile centurion, from the signs, believes together with those about him; but on the other hand the Jews, who had listened intently to *the* prophets and *the* law, remain unbelieving — so great an evil is wickedness. And this centurion, moreover, later bore witness on behalf of Christ.[^1]

But women *were* beholding the things that happened — they *who are*, of all *people*, the most compassionate; and the race that had been condemned enjoys first the contemplation of the good things.[^2] And on the one hand the disciples flee, but on the other hand the women endure. And by "Mary the mother of James and Joses" he means the Theotokos; for James and Joses were children of Joseph from his former wife.[^3] Since, therefore, the Theotokos was called *the* wife of Joseph, reasonably she was also called mother — that is, stepmother — of his children. And the mother of the sons of Zebedee was called Salome; and they say that she too was a daughter of Joseph.

[^1]: Greek *ἐμαρτύρησε … ὑπὲρ Χριστοῦ ὕστερον*. The verb *μαρτυρέω* means both "bear witness" and, in Christian usage, "suffer martyrdom"; with "later, on behalf of Christ" the sense is most likely that the centurion was afterward martyred. Rendered "bore witness" to keep the root, but "was later martyred for Christ" is the probable force.
[^2]: Greek *τὸ γένος τὸ κατακριθὲν* — "the race/kind that had been condemned," i.e. womankind (condemned in Eve); it is women who "enjoy first" (*ἀπολαύει πρῶτον*) the sight of the good things at the resurrection. The sentence is loosely built: *γυναῖκες … θεωροῦσαι* ("women beholding") stands in apposition to the condemned race; the singular verb *ἀπολαύει* agrees with *τὸ γένος*.
[^3]: Theophylact identifies the "Mary the mother of James and Joses" of Matt. 27:56 with the Theotokos herself, on the view that James and Joses were Joseph's sons by an earlier wife, so that the Virgin, as Joseph's wife, was their stepmother (*μητρυιά*) and could be called their "mother."

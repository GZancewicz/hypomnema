# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 47 · Matt. 27:49

> **…rest were saying: Let be, let us see whether Elijah is coming to save him.[^1] (Matt. 27:49)**

The darkness that came to be was not according to a natural sequence, such as *would arise* from an eclipse of the sun coming about naturally; for never, when the moon is fourteen days old, does an eclipse of the sun occur, but whenever the so-called "birth" *of the moon*[^2] takes place, then the natural eclipses of the sun happen. But at the crucifixion the moon was assuredly fourteen days old, for the Passover of the Hebrews was then being celebrated; so that the Passion *was* beyond nature. And the darkness was cosmic, not partial as *it was* in Egypt,[^3] so that it might be shown that the creation mourns at the suffering of the Creator, and that from the Jews the light departed. And let the Jews, who ask to see a sign from heaven,[^4] now behold the sun being darkened. And, the man having been formed on *the* sixth day and having eaten from the tree at *the* sixth hour — for *it was* at the hour of the eating[?][^5] — the Lord, re-fashioning the man and healing the fault, is nailed to the tree on *the* sixth day and at *the* sixth hour.[^6] And in the Hebrew tongue he speaks the prophetic *word*,[^7] so that he might show that he is not at variance with the Old Scripture. And the *saying*,[^8]

[^1]: Greek *σώσων* is a future participle, "*being about* to save / *coming* to save him."
[^2]: Greek *ἡ λεγομένη γέννα* ("the so-called birth"): the technical term for the new moon / lunar conjunction, when the moon is "born." Theophylact's astronomical argument: solar eclipses are possible only at new moon (conjunction), never at the full moon (the fourteenth day of the lunar month), which is when Passover falls; hence the crucifixion darkness cannot have been a natural eclipse.
[^3]: I.e. the plague of darkness in Egypt (Exod. 10:21-23) was localized — covering Egypt but not Goshen; the crucifixion darkness, by contrast, was *κοσμικόν*, worldwide.
[^4]: Cf. the demand for "a sign from heaven," Matt. 16:1 (and 12:38).
[^5]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupted: *τη γὰρ τῆς βρώσεως ἡ ὥρα...* The sense securely intended is *τῇ γὰρ τῆς βρώσεως ὥρᾳ* ("for *it was* at the hour of the eating"), matching the sixth hour of Adam's transgression to the sixth hour of the crucifixion; the printed "ἡ ὥρα" is a garbling of the dative *ὥρᾳ*.
[^6]: Wordplay on *ξύλον* ("tree, wood"): the same word denotes both the tree of Eden, from which the man ate, and the wood of the cross, to which the Lord is nailed. The parallel of "sixth day / sixth hour" and "tree / tree" makes the crucifixion the exact undoing of the Fall. Greek *ἀναπλάττων* = "re-fashioning, re-moulding"; *τὸ σφάλμα ἰώμενος* = "healing the fault / the slip."
[^7]: Greek *τὸ προφητικόν* ("the prophetic *thing*"): i.e. the prophetic verse Jesus quotes ("Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani" = Ps. 21[22]:1), spoken in Hebrew.
[^8]: Greek *τὸ δέ* ("and the *phrase*…") introduces a quotation completed in the next lemma: «Why hast thou forsaken me?».

# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 69 · Matt. 27:65

> **[*He said*] to them, Pilate:[^1] You have a guard;[^2] go, make *it* secure[^3] as you know *how*. But they**

This lemma likewise carries only the Gospel text. It completes Pilate's reply (the *Ἔφη*, "He said," that ended Lemma 68 governs "to them Pilate" here) and breaks off at *Οἱ δὲ* ("But they"), whose main verb comes in Lemma 70 («…having gone, made the tomb secure»).

[^1]: Greek *Ἔφη αὐτοῖς ὁ Πιλᾶτος* ("Pilate said to them"). The verb *Ἔφη* stands at the close of the previous lemma; here the sentence continues with its indirect object and subject. The bracketed "*He said*" is supplied to make the fragment legible; the word order (verb — object — subject) is Theophylact's.
[^2]: Greek *Ἔχετε κουστωδίαν*. *Ἔχετε* is ambiguous between the indicative "You have a guard *at your disposal*" and the imperative "Take/Have a guard." The indicative is given in the body; the imperative ("Take a guard *for yourselves*") is an old and defensible reading. *κουστωδία* is the Latin loanword *custodia* ("guard, watch, guard-detail"), which Theophylact himself glosses at Lemma 72; left as "guard" here.
[^3]: Greek *ἀσφαλίσασθε ὡς οἴδατε* ("secure *it* as you know *how*"). The object "it" (the tomb) is carried by the verb alone and is supplied; "how" completes the elliptical *ὡς οἴδατε* ("as you know").

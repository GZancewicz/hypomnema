# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 36 · Matt. 27:37

> **...they placed above his head his accusation[^1] written: "This is Jesus the king of the Jews."**

It was called *the* Place of a Skull because those who have received *it* from the tradition of the Fathers say that Adam was buried there; for just as we all died in Adam, so it was necessary that we be made alive in Christ.

But do not be troubled hearing the evangelists — this one, on the one hand, saying that vinegar with gall was brought to the Lord, but Mark, wine mingled with myrrh,[^2] and John, vinegar and gall with hyssop; for many things were done by many *people*, inasmuch as *it* was a disorderly crowd, and one *doing* one thing, another another. It is likely, therefore, that the one offered wine, but the other vinegar with gall.

Many were the modes of death, but nevertheless Christ is put to death by *the* cross, in order that he might both sanctify the tree[^3] through which we were cursed, and might bless all things: both the heavenly things, which are signified by the topmost part of the cross, and the things beneath the earth, which are signified by the foot-rest,[^4] and the ends of the earth, both the Eastern and the Western, which are signified by the side parts of the cross; but also in order that, having stretched out his hands, he might embrace and gather together the scattered children of God.

The soldiers also divide his garments among themselves, as of *one who was* poor and had nothing else.[^8] But that which another evangelist called "title," this Matthew calls "accusation";[^5] for they inscribed for what cause he was crucified — that *it was* as king of the Jews, and as a revolutionary.[^6] They, therefore, on the one hand, inscribed the *"King"* by way of slander. Nevertheless this testimony *is* trustworthy, as having been brought forward by the enemies. For the Lord is king, having come for this very thing, that he might save the Jews; but since the fleshly Jews did not wish him to reign over them, he becomes king of the spiritual Jews, that is, of those who confess; for "Jew" means "one who confesses."[^7]

[^1]: Greek *αἰτίαν* — "charge, accusation, cause" (the legal ground of the sentence). The wordplay with the "title" (*τίτλος*) of the other evangelists is taken up in the exposition below.
[^2]: Greek *ἐσμυρνισμένον οἶνον* ("wine mingled with myrrh," Mark 15:23). Theophylact harmonizes the differing accounts by supposing that different people in the crowd offered different draughts.
[^3]: Greek *τὸ ξύλον* — "the tree / the wood," i.e. the wood of the cross set against the tree of Eden "through which we were cursed."
[^4]: Greek *τοῦ ὑποποδίου* — the *suppedaneum*, the foot-support of the cross; here allegorized as the things "beneath the earth" (*τὰ ὑποχθόνια*).
[^5]: The wordplay is between *τίτλος* (Latin *titulus*, "inscription, notice") used by another evangelist (cf. John 19:19) and Matthew's *αἰτία* ("charge, accusation"); the two name the same placard from different angles.
[^6]: Greek *νεωτεριστής* — an innovator, agitator, one who "stirs up new things," i.e. a seditious revolutionary against the *status quo*.
[^7]: Theophylact's etymology: *Ἰουδαῖος … ἐξομολογούμενος λέγεται* — "Jew" (Judah) is derived from the sense of confession/thanksgiving (*ἐξομολόγησις*), following the Hebrew name Yehudah, connected with the verb "to praise/give thanks/confess." Hence the "spiritual Jews" are "those who confess" (*οἱ ἐξομολογούμενοι*).
[^8]: Greek *ὡς εὐτελοῦς, καὶ μηδὲν ἄλλο ἔχοντος* — *εὐτελής* is "cheap, of little value, mean"; applied to a person here, in tandem with "had nothing else," it denotes poverty — one of small means whose only property was the garments now divided. Rendered "poor"; alternatives: "of small means," "of little account."

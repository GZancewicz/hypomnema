# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 72 · Matt. 27:66 (exposition, continued)

This lemma continues the exposition of Lemma 71; it opens with the second dominical saying from which the Jews might have learned of the third day, then closes Theophylact's comment on the pericope and passes over into the chapter-headings of the resurrection narrative.

«Destroy this temple;»[^1] for previously, on the one hand, not having understood this, but having supposed that he speaks concerning the Jewish temple, they testified against him;[^2] but now, on the other hand, having known that he called the temple his own body, they are afraid, and call him a deceiver — not having loosed[^3] their wickedness even after *his* death.

But "custodia," among the Romans, means the guard;[^4] the soldiers, therefore, appointed to the guarding, he names "custodia."

*Concerning the resurrection of the Lord. Concerning the guards of the tomb, witnesses of the resurrection.*[^5]

*Concerning the manifestation of Christ to the apostles and *the* women in Galilee.*[^6]

But late on *the* Sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first *day*[^7]

[^1]: Greek *«Λύσατε τὸν ναὸν τοῦτον»* (John 2:19), the saying reported at the trial (Matt. 26:61). *Λύσατε* is "destroy, break down, dissolve"; the choice of verb sets up the wordplay in note 3.
[^2]: Greek *κατεμαρτύρησαν αὐτοῦ* ("they testified against him," *κατα-μαρτυρέω* with genitive) — the false witness of Matt. 26:60–61, who reported this saying as a threat against the Jerusalem temple.
[^3]: Greek *μηδὲ μετὰ θάνατον τὴν κακίαν λύσαντες* ("not having loosed their wickedness even after *his* death"). A deliberate word-play on the lemma: they were bidden "*loose* (λύσατε) this temple," and now, after that temple (his body) has been destroyed, they have still not "*loosed* (λύσαντες)" — i.e. let go, dissolved — their own malice. The pun on *λύω* is lost in English; "loose" is used in both places to keep it visible. *μετὰ θάνατον* ("after death") is anarthrous; "*his*" (Christ's death) is supplied.
[^4]: Greek *Κουστωδία δὲ λέγεται παρὰ Ῥωμαίοις ἡ φυλακή* ("but among the Romans the guard is called *custodia*"). Theophylact glosses the Latin loanword *custodia* (κουστωδία), used in the lemma, with the native Greek *φυλακή* ("guard, watch, guarding"); he then explains that the soldiers detailed *to guard* (*φυλάττειν*) are what the word names.
[^5]: These lines are the chapter-headings (κεφάλαια / τίτλοι) of the following section — the standard rubrics that stand in the manuscripts at the head of the resurrection narrative — not part of the running exposition. Set in italics accordingly. Greek: *Περὶ τῆς τοῦ Κυρίου ἀναστάσεως. Περὶ τῶν φυλάκων τοῦ τάφου, μαρτύρων τῆς ἀναστάσεως.*
[^6]: Greek *Περὶ τῆς ἐμφανίσεως τοῦ Χριστοῦ τοῖς ἀποστόλοις καὶ γυναιξὶν ἐν Γαλιλαίᾳ* ("Concerning the manifestation of Christ to the apostles and women in Galilee"). The OCR prints *ἀκρστόλοις* for *ἀποστόλοις*, a secure correction. *ἐμφάνισις* ("manifestation, showing-forth, appearing").
[^7]: Greek *Ὀψὲ δὲ Σαββάτων, τῇ ἐπιφωσκούσῃ εἰς μίαν* — the opening words of Matt. 28:1, breaking off mid-phrase ("…toward the first *day* of the week"). This is the scripture lemma of the next chapter (ΚΗʹ) and is picked up there; it appears at the tail of this file only as the transition into the resurrection account.

# Matthew 27 (ΚΖʹ) — Lemma 57 · Matt. 27:50–53 (cf. Luke 23:46)

> **«Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.»**[^1]

For not by compulsion, but willingly, did he give up the spirit. For this is what "I commit" signifies; and it indicates also that he is about to take it back again — for such is a deposit, *being* given back again.[^2] And thanks *be* to the Lord, that, that one having died and his spirit having been committed into the hands of the Father, from that time the souls of the saints also are placed into the hands of God, but not, as before, in the recesses of Hades.[^3] So that the death of Christ has become a sanctification for us; wherefore also with a loud voice he summons death, *which* dared not approach unless it were called.

And the veil of the temple was a certain linen cloth hanging in the middle of the temple, and walling off the inner things from the outer, like a kind of partition-wall. This, accordingly, is torn, God showing that the temple — the inaccessible, the unseen, whose innermost parts the veil walled off — will thus be made common and be profaned, so as to be accessible and visible to all.[^4] But some state other reasons also. For the veil being torn signified, they say, the removal of the letter of the law, and that all the things of the law will be revealed — which formerly were concealed by the letter as by a kind of veil — and that the things before unclear and enigmatic will now be made manifest, having found their outcome in Christ.[^5] And it is possible to say this also, that [it was the custom of the Jews] in griefs[^6] to tear their tunics. And now, therefore, the divine temple, as though vexed at the death of God, tore off its own garment, the veil. And one might say other things as well, but these suffice.[^7]

And the elements were shaken, at once both showing the one suffering to be *the* Creator, and at the same time making clear that the *present* state of things will be moved; for Scripture assigns the earthquake to the removal of *existing* affairs.[^8] There occurred, therefore, the transfer of God's visitation from the Jews to the nations; and the rocks — that is, the stony hearts of the gentiles — were split, and received the seed of truth, and those who had been deadened by their sins rose up, and came into the holy city, the Jerusalem above, and were made manifest to the many, those traveling by the broad way; and having been made manifest to them, they became to them an archetype of a good life and of conversion. For he who has seen the man formerly deadened by the passions, *now* converted and gone up into the holy city in the heavens, imitates him altogether, and himself also is converted.

These things, on the one hand, therefore, have been thought out rather elaborately;[^9] but you, know that the raising of the dead which occurred at the cross of the Lord made clear also the freedom of the souls in Hades. And those who had risen then were seen by many, so that what had happened might not seem to be a phantasm; and these rose for the sake of the sign, and it is evident that they died again. But some say that after Christ rose, these also rose, and no longer died; but this I do not know whether one ought to accept.

[^1]: The embedded scripture quotation is Luke 23:46 (*Πάτερ, εἰς χεῖράς σου παρατίθημι τὸ πνεῦμά μου*), given as the answer to the previous lemma's question "But what was the cry?" Greek *παρατίθημι* is "I place beside / entrust / commit / deposit"; rendered "commit," but its commercial-legal sense of lodging a *deposit* (*παραθήκη*) is the pivot of the comment just below, so the same root is kept visible.
[^2]: Greek *τοιοῦτον γὰρ ἡ παραθήκη, πάλιν ἀποδιδομένη* — "for such is the deposit (*παραθήκη*), *being* given back again": a deposit is by its nature something entrusted to be returned to the one who lodged it, hence the point that Christ will take his spirit back. (Source OCR *ἀποἘιδομένη* securely = *ἀποδιδομένη*.)
[^3]: Greek *ἐν τοῖς τοῦ ᾅδου μυχοῖς* — "in the recesses/innermost chambers of Hades." (Source OCR *iiατρὸς* = *Πατρὸς*, "of the Father.")
[^4]: The clause ends in the source with a Greek question mark (*βεβηλωθήσεται;*), but the sense is plainly declarative; punctuated as a statement. Greek *χυδαιωθήσεται* ("will be made common/vulgarized") and *βεβηλωθήσεται* ("will be profaned") are paired near-synonyms; both future passives.
[^5]: Greek *φανερωθήσεται νῦν εἰς τὸν Χριστὸν ἐκβάντα* — the participle *ἐκβάντα* ("having gone out / issued / turned out, resulted") is used of things that "come to pass" or "find their outcome"; rendered "having found their outcome in Christ." An alternative is "the things … having issued forth into Christ."
[^6]: The source is corrupt here: *Ἔστι δὲ καὶ τοῦτο εἰπεῖν, ὅτι μίαις, περιῤῥήγνυσθαι τοὺς χιτῶνας.* A subject-clause has dropped out (something like "it was the custom of the Jews"), and *μίαις* is a garbled dative — most plausibly *ἀνίαις* ("in griefs/distresses"), which fits the following image of the temple, grieved, tearing its garment; supplied in brackets and flagged. The custom of rending garments in mourning is the point.
[^7]: Source OCR *ἀλλομύτάρκη ταῦτα* securely = *ἀλλ᾽ ἀρκεῖ ταῦτα* (or *ἀλλ᾽ ἀρκετὰ ταῦτα*), "but these suffice."
[^8]: Greek *ἐπὶ τῆς τῶν πραγμάτων μεταθέσεως τάττει* — "assigns/ranks [the earthquake] with respect to the transposition (*μετάθεσις*) of affairs (*πράγματα*)," i.e. Scripture uses earthquakes as a sign of a change in the order of things. The same word *μετάθεσις* is picked up in the next sentence for the "transfer" of God's visitation from Jews to gentiles.
[^9]: Greek *περιεργότερον ἐνοήθη* — *περίεργος* means "over-curious, elaborate, done with excessive pains"; the comparative here concedes that the foregoing allegory (rocks = gentile hearts, etc.) has been developed rather more ingeniously than plainly, before Theophylact gives the simpler literal sense ("but you, know that…").

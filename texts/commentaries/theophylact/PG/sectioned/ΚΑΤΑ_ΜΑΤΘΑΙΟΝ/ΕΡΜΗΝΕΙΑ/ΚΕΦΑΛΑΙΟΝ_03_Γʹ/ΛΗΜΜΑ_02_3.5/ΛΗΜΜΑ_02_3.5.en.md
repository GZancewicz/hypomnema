# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 3:1

> **John the Baptist comes.**[^1]

For this reason John was sent from God, so that, reproving the Jews, he might persuade them to come to a perception[^2] of their own evils, and thus to receive Christ. For unless someone should take perception of *his* sins, he does not come to repentance. Accordingly, John was sent.

[^1]: Greek «Παραγίνεται Ἰωάννης ὁ Βαπτιστής» — the verb is a historic present, «παραγίνεται» ("comes / arrives / is present"); rendered "comes" to keep the present tense Theophylact quotes.
[^2]: Greek «εἰς αἴσθησιν ἐλθεῖν τῶν οἰκείων κακῶν». The noun «αἴσθησις» means "perception, sensation, awareness"; here the coming-to-*awareness* of one's own evils. Rendered "perception" (and again just below for the repeated «αἴσθησιν»), keeping the same gloss for the repeated word; "awareness" or "consciousness" would render the sense equally.

# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 3:13–14

> **…John, to be baptized by him; but John was preventing him, saying:**[^1]

Being pure, he is baptized, in order that he might wash us clean, and in order that he might show us that, whenever we are about to be baptized, *we ought* to purify ourselves beforehand — lest we defile the baptism, being afterwards easily soiled through our evil habit.[^2] And John prevents him, so that those seeing might not suppose him too to be one of the many being baptized unto repentance.[^3]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 28. Greek «Ἰωάννην, τοῦ βαπτισθῆναι ὑπ´ αὐτοῦ· ὁ δὲ Ἰωάννης διεκώλυεν αὐτὸν, λέγων.» — "…John, to be baptized by him; but John was preventing him, saying." *διεκώλυεν* is imperfect ("was preventing / kept preventing"), conveying attempted, ongoing hindrance; cf. Matt. 3:14. The genitive articular infinitive *τοῦ βαπτισθῆναι* expresses purpose ("in order to be baptized").
[^2]: Greek «ἵνα δείξῃ ἡμῖν, ἐπ´ ἂν μέλλωμεν βαπτισθῆναι, προκαθαίρεσθαι» — «ἐπ´ ἂν» is *ἐπὰν* ("whenever, as often as"). *προκαθαίρεσθαι* ("to purify oneself beforehand") depends on "he might show," left without an explicit "that we ought," which is supplied. *ῥυπαινόμενοι* ("being soiled / made filthy") and *μολύνωμεν* ("we defile") are a paired image of dirtying.
[^3]: Greek «ἵνα μὴ νομίσωσιν οἱ ὁρῶντες, καὶ αὐτὸν ὡς ἕνα τῶν πολλῶν εἰς μετάνοιαν βαπτιζόμενον». The OCR «νομέσωσιν» is a certain misprint for *νομίσωσιν* ("they might think/suppose"). *καὶ αὐτὸν* = "him also / him too," i.e. Jesus reckoned among the crowd. *εἰς μετάνοιαν* — "unto/for repentance."

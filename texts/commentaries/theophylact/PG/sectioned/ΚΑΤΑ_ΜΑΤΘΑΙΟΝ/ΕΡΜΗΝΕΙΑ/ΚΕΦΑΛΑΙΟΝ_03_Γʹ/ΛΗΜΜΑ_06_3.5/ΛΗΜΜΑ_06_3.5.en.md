# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 3:4

> **And a leathern girdle about his loin.**

All the saints are brought in girded by the Scripture, as being always at work; for the careless and the luxurious[^1] are not girded, as now the Saracens.[^2] Or *it is* because they had mortified[^3] the passions of desire; for the leather is a part of a dead animal.

[^1]: Greek «τρυφηλοί» — "given to luxury, soft, effeminate, self-indulgent," from τρυφή ("luxury, softness"). Contrasted with the girded, active saints; rendered "the luxurious."
[^2]: Greek «οἱ Σαρακηνοί» — a contemporary (11th-c. Byzantine) allusion, "the Saracens," here cited as an example of the ungirded, loose-robed and indolent. Kept as Theophylact writes it.
[^3]: Greek «ἀνέκρωσαν» (from ἀπονεκρόω, "put to death, mortify, deaden"). The leather girdle, taken from a dead beast, signifies the deadening of the passions of desire; rendered "had mortified."

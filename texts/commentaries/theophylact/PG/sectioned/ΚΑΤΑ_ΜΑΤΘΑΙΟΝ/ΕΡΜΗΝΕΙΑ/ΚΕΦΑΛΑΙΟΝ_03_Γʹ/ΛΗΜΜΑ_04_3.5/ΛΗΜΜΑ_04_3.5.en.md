# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 3:2-3

> **For the kingdom of the heavens has drawn near.**

By "kingdom of *the* heavens" he means the first and second coming of Christ, and the virtuous life. For whenever, walking upon earth, we conduct ourselves[^1] as in heaven, not living passionately,[^2] we have *the* kingdom of *the* heavens.

For this is he *who was* spoken of by Isaiah the prophet, saying: "A voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way of *the* Lord, make his paths straight." By "way," the Gospel is meant; but by "paths," the ordinances of the law, as *being* worn-down and old.[^3] Accordingly he says: Become ready for the evangelical way of life, and make the paths[^4] of the law straight too — that is, spiritual. For the spirit is straight.[^5] Whenever, therefore, you should see a Jew understanding the things of the law carnally, say that this *man* has not made the paths straight — that is, he does not understand the law spiritually.

[^1]: Greek «πολιτευώμεθα» (from πολιτεύομαι, "to live as a citizen, order one's public conduct"). It denotes the whole manner of one's civic/moral life; rendered "conduct ourselves," with "live as citizens" as the more literal alternative.
[^2]: Greek «μὴ ἐμπαθῶς ζῶντες». The adverb «ἐμπαθῶς» means "subject to the passions (πάθη)," not merely "with emotion"; the sense is living in bondage to the passions. Rendered "passionately," but the technical force is "in a passion-driven way."
[^3]: A word-play: the «τρίβοι» ("paths," well-trodden tracks) are called «κατατετριμμένα» ("worn-down, rubbed away") — both from the root τρίβω, "to rub, wear." Theophylact glosses the "paths" as the legal ordinances precisely because they are *worn out* and *old*. The pun cannot be reproduced in English.
[^4]: OCR corruption: the text reads «καὶ τὰς νομικὰς δὲ Ἀνελὰς εὐθείας». «Ἀνελὰς» is garbled and is best restored as «τρίβους» ("paths"), matching the object throughout the passage ("make the paths of the law straight"); translated on that restoration.
[^5]: Greek «τὸ πνεῦμα… εὐθές ἐστιν» — "straight / upright / direct." The same adjective «εὐθύς» ("straight") that the prophecy applies to the "paths" is here applied to the spirit, grounding the equation of "straight paths" with the "spiritual" reading of the law.

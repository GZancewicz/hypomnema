# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 3:7

> **Offspring of vipers, who showed you to flee from the coming wrath? (Matt. 3:7)**[^1]

Bitterly he converses with them, knowing their crookedness; but nevertheless he also praises them, saying: Who showed you? For he marvels how this came to pass — namely, that their wicked generation repented. And he calls them offspring of vipers, because just as these *creatures*, devouring the wombs, thus come forth,[^2] so also these *men* were murdering their fathers — that is, their teachers, the prophets. And "from the coming wrath" — "of Gehenna," he says.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Γεννήματα ἐχιδνῶν, τίς ὑπέδειξεν ὑμῖν φυγεῖν ἀπὸ τῆς μελλούσης ὀργῆς;» The verb *ὑπέδειξεν* means literally "pointed out, indicated, gave a hint/warning to"; rendered "showed" here to keep the comment's play (John "showed / gave them a hint," and Theophylact says he "marvels" at it). "Who warned you" is a defensible alternative.
[^2]: Theophylact grounds the insult "offspring of vipers" in a piece of natural lore: the young vipers were thought to eat their way out through the mother's womb, thereby killing the parent. The Greek *κατεσθίοντα τὰς μήτρας* = "eating through / devouring the wombs." The point of the analogy: as the young viper destroys the parent that bore it, so these men destroyed the prophets, their spiritual fathers.
[^3]: The source has several minor OCR corruptions here, all securely recoverable: *γινώσκον* → *γινώσκων* ("knowing"); *ἓὲ* → *δὲ*; *ἑεότι* → *ὅτι* ("because"); *ἂιδασκάλους* → *διδασκάλους* ("teachers"). The final clause glosses the lemma's «ἀπὸ τῆς μελλούσης ὀργῆς» ("from the coming wrath") as *τῆς γεέννης* ("of Gehenna").

# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 3:10

> **Not bearing good fruit.**[^1]

He did not say "Not *having* borne," but "Not bearing."[^2] For one must bear fruit always. For if yesterday you showed mercy, but today you plunder, you are not well-pleasing.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Μὴ ποιοῦν καρπὸν καλόν» — the continuation of Matt. 3:10.
[^2]: The whole comment turns on a participle-tense contrast that English cannot show by word-shape alone: *ποιοῦν* is the **present** participle ("bearing," ongoing), whereas *ποιῆσαν* is the **aorist** participle ("having borne," a single completed act). Theophylact's point: the Gospel does not condemn the tree that *once failed* to bear, but the tree that is *not now bearing* — the requirement is continual fruitfulness. "*Having*" is italicized to flag the tense that "borne" alone would not convey.
[^3]: Greek *οὐκ εὐαρεστεῖς* — *εὐαρεστέω* = "to be well-pleasing" (sc. to God); the object is unexpressed in the Greek. The tenses again carry the sense: *ἠλέησας* ("you showed mercy," aorist) set against *ἀρπάζεις* ("you plunder / rob," present) — a past act of mercy does not offset present rapacity.

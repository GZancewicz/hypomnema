# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 3:6

> **were baptized in the Jordan by him, confessing their sins. (Matt. 3:6)**[^1]

Even if they were being baptized, yet nevertheless the baptism of John did not have remission of sins. But John was proclaiming repentance only, and the *words* "unto remission of sins"[^2] — that is to say, he was guiding *them* to the baptism of Christ, from whom is the remission of sins.[^3]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in the previous lemma (Matt. 3:5, "Then there was going out to him Jerusalem…"). OCR *ἀξομολογούμενοι* = *ἐξομολογούμενοι* ("confessing"). *ἐβαπτίζοντο* is imperfect ("were being baptized").
[^2]: The Greek here is corrupt: the source reads *καὶ εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν ἱφεοινἀντὶ τοῦ, εἰς τὸ τοῦ Χριστοῦ βάπτισμα ὡδήγει*. The garbled *ἱφεοινἀντὶ τοῦ* securely contains *ἀντὶ τοῦ* ("that is to say / instead of *saying*"), the standard formula introducing a paraphrase; the intervening letters are unrecoverable and left untranslated. The phrase *εἰς ἄφεσιν ἁμαρτιῶν* ("unto remission of sins") is the Gospel description of John's baptism (cf. Mark 1:4; Luke 3:3), which Theophylact is here glossing. OCR *κβαπτίζοντο* = *ἐβαπτίζοντο*, *τκήρυττεν* = *ἐκήρυττεν*, *Ἱοάννου/ἱάπτισμα* = *Ἰωάννου/βάπτισμα*.
[^3]: Greek *παρ᾽ οὗ ἡ ἄφεσις τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν* (OCR *σαρ´ οὗ*). The relative *οὗ* is genitive and could be masculine ("from whom," i.e. Christ) or neuter ("from which," i.e. the baptism, its grammatically nearest antecedent, *τὸ βάπτισμα*). "From whom" (Christ) is chosen for the sense; "from which" (the baptism) is the alternative.

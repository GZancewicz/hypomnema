# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 3:11

> **Of whom I am not fit to bear the sandals.**[^1]

I am not even his lowest slave, he says, so as to bear these. And by "sandals" you will understand also his two descents: the one from heaven to earth, and the one from earth into Hades.[^2] For the sandals are the leathern flesh and the deadening.[^3] These two descents, therefore, the Forerunner is not able to bear, being unable to comprehend how they came about—[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Οὗ οὐκ εἰμὶ ἱκανὸς τὰ ὑποδήματα βαστάσαι» (Matt. 3:11). The source's opening «Οὐ» is an OCR slip for the relative *Οὗ* ("of whom"), as the sense and the next clause require. Matthew's verb is *βαστάσαι* ("to bear, carry"), not Mark's *λῦσαι* ("to loose"); rendered "bear," which also sets up the wordplay of the comment (the Forerunner cannot "bear" = carry = comprehend the two descents).
[^2]: Greek *τὰς δύο καταβάσεις … τὴν ἐκ γῆς εἰς τὸν ᾅδην* — the two "descents" (*καταβάσεις*): the Incarnation (heaven → earth) and the descent into Hades (earth → Hades).
[^3]: Greek *ἡ δερματίνη σὰρξ, καὶ ἡ νέκρωσις* — "the leathern flesh and the deadening." *δερματίνη* ("of skin, leathern") ties the two descents to the "sandals" (leather footwear): the fleshly body worn in the Incarnation, and *νέκρωσις* ("putting to death, mortification, the state of deadness") answering to the descent into Hades. "Deadening" is chosen to keep the process-sense of the noun; "deadness / mortification" are equally defensible.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off here mid-sentence at *πῶς ἐγένοντο* ("how they came about"); the OCR gives no more. The thought — that the two descents surpass the Forerunner's comprehension — is left grammatically incomplete in the source. Translated as far as the legible text runs.

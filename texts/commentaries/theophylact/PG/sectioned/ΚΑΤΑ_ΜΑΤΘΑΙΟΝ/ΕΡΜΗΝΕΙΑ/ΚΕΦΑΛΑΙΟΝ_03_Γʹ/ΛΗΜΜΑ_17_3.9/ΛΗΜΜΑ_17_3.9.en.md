# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 3:9

> **For I say to you that God is able even from these stones to raise up children to Abraham. (Matt. 3:9)**[^1]

By "stones" the nations too are understood, from whom many believed. But nevertheless John also says this thus, simply: that even from stones *God* is able to make children for Abraham. For indeed the womb of Sarah too was a stone, on account of its barrenness, but nevertheless she bore *a child*; and the Lord also raised up children for Abraham from the stones. When? When, as he was being crucified, many, having seen the rocks split, believed.[^2]

[^1]: Greek «Λέγω γὰρ ὑμῖν ὅτι δύναται ὁ Θεὸς καὶ ἐκ τῶν λίθων τούτων ἐγεῖραι τέκνα τῷ Ἀβραάμ.» *ἐγεῖραι* (aorist infinitive of *ἐγείρω*) = "raise up"; the same verb (*ἤγειρε*, "raised up") returns at the end of the comment for the resurrection-at-the-crucifixion scene, a verbal echo kept in English.
[^2]: Theophylact gives the lemma two senses. First, *μὲν*: allegorically the "stones" are the Gentile *nations* (*τὰ ἔθνη*). Second, the plain sense: God can literally make descendants from stones — illustrated by Sarah's barren womb (called "a stone," *λίθος*, for its sterility, *στείρωσις*) which yet bore Isaac, and by the rocks that split (Matt. 27:51, *τὰς πέτρας ῥαγείσας*) at the crucifixion, at which many believed and so became children of Abraham. Note the shift from *λίθος* (the lemma's word) to *πέτρα* (the split rocks). Minor OCR: *ποῦτο* → *τοῦτο*; *Σάῤῥας* = Sarah.

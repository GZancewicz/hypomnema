# Matthew 3 (Γʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 3:15–16

> **Then he permits him; and Jesus, having been baptized.**[^1]

Being thirty years old he is baptized, since this age receives all the *kinds of* sins.[^2] For in the first age, on the one hand, *there is* much thoughtlessness;[^3] but in the second, on the other hand, that of the younger *men,* *there is* much flame of desire and of anger; and in this *age,* that of grown men, *there is* much love of money. He waited, therefore, *for* this age, that through all the ages he might fulfil the law, and might sanctify us.

[^1]: Greek «Τότε ἀφίησιν αὐτόν· καὶ βαπτισθεὶς ὁ Ἰησοῦς.» (OCR *θαπτισθεὶς*, securely = *βαπτισθεὶς*, "having been baptized"). *ἀφίησιν* is the same verb *ἀφίημι* rendered "permit" in the lemma of §33: "then he lets him *be* / permits him." The lemma spans the close of Matt. 3:15 ("then he suffered him") into the opening of 3:16 ("and Jesus, having been baptized"); it breaks off mid-clause and is continued in Lemma 36.
[^2]: Greek *αὕτη ἡ ἡλικία δέχεται πάντα τὰ ἁμαρτήματα* — "this age/stage-of-life admits all the sins," i.e. is liable to sins of every kind. *ἡλικία* ("age, time of life, stage of life, maturity") is the pivot word of the whole comment and recurs below; rendered "age" throughout to keep the thread visible.
[^3]: OCR *ἀνόηνον*; securely = *ἀνόητον* ("foolishness, thoughtlessness, want of understanding"), neuter substantive. The three stages are set in a *μὲν … δὲ … δὲ* series: childhood (folly), youth (*τῶν νεωτέρων*, the flame of *ἐπιθυμία* and *θυμός* — desire and anger), and full manhood (*τῶν ἀνδρῶν*, *φιλοχρηματία*, love of money).

# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 22:28

> **…resurrection, of which of the seven shall she be wife?[^1] For all had her.**

The Pharisees having been muzzled[^2] together with the Herodians, again the Sadducees make trial *of him*. Now the sect of these *men* was of this sort: they believed neither *in* a resurrection, nor *in* a spirit, nor *in* an angel, being disposed contrariwise to the Pharisees. Therefore they compose a thing *that had* not happened. For grant[^3] that the two took her and died — how would the third not have taken it for an ill omen[^4] and refused the marriage, being schooled by those before him? Accordingly, then, they fabricate *it*, supposing that they would throw Christ into perplexity, so that they might discredit the resurrection; and they take Moses as advocate for their fabrication. And they said the brothers were seven, so that [?to make the difficulty the greater][^5] … "and of which of them," they say, "shall the woman be?" Therefore one might say: O foul Sadducees, the woman shall belong to the one who married *her* first, if indeed we should concede that even in the resurrection there is marriage; for the others *are* supposititious,[^6] not lawful husbands.

[^1]: Greek «τίνος τῶν ἑπτὰ ἔσται γυνή;» (OCR «γυνής» with a stray final sigma → **γυνή**). "shall she be wife" — the subject "she" is supplied; γυνή carries both "woman" and "wife."
[^2]: Greek «Ἐπιστομισθέντων» (from *ἐπιστομίζω*, "to put something into the mouth," hence "to muzzle, stop the mouth, silence"). The vivid image is kept. In the OCR a stray "κ" precedes the word, an artifact (perhaps a marginal section-letter), and is omitted.
[^3]: Greek «Θὲς γάρ» — imperative of *τίθημι*, "put *the case*, suppose, grant," introducing a hypothetical.
[^4]: Greek «οὐκ οἰωνίσατο» (from *οἰωνίζομαι*, "to take an omen from the flight of birds," hence "to regard as ominous / draw a foreboding from"). The point: the third brother, warned by the deaths of the first two, would have shrunk from the match as ill-omened. Rendered "taken it for an ill omen."
[^5]: The Greek is defective here: «…ἵνα ἐπὶ [ ] καὶ τίνος αὐτῶν, φασίν, ἔσται ἡ γυνή;». A clause is lost between «ἵνα ἐπί» ("so that … upon/toward") and the resumed question; the sense is evidently that they multiply the brothers so as to press the difficulty to its extreme. The bracketed rendering is a conjectural filler, not in the legible text.
[^6]: Greek «ὑποβολιμαῖοι» — "supposititious, counterfeit, foisted in" (properly of a changeling child substituted for another). Theophylact's point: only the first husband is a true husband; the rest are spurious, so no rivalry over the woman arises even on the Sadducees' own premise.

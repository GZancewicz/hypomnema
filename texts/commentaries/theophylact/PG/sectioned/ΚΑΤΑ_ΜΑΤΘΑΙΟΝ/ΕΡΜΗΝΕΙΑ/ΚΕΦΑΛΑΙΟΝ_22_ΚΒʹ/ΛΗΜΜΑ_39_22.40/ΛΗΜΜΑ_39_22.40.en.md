# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 22:40

> **On these two commandments the whole law and the prophets hang."[^1]**

Out of measureless envy the tempter approaches; for since they saw the Sadducees put to shame, and the Lord praised for *his* wisdom, they draw near tempting *him*, *to see* whether he adds anything to the first commandment, as *one* correcting the law, so that they might find a handle against him. But the Lord, making public their wickedness — that they came not for the sake of learning, but from not having love, and rather because of envying and being jealous — shows[^2] love as surpassing the commandments. And he teaches that one must love God not partially, but *that we must* give ourselves wholly to God.

For we discern these three differences concerning the soul of man: the vegetative, the animal, the rational.[^3] For in respect of that whereby, on the one hand, it grows and is nourished and generates the like, it is likened to plants; but in respect of that whereby it is angered and desires, to the animals; and in respect of that whereby it thinks, it is called rational. See, therefore, these three signified here:

[^1]: The lemma quote is OCR-garbled as «ὅλος ὁ νόμος καὶ οἱ προφῆται κοέμανται»; *κοέμανται* is a certain error for **κρέμανται** ("hang / depend"), giving «On these two commandments the whole law and the prophets hang» (Matt. 22:40). The clause opens with *Ἐν ταύταις ταῖς δυσὶν ἐντολαῖς*, carried over from the end of Lemma 38.
[^2]: The OCR reads *κείννυσι* (and, in the parallel clause above, likewise a corrupt form); it is securely *δείκνυσι* ("shows / demonstrates"). Governed by the participle *δημοσιεύων* ("making public"), the sentence structure is: "the Lord, making public their wickedness…, shows love as surpassing the commandments."
[^3]: Theophylact's tripartite psychology: *τὸ φυτικόν* (the vegetative / plant-like), *τὸ ζωῶδες* (the animal / living-creature part), *τὸ λογικόν* (the rational). The three are named in that order here but are mapped onto the three clauses of the first commandment («heart… soul… mind») in the following lemmata (40–42) in a different order — heart → animal, soul → vegetative, mind → rational.

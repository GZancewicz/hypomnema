# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 22:16

> **they send to him their disciples with the Herodians. (Matt. 22:16)**[^1]

The thing that was happening was a plot; for this reason Luke also names such men "suborned agents,"[^2] as *men* sent underhandedly to plot against Christ. And the Herodians were either the soldiers of Herod, or those who supposed Herod to be the Christ; for since, when the rulers from Judah had failed, Herod reigned as king, they supposed that he is the Christ.[^3] Accordingly the Pharisees come with these to ensnare him. And hear how they speak with him, saying:[^4]

[^1]: The quotation continues from the previous lemma («…καὶ»); hence the anarthrous plural verb «ἀποστέλλουσιν» ("they send") opens this lemma mid-sentence.
[^2]: Greek *ἐγκαθέτους*, from Luke 20:20; an *ἐγκάθετος* is one secretly instigated or hired to lie in wait and entrap — a suborned agent, spy, or *agent provocateur*. Rendered "suborned agents" to capture the sense of men set on covertly by another.
[^3]: Greek *ἐκλιπόντων τῶν ἀρχόντων Ἰούδα* — "the rulers from Judah having failed," an allusion to Gen. 49:10 (*οὐκ ἐκλείψει ἄρχων ἐξ Ἰούδα*, "a ruler shall not fail from Judah"). The reasoning: the native rulership of Judah having ceased with the rise of the Idumaean Herod, some took Herod's kingship to be the fulfilment marking the Messiah.
[^4]: The sentence breaks off at the introduction to direct speech; the words spoken follow in the next lemma («Διδάσκαλε, οἴδαμεν…»).

# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 22:39

> **but *the* second *is* like unto it,[^1] (Matt. 22:39)**

*the one* rousing *us* toward the just *dealings* toward men.[^2] For two are the *things* that lead unto destruction: evil doctrines,[^3] and a corrupted life. In order, on the one hand, therefore, that we may not fall into impious doctrines, it is necessary to love God; but in order that we may not have a corrupted life, it is necessary to love the neighbor. For the one loving the neighbor fulfills all the commandments; and the one fulfilling the commandments loves God. So that these two commandments are welded together and stand firm through one another,[^4] and they encompass all the other commandments. For who, loving God and the neighbor, will either steal, or bear malice, or commit adultery, or murder, or commit fornication?

But this lawyer, on the one hand, came from the beginning tempting *him*, then, having been bettered by the answer of Christ, was praised — as also Mark says, that Jesus, having looked upon him, welcomed him,[^5] and said to him: "Thou art not far from the kingdom of God."[^6]

[^1]: Greek *δευτέρα δὲ ὁμοία αὐτῆς* — Matt. 22:39 (the folder's draws-from verse is given as 22:40). *δέ* = "but/and"; supplied "*the*" and "*is*" are italicized. Theophylact quotes only these opening words; the Gospel clause continues "Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself."
[^2]: Greek *εἰς τὰ πρὸς ἀνθρώπους δίκαια διεγείρουσα* — the parallel gloss to the first commandment (feminine participle *διεγείρουσα*, "rousing, stirring up," agreeing with *ἐντολή*). *τὰ πρὸς ἀνθρώπους δίκαια* = literally "the just *things* toward men," i.e. justice in one's dealings with people; "*dealings*" is supplied.
[^3]: Greek *δόγματα πονηρά* (OCR *όγματα*); the reading is secure. *δόγμα* here = "doctrine, teaching/opinion," picked up just below as *δόγματα ἀσεβῆ* ("impious doctrines").
[^4]: Greek *δι᾽ ἀλλήλων συγκροτοῦνται καὶ συνίστανται* — *συγκροτέω* is "to weld/beat together, knit into a compact whole"; *συνίστημι* here "hold together, stand firm/established." The pair stresses that the two commandments mutually reinforce and consolidate each other.
[^5]: Greek *ἐμβλέψας αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς, ἠσπάσατο αὐτόν* ("Jesus, having looked upon him, *greeted/welcomed* him"). The received text of Mark 12:34 has instead "when Jesus saw that he answered discreetly" and no verb of greeting; *ἠσπάσατο* ("greeted, welcomed, embraced") is Theophylact's paraphrase, translated as it stands.
[^6]: Greek *Οὐ μακρὰν εἶ τῆς βασιλείας τοῦ Θεοῦ* — Mark 12:34.

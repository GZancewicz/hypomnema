# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 49 · Matt. 22:45-46

> **[If therefore][^1] David calls him Lord, how is he his son? And no one was able to answer him a word, nor did anyone dare from that day to question him any longer. (Matt. 22:45-46)**

Since they supposed him to be a mere man,[^2] he overturns their opinion, and teaches the truth[^3] through the prophecy of David, that he is also Lord, proclaiming his own divinity. For when the Pharisees had said that the Christ is son of David — that is, a mere man — he says: How then does David name him Lord? and not simply Lord, but in *the* Spirit,[^4] that is, having had him revealed *to himself* through the grace of the Spirit?[^5] But he says these things not denying *that he is a* son of David, but showing this: that he is not *a* mere man, descended from the Davidic seed alone. And the Lord asks these things so that either — having said that "We do not know" — they might ask and learn; or — having said the truth — they might believe; or — having nothing to say — they might be put to shame and withdraw, no longer daring to question him.

[^1]: The lemma opens mid-sentence: the preceding lemma (48) breaks off at «Εἰ οὖν» ("If therefore"), and the protasis is completed here by «Δαυΐδ καλεῖ αὐτὸν Κύριον…» ("David calls him Lord…"). The bracketed "If therefore" is supplied from lemma 48 to make the sentence stand on its own.
[^2]: Greek *ψιλὸς ἄνθρωπος*, literally "a bare/mere man" — i.e. a merely human being, without divinity. This is the standard technical antithesis Theophylact presses throughout the passage (mere man vs. Lord/God); rendered "mere man" consistently. Anarthrous, hence the supplied "*a*."
[^3]: The OCR reads «τὸ ἐλγθὲ-»; securely = **«τὸ ἀληθὲς»** ("the truth"), confirmed by the recurrence of the phrase *εἰπόντες τὸ ἀληθές* ("having said the truth") later in the same comment.
[^4]: Greek *ἐν πνεύματι* — "in *the* Spirit" or "in spirit." The phrase echoes the Gospel lemma itself (Matt. 22:43, «πῶς οὖν Δαυὶδ ἐν πνεύματι καλεῖ αὐτὸν κύριον»); Theophylact glosses it in the next clause as the Spirit's inspiring grace, so "in *the* Spirit" (the Holy Spirit) is the intended sense. The Greek is anarthrous; the supplied article is italicized.
[^5]: Greek *διὰ τῆς τοῦ Πνεύματος χάριτος ἀποκαλυφθεὶς αὐτόν*. The syntax is loose: *ἀποκαλυφθείς* is a nominative aorist passive participle agreeing with David ("having been revealed-to"), yet it governs an accusative *αὐτόν* ("him," i.e. Christ). The sense is that David, through the Spirit's grace, had Christ revealed to him. Rendered "having had him revealed *to himself*" to keep David as subject while preserving the accusative object; a smoother alternative would be "to whom he [Christ] had been revealed through the grace of the Spirit."

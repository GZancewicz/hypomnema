# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 22:3, 7

> **Call those who have been called. (cf. Matt. 22:3)**[^1]

If they had been called, how were they going to call them again? Learn therefore that each of us has been called from nature to the good, being called by reason, the innate teacher.[^2] Nevertheless God sends also the outward teachers, so that those who have been called naturally by reason, these too may call from without. And the king sent his armies — that is, the Roman *ones* — and destroyed the disobedient Jews, and burned their city Jerusalem, as also the truth-loving Josephus says.[^3]

[^1]: These words continue the sentence broken off at the end of the preceding lemma (07): "and here you will inquire how he says, 'Call those who have been called.'" The phrase «Καλέσατε τοὺς κεκλημένους» ("Call those who have been called") corresponds most closely to Matt. 22:3 («ἀπέστειλε τοὺς δούλους αὐτοῦ καλέσαι τοὺς κεκλημένους»); Theophylact renders it as a direct imperative. The substance of the lemma, however (the armies and the burning of Jerusalem), expounds Matt. 22:7 — hence the double reference in the header.
[^2]: Greek «ὑπὸ τοῦ λόγου τοῦ ἐμφύτου διδασκάλου καλούμενος». Here *λόγος* is "reason" (the rational faculty), described in apposition as "the innate teacher" (*ὁ ἔμφυτος διδάσκαλος*); the phrase could also be read "by the word, the innate teacher." The "calling from nature" is the inward summons of reason, distinguished below from the "outward teachers."
[^3]: The reference is to Flavius Josephus's account of the Roman destruction of Jerusalem (A.D. 70) in *The Jewish War*; *φιλαλήθης* = "truth-loving."

# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 22:13-14

> **Having bound his hands and feet, (Matt. 22:13)**

That is, the active powers of his soul.[^1] For in the present age, on the one hand, it is possible to do and to work something, but in the age to come, on the other hand, all the active powers of the soul are bound; and it is not possible to do anything good for the counterbalancing of *one's* sins.[^2] And "the gnashing of the teeth" *is* the repentance which will then come about to no profit.

"But many are called" — for God calls many, or rather all; "but few chosen." Few also *are* those who are saved and worthy of being chosen by God; so that, on the one hand, the calling *is* God's, but whether *we* become chosen or not, on the other hand, is ours. Accordingly he shows that the parable was spoken on account of the Jews, who, on the one hand, were called, but were not chosen, on the other hand, inasmuch as they did not hearken.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *τὰς πρακτικὰς τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτοῦ δυνάμεις* — "the practical/active powers of his soul," i.e. the soul's faculties for action (*πρᾶξις*); the same adjective recurs just below and is kept as "active."
[^2]: Greek *εἰς ἀντισήκωσιν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν* — *ἀντισήκωσις* is "a counterbalancing, weighing-against," the offsetting of sins by good deeds set in the other scale-pan; "counterbalancing" preserves the metaphor.
[^3]: Greek *ὡς μὴ ἀκούσαντες* — "inasmuch as they did not hear"; *ἀκούω* here in the sense "hear favourably, heed, obey," hence "hearken" rather than mere auditory hearing.

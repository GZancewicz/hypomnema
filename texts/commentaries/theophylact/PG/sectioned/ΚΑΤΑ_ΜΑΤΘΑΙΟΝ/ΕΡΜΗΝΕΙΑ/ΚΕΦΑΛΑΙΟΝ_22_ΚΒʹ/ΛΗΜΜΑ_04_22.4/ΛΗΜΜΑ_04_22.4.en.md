# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 22:4

> **Again he sent other servants, saying: Tell those who have been called: Behold, I have prepared my breakfast[^1]; my bulls and the fatlings[^2] *are* slaughtered, and all things *are* ready[^3]; come to the wedding-feast. But they (Matt. 22:4)**

*This lemma carries only the Gospel text. Theophylact quotes the parable continuously across lemmata 1–7 and gives his exposition in lemma 7.*

[^1]: Greek *ἄριστον* — the earlier, morning/daytime meal (as against *δεῖπνον*, the evening "supper"). Theophylact makes the choice of word bear weight in lemma 7: it is called *ἄριστον* because the mystery was disclosed even in the former ages, whereas elsewhere it is called *δεῖπνον* because it appeared fully "toward evening," at the end of the ages. Rendered "breakfast" to keep the early-meal / evening-meal contrast with "supper" on which Theophylact's argument depends (and to agree with the exposition in lemma 7).
[^2]: Greek *οἱ ταῦροι* — literally "the bulls" (not "oxen"; so rendered here and in the exposition of lemma 7). *τὰ σιτιστά* — literally "the grain-fed *animals*," fatted beasts; Theophylact later (lemma 7) etymologizes *σιτιστά* from *σῖτος* ("grain, wheat"). Rendered "the fatlings."
[^3]: The OCR reads *πάντα ἱτοικα*, securely *πάντα ἕτοιμα* ("all things ready"). The supplied "*are*" (twice, with *τεθυμένα* and with *ἕτοιμα*) renders the periphrastic/verbless predicate. The lemma breaks off at *Οἱ δὲ* ("But they"), continuing into lemma 5.

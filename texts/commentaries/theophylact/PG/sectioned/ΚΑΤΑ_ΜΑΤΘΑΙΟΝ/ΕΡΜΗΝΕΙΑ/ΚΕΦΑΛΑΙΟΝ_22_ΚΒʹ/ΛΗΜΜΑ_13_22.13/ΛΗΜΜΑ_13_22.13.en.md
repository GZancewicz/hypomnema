# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 22:13-14

> **the king said to the servants: Having bound his hands and feet, take him and cast *him* out into the outer darkness; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of the teeth; for many are called, but few chosen. (Matt. 22:13-14)**[^1]

The entrance into the wedding, on the one hand, takes place without discrimination; for by grace alone were we all called, both good and evil; but the life afterward of those who have entered, on the other hand, will not be unexamined, but rather the king makes a very great examination of those who are found filthy after entering into the faith.[^2]

Let us therefore shudder, having considered that, if someone should not have a pure life, faith alone profits him nothing; for he is not only cast out of the wedding, but is also sent away into the fire. But who is the one wearing filthy garments? He who has not put on bowels of compassion, kindness, brotherly love.[^3] Many therefore, deceiving themselves with empty hopes, suppose that they will attain the kingdom of the heavens, and they insert themselves into the company of those reclining *at table*, imagining great things concerning themselves.

But the Lord, pleading justice with that unworthy man, shows us these two things: one, on the one hand, that he is loving of mankind; but the other, that we too must not condemn certain people, even if they should sin openly, unless the sinners be convicted. The Lord therefore says to the servants — the avenging angels —[^4]

[^1]: The quotation runs on from the previous lemma, which breaks off at «Τότε» ("Then"); hence this lemma opens mid-sentence with «εἶπεν ὁ βασιλεὺς» ("the king said"). The object of the two imperatives divides: «ἄρατε αὐτὸν» ("take him") carries the pronoun, while «ἐκβάλετε» ("cast out") has none, so the second "*him*" is supplied. «τὸ σκότος τὸ ἐξώτερον» is literally "the darkness, the outer *darkness*." The closing sentence «πολλοὶ γάρ εἰσι κλητοί, ὀλίγοι δὲ ἐκλεκτοί» is Matt. 22:14.
[^2]: The OCR of this lemma carries several minor, securely recoverable corruptions: «δεακρίσεως» → *διακρίσεως* ("discrimination"), «πονηρ-ὶ» → *πονηροί* ("evil"), «σἰσελθεῖν» → *εἰσελθεῖν* ("to enter"), «εὐρισκοιμένον» → *εὑρισκομένων* ("found"), «2ρίξωμεν» → *Φρίξωμεν* ("let us shudder"), «ὡφελεῖ» → *ὠφελεῖ* ("profits"), «ἂείκνυσιν» → *δείκνυσιν* ("shows"). None affects the sense.
[^3]: Greek *σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμῶν, χρηστότητα, φιλαδελφίαν* — "bowels of compassion, kindness, brotherly love," echoing the catalogue of Col. 3:12 (*σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ, χρηστότητα*). *σπλάγχνα* ("inward parts, bowels") is the seat of the affections; rendered with the traditional "bowels of compassion" to keep the physical image visible.
[^4]: Greek *τοῖς διακόνοις, ἀγγέλοις τιμωρητικοῖς* — "to the servants, *that is,* the punishing/avenging angels"; *τιμωρητικοῖς* is adjectival ("punitive"). The sentence breaks off here and is completed in the next lemma, which takes up «Δήσαντες αὐτοῦ χεῖρας καὶ πόδας».

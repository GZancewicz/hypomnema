# Matthew 22 (ΚΒʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 22:22

> **…hearing, they marveled, and having left him, they went away.**[^1] (Matt. 22:22)

Thinking to soften him and to make *him* slack by *their* praises, they flatter *him*, in order that, having been made slack, he might say that one must not give the tribute, and that from this they might seize him as a rebel[^2] and *as one* stirring up the people against Caesar. For this reason they also bring along the Herodians, as being of the king, in order that they might arrest him as a revolutionary.

"But you do not regard a person,"[^3] they say — that is, "you would say nothing to *win* the favor of Pilate or of Herod; tell us therefore: ought we also to be tributary to men, and to bring them the tax,[^4] just as *we bring* also to God the didrachm[^5] — or should we pay tax to God alone, and not also to Caesar?" But this they were saying, as I said, in order that, if he should say that one must not pay tax to Caesar, they might seize him and kill *him*, like those around Theudas and Judas, who said that one must not offer sacrifice on behalf of the name of Caesar.[^6] But Jesus, through the fact that Caesar had been stamped upon the coin, persuades them that one must render to Caesar his own things — that is, the things bearing his image — and that, in bodily and outward *matters*, *one must* be subject to the one who reigns, but in inward and spiritual *matters*, to God.

But it is necessary to understand *it* also thus: that each of us ought to render and to fling back the things of Caesar — that is, the things of the demon who rules over the things below — to that very one. For instance: you have anger from Caesar; cast this upon him, be angry against him — for thus you will be able also to render the things of God to God. But indeed, we too being twofold — for we are composed of soul and body — to the body, on the one hand, as to Caesar, we owe sustenance and coverings; but to the more divine *element* in us, the fitting things.[^7]

[^1]: This clause completes the sentence begun at the end of Lemma 22 (which broke off at "And"). *ἀκούσαντες* and *ἀφέντες* are aorist participles, kept as participles ("hearing," "having left").
[^2]: The OCR reads «σταπαστὸν … ἀνασιίσντα»; securely = **«στασιαστὸν … ἀνασείοντα»** ("a rebel / seditionist … stirring up, agitating"). Low-stakes reconstruction, noted for consistency.
[^3]: *οὐ λαμβάνεις πρόσωπον* — literally "you do not receive a face," a Hebraism for showing partiality (cf. *πρόσωπον λαμβάνειν*). Theophylact here paraphrases the flatterers' words; the Gospel lemma itself (Lemma 17) had *οὐ βλέπεις εἰς πρόσωπον ἀνθρώπων* ("you do not look upon *the* face of men"). Rendered literally to keep the idiom visible.
[^4]: A cluster of tax-vocabulary turns on one root: *ὑποτελεῖς εἶναι* ("to be tributary / liable to tax"), *τὸ τέλος* ("the tax / toll / customs-due"), *τελέσωμεν* ("should we pay tax"). The wordplay (*τέλος / τελέω*) cannot be reproduced in English; rendered "tributary / tax / pay tax."
[^5]: *τὸ δίδραχμον* — the didrachm, i.e. the temple half-shekel tax (cf. Matt. 17:24), contrasted here with the civil *κῆνσος*. The verb of the comparison is elided; supplied "*we bring*."
[^6]: The OCR is corrupt here: «ὡς τοὺς περὶ αΑλν καὶ Ἰούδαν, τοὺς λέγοντας μὴ δαχν θήεικ πὲρ τοῦ ὀνόματος». Reconstructed as **«ὡς τοὺς περὶ Θευδᾶν καὶ Ἰούδαν, τοὺς λέγοντας μὴ δεῖν θύειν ὑπὲρ τοῦ ὀνόματος τοῦ Καίσαρος»** — a reference to Theudas and Judas *the Galilean* and their revolts against Rome (cf. Acts 5:36–37; Josephus recalls Judas' refusal of the Roman census). *αΑλν* = *Θευδᾶν*; *δαχν* = *δεῖν* ("ought / must"); *θήεικ* = *θύειν* ("to sacrifice"); *ὑπὲρ τοῦ ὀνόματος τοῦ Καίσαρος* = "on behalf of the name of Caesar." The point is the offering of sacrifice on behalf of the emperor, refusal of which marked one as seditious. (An earlier reading of this clause conjectured a tax-verb, *θεῖναι*, "to lay/impose [tribute]," on the analogy of the surrounding tribute-context; but the natural construction with *ὑπέρ* and the received text favor *θύειν*, "to sacrifice." Reading uncertain.)
[^7]: The final clause is elliptical: the verb *ὀφείλομεν* ("we owe") from the parallel clause is understood — "but to the more divine *element* in us *we owe* the fitting things." *τὰ ἁρμόδια* = "the fitting / suitable things" (what befits the soul). *διττοί* = "twofold / double."

# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 19:20

> **The young man says to him: All these things have I kept from my youth. What do I still lack?**[^1]

Some slander[^2] this man as a boaster and a braggart.[^3] For how did he succeed[^4] in loving the neighbor, if indeed he was rich? For no one who loves the neighbor as himself is richer than the neighbor;[^5] and every man is a neighbor. But some understand this thus: Suppose (he says)[^6] that I had kept all these things; what still is lacking to me?

[^1]: Greek «Λέγει αὐτῷ ὁ νεανίσκος· Πάντα ταῦτα ἐφυλαξάμην ἐκ νεότητός μου. Τί ἔτι ὑστερῶ;» (Matt. 19:20). The verb *ὑστερῶ* means "I fall short, I am lacking, I am in want"; "What do I still lack?" (alternatively "In what am I still deficient?").
[^2]: Greek *διαβάλλουσι*, "slander, accuse, traduce."
[^3]: Greek *κομπαστὴν καὶ ἀλαζόνα* — "a vaunter and an impostor/braggart." The pair denotes empty self-display; *ἀλαζών* carries the sense of one who claims more than is true.
[^4]: Greek *κατώρθωσε*, "achieved, brought off successfully, made right" — here, "how did he manage to fulfill the loving of the neighbor."
[^5]: The argument: one who truly loved his neighbor as himself would not remain wealthier than that neighbor (he would share so as to equalize); and since every man is a neighbor, a rich man cannot have kept this commandment. Greek «οὐδεὶς γὰρ ἀγαπῶν τὸν πλησίον ὡς ἑαυτὸν, πλουσιώτερός ἐστι τοῦ πλησίον· πᾶς δὲ ἄνθρωπος, πλησίον.»
[^6]: Greek «Ὑπόθες, φησίν, ὅτι ταῦτα πάντα φυλαξαίμην· τί μοι ἔτι λείπει;» This second interpretation reads the young man's words as a hypothesis: "Grant (φησίν, 'he says') that I had kept all these — what still is lacking to me?" The optative *φυλαξαίμην* marks the supposition. OCR *οβτω* = *οὕτω*.

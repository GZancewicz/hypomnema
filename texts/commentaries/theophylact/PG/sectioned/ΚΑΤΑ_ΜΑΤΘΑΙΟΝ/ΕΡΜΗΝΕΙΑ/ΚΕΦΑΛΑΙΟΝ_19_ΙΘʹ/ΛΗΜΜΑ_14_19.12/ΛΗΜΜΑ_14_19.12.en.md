# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 19:12

> **there are eunuchs who were made eunuchs by men, and there are eunuchs who made eunuchs of themselves for the sake of the kingdom of the heavens. He who is able to receive *it*, let him receive *it*.**[^1]

Of few, he says, is this achievement of virginity.[^2] For there are certain eunuchs from their mother's womb — that is, there are certain *men* who from *their* natural temperament[^3] remain unmoved toward intercourse, who indeed are chaste without gaining *anything by it*.[^4] There are also those who are made eunuchs by men. But they make eunuchs of themselves for the sake of the kingdom of the heavens — not those who cut themselves off (for this is accursed),[^5] but those who exercise self-control. But understand *it* also thus: There is a eunuch by nature, the one who on account of *his* natural temperament is not readily moved toward sexual pleasures; and by men is made a eunuch the one who from human teaching has cut out the burning[^6] of the fleshly desire; but a eunuch of himself does he make who has been taught not by another, but by himself, and self-taughtly has inclined toward chastity. This one, therefore, is also the best, the one who has been guided not by another, but by himself, toward the kingdom of the heavens. But wishing virtue to be worked in us by *our own* deliberate choice,[^7] he says:

[^1]: Continuation of Matt. 19:12 (the citation begun in Lemma 13). The OCR has several trivial slips, all securely corrected: «φἴτινες» → «οἵτινες» ("who"), «δαυτοὺς» → «ἑαυτοὺς» ("themselves"), «πῶν» → «τῶν» ("of the"). The clause «Ὁ δυνάμενος χωρεῖν, χωρείτω» is quoted again and expounded separately in Lemma 15; here Theophylact repeats it as part of the running citation. *χωρεῖν* means "to make room for, contain, receive, hold" — the supplied object "*it*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek *κατόρθωμα*: a "right achievement, meritorious deed, success in virtue"; here the accomplished feat of virginity.
[^3]: Greek *φυσικὴ κρᾶσις*: literally the "natural blending/mixture," i.e. the bodily constitution or temperament (the mixture of the humors). Rendered "natural temperament" here and just below.
[^4]: Greek *ἀκερδῶς σωφρονοῦσιν* — "they are chaste/self-controlled unprofitably," i.e. their continence earns them no reward, since it is a condition of nature rather than a fruit of moral effort. *σωφρονέω* / *σωφροσύνη* is the virtue of temperance, self-mastery, chastity.
[^5]: Greek *ἐπικατάρατον* ("accursed"); the OCR runs it together as «ἑαυτοὺςπικατάρατον». The parenthesis condemns literal self-castration (cf. the Church's canonical prohibition), against which Theophylact reads the verse figuratively of self-control.
[^6]: Greek *πύρωσις*: "burning, inflammation, fiery heat" — here the inflammation of fleshly desire.
[^7]: Greek *προαιρετικῶς*: "by deliberate choice, by free preference" (from *προαίρεσις*, the deliberate moral choice). Theophylact's point is that Christ leaves virtue to the will rather than compelling it — hence the following words.

# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 19:25–26

> **But having heard *this*, his disciples were exceedingly astounded, saying: Who then can be saved? But Jesus, having looked upon *them*, said to them: With men this is impossible, but with God all things *are* possible. (Matt. 19:25–26)**

The disciples, being lovers of *their fellow* men,[^1] ask not on behalf of themselves — for they were poor — but on behalf of all men. The Lord, therefore, teaches *us* not to measure salvation by human weakness, but by the power of God. For if someone begins not to be covetous, he will advance also to draining off his superfluities, and from this he will proceed also to emptying out the necessities,[^2] and thus he will be prospered by *the* alliance of God.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *φιλάνθρωποι ὄντες* — "being lovers of mankind, kindly *toward others*." The word explains why their alarm is unselfish: not fear for their own salvation (they own nothing), but concern for everyone.
[^2]: A deliberate two-stage climax: *τὰ περιττά* ("the superfluous things," what is left over) then *τὰ ἀναγκαῖα* ("the necessary things," what one needs). The generous man first pours off (*ἀπαντλεῖν*, "bail/draw off," a nautical image) his surplus, and then goes on to empty out (*κενοῦν*) even his necessities.
[^3]: Greek *εὐοδωθήσεται συμμαχίᾳ Θεοῦ* — "he shall be made to prosper by *the* alliance/help of God" (*συμμαχία*, literally "fighting alongside, military alliance"). The dative is instrumental. This is the practical cash-value of "with God all things are possible": what is impossible to human weakness becomes possible through God's fighting alongside.

# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 19:22 (expounding 19:21)

> **But having heard the word, the young man went away grieving; for he had many possessions. (Matt. 19:22)**

"As many things as you said you had accomplished," he says, "these you accomplished in a Jewish manner;[^1] but if you wish *to be* perfect — that is, to be my disciple and a Christian — go, sell your possessions, and give at once all together, not holding back any, so that you may make your almsgiving unceasing." For he did not say, "keep giving to *the* poor," but, "give once and be stripped bare."[^2] Then, since there are some who, on the one hand, are merciful, but on the other hand have a life crammed full of every sort of filthiness, he says:[^3]

[^1]: Greek *Ἰουδαϊκῶς κατώρθωσας* — "you set *these* right in a Jewish way," i.e. according to the mere letter of the Law. Theophylact is glossing the young man's claim in 19:20 ("all these I have kept from my youth"): the works were done, but only in the outward, Judaic manner, not yet in the perfect, Christian manner Christ now demands.
[^2]: The contrast turns on a verbal aspect the English can only half-carry: *οὐ γὰρ εἶπε, δίδου πτωχοῖς, ἀλλὰ, δὸς ἅπαξ καὶ γυμνώθητι*. The present imperative *δίδου* would mean "keep on giving *piecemeal*," whereas the aorist *δὸς* (the word Christ actually uses in the lemma) means "give *once* and be done" — hence "give once and strip yourself bare" (*γυμνώθητι*, "be made naked"). Rendered to keep the once-for-all sense visible.
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at *φησί·* ("he says:"), introducing the next quoted words of the Lord; the thought is completed in the following lemma, which opens with «Καὶ δεῦρο ἀκολούθει μοι» ("And come, follow me").

# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 19:21b–22

> **"And come, follow me,"** — that is, "and have all the other virtue *as well*."

The young man, however, was grieved; for on the one hand he did desire *it*, and the soil of his heart was deep and rich,[^1] but the thorns of wealth were choking *it*;[^2] "for he had," it says, "many possessions." For he who has little, on the one hand, is not held fast by these things in like manner; for in many *possessions* the bond *is* more tyrannical.[^3] But since the Lord was conversing with a rich man, he says that "You shall have treasure in *the* heavens," since you are a lover of money[^4]

[^1]: The OCR here is garbled — *βαθεῖα καὶ πίων ἦν ἡ τῆ τῆς καρδίας αὐτοῦ* — but is securely recoverable as *βαθεῖα καὶ πίων ἦν ἡ **γῆ** τῆς καρδίας αὐτοῦ*, "the **soil** of his heart was deep and rich." The adjectives *βαθεῖα* ("deep") and *πίων* ("rich, fat") are feminine, agreeing with the supplied *γῆ* ("earth, soil"). The whole image is drawn from the Parable of the Sower (Matt. 13): good, deep soil, but choked by the thorns of riches (cf. Matt. 13:22, "the care of the world and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word").
[^2]: Greek *αἱ ἄκανθαι τοῦ πλούτου συνέπνιγον* ("the thorns of wealth were choking *it/together*"), again echoing the Sower parable (*συμπνίγω*, "choke, throttle"). OCR *αυνέπνιγον* = *συνέπνιγον*.
[^3]: Greek *ἐν γὰρ τοῖς πολλοῖς τυραννικώτερος ὁ δεσμός* — "for among many *possessions* the bond *is* more tyrannical." The point: the more one owns, the more tyrannically the attachment binds. *μὲν … δὲ* structure rendered "on the one hand … but."
[^4]: The lemma breaks off in mid-sentence at *ἐπεὶ φιλοχρήματος εἶ* ("since you are a lover of money"), followed in the source only by a stray "Κ" beginning the next section. The thought — that Christ dangles heavenly "treasure" precisely because a money-lover is moved by talk of gain — is left grammatically incomplete here and picked up as the exposition proceeds into the next lemma (19:23–24).

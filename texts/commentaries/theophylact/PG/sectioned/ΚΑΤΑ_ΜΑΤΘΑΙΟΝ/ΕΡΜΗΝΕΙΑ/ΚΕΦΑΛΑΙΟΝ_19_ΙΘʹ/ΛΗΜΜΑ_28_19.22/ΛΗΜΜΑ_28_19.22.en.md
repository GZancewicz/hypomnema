# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 19:23–24

> **But Jesus said to his disciples: Amen I say to you that a rich man will with difficulty enter into the kingdom of the heavens. And again I say to you, it is easier for a camel to pass through *the* eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. (Matt. 19:23–24)**

As long as *a man* is rich — and he himself, on the one hand, has superfluities, while others, on the other hand, *have* not even the necessities — he shall by no means enter into the kingdom of the heavens. But whenever he shakes off everything, then *he is* not rich, and thereafter he shall enter *into the kingdom*.[^1] For *it is* impossible for one having many *things* to enter, just as *it is* also impossible for the camel to pass through *the* eye of a needle. See, therefore, how above, on the one hand, he said *that* to enter *is* difficult, but here, on the other hand, *that it is* altogether impossible. And some say *that* "camel" *is* not the animal, but the thick rope which sailors use for casting the anchors.[^2]

[^1]: The verb is OCR-garbled as *αἱααλεήσεται*. Read here as *εἰσελεύσεται* ("he shall enter"), echoing the lemma's own *εἰσελεύσεται* and the immediately following reason-clause *Ἀδύνατον γὰρ ... εἰσελθεῖν* ("For *it is* impossible ... to enter"): the antithesis is that the rich man cannot enter, but once he shakes off everything he is no longer rich and so *can* enter. An alternative reconstruction is *ἐλεηθήσεται* ("he shall be shown mercy / obtain mercy"), which would fit the almsgiving-and-salvation logic; but the entering/entering parallel is more likely Theophylact's point: *Ἐπειδὰν μέντοι ἀποτινάξηται πάντα, τότε οὐ πλούσιος, καὶ λοιπὸν [εἰσελεύσεται?]*.
[^2]: This preserves the well-known ancient reading of *κάμηλον* ("camel") as *κάμιλον* — a nautical cable or "thick rope" (*τὸ παχὺ σχοινίον*) — the two words being near-homophones. Theophylact reports it as an opinion of "some" (*τινές*), without endorsing it.

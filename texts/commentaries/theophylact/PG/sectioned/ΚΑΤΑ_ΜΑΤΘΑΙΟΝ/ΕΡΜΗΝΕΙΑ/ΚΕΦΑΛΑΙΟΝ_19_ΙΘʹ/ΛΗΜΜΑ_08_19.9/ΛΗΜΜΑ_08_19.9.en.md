# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 19:9

> **…puts away his wife not for fornication, and marries another, commits adultery. And he who marries a woman who has been put away commits adultery.**[^1]

The Pharisees, having seen that the Lord had stopped their mouths,[^2] were thereupon compelled, and they bring Moses forward into the midst as legislating against Christ,[^3] and they say: How then did Moses command to give a bill of divorce and to put *her* away? The Lord, therefore, turning the whole of the accusation back upon their head, makes a defense on behalf of Moses, and says: Moses, in legislating this, does not say it *as* contrary to God, but he legislated this in view of your depravity; so that you might not, wishing through licentiousness to be with other *women*, do away with your former wives. For, being harsh *men*, they would have murdered their wives, had they been compelled to keep them. But now he enjoined *them* to give a bill of divorce to the *wives* hated by their husbands. But I say to you, he says, that it is good to cast out the wife who has committed fornication, as an adulteress; but if anyone casts out her who has not committed fornication, he becomes a contributing cause to her of adultery.[^4] But understand this also: that he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit,[^5] and there comes to be a conjugal union for the believer with Christ. For we have all become of one body with him, and we are members, part *by part*, of Christ.[^6] Rightly, therefore, is no one able to separate such a conjugal union, according to the word of Paul, who says:[^7]

[^1]: The lemma opens with the tail of the Gospel quotation begun in Lemma 7 (Matt. 19:9): *«…ἀπολύσῃ τὴν γυναῖκα αὐτοῦ μὴ ἐπὶ πορνείᾳ, καὶ γαμήσῃ ἄλλην, μοιχᾶται. Καὶ ὁ ἀπολελυμένην γαμήσας, μοιχᾶται.»* — "not for fornication" renders *μὴ ἐπὶ πορνείᾳ* (literally "not upon *the ground of* fornication"). OCR *γαμήαας* is securely *γαμήσας* ("having married / he who marries").
[^2]: Greek *ἐπεστόμισεν* — "muzzled, stopped the mouth of, put to silence." The metaphor is of putting a bit or muzzle on the mouth.
[^3]: Greek *ἀντινομοθετοῦντα* — "legislating in opposition (to)," i.e. setting up a rival / contrary law. The Pharisees set Moses against Christ as a counter-legislator.
[^4]: Greek *παραίτιος αὐτῇ γίνεται μοιχείας* — "he becomes a contributing cause (*παραίτιος*) of adultery to her": by casting out an innocent wife he drives her into a second union counted as adultery. *τὴν πορνευθεῖσαν* / *τὴν μὴ πορνευθεῖσαν* are passive-form participles of *πορνεύω*, "the woman who has committed fornication / who has not committed fornication."
[^5]: Cf. 1 Cor. 6:17, *ὁ κολλώμενος τῷ Κυρίῳ ἓν πνεῦμά ἐστι*. OCR *καλλώμενος* is securely *κολλώμενος* ("he who is joined/glued to"). Theophylact shifts the discourse from marriage to the union of the believer with Christ.
[^6]: Greek *σύσσωμοι* ("of one body together," cf. Eph. 3:6) and *μέλη ἐκ μέρους* ("members in part / part by part," cf. 1 Cor. 12:27). OCR *λριστοῦ σμεν* is securely *Χριστοῦ ἐσμεν* ("we are of Christ").
[^7]: The sentence breaks off into the Pauline citation that opens Lemma 9 (Rom. 8:35): *«Τίς ἡμᾶς χωρίσει ἀπὸ τῆς ἀγάπης τοῦ Χριστοῦ;»* ("Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?").

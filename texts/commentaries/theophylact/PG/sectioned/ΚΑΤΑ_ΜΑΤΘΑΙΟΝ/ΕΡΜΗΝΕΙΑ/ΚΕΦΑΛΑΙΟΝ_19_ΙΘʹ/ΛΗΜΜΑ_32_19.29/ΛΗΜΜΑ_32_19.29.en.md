# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 19:29

> **And everyone who has left houses, or brothers or sisters, or father or mother, or wife or children, or fields, for the sake of my name, shall receive a hundredfold, and shall inherit eternal life. (Matt. 19:29)**

So that no one might suppose that the thing *that was* said fits the disciples only, he broadened the promise to all who do the like. For they shall have, on the one hand, instead of the kinsmen according to *the* flesh, the intimacy with God and the brotherhood; but instead of the fields, paradise; and instead of the houses *made* of stones, the Jerusalem above; instead of a mother, the elder women in *the* Church; instead of a father, the presbyters; instead of a wife, all the faithful women — not by marriage, away *with the thought*![^1] — but by disposition and by spiritual relation and by the benevolent care toward them. But the Lord does not command *us* to separate simply from *our* own *kin*, but *only* when they hinder *us* toward piety. Just as he also commands *us* to despise both soul and body, *yet* not so as to kill ourselves. But see that God, being good, gives not these things only, but adds also eternal life.

Be earnest, therefore, you also, to sell the things *that* belong to you, and to give *them* to the poor. And *your* possessions are: for the irascible *man*, on the one hand, *his* anger; but for the adulterer, the adulterous disposition; and for the *one who bears* malice, *his* malice.[^2] Sell, therefore, these things, and give *them* to the poor demons, *who are* deprived of every good.[^3] Turn the passions back upon the craftsmen of the passions, and then you shall have the treasure — that is, Christ — in your heaven, that is, in your lofty mind. For he has heaven within himself, *the one who* has become such as the heavenly *one*.

[^1]: Greek *ἄπαγε* — literally "begone! / away!", an idiom of strong repudiation ("far be it," "God forbid"); it rejects the notion that the "faithful women" are had as wives in any marital sense.
[^2]: Theophylact plays on *ὑπάρχοντα* ("goods, possessions, the things one has," Matt. 19:21/Luke 12:33) read spiritually: the vices a man "possesses" are the property he is to sell off. Greek *τῷ μὲν θυμώδει ὁ θυμός· τῷ δὲ μοιχῷ ἡ μοιχευτικὴ διάθεσις· τῷ δὲ μνησικάκῳ ἡ μνησικακία* — "for the irascible, anger; for the adulterer, the adulterous disposition; for the grudge-bearer, grudge-bearing."
[^3]: The wordplay continues: give these "goods" (the passions) back to the "poor" (*πτωχοί*) — but here the poor are the demons, *who are* destitute of every good; so one restores each passion to the demon that authored it.

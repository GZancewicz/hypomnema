# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 19:28

> **But Jesus said to them: Amen I say to you, that you who have followed me, in the regeneration,[^1] when the Son of Man shall sit upon *the* throne of his glory, you also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel. (Matt. 19:28)**

Will they then be seated?[^2] No, but through the sitting he indicated the surpassing honor. And will Judas, therefore, be seated? No. For he said "those who have followed," that is, those *who followed* until the end; but that *man did* not *follow* until the end. And besides: often God promises good things to certain *people* who are worthy. But when they should be changed and become unworthy, he takes them away. And likewise also concerning the more forbidding *things*,[^3] often he threatens and does not bring *them upon us*, when we have been changed. But by "regeneration" understand the resurrection.[^4]

[^1]: Greek *παλιγγενεσία* — literally "a becoming-again, rebirth, regeneration." Theophylact himself glosses it at the close as *ἀνάστασις* ("resurrection"); rendered "regeneration" to keep the plain sense of the word he quotes, with his own definition supplied below.
[^2]: The OCR reads «Αρα καθεσθήσονται;»; securely *Ἆρα καθεσθήσονται;* The verb is the future of *καθέζομαι* ("to be seated, to sit"); *Ἆρα* is the interrogative particle, rendered "then." The following «διὰ τῆς καθέδρας» (OCR *καθέξρας*) turns on the noun *καθέδρα* ("a seat, a sitting, session"): the honor is shown not by literal enthronement but by the very image of being seated.
[^3]: Greek *ἐπὶ τῶν σκυθρωποτέρων* — "concerning the more gloomy / sullen / forbidding things," i.e. the threatened punishments, set in contrast to the "good things" (*ἀγαθά*) promised just above.
[^4]: The OCR is corrupt here as «Παλιγγενεσίαν δὲ τὴν μνμαστασιν νόες.»; securely recoverable as **«Παλιγγενεσίαν δὲ τὴν ἀνάστασιν νόει»** ("but understand the resurrection *by* 'regeneration'"), *νόει* being the imperative "understand, take *it to mean*."

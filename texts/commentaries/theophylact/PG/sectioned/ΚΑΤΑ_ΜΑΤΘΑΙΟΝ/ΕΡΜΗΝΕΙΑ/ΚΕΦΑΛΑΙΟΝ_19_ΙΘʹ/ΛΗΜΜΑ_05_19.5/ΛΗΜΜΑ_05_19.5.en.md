# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 19:5–6

> **said: "For this cause a man shall leave his father and *his* mother, and shall be joined to his wife, and the two shall be into one flesh. So that they are no longer two, but one flesh. What therefore God has yoked together, let not man separate." (Matt. 19:5–6)**[^1]

O the folly! They thought to muzzle Christ through the questions; for if he should say that it is lawful to put away the wife for every cause, they would be able to say to him: "How then did you say that let no one put away *his wife* except only the adulteress?"[^2] But if he should say that it is not lawful to put away the wife, they would slander him as legislating against Moses. For that *one* commanded to cast out the hated *woman*, even without reasonable cause. What then *does* Christ *do*? He shows that monogamy is *the* work and *the* legislation of him who fashioned us from *the* beginning. For from *the* beginning, he says, God yoked one *man* to one *woman*; so that it behooves not one man to be joined to many *women*, nor one *woman* to many *men*, but, as they were yoked together from *the* beginning, *so* to remain, not tearing apart the cohabitation irrationally.

And he did not say, "I made male and female," so that he might not strike at them, but indefinitely, "he who made."[^3] Thus therefore, he says, the good of marriage is so earnestly prized by God, that he even permitted *them* to leave *their* parents and to cleave to *their* husbands. But how *is it that* in Genesis, on the one hand, it is written that Adam said the *words*, "In return for this a man shall leave his father and *his* mother,"[^4] whereas here, on the other hand, Christ says that God said, "For this cause he shall leave," and the *rest*? We say therefore that it is also *the* word of God.[^5] Since accordingly they became one flesh, and *are* joined together through the union and the natural affection, just as it is accursed to cut one's own flesh, so also *is it* to tear apart the spouses. And he did not say, "Let not Moses separate," so that he might not disturb them, but simply "man," showing the distance between the God who yoked *them* together and the man who dissolves.[^6]

[^1]: The folder assigns this lemma to 19.5, but the Greek quotation runs through 19:6 («Ὃ οὖν ὁ Θεὸς συνέζευξεν, ἄνθρωπος μὴ χωριζέτω»); the header and reference are corrected to Matt. 19:5–6. *συνέζευξεν* / *μὴ χωριζέτω* = "yoked together" / "let not separate"; the yoke-metaphor (*συζυγία*, "yoking," i.e. marriage) recurs through the comment and is kept visible in the rendering.
[^2]: An allusion to Matt. 5:32 / 19:9; Greek *τὴν μοιχαλίδα* = "the adulteress," i.e. the wife guilty of adultery — the sole ground for divorce Christ had allowed. Rendered "except only the adulteress."
[^3]: Theophylact's point turns on the anarthrous, agentless *ὁ ποιήσας* ("he who made") of the quoted verse: Christ speaks impersonally rather than saying "I made," *ἵνα μὴ πλήξῃ αὐτούς* ("so that he might not strike / wound them"), i.e. so as not to provoke his hearers by claiming the deed as his own.
[^4]: Greek *Ἀντὶ τούτου* ("in return for this / instead of this"), Theophylact's citation of the Genesis wording (Gen. 2:24 LXX), set against the Gospel's *Ἔνεκεν τούτου* ("for this cause / on account of this") just above. The two prepositional phrases are near-synonymous ("because of this"); Theophylact marks the verbal difference between the Genesis text and Christ's citation, and both are rendered distinctly to preserve the contrast he draws.
[^5]: Greek *Λέγομεν οὖν ὅτι καὶ λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ ἐστιν* — terse and elliptical. The sense resolves the puzzle just raised: what Genesis reports Adam as saying is *also* God's word (Adam speaking under divine inspiration), so Christ may rightly ascribe it to God. The subject of *ἐστιν* is unexpressed; "it" is supplied. An alternative construal takes *καὶ* as "even" — "that it is even *the* word of God."
[^6]: The exposition breaks off here without a closing stop (the source has no final punctuation before Lemma 6, which resumes the scripture with the Pharisees' rejoinder about Moses, Matt. 19:7). Greek *τὸ μέσον* = "the middle / interval / difference," here the distinction between *τοῦ συζεύξαντος Θεοῦ* ("the God who yoked *them* together") and *τοῦ διαλύοντος ἀνθρώπου* ("the man who dissolves *it*").

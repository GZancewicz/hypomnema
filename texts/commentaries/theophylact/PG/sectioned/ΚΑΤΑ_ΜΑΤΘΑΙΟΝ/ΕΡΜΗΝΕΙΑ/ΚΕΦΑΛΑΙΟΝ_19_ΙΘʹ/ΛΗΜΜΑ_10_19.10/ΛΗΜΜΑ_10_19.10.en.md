# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 19:10

> **«His disciples say to him: If the case of the man with the woman is thus, it is not expedient to marry.»**[^1] (Matt. 19:10)

The disciples are thrown into confusion, and they say that: If for this reason they were joined together, that they might be one and might remain inseparable throughout the whole of life — so that, even if the woman be otherwise depraved, *one* does not dare to cast her out — it is not expedient to marry; for it is lighter[^2] not to marry, but to fight and to wrestle with natural desires, than to endure an evil wife. For by "the case of the man with the woman" he means the inseparable union.[^3] But some understand *it* also in this way, that: "If the case of the man is thus" — that is, if the man is going to have a case,[^4] that is to say, blame and accusation, as one casting out his wife unlawfully — it is not expedient to marry.[^5]

[^1]: Greek *ἡ αἰτία τοῦ ἀνθρώπου μετὰ τῆς γυναικός*. The noun *αἰτία* is deliberately ambiguous — "cause, ground, condition, relation, case," and also "charge, accusation, blame." Rendered "case" to hold both senses open; Theophylact exploits both meanings below (see note 4). "Man" (*ἄνθρωπος*) here is generic ("the person/husband"). "It is not expedient" renders *οὐ συμφέρει*.
[^2]: Greek *κουφότερον* — "lighter, easier *to bear*." The comparison is of burdens.
[^3]: First interpretation: *αἰτία* = the condition/relation, namely the indissoluble bond (*τὴν ἀδιάσπαστον ἕνωσιν*, "the inseparable union"). So the disciples reason: if marriage is this unbreakable, it is safer not to marry.
[^4]: Here the second sense of *αἰτία* is unfolded — *αἰτίαν … ἤγουν μομφὴν καὶ καταιτίασιν*, "a charge, that is to say, blame and accusation." The single word carries the pun: the "condition" of marriage (first reading) versus the "legal charge/liability" a man incurs by an unlawful divorce (second reading). English cannot reproduce both in one word; hence the footnoting.
[^5]: The final word is OCR *Ὑμῆσαι*, securely *γαμῆσαι* ("to marry"). The stray *ϛθδὲ* that follows in the source is a garbled lead-in to the next lemma, best read as *Ὁ δὲ* ("But he"), which joins *εἶπεν αὐτοῖς* at the head of Lemma 11 (Matt. 19:11, "But he said to them").

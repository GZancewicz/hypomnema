# Matthew 19 (ΙΘʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 19:16–17

> **…having come, said to him: Good teacher, what good thing shall I do that I may have eternal life? But he said to him: Why callest thou me good? No one *is* good, except one — God.**[^1]

This man came, not as tempting, but as wishing to learn, and thirsting for eternal life; but he came to Christ as to a mere man.[^2] And for this reason the Lord says: Why callest thou me good? No one *is* good except one — God. That is: If thou callest me good as *being* one of the teachers, thou sayest *it* ill; for no one of men is properly[^3] good — at once, on the one hand, because we are also changeable, turning away from the good; and at once, on the other hand, because human *goodness*, when compared to the goodness of God, is reckoned wickedness.[^4]

[^1]: Greek «προσελθὼν εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Διδάσκαλε ἀγαθέ, τί ἀγαθὸν ποιήσω ἵνα ἔχω ζωὴν αἰώνιον; Ὁ δὲ εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Τί με λέγεις ἀγαθόν; Οὐδεὶς ἀγαθός, εἰ μὴ εἷς ὁ Θεός.» The lemma continues the sentence begun in Lemma 19 (its subject is the "one" of 19:16). OCR *προσελθὸν* = *προσελθών*; *Ὁ ὲ* = *Ὁ δὲ*; *λέγε ς* = *λέγεις*; *εἴς* = *εἷς*. This is Matt. 19:16–17.
[^2]: Greek *ὡς ἀνθρώπῳ ψιλῷ* — "as to a bare/mere man," i.e. regarding Christ as merely human and not as God.
[^3]: Greek *κυρίως*, "properly, in the strict sense, in the primary meaning."
[^4]: Theophylact gives two coordinated grounds with *ἅμα μὲν … ἅμα δὲ* ("at once … at once," i.e. "partly … partly / both … and"). Rendered here to keep both the temporal-coincidence force of *ἅμα* and the *μὲν … δὲ* correlation visible. Greek: «ἅμα μὲν, ὅτι καὶ εὐμετάβολοί ἐσμεν ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ τρεπόμενοι· ἅμα δὲ, ὅτι καὶ συγκρινομένη πρὸς τὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ ἀγαθότητα ἡ ἀνθρωπίνη, πονηρία νομίζεται.» The noun *ἀγαθότης* ("goodness") is supplied with "human," which is feminine and agrees with an understood *ἀγαθότης*.

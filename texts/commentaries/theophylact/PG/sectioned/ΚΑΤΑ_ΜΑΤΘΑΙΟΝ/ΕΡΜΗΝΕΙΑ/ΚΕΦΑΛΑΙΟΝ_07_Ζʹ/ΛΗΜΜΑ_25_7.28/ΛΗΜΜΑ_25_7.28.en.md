# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 7:28–29

> **And it came to pass, when Jesus had finished these words, the crowds were astonished at his teaching. For he was teaching them as *one* having authority, and not as the scribes. (Matt. 7:28–29)**

Not the rulers were astonished — for how could they be, they who envied him? — but the guileless multitude.[^1] But they marveled not at the diction of the words, but at the boldness;[^2] for he was showing forth authority even above the prophets. For those said, "Thus says the Lord"; but Christ, as God: "I say to you."

[^1]: Greek *τὸ ἄκακον πλῆθος* — *ἄκακος* means "without malice / guileless / innocent," here contrasted with the envious *ἄρχοντες* ("rulers"). Rendered "guileless multitude"; "innocent" or "simple" would also serve.
[^2]: Greek *παῤῥησία* — "frankness, freedom / boldness of speech, outspokenness." It denotes speaking openly and without reserve; here the crowds marvel not at Christ's phrasing but at the confident, authoritative freedom with which he speaks (picking up *ὡς ἐξουσίαν ἔχων*, "as having authority," from the lemma). Rendered "boldness"; "frankness" or "freedom of speech" are alternatives.

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 7:21

> **Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of the heavens, but he that does the will of my Father who is in the heavens.** (Matt. 7:21)

Here he shows himself to be Lord, in saying: "Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord" — so that he calls himself God. And he teaches us that, if we should have faith without works, we are profited nothing thereby; for *by* "he that does the will," he did not say "he that has done it once," but "he that does it until death." And he did not say, "the will that is mine," lest he should offend[^1] those who hear, but, "*the will* of my Father"; but there is altogether one will of Father and Son, provided[^2] the Son is not an apostate.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *σκανδαλίσῃ* — "cause to stumble, give offence to, entrap." The concern is that hearers, not yet ready for so direct a claim of divinity, would be scandalized if he plainly demanded *his own* will be done; rendered "offend."
[^2]: Greek *ἐὰν οὐκ ἔστιν ἀποστάτης ὁ υἱός* — literally "if the Son is not an apostate," here a logical proviso: since the Son is not in rebellion against the Father, their will is necessarily one and the same. Rendered "provided …" to bring out the argumentative force.
[^3]: Greek *ἀποστάτης* — "rebel, deserter, apostate" (one who "stands off / revolts"). Theophylact's point is that only a Son at variance with the Father could have a will distinct from his; since the Son is no rebel, Father and Son share one will — hence saying "my Father's will" is no different from saying "mine."

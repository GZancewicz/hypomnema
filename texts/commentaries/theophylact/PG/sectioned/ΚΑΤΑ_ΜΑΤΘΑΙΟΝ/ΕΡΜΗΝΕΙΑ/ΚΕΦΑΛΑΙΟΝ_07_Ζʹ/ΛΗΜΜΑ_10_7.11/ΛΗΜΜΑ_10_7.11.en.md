# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 7:11

> **If therefore you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father who is in the heavens give good things to those who ask him? (Matt. 7:11)**

He names men "evil," as comparing them with respect to God; since our nature is good, as being God's creation, but according to our purpose…[^1]

[^1]: The exposition breaks off mid-word at «κατὰ δὲ προαί-», that is *προαίρεσιν* ("but according to *our* choice / deliberate purpose") — the sectioning cuts the sentence short. The sense implied is that our nature is good as God's handiwork, "but by our own *choice* we become evil." The antithesis is *φύσις* ("nature") vs. *προαίρεσις* ("moral choice / deliberate purpose"): men are called evil not by nature but by their chosen disposition.

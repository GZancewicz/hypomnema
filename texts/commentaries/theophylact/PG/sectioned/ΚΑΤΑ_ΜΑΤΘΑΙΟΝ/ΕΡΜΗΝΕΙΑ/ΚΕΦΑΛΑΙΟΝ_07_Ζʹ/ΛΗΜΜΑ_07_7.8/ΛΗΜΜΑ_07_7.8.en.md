# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 7:8

> **…who asketh receiveth, and he who seeketh findeth, and to him who knocketh it shall be opened. (Matt. 7:8)**[^1]

He enjoined upon us in the things preceding great and difficult things. Therefore he shows here how they will be accomplished, and that it is through continual prayer. For "Ask," he said — in the sense of "continually";[^2] for he did not say, "Ask once." Then also from a human example he confirms the thing said.

[^1]: This file resumes mid-verse: the head of the quotation, «πᾶς γὰρ ὁ» ("for every one *who*"), stands at the close of the previous lemma (06), and this lemma carries the continuation «…αἰτῶν λαμβάνει, καὶ ὁ ζητῶν εὑρίσκει, καὶ τῷ κρούοντι ἀνοιγήσεται» (Matt. 7:8). OCR "Κκρούοντι" is securely κρούοντι ("*who* knocketh").
[^2]: Greek *Αἰτεῖτε … ἀντὶ τοῦ ἀεί* — literally "'Ask' … instead of 'always.'" Theophylact reads the present imperative *αἰτεῖτε* as denoting continual, ongoing asking; the gloss *ἀντὶ τοῦ ἀεί* means "*it stands* for 'continually.'" His point turns on the contrast with the aorist «Αἰτήσασθε ἅπαξ» ("Ask once").

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 7:14

> **… strait *is* the gate, and afflicted *is* the way that leads away into life, and few are those who find it!**[^1] (Matt. 7:14)

The "How"[^2] is exclamatory. For he marvels — oh![^3] — how strait it is! How then does he say elsewhere: "My yoke *is* light"?[^4] Because of the recompenses *that are* to come.

[^1]: This lemma opens mid-sentence: the exclamatory "How" (τί) that governs it stands at the very end of the previous lemma («Τί …»), so the sense is "How strait *is* the gate …!" The OCR reads «στενὴ ἡ πόλη» ("the city"), an error for «ἡ πύλη» ("the gate"), as the parallel with the preceding lemma (πλατεῖα ἡ πύλη, "wide *is* the gate") and the comment on στενή ("strait") both require. Greek *τεθλιμμένη* (perfect passive participle of *θλίβω*, "to press, compress") is here rendered "afflicted"; it denotes a way that is pressed-in, straitened, hard — the antithesis of the εὐρύχωρος ("spacious") road of the previous lemma. The OCR *ἀλίγοι* is *ὀλίγοι* ("few").
[^2]: Greek *τί* — here not the interrogative "what?" but the exclamatory "how!" introducing an outburst of wonder, as Theophylact himself explains (θαυμαστικόν, "expressing astonishment").
[^3]: Greek *βαβαί*, an interjection of astonishment ("oh!", "wow!"); retained here as a plain exclamation.
[^4]: Matt. 11:30. Greek *ὁ ζυγός μου ἐλαφρός* ("my yoke *is* light"). Theophylact raises the apparent contradiction — the way is "strait" here, yet the yoke is "light" there — and resolves it by the reward to come.

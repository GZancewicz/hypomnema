# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 7:19-20

> **Every tree not making good fruit is cut down, and cast into fire. So then from these fruits you shall know them.**[^1] (Matt. 7:19-20)

These things are aimed[^2] at *the* Jews, for John too used to say such things to them.[^3] And he likens the human being to a tree, because he can be grafted[^4] from fruitless[^5] sin into virtue.

[^1]: The lemma conflates Matt. 7:19 with the recapitulating clause of 7:20; Theophylact's text reads *ἄραγε* ("so then, therefore") where the common text has *ἄρα γε*. Rendered "So then."
[^2]: Greek *ἀποτείνεται* — "is stretched out toward, directed / aimed at"; rendered "are aimed at."
[^3]: Cf. Matt. 3:10, John the Baptist: "every tree which does not bring forth good fruit is cut down and cast into fire" — the same words, here noted as spoken to the Jews. The OCR *Κλεγε* is *ἔλεγε* ("used to say"), and *Λένδρῳ* just below is *δένδρῳ* ("to a tree"); both corruptions are secure.
[^4]: Greek *ἐγκεντρισθῆναι* — "to be grafted in" (the horticultural term of Rom. 11:17-24). The man-as-tree can be grafted over from barrenness into fruitfulness.
[^5]: Greek *ἄκαρπος* — "fruitless, barren"; sin is "fruitless" as bearing nothing good, the counterpart to the "fruits" by which the tree is known.

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 7:26–27

> **…who hears these words of mine, and does not do them, shall be likened to a foolish man,[^1] who built his house upon the sand; and the rain came down, and the rivers came, and the winds blew, and beat against[^2] that house, and it fell, and great was its fall. (Matt. 7:26–27)**

He did not say, "I will liken him," but, "He shall be likened" — of his own doing[^3] — to a foolish man: that one who believes but does not act. This one, therefore, builds upon sand, upon the unsoundness of *his* works.[^4] For this reason he also falls under temptations; for whenever there is a striking — that is, whenever a temptation strikes against him[^5] — he falls a great fall. But none of the unbelievers falls, for those men always lie on the ground; whereas the believer, that one falls. And for this reason the fall is great, because it is one being a Christian who falls.[^6]

[^1]: «ἀνδρὶ μωρῷ» — *μωρός*, "foolish / dull / stupid," the opposite of the *φρόνιμος* ("prudent") man of Lemma 22.
[^2]: «προσέκοψαν» (from *προσκόπτω*), "struck / dashed / beat against" that house — differing from «προσέπεσον» in the parallel about the house on rock (Lemma 23). This verb is picked up in the comment below («προσκόψῃ… προσκρούσῃ»).
[^3]: Theophylact turns on the shift of voice: of the wise builder Christ said «Ὁμοιώσω αὐτόν» ("I will liken him" — active, the Lord doing the likening), but of the fool «Ὁμοιωθήσεται» ("he shall be likened" — passive). Theophylact glosses the passive with «ἀφ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ», "of himself / by his own agency": the fool brings the likeness upon himself. Rendered "of his own doing."
[^4]: «ἐπὶ σαθρότητος τῶν ἔργων» — *σαθρότης*, "rottenness / unsoundness / decay"; the fool builds "upon the unsoundness of *his* works." The supplied "*his*" is not in the Greek but is the sense (the works of the man who believes but does not act).
[^5]: «ὅταν γὰρ προσκόψῃ, τουτέστι, προσκρούσῃ αὐτῷ πειρασμός» — "for whenever it strikes/stumbles — that is, whenever a temptation strikes against him." Theophylact glosses the Gospel's «προσέκοψαν» (see note 2) with «προσκρούω» ("dash against, collide with"), making *πειρασμός* ("temptation / trial") the subject that strikes the man. The subject of the first verb «προσκόψῃ» is left indefinite ("there is a striking"); the gloss supplies it.
[^6]: «ὅτι χριστιανὸς ὢν πίπτει» — "because, being a Christian, he falls": the greatness of the fall lies not in the height but in the dignity of the one who falls — the unbeliever cannot fall, since he already lies on the ground.

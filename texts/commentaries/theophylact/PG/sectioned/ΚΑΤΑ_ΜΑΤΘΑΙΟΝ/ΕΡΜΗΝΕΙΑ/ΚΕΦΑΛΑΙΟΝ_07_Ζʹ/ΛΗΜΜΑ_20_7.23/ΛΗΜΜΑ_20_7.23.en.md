# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 7:23

> **…*And then* I will confess to them: I never knew you; depart from me, you who work lawlessness.[^1] (Matt. 7:23)**

Many at the beginning of the preaching cast out demons, even *while* being unworthy, the demons fleeing on account of the name of Jesus; for indeed grace works even through the unworthy, just as we are also sanctified through unworthy priests. And Judas, moreover, performed signs, and *so did* the sons of Sceva.[^2] But the *"I never knew you"* *stands* in place of, "Not even then, when you were working wonders, did I love you"; for love *is* knowledge—[^3]

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence broken off at the end of Lemma 19 («Καὶ τότε», "And then—"); the supplied *"And then"* here is repeated for sense and italicized. «ὁμολογήσω» "I will confess / profess / declare openly." «τὴν ἀνομίαν» from *ἀνομία* "lawlessness"; «οἱ ἐργαζόμενοι τὴν ἀνομίαν» "you who work lawlessness" — rendered thus rather than the familiar "iniquity" to keep the plain force of *ἀ-νομία*, "without law."
[^2]: The sons of Sceva: Acts 19:13–16 — where, in fact, they attempt to use the name of Jesus and are overpowered by the evil spirit. Theophylact cites them together with Judas (who "performed signs," yet betrayed Christ) as instances bearing on the point that the name and grace are not proof of the worthiness of the one through whom they operate. Translated as it stands, per the rule of following the text on the page.
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at «γνῶσις γὰρ ἡ ἀγάπη» and is completed at the head of Lemma 21 by «λέγεται ἐνταῦθα» — i.e. «γνῶσις γὰρ ἡ ἀγάπη λέγεται ἐνταῦθα», "for love is here called knowledge." The exegetical point: the Lord's "I never *knew* you" is read as "I never *loved* you," since here to "know" is to love. The predicate is «γνῶσις» ("knowledge"), the subject «ἡ ἀγάπη» ("love").

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 7:6

> **Give not the holy thing to the dogs, neither cast your pearls before the swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turning again rend you. (Matt. 7:6)**[^1]

The dogs are the unbelievers, but the swine are those who believe but have a miry[^2] life. Accordingly, one must not speak the mysteries to the unbelievers, nor the splendid and pearl-like[^3] words concerning theology to the unclean; for the swine trample *them* — that is, they despise the things spoken — but the dogs, turning, tear us to pieces: such things as those called philosophers do, when they hear that God was crucified, they rend us with syllogisms,[^4] reasoning sophistically that this is impossible.

[^1]: Greek «Μὴ δῶτε τὸ ἅγιον τοῖς κυσί, μηδὲ βάλητε τοὺς μαργαρίτας ὑμῶν ἔμπροσθεν τῶν χοίρων, μήποτε καταπατήσωσιν αὐτοὺς ἐν τοῖς ποσὶν αὐτῶν, καὶ στραφέντες ῥήξωσιν ὑμᾶς» (Matt. 7:6). *τὸ ἅγιον* = "the holy thing" (that which is consecrated). The folder-name verse (7.5) is off; this is 7:6.
[^2]: Greek *βορβορώδη βίον* — from *βόρβορος*, "mire, filth, mud"; a "miry / filthy" manner of life. The swine (baptized but wallowing) trample the pearls by contempt; the dogs (unbelievers) turn and rend.
[^3]: Greek *μαργαρώδεις λόγους* — "pearl-like words," picking up *μαργαρίτας* ("pearls") from the lemma; the luminous doctrines "concerning theology" (*περὶ θεολογίας*, i.e. concerning the divine nature).
[^4]: Greek *σπαράττουσιν ἡμᾶς τοῖς συλλογισμοῖς … σοφιζόμενοι* — "they rend us with *their* syllogisms, arguing sophistically"; *σοφιζόμενοι* carries the pejorative sense of clever/sophistical reasoning. The example: the philosophers, hearing that God was crucified, tear at believers with logical arguments that such a thing is impossible.

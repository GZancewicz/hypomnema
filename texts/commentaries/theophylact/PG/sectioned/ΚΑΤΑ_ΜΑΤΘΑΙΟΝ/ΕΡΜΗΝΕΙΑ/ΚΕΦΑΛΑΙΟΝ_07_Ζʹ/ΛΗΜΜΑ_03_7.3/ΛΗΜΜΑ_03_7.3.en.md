# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 7:3–4

> **… seest thou the speck[^1] that is in the eye of thy brother, but the beam that is in thine own eye thou considerest not? Or how wilt thou say to thy brother, "Let me cast out the speck from thine eye"[^2] — and behold, the beam is in thine *own* eye?**

*This lemma-file carries only the continuation of the scripture quotation (Matt. 7:3–4); Theophylact adds no exposition here. His comment follows at 7:5 (Lemma 4). The quotation opens mid-clause, taking up the «Τί δὲ βλέπεις …» ("But why seest thou …") begun at the end of Lemma 2.*

[^1]: Greek *κάρφος* — a small dry twig, straw, or chip; set against *δοκός* ("beam, plank"). Theophylact's whole argument (developed in Lemma 4) turns on the disproportion between the tiny speck and the great beam, so the pair is rendered "speck / beam" to keep the contrast of size vivid.
[^2]: Greek *Ἄφες ἐκβάλω τὸ κάρφος ἀπὸ τοῦ ὀφθαλμοῦ σου* — "the speck **from thine** eye," the eye of the brother being addressed (the received reading uses *ἀπό*, "from," rather than *ἐκ*). OCR "ἐκβόάλω" = **ἐκβάλω** ("let me cast out"), a secure correction.

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 7:13

> **Enter through the narrow gate, because wide is the gate, and spacious is the way that leads away into destruction, and many are those who enter through it. (Matt. 7:13)**[^1]

By "narrow gate" he means the trials,[^2] both the voluntary ones — such as fasting and the rest — and the involuntary ones — such as bonds, persecutions. Just as, therefore, a fleshy[^3] man, or one carrying many things, is not able to enter through the narrow gate, so neither is the luxurious or the rich man; but through the wide gate such people enter. And, showing that both the narrowness is temporary and the breadth is passing, he called them a "gate" and a "way": for the one who suffers hardship passes through the gate — that is, *through* the state of suffering hardship — and the one who lives luxuriously journeys through his luxury as *through* a way. Since, therefore, both are temporary, the better must be chosen. What—[^4]

[^1]: OCR "ΕΙσάλθετε" is securely *Εἰσέλθετε* ("Enter"). Although the folder is labeled 7.12, the lemma Theophylact quotes and expounds here is Matt. 7:13; the header verse is corrected accordingly.
[^2]: Greek *πειρασμούς* — "trials, testings, temptations." Here the range of affliction (fasting, bonds, persecution), so "trials" is preferred over "temptations."
[^3]: Greek *πολύσαρκος* — literally "of much flesh," corpulent; kept concrete as "fleshy" to preserve the physical image of a heavy man unable to squeeze through a narrow gate, paralleled by the *τρυφηλός* ("luxurious, self-indulgent") and the rich.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off at «Τί» ("What / How"), which is in fact the opening word of the next scripture quotation (Matt. 7:14), continued in lemma 13: «στενὴ ἡ πύλη, καὶ τεθλιμμένη ἡ ὁδὸς ἡ ἀπάγουσα εἰς τὴν ζωήν…» ("How narrow *is* the gate, and constrained the way that leads to life…"). There Theophylact notes that this *Τί* is exclamatory (*θαυμαστικόν*).

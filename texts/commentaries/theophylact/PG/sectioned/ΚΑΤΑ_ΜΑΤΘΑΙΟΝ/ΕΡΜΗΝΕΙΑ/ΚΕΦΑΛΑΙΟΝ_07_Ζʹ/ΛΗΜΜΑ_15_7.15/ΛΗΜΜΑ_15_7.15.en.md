# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 7:16-17

> **Do they gather from thorns a grape-cluster, or from thistles figs? So every good tree makes good fruits; but the corrupt tree makes evil fruits.** (Matt. 7:16-17)

Thorns and thistles[^1] are the hypocrites: thorns, as pricking secretly; but thistles, as crafty and of many devices.[^2] And a corrupt tree[^3] is everyone whom the moist and dissolute[^4] manner of life rots.

[^1]: Greek *τρίβολοι* — the caltrop or star-thistle, a plant whose three-pronged burrs prick from every side (the word literally means "three-pointed"). Rendered "thistles" throughout for the plain sense, but the three-cornered barb underlies the next remark.
[^2]: Greek *πολύτροποι* — "of many turns / turnings," hence "versatile, wily, shifty" (cf. the sea-mine *τρίβολος* with its several spikes). The barb pointing every way pictures the many-sided cunning of the hypocrite; rendered "*of* many *devices*."
[^3]: Greek *σαπρόν* — "rotten, putrid, decayed"; rendered "corrupt" to match the lemma. It denotes a tree gone bad through decay, not merely a "worthless" one.
[^4]: Greek *ὑγρὸς καὶ ἐκλελυμένος βίος* — "a moist and unstrung life." *ὑγρός* ("wet, fluid") connotes softness and self-indulgence; *ἐκλελυμένος* (perfect passive of *ἐκλύω*, "to loosen, relax, enervate") connotes a slack, dissolute character. The moisture that "rots" (σήπει) the tree is the play on words: a sodden life makes a man putrid. Rendered "moist and dissolute."

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 7:5

> **Hypocrite, cast out first the beam out of thine own eye, and then shalt thou see clearly[^1] to cast out the speck out of thy brother's eye. (Matt. 7:5)**

He who is about to rebuke others must be blameless. For if he himself, having a beam — that is, a great sin[^2] — finds fault with another who has a speck, he has made that one more shameless.[^3] And the Lord shows that he who sins greatly is not even able to see well the sin of his brother; for he who has a beam in his eye, how could he see another who is but moderately injured?[^4]

[^1]: Greek *διαβλέψεις* — "thou shalt see through / see distinctly, clearly"; the compound *δια-* marks a clearing of sight, apt after the beam is removed.
[^2]: The Greek here is OCR-damaged and elliptical: "Εἰ γὰρ αὐτὸς ἤτοι ἁμάρτημα, ἐπιμέμφεται ἄλλῳ κάρφος ἔχοντι." As it stands it lacks a governing word for the man's own fault. The sense demands the parallel of the beam, so it is securely reconstructed as *Εἰ γὰρ αὐτὸς δοκὸν ἤτοι μέγα ἁμάρτημα ἔχων* — "if he himself, having a beam, that is a great sin"; *ἤτοι* ("that is, namely") equates the beam with the great sin. Translated on that reconstruction.
[^3]: Greek *ἀναισχυντότερον ἐκεῖνον ἐποίησε* (OCR "ἀναισχύντερον") — "he made that one more shameless": by rebuking a lesser sinner while being a greater one, the reprover gives the rebuked man occasion for shamelessness (a ready retort) rather than correction.
[^4]: Greek *πῶς ἂν ἴδοι ἄλλον μετρίως βλαπτόμενον* — literally "how could he see another being moderately harmed?" i.e. one whose injury (the speck) is slight. OCR corruptions in this sentence — "Κτι αὐδὲ δὐναται" = **ὅτι οὐδὲ δύναται**, "βλαπτόμενΟΝ" = **βλαπτόμενον** — are secure.

# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 7:15-16

> **But take heed of the false prophets, who come to you in garments of sheep, but within are ravening wolves; from their fruits you shall know them.** (Matt. 7:15-16)

Crafty are they and impostors,[^1] the heretics; for this reason he says, Take heed. For on the one hand they put forward fair speech[^2] and feign a solemn manner of life, but within is the hook. And a garment of a sheep is also *the* meekness which certain *men* employ, dressing *themselves* up *in it* in order to flatter and to deceive thoroughly. And from the fruit they are recognized — that is, from the works and the *manner of* life. For even if they play a part for a season, yet by those who take heed they are exposed.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἐπιθέται* — literally "those who set upon," hence "assailants," "impostors," "cheats"; paired with *πανοῦργοι* ("crafty, unscrupulous"). Rendered "impostors" to catch the note of fraudulent aggression.
[^2]: Greek *χρηστολογία* — "kindly / plausible / smooth speech," a loaded term (cf. Rom. 16:18, of those who "by good words and fair speeches deceive"); here used with a pejorative edge, the fair-sounding talk that masks the hidden "hook." Rendered "fair speech."
[^3]: Greek *ἐλέγχονται* — "are convicted / reproved / exposed to view." The sense is that their true nature is brought to light and refuted; rendered "are exposed." OCR runs the last two words together as «προσέχουσινελέγχονται» (for *προσέχουσιν ἐλέγχονται*).

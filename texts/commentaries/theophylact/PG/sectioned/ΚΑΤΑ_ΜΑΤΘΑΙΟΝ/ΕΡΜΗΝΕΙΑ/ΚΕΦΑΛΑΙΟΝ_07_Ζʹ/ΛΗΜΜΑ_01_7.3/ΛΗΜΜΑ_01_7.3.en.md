# Matthew 7 (Ζʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 7:1

> **Judge not, that ye be not judged.**[^1]

It is not the reproving[^2] that he forbids, but the condemning. For reproof, on the one hand, is for benefit, but condemnation, on the other hand, is for reproach and utter contempt[^3] — and especially also when someone, having great sins, reproaches others and condemns those who have lesser *ones*, which *sins* God is about to judge.[^4]

[^1]: The lemma «Μὴ κρίνετε, ἵνα μὴ κριθῆτε» is the opening of Matt. 7:1 ("Do not judge, that ye be not judged"). The folder-name verse (7.3) is off; the Greek Theophylact quotes and expounds here is 7:1.
[^2]: The source is OCR-corrupt at two points, both securely recoverable: "ἔλεγΟΑΡΗΤχος" = **ἔλεγχος** ("reproof, rebuke"), and "κατακμίνειν" = **κατακρίνειν** ("to condemn"). Theophylact turns on the antithesis *ἔλεγχος* (corrective reproof, aimed at profit) vs. *κατάκρισις* (condemnation, aimed at shaming) — rendered "reproof/reproving" against "condemnation/condemning" to keep the pair visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἐξουδένωσις* — "a setting-at-nought, treating as worthless, utter contempt"; stronger than the preceding *ὀνειδισμός* ("reproach, reviling").
[^4]: Greek *ἃ ὁ Θεὸς μέλλει κρίνειν*. The relative *ἃ* is neuter plural; its antecedent is the sins (*ἁμαρτίας*), rendered here "which *sins* God is about to judge" — i.e. matters whose judgment is reserved to God. It could also be read loosely as "which things God will judge."

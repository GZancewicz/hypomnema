# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 26:49-50

> **immediately having come to Jesus, he said: "Hail, Rabbi"; and he kissed him. But Jesus said to him: "Friend, for what are you here?" Then, having come forward, they laid the hands upon Jesus and seized him.** (Matt. 26:49-50)

Do you see the implements of the chief priests? Clubs and swords.[^1] So peaceful were they, and having the spirit of gentleness![^2] But the *phrase* "one of the twelve" the evangelist said in wonder, that although being *one* chosen out, and being ranked among the first, nevertheless he gave himself to the devil. So that you too, O man, be afraid, even if you should be of the most intimate,[^3] lest ever, having grown slack, you fall away. But Judas gives a signal, because it was also night, and they were not able to distinguish *him*; and besides, those who came to seize *him* were not so many from the crowd as from the servants of the chief priest, who perhaps did not at all recognize Jesus. But the disciple makes the teacher known to them by kissing *him*; for, knowing the loving-kindness of the Lord, he grows bold even to kiss *him*. But he bears with *him* up to the very last hour, being eager through longsuffering to win him. But when not even thus was he brought to his senses, then he makes them fall, as John says,[^4] so that even through the falling they might recognize his power; but when not even thus did they desist from their boldness, then he gives himself up. And he calls Judas "friend,"[^5] making a comedy of him and tearing him to pieces, as one who forsooth was giving a friend's kiss. But the *phrase* "For what are you here?" *stands* instead of, "For what purpose are you here?" Is it, then, as a friend? And yet it was not fitting to be present with swords. But as an enemy? And how *is it that* you kiss *me*? Accordingly he convicts him as treacherous.[^6]

[^1]: Greek «Ξύλα καὶ μάχαιραι» (the OCR «Σύλα» is a certain corruption of **Ξύλα**, "clubs/staves of wood"), echoing the «μαχαιρῶν καὶ ξύλων» of the preceding lemma. *σκεύη* is rendered "implements" (equipment, gear, tackle); the tone is ironic.
[^2]: Ironic: the "peace" and "gentleness" are said of an armed mob. Greek «τὸ πνεῦμα τῆς πραότητος ἔχοντες» ("having the spirit of gentleness").
[^3]: Greek «κἂν τῶν οἰκειοτάτων εἴης» — "even if you should be among the most closely-related / most intimate / most household *of people*"; superlative of *οἰκεῖος* (one's own, belonging to the household).
[^4]: Reference to John 18:6, where those who came to arrest Jesus «fell to the ground». Theophylact reads the falling as a demonstration of Christ's power meant to bring the arresters to their senses.
[^5]: Greek *ἑταῖρος* ("companion, comrade, friend") — the same word Jesus uses in the lemma. Theophylact takes it as bitter irony: «κωμῳδῶν αὐτὸν καὶ διασύρων» (lit. "making comedy of him and tearing *him* apart / lampooning"), i.e. Christ exposes the mock friendship of the kiss.
[^6]: The clause is OCR-garbled as «Ἑλέγχει τοίνυν αὐτὸν ι- δόλιον»; securely **«Ἐλέγχει τοίνυν αὐτὸν ὡς δόλιον»** ("Accordingly he convicts/refutes him as treacherous / deceitful").

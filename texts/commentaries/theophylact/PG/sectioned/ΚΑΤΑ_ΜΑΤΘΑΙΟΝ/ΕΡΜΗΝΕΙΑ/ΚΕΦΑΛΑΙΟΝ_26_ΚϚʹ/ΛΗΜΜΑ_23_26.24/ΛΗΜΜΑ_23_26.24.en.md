# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 26:24–25

> **man goes as it is written concerning him; but woe to that man through whom the Son of man is betrayed. Good were it for him if that man had not been born. But Judas, the one betraying him, answering said: Surely it is not I, Rabbi? He says to him: You have said *it*.**[^1]

He reproves the betrayer openly, since, being reproved secretly, he was not being corrected; therefore, for this reason, he makes him manifest, saying, "He who dipped with me," so that even thus he might be corrected. But Judas, being shameless, kept dipping in his very dish, that is, *his* platter.[^2] Then he says: "On the one hand the Son of man goes as it is written concerning him" — that is, even if Christ had been foreordained to suffer for the salvation of the world, yet surely not for this reason is Judas to be honored, but woe to him; for he did not do this as cooperating with the will of God, but *rather* as gratifying his own malice.[^3] For indeed, if you consider carefully, Christ did not even primarily will to be crucified.[^4] And he shows this also through his praying-away the cup;[^5] but since it was necessary — before all the ages, as *it was* otherwise not possible for men to be saved, because of the malice of the enemy — that which was primarily not-willed becomes willed for him.[^4] And in saying that it were good if that man had not been born, he shows that non-existence is better than existence in sins.[^6] But observe also the word "Goes" [*ὑπάγει*].[^7] For it indicates that his putting-to-death will be rather a going-abroad, not a death.

[^1]: Greek «Μήτι ἐγώ εἰμι, Ῥαββί; Λέγει αὐτῷ· Σὺ εἶπας.» As in Lemma 21, *μήτι* expects "no"; rendered "Surely it is not I". *Σὺ εἶπας* literally "You said" — the object "*it*" is supplied.
[^2]: Theophylact glosses the lemma's *τρύβλιον* with a more familiar synonym: «ἐν τῷ αὐτοῦ τρυβλίῳ, ἤτοι πινακίῳ» — "in his very dish, that is, *his* platter".
[^3]: Greek *τὴν οἰκείαν κακίαν ἐκθεραπεύων* — "thoroughly tending / gratifying / indulging his own wickedness"; *ἐκθεραπεύω* is "to serve / court / minister to".
[^4]: The pivotal pair is *προηγουμένως* ("primarily / antecedently / as a first intention") set against what becomes *θελητόν* ("willed, an object of will"), though it was *προηγουμένως ἀθέλητον* ("primarily not-willed"). The point: the crucifixion was not God's antecedent will but became willed consequentially, given the enemy's malice and man's need of salvation.
[^5]: Greek *διὰ τοῦ ἀπεύχεσθαι τὸ ποτήριον* — "through his praying-against / deprecating the cup" (cf. Matt. 26:39).
[^6]: Greek *ἡ ἀνυπαρξία κρείττων τῆς ἐν ἁμαρτίαις ὑπάρξεως* — "non-existence *is* better than existence in sins", opposing the abstract nouns *ἀνυπαρξία* / *ὕπαρξις*.
[^7]: Theophylact dwells on the verb *ὑπάγει* ("goes / withdraws"): it presents Christ's death (*νέκρωσις*, "putting-to-death / mortification") as an *ἀποδημία* ("a journeying abroad, departure") rather than a true *θάνατος* ("death").

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 26:10–11

> **But Jesus, having perceived *it*, said to them: Why do you cause troubles to the woman? For she has worked a good work upon me […] but me you do not have always. (Matt. 26:10–11)**[^1]

The disciples, having heard much concerning almsgiving, and making much account of it, blamed the woman, as *though* God seeks mercy rather than the honor toward himself; but he rebukes the disciples, because they blamed the woman unseasonably. For one must not demand the lofty things from those *who have* just come forward,[^2] and especially from a weak little woman, but *must* rather accept their moderate faith at least. For whenever someone offers a gift to God, do not turn him away, nor break down his eagerness, dismissing him to distribute *it* to *the* poor, but allow *him* to fulfill the offering. If, however, someone taking counsel should use you as an adviser — whether he ought to give to *the* poor, or to offer to God — advise him to give to *the* poor; but after he should have offered, it is superfluous to turn him away. For one must prefer the honor toward God to all things simply, even to almsgiving itself; for do not, because through love-of-mankind[^3] Christ takes the mercy upon himself, think that one ought to despise God, and to practice almsgiving only — since in that case, then, one would have also to rob temples, and from the plundered things to do almsgiving.[^4] But this is not *so*; for that it is not the same thing to have mercy on *the* poor and to honor that same Christ himself and to serve *him*, listen:

[^1]: Greek «Γνοὺς δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Τί κόπους παρέχετε τῇ γυναικί; Ἔργον γὰρ καλὸν εἰργάσατο εἰς ἐμέ… ἐμὲ δὲ οὐ πάντοτε ἔχετε.» The lemma telescopes: after «εἰς ἐμέ» ("upon me," end of v. 10) the source jumps directly to «ἐμὲ δὲ οὐ πάντοτε ἔχετε» ("but me you do not have always," end of v. 11), omitting the intervening clause «πάντοτε γὰρ τοὺς πτωχοὺς ἔχετε μεθ' ἑαυτῶν» — which Theophylact quotes and expounds separately at Lemma 11. The ellipsis […] marks that gap. *Γνοὺς* = "having come to know / having perceived"; *Τί κόπους παρέχετε* = literally "Why do you furnish troubles" = "Why do you trouble."
[^2]: Greek *τῶν ἄρτι προσιόντων*, "those just now approaching / coming forward" — i.e. recent converts, those newly drawing near to the faith. Rendered "those who have just come forward."
[^3]: Greek *φιλανθρωπία* ("love-of-mankind, benevolence"), a recurring term; kept literal to preserve the standard rendering across the corpus.
[^4]: Greek *ἱεροσυλεῖν* = "to commit sacrilege," specifically "to rob a temple / plunder sacred things"; the following *ἐκ τῶν συληθέντων* ("from the plundered things") sustains the image. Theophylact's reductio: if almsgiving alone mattered and honor to God could be despised, one might as well plunder sacred things in order to give alms.

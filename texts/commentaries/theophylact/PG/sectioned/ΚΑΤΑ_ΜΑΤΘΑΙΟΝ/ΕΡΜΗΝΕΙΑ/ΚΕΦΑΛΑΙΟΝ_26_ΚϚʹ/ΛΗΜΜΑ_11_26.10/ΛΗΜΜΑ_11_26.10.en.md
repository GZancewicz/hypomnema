# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 26:11

> **For the poor, it says, you have always with yourselves, but me you do not have always. (Matt. 26:11)**[^1]

You see, then, how it is one thing to serve Christ, and another to have mercy on *the* poor — even if, through love-of-mankind, Christ[^2]

[^1]: Greek «Τοὺς πτωχοὺς γὰρ, φησὶ, πάντοτε ἔχετε μεθ' ἑαυτῶν, ἐμὲ δὲ οὐ πάντοτε ἔχετε.» The folder-name verse (26.10) is off by one; this is Matt. 26:11. *φησὶ* = "it says / he says" (a present word, hence roman).
[^2]: The sentence breaks off mid-clause at «ὁ Χριστὸς» ("Christ"). It is completed in the next lemma, which supplies the sense — that Christ, out of love-of-mankind, reckons *to himself* the mercy shown to the poor, yet honoring him directly (as the woman did) is another thing again.

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 56 · Matt. 26:61-64

> **said: This *man* declared: I am able to destroy the temple of God, and through three days to build it. And the high priest, having risen, said to him: Do you answer nothing? What do these *men* testify against you? But Jesus was silent. And the high priest, answering, said to him: I adjure you by the living God, that you tell us, whether you are the Christ, the Son of God. Jesus says to him: You have said.**[^1] (Matt. 26:61-64)

These *men* were indeed false witnesses. For Christ did not say that "I am able to destroy," but, "Destroy *ye*."[^2] And he did not say, "the temple of God," but, "this temple" — that is, my body. And again he did not say, "I will build," but, "I will raise up."[^2] So that these *men* *are* manifestly false witnesses, alleging that Christ said other things than *those* he said. Therefore Jesus, knowing their tribunal *to be* unlawful, was silent; for those whom the signs did not persuade, how could defenses have persuaded *them*? But the high priest, wishing to cast him into blasphemy, asks *him* — in order that, on the one hand, if he should say that "I am the Son of God," he might be condemned as a blasphemer, but, on the other hand, if he should deny *it*, he might have him as *one* having testified against himself. But the Lord, who grasps the wise in their craftiness,[^3] answers, "You have said," instead of, "Your *own* mouth has confessed, that I am the Son of God."[^4]

[^1]: The lemma runs Matt. 26:61-64. Greek «…εἶπον· Οὗτος ἔφη· Δύναμαι καταλῦσαι τὸν ναὸν τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ διὰ τριῶν ἡμερῶν οἰκοδομῆσαι αὐτόν. Καὶ ἀναστὰς ὁ ἀρχιερεὺς εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Οὐδὲν ἀποκρίνῃ; τί οὗτοί σου καταμαρτυροῦσιν; Ὁ δὲ Ἰησοῦς ἐσιώπα. Καὶ ἀποκριθεὶς ὁ ἀρχιερεὺς εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Ἐξορκίζω σε κατὰ τοῦ Θεοῦ τοῦ ζῶντος, ἵνα ἡμῖν εἴπῃς, εἰ σὺ εἶ ὁ Χριστὸς ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ. Λέγει αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Σὺ εἶπας.» OCR corrections: *ἐσιώπα* ("was silent," garbled «λαιόπα»), *Λέγει* ("says," garbled «Αέγει»). For *Σὺ εἶπας* see note 4.
[^2]: An allusion to John 2:19: «Λύσατε τὸν ναὸν τοῦτον, καὶ ἐν τρισὶν ἡμέραις ἐγερῶ αὐτόν» ("Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up"), which the evangelist glosses as spoken "concerning the temple of his body" (John 2:21). Theophylact's argument is purely verbal: Christ used the imperative *Λύσατε* ("Destroy *ye*"), not *δύναμαι καταλῦσαι* ("I am able to destroy"); "this temple," not "the temple of God"; and *ἐγερῶ* ("I will raise up"), not *οἰκοδομήσω* ("I will build"). The false witnesses altered all three.
[^3]: Greek «ὁ δρασσόμενος τοὺς σοφοὺς ἐν τῇ πανουργίᾳ αὐτῶν», quoting Job 5:13 (cited also at 1 Cor. 3:19): "He who grasps the wise in their own craftiness."
[^4]: Greek *Σὺ εἶπας* — literally "You said *it*" / "You have said," an affirmative idiom meaning "it is as you say" (i.e. "yes"). Theophylact himself supplies the gloss that follows: *ἀντὶ τοῦ, Τὸ σὸν ὡμολόγησε στόμα* ("instead of: 'Your *own* mouth has confessed *it*'") — the admission is drawn from the questioner's own words, not spoken outright by Christ.

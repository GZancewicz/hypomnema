# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 26:13

> **...*wheresoever* this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, there shall also be spoken *that* which this *woman* did, for a memorial of her. (Matt. 26:13)**[^1]

He teaches us, that according to a certain divine impulse the woman did this, prefiguring his death and the burial of his body; for the Lord would not have endured to be anointed with ointment,[^2] unless it signified some mystery. But as God he foretold the thing that was to be, that unto her boast the work which the woman did is about to be spoken of everywhere. But behold the benevolence[^3] of God, how he requites the woman with great grace; for he makes her to be remembered continually, as long as his Gospel is preserved. But how did the ointment contribute to his burial? It was a custom among the Jews to bury the bodies with ointments, as the Egyptians also did, on account of *their* being kept undecayed and without foul odor. Therefore, he says, the woman shows, from the pouring-out of the ointment, that "my body shall be buried." But all these things he says, shaming and putting to confusion[^4] Judas, through whom he was about to be handed over unto burial. But understand also according to the anagogical *sense*[^5] that the leper, on the one hand, is the gentile people, and the sinful woman, on the other hand, is the assembly and Church from among the nations, which poured out the ointment — that is, the faith — upon the head of Christ, that is, *upon* his divinity. For everyone who believes Christ to be God pours ointment down upon the head of Christ; but Judas, having rebuked the woman, as John says, holds the type of the Jews, who even now still murmur against the Church.

[^1]: The lemma carries only the tail of Matt. 26:13; its opening («Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, ὅπου ἐάν…») was quoted in the previous lemma. The OCR corruptions in the fragment — «κῃρυχθῇ» for *κηρυχθῇ*, «λαληθήσοται» for *λαληθήσεται*, «αὔτη» for *αὕτη* — are secure and low-stakes. The supplied "*wheresoever*" (Greek ὅπου ἐάν) is carried over from the preceding clause and italicized as not present in this fragment.
[^2]: Greek *μύρῳ* (OCR «μόρῳ»), from *μύρον* — fragrant oil / perfumed ointment, not "myrrh" proper (*σμύρνα*). Rendered "ointment" throughout; the argument turns on its funerary use, the aromatic oils with which bodies were embalmed.
[^3]: Greek *φιλανθρωπία* — literally "love of mankind," the standard patristic term for God's benevolent kindness toward humanity; "benevolence" keeps it to one word.
[^4]: Greek *δυσωπῶν καὶ ἐντρέπων* — a near-synonymous pair: *δυσωπέω* "to shame, put out of countenance" (make one drop the eyes), *ἐντρέπω* "to put to shame, make ashamed." Rendered "shaming and putting to confusion" to keep both participles visible.
[^5]: Greek *κατὰ ἀναγωγήν* — "according to the anagogical / higher-spiritual sense," the elevated allegorical reading as opposed to the plain historical one.

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 26:32

> **But after my being raised, I will go before you into Galilee. (Matt. 26:32)**[^1]

As God he foretells the things that shall be; but in order that they might not be offended as *though* being accused by him, he said that it is written: "I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered,"[^2] hinting at this: that "I *for my part* was holding you together, but my departing will scatter you." But the Father is said to smite the Son, because by his will — that is, by his permission[^3] — the Jews crucified the Lord; for, being able to prevent *it*, and then not having prevented *it*, he is said to smite because of the concession. Then, dissolving their grief, he brings them good news, that "I will be raised, and I will go before *you*" — that is, "I will arrive ahead of you into Galilee,"[^4] showing from this that, on the one hand, he will forsake Jerusalem, but, on the other hand, he will depart to the nations; for Gentiles inhabited Galilee.[^5]

> **[If] all shall be offended in you, I *for my part* will never be offended. Jesus said to him: —**[^6]

[^1]: The file opens mid-sentence: its first Greek words are «…ἐγερθῆναί με, προάξω ὑμᾶς εἰς τὴν Γαλιλαίαν», the tail of a clause begun at the close of the previous lemma («Μετὰ δὲ τὸ…» = "But after the…"). The full clause is «Μετὰ δὲ τὸ ἐγερθῆναί με, προάξω ὑμᾶς εἰς τὴν Γαλιλαίαν», restored here for the blockquote. The object *ὑμᾶς* ("you") is OCR-garbled in the source as «ὑ ἰς εἰς» but is secure from the exposition, which repeats «προάξω… ὑμᾶς».
[^2]: Zech. 13:7, as quoted by Theophylact: «Πατάξω τὸν ποιμένα, καὶ διασκορπισθήσονται τὰ πρόβατα». The preceding lemma's citation added «τῆς ποίμνης» ("of the flock"); here that phrase is absent. Rendered from his wording, not conformed to any English Bible.
[^3]: The pivot of the passage is *παραχώρησις* ("permission, concession, yielding"), paired with *γνώμη* ("will, intent, judgment"): God is said to "smite" the Son only in the sense that by his permissive will he did not prevent the crucifixion. Greek: «διότι γνώμῃ αὐτοῦ, ἤτοι παραχωρήσει, ἐσταύρωσαν οἱ Ἰουδαῖοι τὸν Κύριον». *παραχώρησις* is here a quasi-technical term for the divine permissive (as opposed to positive) will.
[^4]: Theophylact glosses the verb *προάξω* ("I will go before / lead forward") with *προφθάσω* ("I will get there first, arrive ahead"): «προάξω, τουτέστι, προφθάσω εἰς τὴν Γαλιλαίαν ὑμᾶς». The gloss trades the spatial "go before" for the temporal "arrive ahead," a nuance not reproducible in a single English word for *προάξω*.
[^5]: «τὴν γὰρ Γαλιλαίαν ἐθνικοὶ κατῴκουν» — "for Gentiles (ἐθνικοί) inhabited Galilee," picking up *τὰ ἔθνη* ("the nations / the Gentiles") just above; Galilee "of the Gentiles" figures the Church of the nations that supplants Jerusalem.
[^6]: The file ends with the head of the next scripture unit (Matt. 26:33 and the intro to 26:34): «πάντες σκανδαλισθήσονται ἐν σοὶ, ἐγὼ οὐδέποτε σκανδαλισθήσομαι. Ἔφη αὐτῷ ὁ [Ἰησοῦς]». It breaks off at «ὁ» ("the [Jesus]"); the sentence is completed at the head of Lemma 32 («Ἰησοῦς· Ἀμὴν λέγω σοι…»). The opening «Εἰ» ("If") of Peter's words (26:33) is not in this fragment but is supplied in brackets from the parallel; *ἐγὼ* ("I") is emphatic, rendered "I *for my part*." OCR «πανδαλισθήσομαι» is securely *σκανδαλισθήσομαι*.

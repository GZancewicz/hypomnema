# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 58 · Matt. 26:67-68

> **Then they spat into his face, and buffeted him. And *others* struck *him* with the open hand, saying: Prophesy to us, Christ, who is he that smote you?**[^1] (Matt. 26:67-68)

When he had been condemned, then they displayed against him the *deeds* full of reproach, and mocked him, wrapping his face — as another evangelist says[^2] — with a garment. For since they held him as a prophet, for this reason they mock him in this way. Now to "buffet" (*κολαφίζειν*) is to strike with the hands, the fingers being bent together, and — that I may say *it* more plainly — to cuff with the fist,[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Τότε ἐνέπτυσαν εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ, καὶ ἐκολάφισαν αὐτόν. Οἱ δὲ ἐρράπισαν, λέγοντες· Προφήτευσον ἡμῖν, Χριστέ, τίς ἐστιν ὁ παίσας σε;» Two distinct verbs of striking are used, and Theophylact goes on to define them (note 3): *ἐκολάφισαν* (from *κολαφίζω*, "to strike with the fist, buffet, cuff") and *ἐρράπισαν* (from *ῥαπίζω*, "to strike with the flat/open hand, to slap"). Rendered "buffeted" and "struck with the open hand" respectively to keep the pair distinct. (OCR corrections: *ἐρράπισαν*, *Προφήτευσον*.)
[^2]: Cf. Mark 14:65 / Luke 22:64, where they cover (blindfold) his face before demanding that he prophesy who struck him.
[^3]: The lemma's exposition breaks off here mid-sentence (the Greek ends «…διὰ τοῦ γρόνθου κονδυλίζειν,» with a comma). Theophylact is defining the first verb, *κολαφίζειν*: *γρόνθος* = "fist," and *κονδυλίζειν* = "to strike with the knuckles, to cuff." The complementary definition of the second verb (*ῥαπίζειν*, striking with the open palm) presumably followed but is not preserved in this section of the OCR'd text.

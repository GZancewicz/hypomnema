# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 57 · Matt. 26:64-66

> **Nevertheless I say to you, from now on you shall see the Son of man sitting at *the* right hand of power, and coming upon the clouds of heaven.**[^1] (Matt. 26:64)

From the prophecy of Daniel he discourses with them. For that *man* says that "I saw *one* like *the* Son of man coming upon the clouds."[^2] For since they supposed him to be a deceiver, appearing in a lowly form, he says that "You shall see me then coming with power, and sitting together with the Father" — for here by "power" he means that of the Father — "and coming not from earth, but from heaven."

> **Then the high priest rent his garments, saying that "He has blasphemed; what further need have we of witnesses? Behold, now you have heard his blasphemy. What does *it* seem to you?" But they, answering, said: "He is guilty of death."**[^3] (Matt. 26:65-66)

It was a custom for the Jews, whenever something unbearable came upon *them*, to rend their tunics. So therefore Caiaphas also did this — as *if* over a manifest blasphemy — for the deceiving of the crowd, in order that he might show them that *the man* had blasphemed grievously,[^4] and so then might compel them to say that he is guilty of death. Nevertheless learn thoroughly, that Caiaphas' rending his garments was a symbol of the high priesthood of the old *covenant* being torn asunder.[^5]

[^1]: Greek «Πλὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, ἀπ᾿ ἄρτι ὄψεσθε τὸν Υἱὸν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου καθήμενον ἐκ δεξιῶν τῆς δυνάμεως, καὶ ἐρχόμενον ἐπὶ τῶν νεφελῶν τοῦ οὐρανοῦ.» *Πλὴν* = "nevertheless, however"; *ἀπ᾿ ἄρτι* = "from now on, henceforth." *τῆς δυνάμεως* ("of the power") is the periphrasis for God, which Theophylact glosses below. (OCR «Χἱὸν» → Υἱὸν.)
[^2]: Dan. 7:13. Greek «Εἶδον ὡς Υἱὸν ἀνθρώπου ἐρχόμενον ἐπὶ τῶν νεφελῶν» ("I saw *one* like *the* Son of man coming upon the clouds").
[^3]: Greek «Τότε ὁ ἀρχιερεὺς διέρρηξε τὰ ἱμάτια αὐτοῦ, λέγων ὅτι Ἐβλασφήμησε, τί ἔτι χρείαν ἔχομεν μαρτύρων· ἴδε νῦν ἠκούσατε τὴν βλασφημίαν αὐτοῦ· τί ὑμῖν δοκεῖ; Οἱ δὲ ἀποκριθέντες εἶπον· Ἔνοχος θανάτου ἐστίν.» (OCR corrections: *τί ἔτι*, *ἴδε νῦν*, *δοκεῖ*, *Ἔνοχος*.) *τί ὑμῖν δοκεῖ;* is literally "what does *it* seem to you?" — the idiom for "what is your judgment / what think ye?"; *ἔνοχος θανάτου* = "liable/guilty of death, deserving death."
[^4]: Greek OCR «μεγάλος»; read as adverbial *μεγάλα* (neuter plural used adverbially) / *μεγάλως*, "greatly, grievously."
[^5]: A symbolic (typological) reading: the tearing of the high priest's vestments signifies that the Old-Covenant priesthood was itself being torn away / abolished.

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 26:36

> **[Then] Jesus comes with them into a place called Gethsemane, and says to the disciples: Sit here, until, having gone away, I shall pray there. (Matt. 26:36)**[^1]

Peter, wishing to show that he has a genuine disposition, contradicts the Savior;[^2] for, being freed from the fear *that arose* on account of the betrayal, out of much love and love-of-honor[^3] he began both to rise up against the others and to oppose Christ; but the rest of the disciples too, out of ignorance, were promising — before the trial of the temptations — things which they were not going to accomplish. But he departs to pray by himself; for prayer needs leisure and solitude.[^4]

[^1]: This blockquote continues the sentence broken off at «Τότε» ("Then") in Lemma 34; the file itself begins at «ἔρχεται» ("comes"), so "[Then]" is supplied in brackets from the prior fragment. «Καθίσατε αὐτοῦ» — "Sit here," *αὐτοῦ* being the adverb "here / in this very place." «ἕως οὗ ἀπελθὼν προσεύξομαι ἐκεῖ» keeps the participle as a participle: "until, having gone away, I shall pray there."
[^2]: The exposition looks back to Peter's boast (26:35, the preceding lemma) even though the quoted verse is 26:36; Theophylact treats the two as one movement. *γνησία διάθεσις* — "genuine / authentic disposition."
[^3]: Greek *φιλοτιμία* ("love of honor, ambition, zeal to distinguish oneself"), paired with *ἀγάπη* ("love"): «ἐκ πολλῆς ἀγάπης καὶ φιλοτιμίας». Rendered "love-of-honor" to keep the ambivalence — a good zeal that here overreaches into "rising up against the others (κατεξανίστασθαι) and opposing Christ (ἀνθίστασθαι)."
[^4]: Greek *ἰδιασμός* ("being alone, seclusion, solitude"), a rare noun paired with *σχολή* ("leisure, freedom from occupation"): «Σχολῆς γὰρ καὶ ἰδιασμοῦ δεῖται ἡ προσευχή». The related adverb «ἰδίᾳ» just above = "privately / by oneself."

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 26:46

> **… Rise up, let us go; behold, he who betrays me has drawn near. (Matt. 26:46)**[^1]

Showing that he has no need of the aid[^2] from them when he is about to be delivered up, he says to them: "Sleep on now" — or he says this by way of irony,[^3] as if saying this: "Behold, the betrayer has drawn near, and if it pleases you, and the moment affords *it*,[^4] sleep on." He therefore raises them from that place where he was praying, and he goes toward those who are about to seize him, and goes to meet them beforehand,[^5] as *though* they were about to give him something joyful. Thus also those *things* which he prayed, he prayed for the sake of confirming[^6] the economy, and *as one* willing not to suffer[^7] — in order that the Hebrews might be preserved from the destruction which was about to overtake them because of *their* sin against him.

[^1]: The lemma opens with the tail of Matt. 26:46 («Ἐγείρεσθε, ἄγωμεν …» — "Rise up, let us go …"), continuing the scripture quotation begun in Lemma 41. *ὁ παραδιδούς με* — "the *one* betraying / delivering me up," the present participle marking Judas as the betrayer.
[^2]: Greek *ἐπικουρία* — "aid, succour, reinforcement."
[^3]: Greek *ἢ κατ᾽ εἰρωνείαν τοῦτό φησιν* — "or he says this by way of irony." Theophylact offers two readings of "Sleep on now": either it means he no longer needs their watching, or it is ironic.
[^4]: OCR-garbled: «καὶ εἰ ἀρέσκει μῖν, καὶ παρέχει ὁ καρὸς, καθεύδετε» — securely = «εἰ ἀρέσκει **ὑμῖν**, καὶ παρέχει ὁ **καιρός**» ("if it pleases *you*, and the *moment* affords *it*"). The corruptions *μῖν* → *ὑμῖν* and *καρὸς* → *καιρός* are certain from context.
[^5]: Greek *προϋπαντᾷ* — "goes out to meet beforehand," i.e. advances to meet his captors first rather than waiting or fleeing.
[^6]: Greek *διὰ τὸ πιστώσασθαι τὴν οἰκονομίαν* — "for the sake of giving assurance of / confirming the economy (*οἰκονομία*, the divine dispensation of the incarnation)." The prayers served to make credible the reality of his human nature.
[^7]: Greek *καὶ θέλων μὴ παθεῖν, ἵνα συντηρηθῶσιν οἱ Ἑβραῖοι ἐκ τῆς ἀπωλείας, ἣ ἔμελλε καταλήψεσθαι αὐτοὺς* — literally "and willing not to suffer, in order that the Hebrews might be preserved from the destruction which was about to overtake them." The theologically delicate point (picking up Lemma 36): his prayer that the cup pass is read not as reluctance to redeem but as a wish to spare the Jews the guilt — and consequent ruin — of putting him to death.

# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 26:7

> **For, it says, let my prayer be directed as incense before you, O Lord. (Ps. 141:2)**[^1]

Therefore offer to the divinity of Christ the fragrance *that comes* from the manifold-compounded ointment of the virtues.[^2] But indeed also, if you hold him by doctrine *to be* not only a mere man, but also God, then you have made fragrant his head, that is, his divinity — that is to say, you have theologized *him*.[^3]

[^1]: The lemma is not from Matthew but is a Psalm citation embedded in Theophylact's anagogical exhortation (carried over from the previous lemma: "and perfume him with the ointment of the virtues… and what is the head of Christ but his divinity, to which the fragrance of the virtues is offered"). Greek «Κατευθυνθήτω… ἡ προσευχή μου ὡς θυμίαμα ἐνώπιόν σου, Κύριε» = Ps. 141:2 (LXX 140:2). *Κατευθυνθήτω* = "let it be set straight / directed / made to go straight up."
[^2]: Greek *πολυσύνθετος* ("composed of many ingredients, manifold-compounded"), describing the ointment (*μύρον*) of the virtues as blended from many components; rendered "manifold-compounded" to keep the compounding metaphor visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἐθεολόγησας*, aorist of *θεολογέω*: "to speak of / confess *someone* as God, to theologize." The whole clause turns on a play: to "make fragrant" (*εὐωδίασας*) the "head" (= divinity) of Christ is precisely to *confess him as God*. There is no clean single-word English equivalent; "you have theologized *him*" preserves the technical verb, with the sense "you have proclaimed *him* to be God." Cf. *δογματίζεις* just before ("you hold as doctrine / dogmatize").

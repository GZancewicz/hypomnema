# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 26:2

> **You know that after two days the Passover comes to be. And the Son of Man is delivered up to be crucified.**[^1]

Since he had made mention of *the* kingdom and of recompense,[^2] opportunely then he discourses also concerning his own passion, all but[^3] saying this: that "And those who have crucified me shall be deemed worthy of the fire."[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Οἴδατε ὅτι μετὰ δύο ἡμέρας τὸ Πάσχα γίνεται. Καὶ ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου παραδίδοται εἰς τὸ σταυρωθῆναι». *γίνεται* rendered "comes to be" (present, of the feast arriving); *παραδίδοται* present passive, "is delivered up / handed over."
[^2]: Greek *βασιλείας ἐμνήσθη καὶ ἀνταποδόσεως* — a back-reference to the preceding chapter (Matt. 25), where Christ had spoken of the kingdom and of the recompense (*ἀνταπόδοσις*) rendered to each at the Judgment.
[^3]: Greek *μονονουχὶ* ("all but, well-nigh, as good as"); i.e. this is the unspoken force of his announcing the Passion just after the Judgment scene.
[^4]: Greek «Καὶ οἱ σταυρώσαντές με τοῦ πυρὸς ἀξιωθήσονται» (OCR *σταυρώααντές*). The verb *ἀξιωθήσονται* normally means "shall be deemed worthy / accounted worthy," an honorific term; here it is used with grim irony — the crucifiers will be "deemed worthy" not of the kingdom but of *the fire* (of judgment, cf. Matt. 25:41). The bite of the sentence lies in coupling the honorific verb with punishment; rendered literally to preserve the irony.

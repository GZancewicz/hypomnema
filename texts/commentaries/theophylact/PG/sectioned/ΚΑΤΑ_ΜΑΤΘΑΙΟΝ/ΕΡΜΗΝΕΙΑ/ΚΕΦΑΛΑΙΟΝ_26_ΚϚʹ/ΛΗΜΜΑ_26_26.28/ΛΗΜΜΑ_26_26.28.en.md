# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 26:27-28

> **And having taken the cup, and having given thanks, he gave *it* to them, saying: Drink of it, all *of you*; for this is my blood,[^1] that of the new covenant, which is shed for many for *the* forgiveness of sins. (Matt. 26:27-28)**

Just as the old covenant had a sacrificial victim and blood, so also the new has blood and slaughter.[^2] And he said *it* is shed "for many," instead of "for all." For indeed "all" are also "many."[^3] But for what reason did he not say above, "Take, eat, all *of you*," but here, "Drink of it, all *of you*"?

Some, on the one hand, say that he said this on account of Judas; for Judas, on the one hand, took the bread, but did not eat *it*, but hid *it*, in order to show to the Jews that Jesus calls the bread his own body; but the drink, on the other hand, he drank even unwillingly, being utterly unable to hide it. For this reason, therefore, he said, "Drink, all *of you*."

But others, more anagogically,[^4] say that to partake of solid food, on the one hand, is not *the part* of all, but of the more perfect; but to be given drink, on the other hand, *is the part* of all; for this reason, therefore, he said here, "Drink, all *of you*." For to receive the simpler of the doctrines[^5] *is the part* of all.

[^1]: The OCR here is heavily corrupted: «Τουτο νὰς ἔστι τὸ αἵκά μου» is securely «Τοῦτο γάρ ἐστι τὸ αἷμά μου» ("for this is my blood"). Restored per the standard text of Matt. 26:28, which the comment goes on to expound (the "for many / for all" distinction).
[^2]: Greek *σφάγιον … σφαγήν* — the noun *σφάγιον* ("victim, sacrificial slaughter-offering") and *σφαγή* ("slaughter, slaying"). The pairing sets the sacrificial victim/blood of the old covenant against the blood/slaughter of the new; the cognate terms are kept as "sacrificial victim" and "slaughter."
[^3]: The point turns on *πολλοί* ("many") vs *πάντες* ("all"): Theophylact reads "shed for many" as equivalent to "for all," since the totality ("all") is itself a great number ("many"). Greek: «Καὶ οἱ πάντες γὰρ πολλοί εἰσι».
[^4]: Greek *ἀναγωγικώτερον* — "more anagogically," i.e. by a more elevated/spiritual (rather than literal-historical) mode of interpretation. Retained as the technical exegetical term.
[^5]: Greek OCR *τῶν ἑονιάτων* is corrupt; from the sense (receiving the "simpler" things is open to all) it is best restored as *τῶν δογμάτων* ("of the doctrines/teachings"), paired with "the simpler" (*τὰ ἁπλούστερα*). Rendered "the simpler of the doctrines"; the restoration is probable, not certain, hence flagged.

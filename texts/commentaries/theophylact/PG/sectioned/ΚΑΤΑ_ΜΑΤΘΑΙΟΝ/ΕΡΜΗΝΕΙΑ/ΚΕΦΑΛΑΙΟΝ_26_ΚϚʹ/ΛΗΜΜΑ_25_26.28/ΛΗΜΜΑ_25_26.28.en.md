# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 26:26

> **This is my body.[^1] (Matt. 26:26)**

He shows that the bread which is sanctified on the altar is the very body of the Lord, and not an antitype.[^2] For he did not say, "This is an antitype," but, "This is my body." For by an ineffable operation[^3] it is transformed,[^4] even though it appears to us *as* bread. For since we are weak, and would not have accepted to eat raw flesh, and *the* flesh of a man, for this reason it appears to us on the one hand *as* bread, but is in reality flesh.

[^1]: The lemma «Τοῦτο ἔστιν τὸ σῶμά μου» ("This is my body") repeats the closing words of the lemma quoted in full at Lemma 24 (Matt. 26:26, «Λάβετε, φάγετε· τοῦτό ἐστι τὸ σῶμά μου»). The folder metadata verse (26.28) is off by two; the words expounded here belong to 26:26. The verb form OCR'd as *ἔστιν* is the ordinary *ἐστί(ν)*.
[^2]: Greek *ἀντίτυπον* — "antitype, figure, representation, symbol." The word is the crux of the whole comment: Theophylact insists the consecrated bread is not a mere *ἀντίτυπον* (a symbolic counterpart) but the Lord's actual body. Retained as "antitype" to keep the technical eucharistic term visible.
[^3]: Greek *ἀῤῥήτῳ ἐνεργείᾳ* — "by an ineffable/unspeakable operation (or 'energy')." *ἐνέργεια* here is the divine operative power; "operation" is preferred over "energy" to avoid a modern physical sense.
[^4]: Greek *μεταποιεῖται* — "is transformed / is changed / is made into *something else*." A strong term for the change of the elements; rendered "is transformed." Some would render "is re-made" or "is transmuted."

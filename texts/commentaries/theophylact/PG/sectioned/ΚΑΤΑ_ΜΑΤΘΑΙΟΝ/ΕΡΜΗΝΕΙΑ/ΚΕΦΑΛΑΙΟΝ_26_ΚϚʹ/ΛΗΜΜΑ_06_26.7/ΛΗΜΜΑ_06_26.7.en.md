# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 26:7

> **there came to him a woman having an alabaster jar of costly ointment, and poured *it* down upon his head as he reclined *at table*.**[^1]

Some say there are three women who anointed the Lord, of whom the four evangelists also make mention; but others say these are two — one the *woman* in John, who is Mary the sister of Lazarus, and the one now mentioned in Matthew, who is the same as *the woman* in Luke and in Mark.[^2] And this Simon the leper, some say was also the father of Lazarus, whom, having cleansed from the leprosy, he was being feasted at his house.[^3] And it is said that the Lord too, when he said to the disciples, "Go away to such-a-one, and he will show you an upper room spread *and furnished*,"[^4] sends them to this man; indeed he also received the Lord, as they say, and at his house he celebrated the Passover.

The woman, then, *having seen*[^5] the leper cleansed, took courage that she too would obtain forgiveness, and would be cleansed of the leprosy of the soul; and she showed forth much faith, having poured out ointment so precious, and not sparing it. And she pours this upon the head, offering the honor to the most sovereign part.

And you too, then, if you have leprosy of the soul, even Pharisaic *leprosy* — puffed up, that is, cut off from God[^6] — receive Jesus into your house, and anoint him with the ointment of the virtues; for you are able, for Jesus who has cleansed you from the leprosy, to prepare ointment and to pour *it* down upon his head. And what is the head of Christ, but his divinity, to which the fragrance of the virtues is offered?

[^1]: Greek «προσῆλθεν αὐτῷ γυνὴ ἀλάβαστρον μύρου ἔχουσα βαρυτίμου, καὶ κατέχεεν ἐπὶ τὴν κεφαλὴν αὐτοῦ ἀνακειμένου». *βαρύτιμος* = "of heavy price, very costly." *ἀνακειμένου* (genitive absolute agreeing with "his") = "as he reclined / was reclining at table"; "*at table*" supplied for sense.
[^2]: The two views on the number of anointing women: (a) three distinct women across the four Gospels; (b) two — Mary of Bethany (John 12) and a second woman, identified as one and the same in Matthew, Luke (7:36–50), and Mark (14:3–9). Theophylact reports the opinions without adjudicating.
[^3]: Greek «ὃν ἀπὸ τῆς λέπρας καθαρίσας, εἱστιᾶτο παρ' αὐτῷ» (OCR *εἶστιᾶτο*). The relative *ὅν* ("whom") refers to Simon: Jesus, having cleansed Simon from his leprosy, was being entertained/feasted at Simon's house. *εἱστιᾶτο* is imperfect middle of *ἑστιάω*, "was being feasted / was dining."
[^4]: Cf. Matt. 26:18 / Mark 14:15 / Luke 22:10–12. Greek «Ἀπέλθετε πρὸς τὸν δεῖνα… ἀνώγεων ἐστρωμένον». *ὁ δεῖνα* = "such-a-one, a certain man" (a placeholder name). *ἀνώγεων ἐστρωμένον* = "an upper room spread / furnished" (with couches laid out); "*and furnished*" supplied.
[^5]: The participle is OCR-garbled in the source («γυνὴ λδοῆςπὸν»); the sense securely requires a participle of seeing, *ἰδοῦσα* ("having seen"), governing "the leper cleansed." Supplied in italics as reconstructed.
[^6]: Greek «εἰ λέπραν ἔχεις ψυχικὴν καὶ φαρισαϊκὴν, τετυφωμένην ἤτοι ἀποκεκομμένην Θεοῦ» (OCR *ψόχικὴν*, *φαρισεῖκὴν*, *Θοοῦ*). *τετυφωμένην* = "puffed up, swollen with conceit"; *ἀποκεκομμένην Θεοῦ* = "cut off / severed from God." *ἤτοι* ("that is, or rather") links the two: the Pharisaic leprosy of pride which is a being-cut-off from God.

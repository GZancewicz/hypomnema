# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 26:14-16

> **Then one of the twelve, the *one* called Judas Iscariot, having gone to the chief priests, said: What are you willing to give me, and I *myself* will deliver him up *to you*? And they set for him thirty pieces of silver; and from then he sought a good opportunity, that he might deliver him up. (Matt. 26:14-16)**

When the alien[^1] woman, the harlot, displayed so great honor, then the disciple goes off to betray[^2] him. For not in vain is the "*then* having gone" set down, but that it might show the shamelessness of Judas; and "the Iscariot" he adds, that he might make him more recognizable. For there was also another Judas, the *one* also *called* Lebbaeus. This one, however — the betrayer — was from a certain village, which was called Iscara.[^3] And "they set thirty pieces of silver" *stands* in place of "they agreed, they set apart to give," not as the many understand *it*, in place of "they weighed *it* out."[^4]

[^1]: Greek *ἀλλοτρία* — "belonging to another, alien, a stranger," here of the woman as an outsider, set in contrast to "the disciple," an intimate.
[^2]: Greek *προδώσων*, future participle of *προδίδωμι* — "being about to betray / in order to betray"; kept as a purpose-bearing future participle.
[^3]: Theophylact derives "Iscariot" from a village name *Ἰσκάρα* (elsewhere Kerioth, cf. the traditional "man of Kerioth"), offering it as etymology to distinguish this Judas from Judas Lebbaeus (Thaddaeus).
[^4]: The pun on *ἔστησαν* (from *ἵστημι*), which means both "to set / fix / appoint (hence to agree upon)" and, of a balance, "to weigh out." Theophylact insists the sense is "they agreed, they set apart to give" (*συνεφώνησαν, ἀφώρισαν δοῦναι*), not "they weighed out" (*ἐζυγοστάτησαν*). The body keeps the neutral "set." Greek: «Οἱ δὲ ἔστησαν τριάκοντα ἀργύρια, ἀντὶ τοῦ συνεφώνησαν, ἀφώρισαν δοῦναι, οὐχ ὡς οἱ πολλοὶ νοοῦσιν, ἀντὶ τοῦ ἐζυγοστάτησαν.»

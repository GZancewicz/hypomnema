# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 63 · Matt. 26:75

> **— Peter remembered the word of Jesus, who had said to him that: Before a cock crows, thrice you will deny me. And having gone out outside, he wept bitterly.**[^1]

Peter, overwhelmed by boundless fear,[^2] forgot the promises, and is enslaved to human weakness, as though dead with the fear, and not even knowing what he says.

But understand for me also according to the anagogical *sense*,[^3] that Peter is convicted by the maidservant — that is, by human weakness, *which is* small and befitting a slave[^4] — until the cock, having crowed, brought him to perception. And the cock is the word,[^5] which does not permit us to be slothful and to sleep, but says: "Watch," and: "Awake, you who sleep."[^6] By the word, then, as by a kind of cock, having been roused from sleep, Peter, having gone out outside the court of the high priest — that is, out of the enclosure of the hardened mind[^7] — and having stepped out from insensibility, wept bitterly. For as long, on the one hand, as he was in the court of the hardened mind, he did not weep; for he did not perceive[^8]

[^1]: This opening completes the Gospel citation running across Lemmata 61–63; the closing quotation mark «»» falls here (Matt. 26:75), after which the exposition of the whole denial-scene begins. «καὶ ἐξελθὼν ἔξω» — "and having gone out outside"; Theophylact's text lacks the «ὁ Πέτρος» some witnesses add. The pleonastic ἐξελθὼν ἔξω ("having gone out outside") is kept, since he plays on it below.
[^2]: Greek «Ἀπείρῳ φόβῳ καταγωνισθεὶς» — "overpowered/vanquished by boundless (lit. inexperienced-of-limit) fear." «καταγωνισθείς» is the passive of «καταγωνίζομαι» ("to overpower in the struggle, subdue"): fear wrestles Peter down.
[^3]: «κατὰ ἀναγωγήν» — the anagogical or higher/spiritual sense, as opposed to the plain historical reading; the standard patristic term for lifting the letter to a mystical meaning. "*sense*" is supplied.
[^4]: Greek «τῆς μικρᾶς καὶ δουλοπρεποῦς» — "*which is* small and servile/befitting a slave," describing human weakness. «δουλοπρεπής» ("slave-like") deliberately echoes the maidservant (παιδίσκη, a slave-girl) of the letter: the lowly slave-girl who convicts Peter figures the lowly, slavish weakness of human nature. The wordplay is hard to keep in English; hence the note.
[^5]: Greek «ἀλέκτωρ δέ ἐστιν ὁ λόγος» — "the cock is the λόγος." «ὁ λόγος» is ambiguous: it may mean "the word" (the awakening word of God / of preaching, as the following imperatives suggest) or "reason" (the rational faculty that rouses the sleeper). Rendered "the word" to match the scriptural quotations that follow; "reason" is a defensible alternative.
[^6]: «Γρηγορεῖτε … Ἔγειραι, ὁ καθεύδων» — "Watch" (cf. Mark 13:37) and "Awake, you who sleep" (Eph. 5:14, where the received text reads «ἔγειρε»); Theophylact's «Ἔγειραι» is a middle/variant form of the imperative.
[^7]: Greek «τοῦ πεπωρωμένου νοῦ» — "the hardened/calloused mind." «πωρόω» is to petrify or callous over (as bone or stone); the "court of the high priest" is allegorized as the enclosure (περιοχή) of a mind grown insensible, from which Peter must step out into feeling (αἴσθησις).
[^8]: The text breaks off here at the end of the chapter with OCR «οὐ γὰρ ῃσθάγ…», securely = «οὐ γὰρ ᾐσθάνετο» ("for he did not perceive [his fall/condition]"); the clause is cut off mid-word and its completion is not carried into the next chapter's opening lemma (which begins the fresh citation of Matt. 27:1). Also in this lemma the OCR «φόδῳ»→«φόβῳ» ("fear"), «ἐπελόθετο»→«ἐπελάθετο» ("forgot"), «ἄγρις οὖ»→«ἄχρις οὗ» ("until"), «ἐ ἀλέκτωρ»→«ὁ ἀλέκτωρ» ("the cock") are securely recovered.

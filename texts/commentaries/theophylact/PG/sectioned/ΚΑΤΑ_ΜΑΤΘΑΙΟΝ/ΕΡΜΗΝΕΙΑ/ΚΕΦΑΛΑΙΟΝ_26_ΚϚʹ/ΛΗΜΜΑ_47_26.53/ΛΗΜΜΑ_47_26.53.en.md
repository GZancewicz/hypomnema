# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 47 · Matt. 26:53-54

> **do you think that I am not able now to call upon my Father, and he will present to me more than twelve legions of angels? How then should the Scriptures be fulfilled, that thus it must happen?** (Matt. 26:53-54)

It was Peter who drew the sword, as John says;[^1] and he had a sword, as having just sacrificed the lamb, which they ate. But let us not blame Peter. For he did this not on behalf of himself, but having been zealous on behalf of the teacher; but the Lord, reshaping him into the evangelical polity,[^2] teaches *him* not to use a sword, even if one should think to be avenging God. And Peter, by cutting off the ear, shows that the Jews were sick with a refusal to hear;[^3] then he also sets down the saying of the law, that the murderer is put to death in return; for the law says this, that "those who take *the* sword by *the* sword shall die." And he hints[^4] that by the sword of the Romans shall be destroyed the Jews who took the sword against him. But he did not say, "I am able to present twelve legions of angels," but, "to call upon my Father," saying this by way of the *divine* economy,[^5] as a man, on account of their weakness. For having then displayed many human things — the sweat, the timidity — he would not have seemed to be saying credible things, had he said, "I myself am able to present angels." So instead of the twelve disciples, he says, twelve orders of angels would have stood by me, had I willed. For a legion is the greatest order, six thousand horsemen.[^6] It is necessary, therefore, he says, that all these things happen, so that the Scriptures which foretold them might be fulfilled. But *it is* not because the Scriptures foretold *them* that on this account the Jews are evil; rather, because the Jews were going, out of an evil purpose, to do these things, on this account these things were set down in the Scriptures by the Spirit,[^7]

[^1]: Reference to John 18:10, which alone names Peter as the one who struck. «καθὰ Ἰωάννης φησίν» — "as John says."
[^2]: Greek «μεταρυθμίζων αὐτὸν εἰς εὐαγγελικὴν πολιτείαν» — "re-forming / re-fashioning him into *the* evangelical polity (way of life, citizenship, conduct)." *πολιτεία* denotes a whole manner of life and its governing rule, here the gospel's; rendered "polity" to keep that breadth.
[^3]: A word-play lost in English: «ἀνηκοΐαν ἐνόσουν» — the Jews "were sick with *ἀνηκοΐα*," i.e. **not-hearing / deafness / disobedience** (ἀν- + ἀκοή, "hearing"). Peter's cutting off the servant's *ear* (ὠτίον) becomes a sign of the Jews' diseased *hearing*. Greek «ἀνηκοΐα» pairs unhearing organ and unhearing will.
[^4]: Greek *αἰνίττεται* — "hints darkly, speaks in riddle/enigma"; a veiled prophecy of the Roman destruction of Jerusalem.
[^5]: Greek *οἰκονομικῶς* — "in accordance with the *οἰκονομία*," the divine dispensation/accommodation. Christ speaks "as a man," adapting his words to the disciples' weakness rather than asserting his own power to summon angels. Rendered "by way of the *divine* economy"; "*divine*" is supplied to gloss the technical term.
[^6]: Greek «Λεγεὼν… τὸ μέγιστον τάγμα, ἑξακισχίλιοι ἱππεῖς» — "a legion *is* the greatest order, six thousand horsemen." The equation of a legion with 6,000 cavalry is Theophylact's own; translated as it stands.
[^7]: The sentence trails off with a comma in the source and is not completed within this lemma; the following lemma (48) opens a fresh Gospel quotation («In that hour Jesus said to the crowds…»). The thought — that Scripture records, but does not cause, the Jews' evil — stands complete in sense.

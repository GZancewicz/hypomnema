# Matthew 26 (ΚϚʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 26:29

> **…this fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new with you in the kingdom of my Father. (Matt. 26:29)**[^1]

Having tasted the cup, he thereafter renounces bodily drink; but he promises a certain new manner of tasting in the kingdom, that is, in the resurrection. For having risen, he ate and drank in a certain new manner; for not as one needing bodily food by *the* laws of tasting, but *rather* confirming[^2] the true nature of the body. And reasonably he names his own resurrection "kingdom." For then he abolished death, having appeared *as* king in reality.

Or you might also understand *it* thus: The new drink *is* the revelation of the mysteries of God, which then in the kingdom of God, that is, in the second coming, will be revealed, being new — that is, such as we have not heard at any other time. And Christ is said to drink those things with us, because he considers our benefit *as* his own food and drink.

And[^3]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 27 (Matt. 26:29). Greek «…τούτου τοῦ γεννήματος τῆς ἀμπέλου, ἕως τῆς ἡμέρας ἐκείνης, ὅταν αὐτὸ πίνω μεθ' ὑμῶν καινὸν ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τοῦ Πατρός μου.» *γέννημα τῆς ἀμπέλου* = "offspring/produce of the vine," rendered "fruit of the vine." *καινόν* ("new") is predicative — "*when* I drink it new," which the comment develops as "a new manner (τρόπος) of drinking."
[^2]: Greek *πιστούμενος* — "confirming, giving assurance of, making credible." He ate and drank after the resurrection not from bodily need but to give assurance of the true (fleshly) nature of the risen body. The phrase *τροφῆς σωματικῆς γεύσεως νόμοις* is compressed; rendered "needing bodily food by *the* laws of tasting."
[^3]: The comment ends with a bare *Καὶ* ("And"), which opens the scripture quotation continued in the next lemma (Lemma 29): «καὶ ὑμνήσαντες, ἐξῆλθον εἰς τὸ ὄρος τῶν Ἐλαιῶν» ("and having sung a hymn, they went out to the mount of Olives," Matt. 26:30).

# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 4:9

> **he saith to him: All these things I will give thee, if, having fallen down, thou shouldst worship me. (Matt. 4:9)**

Some, on the one hand, suppose the high mountain *to be* the passion of love-of-money,[^1] unto which the enemy is eager to lead up Jesus; but they do not suppose rightly. For perceptibly[^2] the devil appeared to him; for the Lord did not admit *mere* thoughts[^3] — far be it.[^4] Perceptibly, therefore, he showed him on the mountain all the kingdoms, having set these before *his* eyes according to appearance,[^5] and,[^6]

[^1]: Greek *φιλοχρηματία* ("love-of-money, avarice"); *πάθος* ("passion, affection"). The construction is *νομίζουσιν ὄρος ὑψηλὸν [εἶναι] τὸ … πάθος* — an allegorizing reading (the "exceeding high mountain" of Matt. 4:8 = the passion of avarice) that Theophylact rejects in favor of a literal, sensible appearance. The supplied "*to be*" (infinitive unexpressed in the Greek) is italicized.
[^2]: Theophylact uses the fixed antithesis *αἰσθητῶς* ("perceptibly, by the senses, sensibly") against the *νοητῶς* ("intellectually, in the mind") complex established earlier: the devil was really, bodily present, not a mental image. Rendered "perceptibly" to keep the pair visible.
[^3]: Greek *λογισμούς … οὐκ ἐδέξατο* ("did not receive/admit thoughts, reasonings"); i.e. Christ did not entertain the temptation as a mere inward suggestion of the mind. The OCR reads *λογισμοὺς τὰο*, securely = *λογισμοὺς γὰρ* ("for … thoughts").
[^4]: Greek *μὴ γένοιτο*, the strong deprecatory idiom ("far be it / God forbid / let it not be").
[^5]: Greek *κατὰ φαντασίαν … ὑποστήσας* ("having set up / caused to subsist according to appearance [phantasm]"). *φαντασία* is the "appearance to the imagination" of the same intelligible-vs-sensible complex; here the point is that the kingdoms were presented as a real visual display before Christ's eyes, not merely imagined by him. OCR *σὗν* = *οὖν* ("therefore").
[^6]: The lemma breaks off at *καὶ,* ("and,"); the sentence is completed in the next lemma, which re-quotes «Ταῦτα πάντα σοι δώσω» ("All these things I will give thee").

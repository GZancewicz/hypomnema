# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 4:7

> **Jesus said to him: Again it is written: Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.** (Matt. 4:7)

Gently[^1] Christ repels him, teaching us through meekness[^2] to conquer the demons.

Again,[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἡμέρως* — "gently, tamely, mildly" (from *ἥμερος*, "tame, cultivated"). Christ does not strike back but wards the devil off with a quiet word.
[^2]: Greek *πραότης* — "meekness, mildness, gentleness of temper," the same virtue-complex as *ἡμέρως* above. The pairing (*ἡμέρως … πραότητος*) is Theophylact's lesson: it is by gentleness, not violence, that demons are overcome.
[^3]: The lemma breaks off at *Πάλιν* ("Again,"), the opening word of the next scriptural clause, completed in the following lemma: "Again the devil takes him up into an exceedingly high mountain…" (Matt. 4:8).

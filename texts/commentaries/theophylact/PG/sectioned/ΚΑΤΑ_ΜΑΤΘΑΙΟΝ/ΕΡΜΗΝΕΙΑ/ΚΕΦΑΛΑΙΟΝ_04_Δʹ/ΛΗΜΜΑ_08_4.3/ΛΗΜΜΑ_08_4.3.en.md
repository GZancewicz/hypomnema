# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 08 · Matt. 4:4–5

> **But he, answering, said: It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word proceeding through *the* mouth of God.** (Matt. 4:4)

The testimony is from the Old *Testament*;[^1] for the saying is Moses'.[^2] For indeed the Hebrews also were fed with the manna, which was not bread, but *nourished them* through *the* word of God, fulfilling every need of the Hebrews and becoming everything, whatever anyone desired to eat. For whether the Jew desired fish, or egg, or cheese, the manna furnished him such a taste.

> **Then the devil takes him up into the holy city, and sets him upon the pinnacle of the temple.** (Matt. 4:5)

*It was* a certain part of the temple, such as *the structures* called among us "double-sloping" *roofs*;[^3] for they seem like wings.[^4]

[^1]: Greek *ἀπὸ τῆς Παλαιᾶς* — "from the Old *one*," an elliptical feminine that supplies either *Διαθήκη* ("Covenant/Testament") or *Γραφῆς* ("Scripture"). Rendered "Old *Testament*" per common usage; the supplied noun is italicized.
[^2]: The quotation is Deut. 8:3, spoken by Moses; hence "the saying is Moses'" (*Μωσέως ὁ λόγος*).
[^3]: Greek *οἷα τὰ παρ' ἡμῖν λεγόμενα δίῤῥυτα* — "like the things called among us *dírrhyta*." *δίρρυτος* means "double-flowing / double-draining," i.e. a roof pitched to shed water on both sides. The comparison likens the temple's *πτερύγιον* to such a wing-like double-slope. The supplied nouns ("structures," "roofs") are italicized.
[^4]: The wordplay is untranslatable in full: *πτερύγιον* ("pinnacle," literally "little wing") is glossed by *πτέρυγες* ("wings") — the double-sloped roofs "seem like wings," which is why that part of the temple bears a wing-word for its name.

# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 4:24

> **And the report of him went out into all Syria, and they brought to him all those who were ill, held fast by various diseases and torments, and those moonstruck, and paralytics, and he healed them. (Matt. 4:24)**[^1]

Christ did not demand faith from any of those who were brought to him, because this very thing was *a mark*[^2] of faith — the bringing of them from afar. And by "those who are moonstruck"[^3] he means those who are possessed by demons. For the demon, wishing to sow among men *the notion* that the stars are malevolent,[^4] would watch for the moon when it was full-orbed, and then would set upon *them*, so that the moon might seem to be the cause of the affliction, and the creation of God might be slandered — just as also the Manichaeans were led astray.

[^1]: This is Matt. 4:24 (the folder-name verse "4.23" is off by one; the Greek quotes the "report went out into all Syria" verse). OCR corruptions in the lemma are securely recoverable: «πιοσήνεγκαν» = *προσήνεγκαν* ("they brought"), «αεληνιαζομένους» = *σεληνιαζομένους* ("moonstruck"), «ἐθεράσαυσεν» = *ἐθεράπευσεν* ("he healed"). *τοὺς κακῶς ἔχοντας* = "those having *it* badly / those *who were* ill." *βασάνοις συνεχομένους* = "held fast / gripped by torments."
[^2]: The Greek is elliptical: «αὐτὸ τοῦτο πίστεως ἦν» — literally "this very thing was of faith," i.e. "was *a mark / a work* of faith." The supplied "*a mark*" fills the genitive; no such noun stands in the Greek.
[^3]: *σεληνιαζομένους* ("moonstruck"), from *σελήνη* ("moon"). The word (conventionally rendered "lunatics" and often understood as epileptics) is retained by the literal "moonstruck" because Theophylact's whole comment turns on the etymological link to the moon — the demon exploits the moon's phases to make the affliction *look* moon-caused.
[^4]: Greek *κακοποιοί* — literally "doers of evil / malefic." In astrological usage *κακοποιός* is the technical term for a "maleficent" star or planet (opposed to *ἀγαθοποιός*, "benefic"); Theophylact's point is the pagan/Manichaean belief that certain heavenly bodies work harm. Rendered "malevolent" to keep the plain sense while preserving the astrological register.

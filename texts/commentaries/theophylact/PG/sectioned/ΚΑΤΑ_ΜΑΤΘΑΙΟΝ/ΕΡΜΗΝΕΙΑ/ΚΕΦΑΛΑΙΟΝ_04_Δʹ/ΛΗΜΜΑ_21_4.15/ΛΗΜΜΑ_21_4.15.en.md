# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 4:15–16

> **…Zabulon and land of Nephthalim, *by the* way of *the* sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations; the people who sit in darkness saw a great light; and to those sitting in *the* region and shadow of death, light rose upon them. (Matt. 4:15–16)**[^1]

"Way of *the* sea" — instead *of saying*,[^2] the *land* lying alongside the road of the sea. But *the* "great light" *is* the Gospel; for the Law also was itself a light, but a small *one*. But *the* "shadow of death" *is* sin; for it is a likeness and shadow-sketch[^3] of death. For as death holds fast the body, so this *sin* holds fast the soul. And "the light rose" upon us; for it was not we *who* sought it, but that *light* appeared to us, as it were having pursued us.

*The comment on the next words, "From that time…," runs on into Lemma 22.*[^4]

[^1]: The lemma completes the Isaiah citation begun in Lemma 20 (Isa. 9:1–2). Greek OCR *ῥδὸν* = *ὁδὸν* ("way, road"). Rendered fresh from Theophylact's Greek, not conformed to any English Bible; the anarthrous nouns take supplied italic articles as needed.
[^2]: Greek *ἀντὶ τοῦ* — a standard exegetical formula, "that is to say / instead of *this expression*," introducing a paraphrase. Theophylact glosses the odd phrase "way of the sea" as meaning "the land situated alongside the sea road."
[^3]: Greek *σκιαγραφία* — literally "shadow-painting, an outline sketch in light and shade" (whence "a mere outline, a faint adumbration"). Paired here with *ὁμοίωμα* ("likeness"): sin is only a shadowy sketch of death, not the reality. Rendered "shadow-sketch" to keep the *σκιά* ("shadow") link to "shadow of death" audible.
[^4]: The words *Ἀπὸ τότε* ("From that time") stand at the end of this lemma's text and are taken up, with the verb, at the head of Lemma 22 (Matt. 4:17).

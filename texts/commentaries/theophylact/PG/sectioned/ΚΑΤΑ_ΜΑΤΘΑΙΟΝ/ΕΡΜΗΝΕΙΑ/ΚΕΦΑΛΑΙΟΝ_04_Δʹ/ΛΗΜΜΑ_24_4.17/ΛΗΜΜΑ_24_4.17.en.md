# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 4:18

> **And walking beside the sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon the *one* called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a casting-net into the sea — for they were fishermen[^1] — and he says to them: (Matt. 4:18)**[^2]

These became disciples of John. And while John was still living, they had come to Christ; but when they saw John bound, they turned back again to the fisher's *trade*, and thus…[^3]

[^1]: This clause is heavily OCR-corrupted in the source: *Ζάλλοντας ἀμφίβληστρον εἰς τὴν θάλασσανσαν γὰρ ἀλιεῖτ]*. It is securely restored from the Gospel text as *βάλλοντας ἀμφίβληστρον εἰς τὴν θάλασσαν· ἦσαν γὰρ ἁλιεῖς* ("casting a casting-net into the sea; for they were fishermen"). Greek *ἀμφίβληστρον* is specifically a circular throw-net; rendered "casting-net" to preserve the figure that grounds "fishers of men." The dittography *θάλασσανσαν* and the truncated *ἀλιεῖτ]* are noise, not variant readings.
[^2]: The draws-from label on this folder is 4.17, but the Greek lemma is Matt. 4:18; the header and reference are corrected accordingly. "Simon the *one* called Peter": OCR *Ιέτρον* = *Πέτρον*.
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at *καὶ οὕτως* ("and thus…") and is completed at the head of Lemma 25 with the Lord's words «δεῦτε ὀπίσω μου, καὶ ποιήσω ὑμᾶς ἁλιεῖς ἀνθρώπων» ("Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men"). Theophylact's point: Peter and Andrew, formerly John's disciples, had already approached Christ during John's lifetime, then went back to fishing after John's imprisonment, and are now called a second time — hence "and thus" this present calling.

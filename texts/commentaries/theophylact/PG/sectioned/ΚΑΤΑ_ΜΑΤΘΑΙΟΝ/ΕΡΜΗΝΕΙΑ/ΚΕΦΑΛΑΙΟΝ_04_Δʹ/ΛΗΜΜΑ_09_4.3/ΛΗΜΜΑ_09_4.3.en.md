# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 09 · Matt. 4:6

> **And he says to him: If thou art *the* Son of God, cast thyself down.[^1] For it is written, that he shall charge his angels concerning thee, and upon *their* hands they shall bear thee up, lest ever thou strike thy foot against a stone.** (Matt. 4:6)

"If thou art *the* Son of God" — he all but[^2] says this: "I do not believe the voice from the heavens; nevertheless, thou, if thou art *the* Son of God, show me." And yet, O foul one, if he were *the* Son of God, would he have hurled himself down?[^3] This belongs to your savagery[^4] — to hurl down headlong those who are demon-possessed; but to God's *belongs* the saving. And it is not written, the,[^5]

[^1]: The lemma is OCR-corrupt here as *βάλλει αιαυτὸν κάτω*; securely = **«βάλε σεαυτὸν κάτω»** ("cast thyself down"), the imperative of Matt. 4:6. Restored with certainty; noted for consistency with the other OCR flags.
[^2]: Greek *μονονουχί* — "all but, well-nigh, as good as"; the devil does not say the words that follow outright but as good as says them.
[^3]: Greek *κατεκρήμνισεν ἂν ἑαυτόν* — "would he have cast *himself* down headlong?" Note the slip from second person (*σύ*, "thou") to third (*ἦν … ἑαυτόν*, "he were … himself") as Theophylact steps back to comment on the devil.
[^4]: Greek *ὠμότης* — "rawness, savagery, brutality, cruelty." The genitive is possessive: casting men down headlong is a mark of the devil's own savagery, whereas saving is God's.
[^5]: The sentence breaks off at *Οὐ γέγραπται δὲ τὸ,* ("And it is not written, the,") and is completed in the next lemma, which supplies the quoted words «Ἐπὶ χειρῶν ἀροῦσί σε» — not written concerning Christ but concerning the saints.

# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 4:2

> **And having fasted. (Matt. 4:2)**[^1]

He fasted, so that he might show that fasting is a great weapon against temptations, just as self-indulgence is the beginning of every sin.[^2]

[^1]: The folder-name verse (4.1) refers to the wilderness scene of v. 1; the words Theophylact quotes here, *καὶ νηστεύσας* ("and having fasted"), open Matt. 4:2, so the reference is corrected to 4:2.
[^2]: Greek *τρυφή* — "luxury, soft/dainty living, self-indulgence, sensual pleasure." Rendered "self-indulgence" to set it as the deliberate antithesis of fasting; "luxury" or "indulgent living" would serve equally.

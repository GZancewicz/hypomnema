# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 4:1

> **To be tempted by the devil. (Matt. 4:1)**

He is called "devil" as having slandered God to Adam;[^1] for he said to him that God envies you.[^2] And now, moreover, he slanders virtue to us.

[^1]: A play on the name Διάβολος that cannot be reproduced in English: *διάβολος* ("devil," lit. "slanderer / accuser") is glossed by the cognate participle *διαβαλών* ("having slandered / calumniated"), and picked up again below by *διαβάλλει* ("he slanders"). The whole point turns on the shared root διαβάλλω, "to throw across, set at variance, slander."
[^2]: An allusion to the serpent's words in Eden (cf. Gen. 3:1–5): Greek *ὅτι ὁ Θεὸς φθονεῖ ὑμῖν*, "that God envies you" — the pronoun is plural ("you," i.e., Adam and Eve), though the sentence addresses Adam. Theophylact paraphrases the temptation ("God knows that in the day you eat… you will be as gods") as an accusation that God begrudges them the good.

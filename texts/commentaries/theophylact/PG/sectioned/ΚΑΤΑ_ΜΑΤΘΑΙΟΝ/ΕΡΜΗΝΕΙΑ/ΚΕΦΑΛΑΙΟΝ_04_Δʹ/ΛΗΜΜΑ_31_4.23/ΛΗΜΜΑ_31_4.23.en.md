# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 4:23

> **And healing every disease and every sickness among the people. (Matt. 4:23)**

He begins the signs,[^1] so that he may confirm what he teaches. But "disease," he says, *is* the chronic suffering;[^2] and "sickness," on the other hand, *is* the temporary[^3] irregularity of the body.

[^1]: Greek *τῶν σημείων ἄρχεται* — "he makes a beginning of the signs / miracles." *σημεῖον* is the standard Gospel word for a "sign" *and* a "wonder / miracle"; rendered "signs" to keep the evidential force on which the clause turns ("so that he may confirm what he teaches").
[^2]: The OCR reads «Νάσος δέ ἠασιν ἡ χρονία κακοπάθεια», securely = **«Νόσος δέ φησιν ἡ χρονία κακοπάθεια»** ("but 'disease,' he says, *is* the chronic suffering"). Theophylact draws a fixed distinction: *νόσος* (chronic, long-standing affliction) versus *μαλακία* (a passing bodily disorder). *κακοπάθεια* = "suffering, hardship, bad condition."
[^3]: After «ἡ πρόσκαιρος» ("the temporary") the source carries a stretch of unrecoverable OCR garble — «,,μ, φιῶροιατιωε, οι εονίηαιῖο ἰ οροι.» — before resuming with «ἀνωμαλία τοῦ σώματος» ("irregularity of the body"). The garbled run appears to be corrupted duplication with no additional sense; the clean sentence is «μαλακία δὲ, ἡ πρόσκαιρος ἀνωμαλία τοῦ σώματος» ("but sickness *is* the temporary irregularity of the body"), which is translated. The intruding characters are flagged, not construed.

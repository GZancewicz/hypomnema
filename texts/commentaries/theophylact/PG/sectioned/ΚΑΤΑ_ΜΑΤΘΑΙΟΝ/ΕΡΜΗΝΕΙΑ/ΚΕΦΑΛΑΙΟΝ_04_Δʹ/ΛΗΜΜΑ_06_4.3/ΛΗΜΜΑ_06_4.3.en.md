# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 4:3

> **And the tempter, having approached him, said: If thou art *the* Son of God, speak so that these stones may become loaves. (Matt. 4:3)**[^1]

The robber had heard the voice from heaven.[^2] Again he saw him hungering, and thereupon was in doubt how the Son of God hungers; for this reason, then, he tempts him, so that he might learn. And he flatters him, thinking to catch *him* out by stealth, in saying:[^3]

[^1]: The lemma is OCR-garbled: «Καὶ προσελθὼν αὐτῷ ὁ πειράζων, εἶπενΕ Χὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ, εἰπὲ ἵνα οἱ λίθοι οὖτοι ἄρτοι γέναωνται.» It is securely recoverable as **«…εἶπεν· Εἰ Υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ, εἰπὲ ἵνα οἱ λίθοι οὗτοι ἄρτοι γένωνται»** (the mangled *εἶπενΕ Χὸς* = *εἶπεν· Εἰ Υἱὸς*; *γέναωνται* = *γένωνται*). The next lemma re-quotes «Εἰ Υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ» on its own. *ἄρτοι* = "loaves / bread-cakes," rendered "loaves" (the point of Lemma 7 turns on the plural).
[^2]: Greek *ὁ λῃστής*, "the robber / brigand" — Theophylact's designation for the devil here. "The voice from heaven" is the baptismal declaration just narrated (Matt. 3:17, "This is my beloved Son"), which the tempter had overheard.
[^3]: The exposition breaks off at *ἐν τῷ λέγειν* ("in saying:"), completed by the scripture quoted in the next lemma («Εἰ Υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ»). *ἀμφέβαλλε* — "was in doubt, was of two minds." *νομίζων ὑποκλέψαι* is difficult: *ὑποκλέπτω* is "to steal secretly, filch, purloin, catch by stealth"; the object is unexpressed. The sense is that the devil, flattering, hoped to steal a march on Christ / catch him off guard — "thinking to filch *the truth from him*" or "to trip him up unawares" are equally defensible.

# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 4:13

> **…and having left Nazareth, having come he dwelt in Capernaum, the *city* by the sea, in *the* borders of Zabulon and Nephthalim. (Matt. 4:13)**[^1]

Jesus withdraws, training us not to cast ourselves into dangers. And he withdraws into Galilee, which is "the rolling-down *one*";[^2] for the nations were rolling down into sin. And he dwells in Capernaum, which is "house of consolation";[^3] for in order that he might make the nations a house of the Paraclete, on account of this he came down. But Zabulon is interpreted "nocturnal *place*,"[^4] and Nephthalim, "breadth."[^5] The nations, then, had both night and breadth in *their* life. For they were not walking the narrow way, but the *one* leading into destruction.

[^1]: The lemma opens mid-sentence: it continues the scripture quotation begun in Lemma 18, «Ἀκούσας δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς ὅτι Ἰωάννης παρεδόθη, ἀνεχώρησεν εἰς τὴν Γαλιλαίαν· καὶ…» ("But Jesus, having heard that John was delivered up, withdrew into Galilee; and…"). Greek *τὴν παραθαλασσίαν* = "the *place* by the sea"; the supplied noun "*city*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek *τὴν κατακυλιστήν* ("the rolling-down / down-tumbling *one*"), a feminine form agreeing with "Galilee," offered as an etymology of the name. Theophylact immediately puns on it with the cognate verb *κατεκυλίοντο* ("were rolling down"): the Gentiles were "rolling down" into sin, matching the name of the region into which Christ withdraws. The wordplay is untranslatable; kept visible by rendering both with "roll down."
[^3]: Greek *οἶκος παρακλήσεως* ("house of consolation / comfort / exhortation"), the traditional etymology of "Capernaum." Theophylact turns it at once into *οἶκον τοῦ Παρακλήτου* ("a house of the Paraclete / Comforter"): the noun *παράκλησις* and the title *Παράκλητος* share the same root, so "house of consolation" becomes "house of the Comforter." The pun is lost in English.
[^4]: Greek *νυκτερία*, from *νύξ* ("night"): "of the night, nocturnal." Rendered "nocturnal *place*" to fit the following contrast, where "the nations had night… in *their* life."
[^5]: The source reads OCR *κλατυσμός*, securely = *πλατυσμός* ("a widening, breadth, spaciousness"); confirmed by the recurrence *πλατυσμὸν* in the next sentence. The traditional etymology of "Nephthalim."

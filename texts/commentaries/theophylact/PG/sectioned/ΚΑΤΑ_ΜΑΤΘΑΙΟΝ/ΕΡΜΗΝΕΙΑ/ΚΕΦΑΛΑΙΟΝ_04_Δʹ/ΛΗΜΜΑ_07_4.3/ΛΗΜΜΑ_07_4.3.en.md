# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 07 · Matt. 4:3

> **If thou art *the* Son of God.**[^1] (Matt. 4:3)

But you ask: And what sin was it to make the stones bread? Hear, therefore, that to listen to the devil in any respect is a sin. And besides, see: he did not say, "that this stone may become bread," but "The stones,"[^2] wishing to fling Christ into superfluity.[^3] For to one who is hungry one loaf suffices entirely; for this reason Christ did not listen to him.

[^1]: Greek *Εἰ Υἱὸς εἶ τοῦ Θεοῦ* — the tempter's challenge, resumed from the preceding lemma (Matt. 4:3). *Υἱός* ("Son") is anarthrous in the Greek; the supplied definite article "*the*" is italicized. Present-tense *εἶ* ("art") is a word actually on the page, hence roman.
[^2]: Theophylact's whole point turns on singular vs. plural: the devil said not *ὁ λίθος οὗτος* ("this stone," singular) but *οἱ λίθοι* ("the stones," plural). The contrast is preserved in the rendering.
[^3]: Greek *εἰς περιττότητα* — "into superfluity / excess / more-than-is-needed." The devil, by naming many stones, sought to lure Christ into providing beyond the single loaf that hunger requires. Rendered "superfluity" to keep the sense of excess; the following sentence ("one loaf suffices entirely") glosses it.

# Matthew 4 (Δʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 4:11

> **Then the devil leaveth him; and behold, angels came, and were ministering to him. (Matt. 4:11)**

The three temptations the Lord overcame: that of gluttony,[^1] that of vainglory, and that of love-of-money — that is, love-of-wealth. For these are the rulers of the passions;[^2] having overcome these, therefore, *he overcame* the others also much more. Whence Luke says that he completed every temptation,[^3] having completed the heads of the temptations.[^4] Whence also angels minister to him, so that it might be shown that to us also, after the victory, angels will render service.[^5] For all things Christ both does and shows for our sake, since to him, as God, the angels always minister.

[^1]: Greek *γαστριμαργία* ("gluttony, belly-madness"); *κενοδοξία* ("vainglory, empty conceit"); *φιλοχρηματία* ("love-of-money"), glossed by Theophylact himself with *φιλοπλουτία* ("love-of-wealth, love-of-riches"). These three answer to the three temptations of Matt. 4:3, 4:6, 4:9.
[^2]: Greek *οἱ ἄρχοντες τῶν παθῶν* ("the rulers/chief ones of the passions"). OCR *Οὔτοι* = *Οὗτοι* ("these").
[^3]: Cf. Luke 4:13, *συντελέσας πάντα πειρασμόν* ("having completed / finished off every temptation").
[^4]: Greek *τὰς κεφαλὰς τῶν πειρασμῶν τελέσας* — "having completed the heads of the temptations," i.e. the principal, chief temptations; *κεφαλή* ("head") carries the sense "chief, capital, summary." The rendering keeps the concrete "heads."
[^5]: Three near-synonyms for angelic service are kept distinct: *διακονοῦσιν / διηκόνουν* ("minister," as in the lemma), *ὑπηρετήσουσι* ("will render service, attend as an assistant"), and, below, *ὑπουργοῦσιν* ("serve, minister"). The point: angels serve Christ as God always, but here serve visibly for our instruction, that we too may be served by them after our own victory.

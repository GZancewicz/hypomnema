# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 17:5

> **While he was still speaking, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them, and behold, a voice out of the cloud, saying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I was well pleased;[^1] hear him. (Matt. 17:5)**

You, on the one hand, O Peter, seek that tabernacles made-with-hands come to be; but the Father, on the other hand, having set around *them* another tabernacle not-made-with-hands, the cloud, shows that just as he himself was appearing in a cloud to the ancients, being God, so also *does* his Son.[^2]

And the cloud *is* bright here, not dark as in the old *covenant*,[^3] because he did not wish to terrify, but to teach. And the voice *comes* out of the cloud, so that he might show that it was from God. But the *phrase* "In whom I was well pleased" *stands* instead of, "in whom I rest and take pleasure." And he teaches that "Hear him" — even if he should wish to be crucified, do not oppose him.

[^1]: The lemma is quoted with several OCR slips in the source — «μὐδόκησα» for **εὐδόκησα**, «γεφέλη» for **νεφέλη**, «Θιοῦ» for **Θεοῦ** — all securely recoverable and low-stakes. The verb *εὐδόκησα* is aorist («ἐν ᾧ εὐδόκησα»), rendered "I was well pleased"; it can also be taken as a timeless/gnomic aorist ("in whom I am well pleased"), and Theophylact himself glosses it below by present-tense verbs (*ἐπαναπαύομαι καὶ ἀρέσκομαι*, "I rest and take pleasure"). The plain past rendering is kept in the body, but the sense he draws out is durative.
[^2]: Greek *οὕτω καὶ ὁ αὐτοῦ Υἱός* — elliptical; the verb "appears in a cloud, being God" is carried over from the preceding clause. The supplied "*does*" makes the parallel explicit.
[^3]: Greek *οὐ γνοφώδης ὡς ἐν τῇ παλαιᾷ* — "not dark/gloomy as in the old *one*." The feminine *τῇ παλαιᾷ* has no expressed noun; "covenant" (*διαθήκη*) is supplied, the natural referent, contrasting the Sinai theophany's dark cloud (cf. Exod. 19:16, 20:21) with this bright one.

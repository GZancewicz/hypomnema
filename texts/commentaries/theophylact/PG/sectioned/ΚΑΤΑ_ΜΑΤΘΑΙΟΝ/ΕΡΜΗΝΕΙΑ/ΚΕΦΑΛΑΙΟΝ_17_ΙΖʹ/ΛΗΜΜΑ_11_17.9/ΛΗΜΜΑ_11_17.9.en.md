# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 17:9

> **And as they were coming down from the mountain, Jesus commanded them, saying: Tell the vision[^1] to no one, until the Son of Man be risen from *the* dead. (Matt. 17:9)**[^2]

On account of humility he charges *them* to tell no one, and at the same time so that those hearing such things might not be scandalized, seeing him later crucified. For they would have supposed him to be a deceiver, having done the God-befitting things according to appearance.[^3]

But you, learn that after six days — that is, after the world *that was* fashioned in six days — *comes* the contemplation of God;[^4] for if you do not go beyond the world and be raised up on the mountain, you shall by no means see *the* bright things: neither the face of Jesus — I mean the divinity — nor the garments — *that is,* the flesh; but then you would see both Moses and Elias conversing together with Jesus; for the law and the prophets and Jesus speak one *thing* and are in agreement.

But also, whenever you find someone interpreting the meaning of the Scripture brightly, know that this *man* sees the face of Jesus brightly; and if he also makes clear and makes bright the words, this *man* sees also the garments of Jesus white; for the words are the garments of the meanings.[^5] But do not say, according to Peter,[^6]

[^1]: Greek *τὸ ὅραμα* — "the vision / the thing seen / the sight." Rendered "the vision"; it denotes concretely what was witnessed on the mountain, not a mental apparition.
[^2]: The lemma is OCR-garbled — «ἐν. κταίλατο» for **ἐνετείλατο** ("commanded"), «τοῦ ὕρους» for **τοῦ ὄρους** ("the mountain"), «τὸ ἕραμα» for **τὸ ὅραμα** — all securely recoverable.
[^3]: Greek *κατὰ φαντασίαν ποιήσαντα τὰ θεοπρεπῆ* — "having done the God-befitting things *κατὰ φαντασίαν*," i.e. by mere appearance/illusion, not in reality. The phrase carries the anti-docetic charge Theophylact is refuting in advance: the crowds might have thought the divine works were a phantasm. *τὰ θεοπρεπῆ*, "the things befitting God," here the transfiguration-glory.
[^4]: Theophylact turns to anagogy on the "six days" of 17:1: the number figures the six-day creation, so that the "contemplation of God" (*ἡ τοῦ Θεοῦ θεωρία*) is reached only by one who transcends the created world. The genitive *τοῦ Θεοῦ θεωρία* is read as "contemplation *of* God" (God as object of vision).
[^5]: Greek *ἱμάτια γὰρ τῶν νοημάτων αἱ λέξεις* — "for the words/expressions (*λέξεις*) are the garments of the meanings/thoughts (*νοήματα*)." The allegory maps the Transfiguration onto exegesis: Christ's shining *face* = the divinity / the inner sense, his white *garments* = the letter / the very wording of Scripture. *λέξις* is the verbal expression, *νόημα* the thought it clothes.
[^6]: The comment breaks off at *Ἀλλὰ μὴ εἴπῃς κατὰ Πέτρον* ("But do not say, according to Peter,"); the sentence is completed in Lemma 12, which supplies Peter's words «It is good for us to be here».

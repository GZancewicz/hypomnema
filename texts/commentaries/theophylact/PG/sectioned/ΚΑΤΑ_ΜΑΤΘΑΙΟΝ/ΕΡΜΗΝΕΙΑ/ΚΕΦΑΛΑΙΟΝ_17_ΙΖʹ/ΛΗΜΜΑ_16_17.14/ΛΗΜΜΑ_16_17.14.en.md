# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 17:14–15

> **And when they had come to the crowd, there came to him a man kneeling to him, and saying: Lord, have mercy on my son, for he is moonstruck and suffers badly. For many times he falls into the fire, and many times into the water. (Matt. 17:14–15)**[^1]

This man appears to be exceedingly unbelieving, both from the things which Christ said to him — "O unbelieving generation" — and because he himself accuses the disciples; but *it was* not the moon *that was the* cause, but the demon watched for the moon *when it was* full, and then set upon *him*, so that the works of God might be blasphemed as evil-doing.[^2] But do you consider also that every foolish *man* is altered like the moon, according to that which is written[^3] — at one time, on the one hand, appearing great in virtue, but at another time being diminished and vanishing away. This *man*, therefore, is moonstruck and falls down, both into fire — the fire of anger and of desire — and into water, the waves of the life *full of* many cares; in which *is* the leviathan, the devil,[^4] that is, the *one* who reigns in the waters. Or *are* not the cares of the rich waves *following* one upon another?

[^1]: *σεληνιάζεται* — literally "he is moon-struck," of an affliction (epilepsy) thought to wax and wane with the moon; rendered "is moonstruck" throughout to preserve the wordplay with *σελήνη* ("moon") on which the whole comment turns. OCR «σεληνιάζεγαι» = σεληνιάζεται, «βδωρ» = ὕδωρ ("water") — secure.
[^2]: The demon deliberately struck at the full moon so that people would blame the moon (a work of God) and call God's creation harmful; *ἵνα βλασφημηθῶσι τὰ ἔργα τοῦ Θεοῦ ὡς κακοποιά*. OCR «Τα» here stands for ἵνα ("so that").
[^3]: *κατὰ τὸ γεγραμμένον* ("according to that which is written") — an allusion to Sirach 27:11: "the fool changes like the moon" (ὁ μωρὸς ὡς σελήνη ἀλλοιοῦται).
[^4]: *λευϊαθὰν* (Leviathan), glossed by Theophylact as the devil, "the one reigning in the waters" (cf. Ps. 74:13–14; Job 40–41). OCR «πολυφρόντιδας» = πολυφροντίδος ("full of many cares").

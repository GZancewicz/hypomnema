# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 17:25–26

> **And when he had entered into the house, Jesus anticipated[^1] him, saying: What do you think,[^2] Simon? From whom do the kings of the earth receive tolls or census-tax[^3] — from their *own* sons, or from strangers? Peter says to him: From strangers. Jesus said to him: Then indeed the sons are free. (Matt. 17:25–26)**

As God, although he had not heard *it*, he knew[^4] what those *men* had discussed with Peter; wherefore also he anticipates him, saying: If the kings of the earth do not receive tribute from their own sons, but from strangers, how shall the heavenly King receive the didrachma from me, his Son? For this, as I said above, was brought to the priests and to the temple. If therefore the sons of the kings below are free — that is, paying nothing — how much more I!

[^1]: Greek *προέφθασεν αὐτὸν* — "anticipated / forestalled him," that is, Jesus spoke first, before Peter could report the collectors' question. The verb reappears just below (*προφθάνει*).
[^2]: Greek *Τί σοι δοκεῖ* — literally "What seems *it* to you?", the standard idiom for "What do you think?" Rendered by the idiom in the body.
[^3]: Greek *τέλη ἢ κῆνσον* — "tolls or census-tax." *κῆνσος* is the Latin *census*, the poll-tax; *τέλη* the indirect duties or customs. Kept as a pair to preserve the two distinct levies Theophylact's text names.
[^4]: The OCR is defective here (« [δει τί διελέχθησαν »); securely restored as *ᾔδει* ("he knew"), governed by *ὡς Θεὸς … καίτοι μὴ ἀκούσας* ("as God, although not having heard"). The sense is certain.

# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 17:18

> **—Jesus rebuked him, and the demon went out from him, and the child was healed from that hour. (Matt. 17:18)**[^1]

Do you see the man attributing his own fault of unbelief to the disciples, as *though* they had been too weak concerning the healing? The Lord, therefore, putting him to shame, since he was accusing the disciples, says: "O unbelieving generation" — that is, "The fault is not so much *a matter* of their weakness as of your unbelief; for *your unbelief*, being great, overcame their commensurate power." But in reproaching the man, he reproaches all in common as unbelieving, even those standing around. And in saying, "How long shall I be with you?" he shows that he longs for the suffering upon the cross, and *for* the departure from them. For *he means*: "How long shall I dwell together with *these* insolent and unbelieving *men*?" But Jesus rebuked him — whom? The moonstruck *one*.[^2] From this, at any rate, it appears that this *man* too, being himself also unbelieving, became for himself the occasion of the demon through *his* unbelief.

[^1]: The lemma opens with the completion of the sentence begun as «καὶ» at the close of the previous lemma: «καὶ ἐπετίμησεν αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς …» (Matt. 17:18). OCR «ὅαιμόνιον» = δαιμόνιον ("demon"), «αὸν» = τὸν, «ἴἵασιν» = ἴασιν ("healing"), «ἁπίστοις» = ἀπίστοις, «Ἐπετίἀησε» = Ἐπετίμησε, «ἀπιατίαν» = ἀπιστίαν — all secure.
[^2]: *Ἐπετίμησε δὲ αὐτῷ ὁ Ἰησοῦς, τίνι; Τῷ σεληνιαζομένῳ*: the point of the rhetorical question is that Jesus rebuked the moonstruck boy himself (not only the demon) — evidence, Theophylact argues, that the boy too was unbelieving and so *πρόξενος* ("procurer / occasion / provider") of the demon against himself. *σεληνιαζομένῳ* again renders "the moonstruck one," continuing the vocabulary of Lemma 16.

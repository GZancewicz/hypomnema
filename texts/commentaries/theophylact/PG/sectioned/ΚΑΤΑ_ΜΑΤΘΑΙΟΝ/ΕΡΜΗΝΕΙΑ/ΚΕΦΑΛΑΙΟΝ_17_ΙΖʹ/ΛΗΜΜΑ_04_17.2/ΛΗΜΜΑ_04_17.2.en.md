# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 17:2

> **he was transfigured before them. And his face shone as the sun, but his garments became white as the light. (Matt. 17:2)**

Into a high mountain he brings them up, showing that unless one is exalted, he does not become worthy of such contemplations.[^1] And privately, because Christ was accustomed to do the more marvelous[^2] wonderworkings in secret, so that, being seen by the many as God, he might not be reckoned to be a man *only* in appearance.[^3] And, hearing "transfiguration," do not think that he then cast off the body, but that, the body remaining in its shape,[^4] he appeared more radiant, the divinity having displayed a little its rays, and this as *much as* they were able to see. For on account of this he also, above, called the transfiguration "*the* kingdom of God,"[^5] as *the transfiguration* having manifested the ineffable of *his* authority, and as having shown that he is *the* genuine Son of the Father, and as bearing the dignity of the second coming, through the unspeakable radiance of the face of Jesus.

[^1]: Greek *θεωριῶν*, from *θεωρία* — "contemplation, spiritual seeing, vision." The ascent-height is read allegorically: only the one "raised up / exalted" (*ὑψωθῇ*) is worthy of such sights.
[^2]: Greek *παραδοξοτέρας*, comparative of *παράδοξος*, "paradoxical, contrary to expectation, marvelous."
[^3]: Greek «ἵνα μὴ ὁρώμενος τοῖς πολλοῖς ὡς Θεὸς, κατὰ φαντασίαν ἄνθρωπος νομισθῇ». The syntax admits more than one construal. Taken here as: the *μὴ* negates the main verb *νομισθῇ* — "so that, being seen by the many as God, he might not be reckoned a man *only* in appearance (i.e., a phantasm)." On this reading he works marvels secretly lest the crowds, dazzled into seeing only godhead, doubt the reality of his manhood. Alternatively *μὴ* could attach to the participle ("so that, *not* being seen by the many as God, he might be reckoned a man according to appearance"), but this fits the flow less well. *κατὰ φαντασίαν* = "according to appearance / in imagination"; the noun *φαντασία* carries the technical sense "imaginary, unreal, phantasmal."
[^4]: The Greek here is OCR-corrupt: «ἐπὶ τοῦ σχήΟΑΙΝΤ», securely recoverable as *ἐπὶ τοῦ σχήματος*, "in its shape / figure."
[^5]: A reference back to Matt. 16:28 ("there are some standing here who shall not taste of death till they see... the kingdom of God" / "the Son of Man coming in his kingdom"); Greek *ἀνωτέρω εἴρηκε*, "he has said / called above." The three feminine participles (*ἐκφήνασαν*, *δείξασαν*, *φέρουσαν*) all agree with "the transfiguration" (*τὴν μεταμόρφωσιν*): the transfiguration is called "the kingdom of God" because it *manifested*, *showed*, and *bore* these things.

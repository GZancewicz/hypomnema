# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 17:27

> **But that we may not offend them, having gone to the sea cast a hook, and take up the first fish that comes up; and having opened its mouth, you will find a stater;[^1] that taking, give to them for me and you. (Matt. 17:27)**

That we may not, he says, be reckoned as scorners and despisers of the law, and cause offense, give the toll; for I give *it* not as owing *it*, but as correcting their weakness. For we learn from this, that we must not cause offense where we are not harmed; for where we are harmed by some deed, we must not concern ourselves about those who are offended without reason.[^2]

In order, then, that he might show that he is God, and ruling over the sea *also*, he sent Peter to fetch the stater out of the fish; and at the same time we also learn a certain mystery. For our nature is the fish, having been plunged in a deep of unbelief, but the apostolic word drew us up, and in our mouth found the stater — the oracles of the Lord and the confession of Christ. For he who confesses Christ, that *man* has the stater in *his* mouth, a stater drawing two didrachmas.[^3] Since Christ also has two natures, being God and man. This stater, therefore, *namely* Christ, was given for two — for Jews and gentiles, and for righteous and sinners.

But whenever you also see some money-lover, having nothing else through his mouth except gold and silver, know this *man* to be a fish, swimming in the sea of *this* life;[^4] but if some teacher like Peter[^5] be found, he hooks this *man*, and casts out from his mouth the gold and the silver.

Some, however, suppose the stater *to be* a precious stone found in Syria; but others, an exagion, a fourth of a coin.[^6]

[^1]: Greek *στατήρ*, the stater, a coin worth two didrachmas (four drachmas) — hence exactly the sum for two persons' temple tax, "for me and you." Theophylact exploits this two-didrachma value in the exposition below (the two natures of Christ), so the coin-name is kept rather than converted.
[^2]: Greek *οὐ δεῖ φροντίζειν τῶν ἀλόγως σκανδαλιζομένων* — "we must not concern ourselves about those offended irrationally / without reason." The point: where a good deed itself would be harmed by yielding, one need not defer to those who take needless offense.
[^3]: Greek *στατῆρα δύο δίδραχμα ἕλκοντα* — "a stater drawing two didrachmas," i.e. weighing / worth two didrachmas (*ἕλκω* of a coin = "to weigh, be worth"). This two-in-one worth grounds the christological turn that follows: the one stater equal to two didrachmas figures the one Christ in two natures.
[^4]: Greek *νηχόμενον* ("swimming"), restored from the OCR *νηλβμενον*; the reading is secure from sense and the fish-image.
[^5]: Greek *κατὰ Πέτρον* ("after the manner of Peter, like Peter"), for the OCR *κατὰ Πέτρῳ*.
[^6]: Greek *οἱ δὲ, ἐξάγιον, τέταρτον νομίσματος* — "but others, an *exagion*, a fourth of a *nomisma*." The *exagion* was a Byzantine weight/coin-unit; the *nomisma* the standard gold coin. Theophylact reports these two alternative identifications of the stater without endorsing either; rendered as reported.

# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 17:4

> **"But Peter answering said to Jesus: Lord, it is good for us to be here; if you wish, let us make here three tents,[^1] one for you, and one for Moses, and one for Elijah." (Matt. 17:4)**

Peter, wishing that Christ might not suffer, on account of *his* great love, says that "It is good to remain here, and not to go down and be murdered. For even if someone should come here, we have both Moses and Elijah *to* work together with us. For Moses, on the one hand, overcame the Egyptians, but Elijah brought down fire from heaven; so that against the enemies *who will* come here also, they will be such *as to do the like*." And these things he was saying out of much cowardice, as Luke says, not knowing what he says.[^2] For the strangeness astounded him; or he really did not know what he was saying, wishing Jesus to remain on the mountain, and not to go down and suffer for us. And, fearing lest he might seem presumptuous,[^3] he says the *word*, "If you wish."

[^1]: Greek *σκηνάς*, "tents / tabernacles / booths."
[^2]: Cf. Luke 9:33, "not knowing what he said." Greek *δειλίας*, "cowardice, timidity, fearfulness."
[^3]: Greek *αὐθάδης*, "self-willed, presumptuous, arrogant." Peter softens his proposal with "if you wish" lest he seem to be dictating to the Lord.

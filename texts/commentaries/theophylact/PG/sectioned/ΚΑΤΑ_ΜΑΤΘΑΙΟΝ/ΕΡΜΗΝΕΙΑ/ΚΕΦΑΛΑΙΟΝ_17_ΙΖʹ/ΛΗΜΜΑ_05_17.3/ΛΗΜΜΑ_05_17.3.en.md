# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 17:3

> **"And behold, there appeared to them Moses and Elijah, talking together with him." (Matt. 17:3)**

What were they talking together about? "The departure,"[^1] says Luke, "which he was about to fulfill in Jerusalem" — that is, the cross. But why did Moses and Elijah appear? So that it might be shown that he is Lord both of *the* law and of *the* prophets, and that *he is Lord* of *the* living and of *the* dead; for Elijah, on the one hand, *is* a prophet, and still lives; but Moses *is* a lawgiver, and has died. But also so that it might appear that he is not contrary to the law nor an adversary of God.[^2] For Moses would not talk together with one opposing his own *teachings*, nor would Elijah the zealot have endured him, *if* he were an adversary of God. And further, so that he might dissolve the suspicion of those saying him *to be* Elijah or one of the prophets.

But whence did the disciples know that they were Moses and Elijah? For *it was* surely not from images, since to make images of men then seemed impious.[^3] It seems, therefore, that from the words which they were speaking they recognized them; for perhaps Moses, on the one hand, was saying: "You are he whose passion I prefigured, having slain the lamb and having completed the Passover"; and Elijah, on the other hand: "You are he whose resurrection I prefigured in the son of the widow,"[^4] and some such things. Therefore, showing these *men* to the disciples, he teaches them to imitate these *very men*: and, according to Moses, on the one hand, to be meek and *skilled in* leading the people;[^5] but, according to Elijah, to be zealous and unbending when *there is* occasion, and ready to face danger, just as these *two were* for the sake of the truth.

[^1]: Greek *τὴν ἔξοδον* (Luke 9:31), "the exodus / departure" — a euphemism for his death; Theophylact glosses it "the cross."
[^2]: Greek *ἀντίθεος*, "opposed to God, hostile to God, a rival god." Rendered "adversary of God" both times; the word is stronger than mere "contrary" (*ἐναντίος*) used just before of the law, carrying the charge of setting oneself up against God.
[^3]: I.e., the disciples could not have identified them by comparison with portraits, since making images of men was reckoned impious. Greek *εἰκόνων* / *εἰκόνας*, "images / likenesses / icons."
[^4]: An allusion to Elijah's raising of the widow of Zarephath's son (1 Kings 17:17–24), read as a type of the resurrection.
[^5]: Greek *δημαγωγικούς*, from *δημαγωγός* — here without the later pejorative sense; "able to lead / guide the people," a good popular leadership paired with Mosaic meekness.

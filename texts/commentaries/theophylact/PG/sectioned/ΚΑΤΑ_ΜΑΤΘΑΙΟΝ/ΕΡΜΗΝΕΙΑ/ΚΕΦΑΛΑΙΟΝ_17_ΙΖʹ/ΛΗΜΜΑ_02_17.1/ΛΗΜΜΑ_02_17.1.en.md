# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 17:1

> **"But after these words there came to pass about eight days,"** and what follows;[^1]

for Luke counts both the first *day* and the last, on which they went up into the mountain; but Matthew, the middle *ones* only. And he took, on the one hand, Peter, as loving him[^2] exceedingly; but John, as being loved; and James, as being himself also a zealot. And *it is* clear that he had zeal, from his both promising to drink the cup,[^3] and from Herod's slaying him with *the* sword to please the Jews.[^4]

[^1]: This continues the sentence begun in the previous lemma. The quoted words are Luke 9:28 (Greek «Μετὰ δὲ τοὺς λόγους τούτους ἐγένοντο ὡσεὶ ἡμέραι ὀκτώ»), which Theophylact adduces to reconcile Luke's "about eight days" with Matthew's "after six days": Luke counts the first and last days inclusively, Matthew only the intervening six.
[^2]: Rendering the trio with μὲν … δὲ … δὲ: "on the one hand … but … and." Theophylact distributes the three: Peter *loving*, John *being loved*, James a *zealot*.
[^3]: Cf. Matt. 20:22–23, where James (and John) promise to drink the cup Christ drinks.
[^4]: An allusion to James's martyrdom under Herod Agrippa (Acts 12:2–3), where Herod killed James "with the sword" and, seeing it pleased the Jews, proceeded against Peter. Greek *πρὸς ἀρέσκειαν τῶν Ἰουδαίων*, "for the pleasing of the Jews." The point: James's being singled out for that death evidences the *zeal* that marked him.

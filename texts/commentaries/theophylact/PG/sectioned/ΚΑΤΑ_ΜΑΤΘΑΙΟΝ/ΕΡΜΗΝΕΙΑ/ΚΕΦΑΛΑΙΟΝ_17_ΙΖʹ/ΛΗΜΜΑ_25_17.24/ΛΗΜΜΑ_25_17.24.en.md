# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 17:24

> **And when they had come into Capernaum, they who receive the didrachmas[^1] came to Peter, and said: Does your teacher not pay the didrachmas? He says, Yes. (Matt. 17:24)**

God, instead of the firstborn of the Hebrews, willed the tribe of Levi to be consecrated to him. But the tribe of Levi, when numbered, was found *to be* twenty-two thousand; whereas the firstborn *were* twenty-two thousand and two hundred seventy-three;[^2] instead therefore of these firstborn who were in excess of the tribe of Levi, God appointed that a didrachma be given to the priests. From then on, therefore, it became a custom that simply all the firstborn should pay the didrachma, which is five shekels, that is, two hundred obols.[^3] Since therefore the Lord also was a firstborn, he too used to pay *it*; but respecting Christ, perhaps, on account of the miracles, they do not question him, but Peter — or rather, *doing so* craftily, as if saying something like this: This teacher of yours, *who is* opposed to the law, has he then consented to pay the didrachmas?

[^1]: Greek *δίδραχμον* (plural *τὰ δίδραχμα*), the "double-drachma," here the annual half-shekel temple tax levied on every adult male Israelite (cf. Exod. 30:13). Rendered by its coin-name "didrachma" throughout rather than smoothed to "temple tax," since Theophylact's whole exposition turns on the coinage and its reckoning.
[^2]: The figures follow the census of Num. 3. The Levites numbered 22,000 (Num. 3:39); the firstborn of Israel numbered 22,273 (Num. 3:43); the 273 firstborn "in excess of" (Greek *τῶν ἐπέκεινα τῆς τοῦ Λευῒ φυλῆς ὄντων*) the number of the Levites who redeemed them were ransomed at five shekels each (Num. 3:46–47). Theophylact compresses the account, so that "instead of these firstborn who were in excess" stands for that surplus which had no Levite to stand in its place.
[^3]: Greek *πέντε σίκλοι, ἤγουν διακόσιοι ὀβολοί* — "five shekels, that is, two hundred obols." The equivalence (one shekel = forty obols) is Theophylact's own gloss on the coin's worth; retained as it stands.

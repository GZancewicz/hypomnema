# Matthew 17 (ΙΖʹ) — Lemma 22 · Matt. 17:20–21

> **"You shall say to this mountain, 'Move,'"**

that is, When you say *it*, then it shall move. Therefore the apostles did not say *it*, since neither *the* occasion called *for it* nor *any* necessity *did*; and for this reason mountains were not moved — whereas, if indeed they had said *it*, they would have moved. But this kind, that of the demons, is cast out through prayer and fasting. For it is necessary that those very ones especially who are demon-possessed should fast, and *also* those who are about to heal them; and *the* prayer *is effective* then, when it comes about not with drunkenness, but with fasting. And understand also that the whole faith *is* a grain of mustard seed — considered cheap, on the one hand, because of the foolishness of the preaching,[^1] yet, if it happens *upon* good soil, growing into a tree, in which the birds of heaven — the thoughts that fly highest[^2] — make their nest. Whoever, therefore, has the whole of faith, that one is able to say to this mountain, "Move" — that is, to the demon; for he was pointing to the demon that had gone out.[^3]

[^1]: Greek **διὰ τὴν μωρίαν τοῦ κηρύγματος** ("because of the foolishness of the preaching"), an echo of 1 Cor. 1:21. The «μὲν» here ("on the one hand") is answered by the following «πλὴν» ("yet, nevertheless"), rendered so as to keep the antithesis visible.
[^2]: Greek **οἱ ἀνωτάτω πετόμενοι λογισμοί** ("the reasonings / thoughts that fly highest"), Theophylact's allegorical gloss on "the birds of heaven" (cf. Matt. 13:32): the loftiest thoughts lodge in the grown tree of faith. The Greek sentence is anacoluthic — the "yet…" clause has no finite main verb — and the translation preserves the trailing construction.
[^3]: i.e., the "mountain" is here interpreted as the demon; «ἐδείκνυε γὰρ τὸ ἐξελθὸν δαιμόνιον» — "for he was pointing to the demon that had gone out" (the demon of the possessed boy, subject of the preceding lemmata).

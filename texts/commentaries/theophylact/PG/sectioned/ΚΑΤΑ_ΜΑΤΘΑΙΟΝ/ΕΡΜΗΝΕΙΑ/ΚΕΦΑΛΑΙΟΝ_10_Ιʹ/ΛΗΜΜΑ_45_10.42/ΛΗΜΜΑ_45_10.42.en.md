# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 45 · Matt. 10:42

> **And whoever shall give one of these little ones only a cup of cold *water* to drink, in *the* name of a disciple, amen I say to you, he shall by no means lose his reward.**[^1]

So that no one may put forward poverty *as a pretext*,[^2] he says that: Even if you give a cup of cold water, for the sake of that man's being my disciple, even for this you will receive the reward. But a cup of cold *water* is given also by one who instructs a man who is inflamed with the fire of anger[^3] and of the desires; and *by* making him to be named a disciple of Christ, neither this one, therefore, loses his reward.[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Καὶ ὃς ἐὰν ποτίσῃ ἕνα τῶν μικρῶν τούτων ποτήριον ψυχροῦ μόνον, εἰς ὄνομα μαθητοῦ, ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, οὐ μὴ ἀπολέσῃ τὸν μισθὸν αὐτοῦ.» (Matt. 10:42). *ψυχροῦ* is the genitive "of cold *water*," the noun ("water," *ὕδατος*) being understood; supplied in italics. *ἀμήν* is retained as "amen" per convention rather than rendered "truly."
[^2]: The OCR reads *νίαν προβάλληταί τις*; securely *πενίαν προβάλληταί τις* — "lest anyone put forward poverty (as an excuse)." The reconstruction is confirmed just below, where Christ's answer is that even a cup of cold water suffices, i.e. no one is too poor to give.
[^3]: Greek *θυμοῦ* (OCR *Μοιμοῦ*): "anger, wrath, passion." Theophylact turns on a word-play lost in English: the *cold* (*ψυχρόν*) water cools the man *inflamed* (*φλογιζόμενον*) with the *fire* (*πῦρ*) of *θυμός* (anger) and *ἐπιθυμία* (desire) — so that spiritual instruction which quenches these passions is itself the "cup of cold water." Greek phrase: *φλογιζόμενον τῷ τοῦ θυμοῦ πυρὶ καὶ τῶν ἐπιθυμιῶν*.
[^4]: OCR *αὐτλων* → *αὐτὸν* ("him"); *μιαθὸν* → *μισθὸν* ("reward"). The subject of "loses" is the teacher/instructor, who by making his pupil a disciple of Christ likewise does not forfeit his reward.

# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 10:2

> **Now of the twelve apostles the names are these: First, Simon who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother. (Matt. 10:2)**

He lists the names of the apostles on account of the false apostles.[^1] And he sets Peter and Andrew first, because they were also first-called.[^2] Then those of Zebedee; and he ranks James before John;[^3] for not according to worth, but simply according to what chanced *to fall in* does he list these.[^4] Accordingly, then, he says:

[^1]: Greek *ψευδαποστόλους* — "false apostles" (the term of 2 Cor. 11:13); the naming of the true Twelve serves to distinguish them from these.
[^2]: Greek *πρωτόκλητοι*, "first-called" — Andrew is by tradition ὁ Πρωτόκλητος, "the First-called" (cf. John 1:40–41).
[^3]: The verbs are distinct: *προτίθησι* ("he sets first, puts forward") of Peter and Andrew, and *προτάττει* ("he ranks before, places ahead") of James over John; both are kept visible in the rendering.
[^4]: Greek «κατὰ τὸ παραπεσόν» — "according to what fell in *the way*," i.e. as it chanced, at random, rather than by any order of rank (*κατὰ τὴν ἀξίαν*, "according to worth"). *γοῦν* ("accordingly, at any rate") introduces the resumed quotation.

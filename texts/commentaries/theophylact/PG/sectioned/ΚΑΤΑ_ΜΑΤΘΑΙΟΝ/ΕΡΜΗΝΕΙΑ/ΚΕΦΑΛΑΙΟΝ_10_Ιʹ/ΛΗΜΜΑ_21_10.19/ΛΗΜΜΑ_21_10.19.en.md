# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 10:19–20

> **But when they deliver you up, be not anxious how or what you shall speak; for it shall be given to you in that hour what you shall speak. For it is not you who are the ones speaking, but the Spirit of your Father, which speaks in you.** (Matt. 10:19–20)

Lest they should say, "And how shall we be able to persuade wise men, being unlearned?",[^1] he bids them to be of good courage and not to be anxious. For when we are about to converse among believers, we must prepare beforehand and be ready for a defense, as Peter exhorts;[^2] but among crowds and raging kings[^3] he promises his own strength, so that we may not be afraid. For to confess is ours, but to make a defense wisely is God's. And lest you should suppose that the aptitude for defenses is natural,[^4] he says, "It is not you who speak, but the Spirit."

[^1]: Greek *ἰδιῶται ὄντες*: *ἰδιώτης* is the "private / unschooled / untrained" person — a layman without professional skill (here, rhetorical skill), opposed to the *σοφοί* ("wise men") they must persuade. Rendered "unlearned" for the sense; "being laymen / untrained men" would do equally.
[^2]: An allusion to 1 Peter 3:15, «ἕτοιμοι ἀεὶ πρὸς ἀπολογίαν» — "*being* ready always for a defense *of the hope that is in you*." The shared word *ἀπολογία* ("defense") ties the exhortation to the verse under comment. The OCR «δαῤῥεῖν» above is securely «θαῤῥεῖν» ("to be of good courage").
[^3]: Greek *μεταξὺ … ὄχλων καὶ βασιλέων μαινομένων*; *μαινομένων* = "raging / frenzied / mad." The OCR «μαινομόνων» is a certain slip for «μαινομένων».
[^4]: Greek *φυσική … ἡ πρὸς τὰς ἀπολογίας ἐπιτηδειότης*: *ἐπιτηδειότης* = "fitness, aptitude, suitability"; *φυσική* = "natural, *arising from one's own nature*." The point: the readiness to answer is not an innate faculty of theirs but a gift of the Spirit.

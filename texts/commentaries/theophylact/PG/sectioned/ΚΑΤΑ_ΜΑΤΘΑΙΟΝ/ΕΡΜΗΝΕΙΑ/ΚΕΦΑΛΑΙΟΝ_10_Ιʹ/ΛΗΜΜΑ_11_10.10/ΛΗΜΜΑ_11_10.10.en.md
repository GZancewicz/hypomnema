# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 10:9–10

> **…a bag for the road, nor two tunics, nor sandals, nor staffs; for the worker is worthy of his food. (Matt. 10:10)**[^1]

He trains them unto all exactness;[^2] therefore, making them entirely free of superfluity[^3] and free from care,[^4] he does not even allow them to have staffs. For this is exact possessionlessness[^5] — the thing that makes the one teaching about possessionlessness to be believed. Then, so that they may not say, "From where shall we be fed?"[^6] he says:[^7]

[^1]: Greek «πήραν εἰς ὁδὸν, μηδὲ δύο χιτῶνας, μηδὲ ὑποδήματα, μηδὲ ῥάβδους· ἄξιος γὰρ ὁ ἐργάτης τῆς τροφῆς αὐτοῦ ἐστιν.» *πήραν* = "a beggar's/traveler's bag, wallet, pouch." *ῥάβδους* is plural, "staffs/rods"; Matthew's prohibition here differs from Mark 6:8 (which permits a single staff) — Theophylact follows Matthew's plural and forbids them outright. *ἄξιος… τῆς τροφῆς* = "worthy of his food/nourishment"; *γὰρ* = "for."
[^2]: Greek *Εἰς πᾶσαν ἀκρίβειαν αὐτοὺς γυμνάζει* (OCR *κκριοειαν* = **ἀκρίβειαν**) — *ἀκρίβειαν* = "exactness, strictness, precision"; *γυμνάζει* = "he trains, exercises *as in a gymnasium*."
[^3]: Greek *ἀπερίττους* — "free of what is superfluous, without excess, unencumbered."
[^4]: Greek *ἀμερίμνους* — "free from care/anxiety, untroubled about provision."
[^5]: Greek *ἡ ἀκριβὴς ἀκτημοσύνη* — "exact/strict possessionlessness"; see the note on *ἀκτημοσύνη* at Lemma 9. The point: only one who owns literally nothing is credible when he teaches others to own nothing. OCR *ἑοδάσκοντα* = **διδάσκοντα** ("the one teaching").
[^6]: Greek *Πόθεν διατραφησόμεθα;* (OCR *διατραφησέμαβα*) — "From where shall we be fed/sustained?"; *Πόθεν* = "whence."
[^7]: The sentence breaks off into the next lemma, which repeats and expounds «The worker *is* worthy of his food».

# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 10:1

> **And having summoned his twelve disciples, he gave to them authority over unclean spirits, so as to cast them out, and to heal every disease and every infirmity.[^1] (Matt. 10:1)**

Twelve disciples he chooses, according to the number of the twelve tribes; and having added power to these, he sent them forth, although indeed they were few; for few are those who journey through the narrow *way*.[^2]

[^1]: Greek «θεραπεύειν πᾶσαν νόσον καὶ πᾶσαν μαλακίαν». The pair *νόσος* / *μαλακία* is Matthew's own (cf. Matt. 4:23; 9:35): *νόσος* is "disease, sickness," *μαλακία* literally "softness, weakness," hence "infirmity, ailment." Rendered "disease" / "infirmity" to keep the two words distinct rather than collapsing both to "sickness." (The OCR reads «ἀκαθάρνων» and «ἑκβάλλειν» for «ἀκαθάρτων» and «ἐκβάλλειν»; securely recoverable.)
[^2]: The clause is legible as «ὀλίγοι γὰρ οἱ διὰ τῆς στενῆς ὁδεύοντες» — "for few *are* those who journey through the narrow *[way]*" (cf. Matt. 7:14, «στενὴ ἡ πύλη … ἡ ὁδός»; the noun "gate/way" is not written and is supplied). The remainder of the line («ὁδεύοιΟΑΡΗΓ … ΝΕαε. Π.») is OCR garble — the Latin parallel column of Migne intruding — and is unrecoverable; the exposition resumes in the following lemma.

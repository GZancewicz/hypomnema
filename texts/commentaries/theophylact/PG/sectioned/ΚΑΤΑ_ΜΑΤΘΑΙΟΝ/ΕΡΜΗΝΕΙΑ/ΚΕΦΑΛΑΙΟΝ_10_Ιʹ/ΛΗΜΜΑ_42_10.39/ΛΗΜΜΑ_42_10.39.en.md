# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 10:39

> **He who finds his soul shall lose it; and he who loses his soul for my sake shall find it.[^1] (Matt. 10:39)**

He seems to find his soul who procures for himself the life in *the* flesh[^2] — who also loses it, being consigned to eternal punishment; but he who loses his soul and dies — not as a robber, nor as one strangling himself,[^3] but for the sake of Christ — that one saves it,[^4]

[^1]: Greek *ψυχή* ("soul") runs through both lemma and comment and turns on its double sense — "soul" and "life (as a living self)." Theophylact keeps *ψυχή* distinct from *ζωή* just below (*τὴν ἐν σαρκὶ ζωήν*, "the life in the flesh"), so *ψυχή* is rendered uniformly "soul" to preserve his wordplay, though the saying's plain force is "he who *saves his life* shall lose it." The opening article *Ὁ* stood, orphaned, at the close of lemma 41; it is restored here to the quotation it begins.
[^2]: Greek *ὁ περιποιούμενος τὴν ἐν σαρκὶ ζωήν* — "he who procures/preserves for himself the life in *the* flesh," i.e. one who clings to bodily life. *περιποιέομαι* = "keep safe, secure for oneself, procure."
[^3]: Greek *πνίγων ἑαυτόν* — "strangling / choking himself," i.e. one who takes his own life. Theophylact distinguishes the martyr's loss of life for Christ both from the criminal (*λῃστής*, "robber") and from the suicide.
[^4]: The sentence breaks off at the comma (*ἐκεῖνος σώζει αὐτήν,*, "that one saves it,"); the thought is completed in the following lemma (43).

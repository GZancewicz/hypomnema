# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 10:37

> **He who loves father or mother above me is not worthy of me; and he who loves son or daughter above me is not worthy of me.[^1] (Matt. 10:37)**

Do you see that *it is* then *that* one must hate one's parents and one's children — whenever they wish to be loved more than Christ? And why do I speak of father and mother and children? Hear the greater *thing*:[^2]

[^1]: The folder-name verse (10.36) is corrected here: the Greek expounded is Matt. 10:37, *ὁ φιλῶν πατέρα ἢ μητέρα ὑπὲρ ἐμὲ...*. The repeated *ὑπὲρ ἐμέ* ("above me / more than me") governs the comparison; rendered "above me," with the comparative force made explicit in the exposition's *πλέον Χριστοῦ ἀγαπᾶσθαι* ("to be loved more than Christ").
[^2]: The lemma breaks off at the colon (*ἄκουε·*, "hear:"); the "greater thing" — the saying about the cross — is quoted and expounded in the following lemma (40). Theophylact reads the Gospel's "loves… above me" as equivalent to "hating" (*μισεῖν*) parents and children, i.e. loving them less than Christ, importing the sharper verb from the parallel wording (cf. Luke 14:26).

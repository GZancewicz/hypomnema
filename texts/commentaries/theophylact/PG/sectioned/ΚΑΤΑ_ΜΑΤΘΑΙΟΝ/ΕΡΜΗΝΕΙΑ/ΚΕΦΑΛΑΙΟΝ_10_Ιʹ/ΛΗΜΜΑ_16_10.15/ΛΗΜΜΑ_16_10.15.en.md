# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 10:15

> **—it will be more bearable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah in the day of judgment, than for that city.**[^1]

He wishes them to shake off the dust, showing that they received nothing from there, on account of their disobedience; or, that it might be a testimony of the long journey which the apostles walked, and *yet* did not benefit them.[^2] It will therefore be more bearable for the Sodomites than for those who disobey, because the Sodomites, having been punished here, will there be punished more moderately.

[^1]: This completes the sentence broken off in the previous lemma («Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν—», "Verily I say to you—"). The source reads «τῇ πλαι ἐκείνῃ», an OCR corruption of **«τῇ πόλει ἐκείνῃ»** ("that city"), secured both by the parallel «τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης» in the previous lemma and by the sense.
[^2]: Greek *ὥστε μαρτύριον εἶναι τῆς μακρᾶς ὁδοιπορίας ἣν ἐβάδισαν, καὶ αὐτοὺς οὐκ ὠφέλησαν*. The subject continues from *ἐβάδισαν* ("they walked") — the apostles — while *αὐτούς* (accusative) is the object: the dust is a testimony of the long road the apostles walked, and *yet* they did not benefit *them* (the townsfolk), i.e. the arduous visit profited the inhabitants nothing because of their unbelief, and so stands as a witness against the city at the judgment. It could alternatively be construed with the townsfolk as subject ("and *they* [the townsfolk] did not benefit *them* [the apostles]"), but subject-continuity favors the former.

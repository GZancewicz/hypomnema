# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 10:15

> **—it will be more bearable for *the* land of Sodom and Gomorrah in *the* day of judgment, than for that city.**[^1]

He wishes *them* to shake off the dust, showing that they received nothing from there, on account of their disobedience; or, that it might be a testimony of the long journey which they walked, and *yet* they did not benefit them.[^2] It will therefore be more bearable for the Sodomites than for those who disobey, because the Sodomites, having been punished here, will there be punished more moderately.

[^1]: This completes the sentence broken off in the previous lemma («Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν—», "Verily I say to you—"). The source reads «τῇ πλαι ἐκείνῃ», an OCR corruption of **«τῇ πόλει ἐκείνῃ»** ("that city"), secured both by the parallel «τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης» in the previous lemma and by the sense.
[^2]: Greek *ὥστε μαρτύριον εἶναι τῆς μακρᾶς ὁδοιπορίας ἣν ἐβάδισαν, καὶ αὐτοὺς οὐκ ὠφέλησαν*. The subjects are unmarked and ambiguous: most naturally, the dust is a testimony of the long road which *the apostles* walked, and *that the townsfolk* did not benefit *them* (i.e. gave the apostles nothing in return for the arduous visit) — hence a witness against the city at the judgment. It could alternatively be read as "and *they* [the apostles] did not profit *them* [the townsfolk]," but the punitive drift ("more bearable for the Sodomites") favors the former.

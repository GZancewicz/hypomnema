# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 10:16b

> **Become therefore prudent as the serpents, and innocent as the doves. (Matt. 10:16b)**[^1]

He wishes the disciples to be prudent also. For, so that you might not, hearing these *called* sheep, suppose that the Christian must always be simple-minded,[^2] he says that he must be prudent also, knowing how one must conduct oneself in the midst of many enemies. For as the serpent gives all the rest of its body to be struck, but guards its head, so also let the Christian give all his own things, and his body, to those who strike him; but let him guard the head, which is Christ and the faith in him. And just as the serpent, being squeezed tight through some narrow hole and passing through, strips off its old skin, so also let us, journeying by the narrow way, strip off the old man.[^3] But since the serpent is also harmful, he commands us to be innocent — that is, simple and guileless and not harmful, as the doves; for these, even when deprived of their young and driven off, return again to their masters. Be therefore prudent as a serpent, so as not to be laughed at in your life, but to order all the things concerning you blamelessly; but with respect to harming others, be as a dove, that is, guileless.[^4]

[^1]: Theophylact quotes «Γίνεσθε οὖν φρόνιμοι ὡς οἱ ὄφεις, καὶ ἀκέραιοι ὡς αἱ περιστεραί». *φρόνιμος* is rendered "prudent" (also "shrewd, sensible") and *ἀκέραιος* "innocent" (literally "unmixed, unadulterated," hence "pure, guileless"); the exposition below glosses *ἀκέραιος* as *ἁπλοῦς καὶ ἀπονήρους* ("simple and guileless").
[^2]: Greek *ἠλίθιον* — "silly, foolish, simple-minded"; here the negative sense ("stupid"), against which prudence is enjoined.
[^3]: The serpent-imagery turns on *ἀποδύεται τὸ παλαιὸν δέρμα* ("sheds its old skin") answering *τὸν παλαιὸν ἀποδυώμεθα ἄνθρωπον* ("let us put off the old man," cf. Eph. 4:22, Col. 3:9). The same verb *ἀποδύομαι* ("strip off") is used of both skin and "old man," and is rendered "strip off" in both places to keep the pun visible.
[^4]: *ἀπόνηρος* — "free from malice/wickedness," the same word rendered "guileless" above; the dove models harmlessness toward others, as the serpent models self-preserving prudence.

# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 10:25

> **it is enough for the disciple that he become as his teacher, and the servant as his lord.**[^1]

He teaches here to endure insults.[^2] For if I, the teacher and master, endured *them*, much more you, the disciples and servants. But you ask how he says, "A disciple is not above the teacher," seeing that we behold many disciples better than their teachers? Learn, therefore, that as long as they are disciples, they are less than their teachers; but if they become better, they are no longer disciples any more — just as also the servant, as long as he is a servant, is not above his lord.

[^1]: Greek «ἀρκετὸν τῷ μαθητῇ ἵνα γένηται ὡς ὁ διδάσκαλος αὐτοῦ, καὶ ὁ δοῦλος ὡς ὁ κύριος αὐτοῦ» — Matt. 10:25a, continuing the sentence begun in the previous lemma. *ἀρκετὸν* is a bare predicate adjective ("*it is* enough / sufficient"); the "*it is*" is supplied. The second clause has no verb, "become" being carried over from the first: "and the servant *become* as his lord."
[^2]: Greek *ἀνέχεσθαι τῶν ὕβρεων* (OCR *ἀνένεσθαι τῶν ὄβρεων*), securely = "to bear / put up with insults (outrages, abusive treatment)." *ὕβρις* covers insolent, humiliating mistreatment, not merely verbal insult.

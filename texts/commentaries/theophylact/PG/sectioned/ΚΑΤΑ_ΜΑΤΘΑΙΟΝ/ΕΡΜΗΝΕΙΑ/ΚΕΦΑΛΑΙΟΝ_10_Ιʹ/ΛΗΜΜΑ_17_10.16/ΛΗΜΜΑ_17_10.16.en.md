# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 10:16a

> **Behold, I send you forth as sheep in *the* midst of wolves. (Matt. 10:16a)**

He armed them with signs, he made *them* confident concerning food, having opened to *them* the doors of the worthy. Now he tells also the terrible things that are to befall *them*, showing *his* foreknowledge. But he consoles *them*, through the *word* "I."[^1] "I," he says, "the mighty *one*. So then, be confident; for you will be unconquerable." And he prepares them for suffering; for as *it is* impossible for a sheep in *the* midst of wolves not to suffer, so also *is it* for you among the Jews. But even if you suffer, do not be angry; for I wish you to be gentle as sheep, and thus rather to conquer.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *διὰ τοῦ ἐγώ* — literally "through the 'I'": the consolation lies in the emphatic pronoun *ἐγώ* of «Ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ ἀποστέλλω», i.e. it is *he himself* (the mighty one) who sends them.
[^2]: Greek *καὶ οὕτω μᾶλλον νικᾶν* — "and thus (i.e. by gentleness, not anger) rather to conquer"; the infinitive continues under *βούλομαι* ("I wish… and *[I wish you] thus* to conquer").

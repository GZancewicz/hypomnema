# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 10:10

> **The worker is worthy of his food,**[^1]

that is, you will be fed from the disciples.[^2] For they owe this to you, as *to* workers.[^3] But he said "of food," not "of luxury."[^4] For teachers ought not to live luxuriously.[^5]

[^1]: Greek «Ἄξιος ὁ ἐργάτης τῆς τροφῆς αὐτοῦ» — Theophylact re-quotes the closing words of the previous lemma to comment on them by themselves. The clause has no verb; the supplied copula "is" is left roman.
[^2]: Greek *ἐκ τῶν μαθητῶν τραφήσεσθε* — "you will be fed from the disciples," i.e. from those you teach (*μαθηταί*, "learners, pupils"); *τραφήσεσθε* is future passive.
[^3]: Greek *Χρεωστοῦσι γὰρ τοῦτο ὑμῖν ὡς ἐργάταις* — *Χρεωστοῦσι* = "they owe *as a debt*"; the sustenance is not alms but wages due. *ὡς ἐργάταις* = "as *to* workers."
[^4]: Greek *Τροφῆς δὲ εἶπεν, οὐ τρυφῆς* — an untranslatable one-letter word-play: **τροφῆς** ("of food, nourishment") against **τρυφῆς** ("of luxury, daintiness, soft living"). The point is that the Lord promises the teacher bare sustenance, not indulgence. The English cannot reproduce the near-identity of the two Greek words; hence the note.
[^5]: Greek *Οὐ γὰρ δεῖ τρυφᾷν τοὺς διδασκάλους* (OCR *διδασκὸὶλους* = **διδασκάλους**) — *τρυφᾷν* = "to live luxuriously, be soft, revel"; the cognate verb of *τρυφῆς* above.

# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 10:34–35

> **…*have I* come to cast peace, but a sword.[^1] For I came to divide a man against his father, and a daughter against her mother; and a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law;[^2] and…**[^3]

[^1]: This lemma opens mid-clause: the negative *οὐκ* ("not") stands at the close of the preceding lemma (36), «...οὐκ», so that the full Gospel sentence reads *οὐκ ἦλθον βαλεῖν εἰρήνην, ἀλλὰ μάχαιραν* ("I came **not** to cast peace, but a sword," Matt. 10:34). The supplied "*have I*" italicizes the verb-subject carried by the Greek finite form *ἦλθον*; *βαλεῖν* is rendered "to cast" (throw/put), keeping the concrete verb rather than a smoother "bring."
[^2]: Greek *νύμφην κατὰ τῆς πενθερᾶς αὐτῆς*. *νύμφη* means both "bride" and "daughter-in-law"; paired here with *πενθερά* ("mother-in-law") the sense is "daughter-in-law." Rendered so, with the ambiguity flagged.
[^3]: This lemma carries only the continuation of the Gospel text and breaks off at *καὶ* ("and…"); it has no exposition of its own. Theophylact's comment on these verses begins in the following lemma (38), which resumes the quotation with «...the enemies of the man are his own household.»

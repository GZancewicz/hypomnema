# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 38 · Matt. 10:36

> **…the enemies of the man *are* his own household.[^1] (Matt. 10:36)**

Not everywhere is concord a good *thing*; but there is *a time* when division too *is good*. A sword, at any rate, is the word of the faith, which cuts us off from the attachment to *our* household-folk and kinsmen, when they hinder us with respect to the reverence of God.[^2] For he does not say simply to be divided — that is, to be separated — from these, but *rather* whenever they would not go along *with us*, but even more, whenever they would hinder *us* toward the faith.

[^1]: The lemma resumes the Gospel text broken off at the end of lemma 37 (*καὶ*, "and…"); the full clause is *καὶ ἐχθροὶ τοῦ ἀνθρώπου οἱ οἰκιακοὶ αὐτοῦ* ("and the enemies of the man *are* his own household," Matt. 10:36). *οἱ οἰκιακοὶ αὐτοῦ* = "the members of his own household / his own housefolk." The copula "*are*" is supplied.
[^2]: Greek *θεοσέβεια* — "reverence toward God, piety, godliness." Rendered "reverence of God" to keep the God-directed force of the compound rather than the more general "piety." The play on *μάχαιρα* ("sword," Matt. 10:34) reappears here: Theophylact glosses the "sword" as *ὁ τῆς πίστεως λόγος*, "the word of the faith," which severs harmful attachments.

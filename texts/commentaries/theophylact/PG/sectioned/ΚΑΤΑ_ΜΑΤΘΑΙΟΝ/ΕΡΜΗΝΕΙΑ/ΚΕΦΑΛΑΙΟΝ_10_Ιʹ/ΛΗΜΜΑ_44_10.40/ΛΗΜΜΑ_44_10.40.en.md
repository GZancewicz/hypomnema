# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 10:40–41

> **[…] *receiving*, receives him who sent me. He who receives a prophet in *the* name of a prophet, shall receive a prophet's reward. And he who receives a righteous man in *the* name of a righteous man, shall receive a righteous man's reward.**[^1]

He rouses us on, that we may welcome those *sent* from Christ. For he who honors his disciples, honors him, and through him the Father also. But one must welcome the righteous and the prophets in *the* name of a righteous man and of a prophet — that is, because they are righteous men and prophets. Not on account of some patronage and support *they may have* with the kings,[^2] but even if perchance someone has the outward form[^3] of a prophet, yet falls short in *the* deed, do you receive him as a prophet. And God, as though you had in reality received a righteous man, will recompense you. For this is what "he shall receive a righteous man's reward" signifies; or you will also understand it in another way, that the one who received will himself too be reckoned righteous, and will receive a reward such as the righteous receive.

[^1]: Greek «… δεχόμενος, δέχεται τὸν ἀποστείλαντά με. Ὁ δεχόμενος προφήτην εἰς ὄνομα προφήτου, μισθὸν προφήτου λήψεται. Καὶ ὁ δεχόμενος δίκαιον εἰς ὄνομα δικαίου, μισθὸν δικαίου λήψεται.» (Matt. 10:40b–41). The lemma opens mid-word with the participle *δεχόμενος* ("receiving") carried over from Lemma 43; the completed clause is "and he who receives me, receives him who sent me." The phrase *εἰς ὄνομα προφήτου/δικαίου* is rendered "in *the* name of a prophet / a righteous man," which Theophylact glosses below as "because they are prophets / righteous men."
[^2]: Greek *Μὴ διά τινα προστασίαν καὶ ἀντίληψιν παρὰ τοῖς βασιλεῦσιν* — literally "not on account of some patronage (*προστασία*) and support/assistance (*ἀντίληψις*) with the kings." The sense: welcome a prophet not for the worldly advantage or protection he might procure you with rulers, but simply because he is a prophet. *παρὰ τοῖς βασιλεῦσιν* ("with/before the kings") could also be taken "from the kings."
[^3]: Greek *σχῆμα* — "outward form, appearance, guise, bearing." Theophylact contrasts the mere *σχῆμα* of a prophet with the *ἔργον* ("deed, work, reality"): even a man who has only the appearance of a prophet and falls short in fact is to be received, and God will reward the receiver as though he had received a genuine one.

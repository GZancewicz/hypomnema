# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 20 · Matt. 10:18

> **…and before governors and kings shall you be brought for my sake, for a testimony to them and to the nations.**[^1] (Matt. 10:18)

You see, this is what it is to be prudent: to take heed and not to give occasion[^2] to those wishing to afflict *you*, but prudently to manage the things concerning oneself. If the persecutor wants money, if *he wants* honor, give *it*, so that he may not have occasion; but if he takes away the faith, guard your head.[^3] And not only to the Jews is he about to send the disciples, but also to the nations; wherefore also he says, "For a testimony to them and to the nations," that is, for a reproof of them, *since they are* not believing.[^4]

[^1]: This lemma continues the sentence broken off in the previous one (Matt. 10:17), which ended «καὶ ἐπὶ» ("and before"); here it is completed: «ἡγεμόνας δὲ καὶ βασιλεῖς ἀχθήσεσθε ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ». The postpositive δέ carries the connective force of the whole «καὶ ἐπὶ … δέ» construction, rendered here by the leading "and." Greek *τοῖς ἔθνεσιν* is rendered "to the nations"; it means the Gentiles specifically, as the exposition makes explicit by contrasting *Ἰουδαίους* ("Jews") with *τὰ ἔθνη*.
[^2]: Greek *ἀφορμή* ("occasion, pretext, starting-point, ground *for action*"); the same word recurs just below ("so that he may not have occasion") and is kept uniform.
[^3]: The image of guarding "the head" continues the serpent-simile of the preceding lemma on Matt. 10:16, where Theophylact glossed the head as "Christ and faith in him": the Christian yields body and goods to those who strike, but keeps the head, which is the faith, inviolate.
[^4]: Greek *εἰς ἔλεγχον αὐτῶν μὴ πιστευόντων*: *ἔλεγχον* = "reproof, conviction, exposure"; the genitive absolute–like *μὴ πιστευόντων* ("not believing") gives the ground — the testimony stands as a reproof against them precisely because they do not believe. The OCR garbles the final word as «πιστευόντ ΤΑν», securely = «πιστευόντων».

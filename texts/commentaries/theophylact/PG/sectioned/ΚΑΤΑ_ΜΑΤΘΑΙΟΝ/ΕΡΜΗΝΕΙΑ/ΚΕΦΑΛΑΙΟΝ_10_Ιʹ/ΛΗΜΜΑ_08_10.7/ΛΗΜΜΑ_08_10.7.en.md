# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 10:5–7

> **But as you go, preach, saying that The kingdom of the heavens has drawn near. (Matt. 10:7)**[^1]

"These"[^2] — which? The fishermen, the unlettered men,[^3] the tax collectors. But primarily[^4] he sends them to the Jews, so that they may not have *ground* to say that they were sent to the Gentiles — "and for this reason we, the Jews, did not believe." Therefore he makes the Jews without excuse.[^5] But he joins the Samaritans to the Gentiles, since, being Babylonians, they settled Judea, and did not even receive the prophets, but only the five books of Moses.[^6] And by "kingdom of the heavens," understand also the coming enjoyment.[^7] And he arms them with the miracles, saying:[^8]

[^1]: Greek «Πορευόμενοι δὲ κηρύσσετε, λέγοντες, ὅτι Ἤγγικεν ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν.» The participle *Πορευόμενοι* is kept participial ("as you go / going"); *δὲ* = "but." *Ἤγγικεν* is a perfect, "has drawn near." The header range (10:5–7) reflects that the exposition below reaches back over the whole sending, though the lemma quoted at its head is 10:7.
[^2]: Greek *Τούτους* — the demonstrative resumes the opening words of the pericope, «Τούτους τοὺς δώδεκα ἀπέστειλεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς» ("These twelve Jesus sent," Matt. 10:5), which fell in the earlier lemma. Theophylact quotes the single word and then asks who they were.
[^3]: Greek *τοὺς ἰδιώτας* — "private/unofficial persons," hence "laymen, untrained, unlettered common men"; the point is their lack of learning and standing, against which the mission's success shows divine power.
[^4]: Greek *Προηγουμένως* — "in the first instance, preferentially, primarily"; he sends them to the Jews *first* and by preference.
[^5]: Greek *Ἀπροφασίστους* (OCR *Ἀπροφασίοτους*) — "without pretext/excuse," lit. "unable to plead an excuse."
[^6]: Greek *Συνάπτει δὲ τοὺς Σαμαρείτας τοῖς ἐθνικοῖς* — "he joins the Samaritans to the Gentiles," i.e. he classes them with the Gentiles (the charge forbids going to both). *κατῴκησαν* = "they settled/colonized." The historical claim (Samaritans as transplanted "Babylonians" who received only the Pentateuch, not the prophets) is Theophylact's own; translated as it stands.
[^7]: Greek *βασιλείαν δὲ οὐρανῶν, καὶ τὴν μέλλουσαν ἀπόλαυσιν νόει* — *νόει* is imperative, "understand." *τὴν μέλλουσαν ἀπόλαυσιν* = "the coming/future enjoyment (fruition, delight)." The sense: take "kingdom of the heavens" to signify also the enjoyment that is to come.
[^8]: Greek *Ὁπλίζει δὲ αὐτοὺς τοῖς θαύμασι, λέγων* — *Ὁπλίζει* = "he arms/equips." The sentence breaks off into the next lemma, which supplies the words «Heal *those who are* sick…».

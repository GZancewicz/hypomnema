# Matthew 10 (Ιʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 10:8

> **Heal *those who are* sick, cleanse lepers, cast out demons. Freely you received, freely give. (Matt. 10:8)**[^1]

Nothing so befits the teacher as humility and possessionlessness.[^2] Accordingly, these two things he shows here, saying, "Freely you received"; for do not be high-minded,[^3] as *though* having and giving such good things. For freely and by grace you have these things — but, along with being humble-minded, become also free from love of money.[^4] "For freely," he says, "give." But, more perfectly pulling up the root of all evils,[^5] he says:[^6]

[^1]: Greek «Ἀσθενοῦντας θεραπεύετε, λεπροὺς καθαρίζετε, δαιμόνια ἐκβάλλετε. Δωρεὰν ἐλάβετε, δωρεὰν δότε.» The participle *Ἀσθενοῦντας* ("*those who are* ailing/sick") is rendered with a supplied relative clause. Theophylact's text lacks the clause «νεκροὺς ἐγείρετε» ("raise *the* dead") that stands in many witnesses of Matt. 10:8; translated as it stands on the page. *Δωρεὰν* = "freely, as a gift, gratis."
[^2]: Greek *ἀκτημοσύνη* — a fixed ascetic term for the state of owning nothing: "possessionlessness, freedom from possessions, poverty *of one's own choosing*." Rendered consistently "possessionlessness" throughout this lemma and the next; it is paired here with *ταπείνωσις* ("humility, lowliness").
[^3]: Greek *ὑψηλοφρονεῖτε* — "be high-minded, proud, haughty"; the deliberate opposite of *ταπεινοφρονεῖν* ("to be humble-minded"), which appears just below.
[^4]: Greek *ἀφιλοχρήματοι γένεσθε* — "become free from love of money / not money-loving." *σὺν τῷ ταπεινοφρονεῖν* = "together with being humble-minded."
[^5]: Greek *ἀνασπῶν τὴν ῥίζαν πάντων τῶν κακῶν* — "tearing/pulling up the root of all evils." An allusion to 1 Tim. 6:10 (φιλαργυρία, love of money, as the root of all evils); *Τελεώτερον* = "more perfectly, more completely."
[^6]: The sentence breaks off into the next lemma, which supplies the words «Do not acquire gold, nor silver…».

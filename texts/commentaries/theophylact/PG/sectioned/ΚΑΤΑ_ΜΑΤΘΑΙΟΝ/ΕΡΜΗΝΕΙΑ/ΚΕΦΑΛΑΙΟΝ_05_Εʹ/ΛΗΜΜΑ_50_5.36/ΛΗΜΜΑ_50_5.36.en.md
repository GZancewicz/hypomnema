# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 50 · Matt. 5:37

> **But that which is beyond these is of the evil one.** (Matt. 5:37b)[^1]

Swearing, he says — being superfluous beyond the "yea" and the "nay" — is of the devil. But you will say: "And so then was the law of Moses, in commanding to swear, evil?" Learn, therefore, that swearing was not evil then; but after Christ it is evil — just as also being circumcised, and, simply, Judaizing. Since being suckled, too, befits infants, but is a shame for men,[^2]

[^1]: Greek «Τὸ δὲ περισσὸν τούτων, ἐκ τοῦ πονηροῦ ἐστι». The word περισσόν means "surplus, excess, what is beyond/more than"; rendered "that which is beyond these" (i.e. beyond the plain yes and no). ἐκ τοῦ πονηροῦ is grammatically ambiguous between neuter ("of evil") and masculine ("of the evil one / the devil"); Theophylact's own gloss below — «τοῦ διαβόλου ἐστίν» ("is of the devil") — shows he reads it as masculine, so it is rendered "of the evil one" here.
[^2]: The sentence breaks off with a comma; the analogy (as suckling suits the infant but shames the grown man, so an observance right for the old covenant is unfitting after Christ) is left incomplete at the end of this lemma. Greek «τοῖς μὲν παιδίοις ἁρμόζει, τοῖς δὲ ἀνδράσιν αἰσχύνη» carries the μέν … δέ pair, rendered as a plain contrastive ("befits infants, but is a shame for men").

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 43 · Matt. 5:28–30

> **But I say to you, that every one who looks at a woman for the purpose of desiring her, has already committed adultery with her in his heart. (Matt. 5:28)**

That is, the one who stands there and busies himself curiously *about her*, and kindles the desire from the looking, and again looks so as to desire the more.[^1] But if he did not add the deed, what of this? He did not have the strength;[^2] since, if he should have the strength, at once he will accomplish the evil. Nevertheless, know that if we should desire, and then be hindered from doing the deed, it is manifest that we have been sheltered by grace. And the women too, if they adorn themselves in order to please some, even if they do not please, nevertheless those women have mixed the potion,[^3] even if no one drinks.

> **But if your right eye scandalizes you,[^4] pluck it out, and cast *it* from you; for it is profitable for you that one of your members should perish, and not that your whole body be cast into Gehenna. And if your right hand scandalizes you, cut it off and cast *it* from you. For it is profitable for you that one of your members should perish, and not that your whole body be cast into Gehenna. (Matt. 5:29–30)**

Hearing "eye" and "hand," do not suppose that this is said concerning the members *of the body*; for he would not have added the "right" *eye* and the "right" *hand*; but it is said concerning those who seem to be friends and are harming us. For example: there is a certain young man, having licentious friends, and he is harmed; these, therefore, cut off, he says. For perhaps you will save even those, when they have come to their senses; but if not, at least *you will save* yourself. But if you cherish the friendship toward them, you will both perish.

[^1]: Here the OCR is corrupt: «καὶ πάλιν βλέπων πρὸς τὸ πλεῖον ἐπικαρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ». The sense «πρὸς τὸ πλεῖον ἐπιθυμῆσαι» ("so as to desire the more") is securely recoverable and completes the thought of the lemma; the trailing «ἐπικαρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ» appears to be a garbled repetition of the lemma's «ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ» and is not rendered separately.
[^2]: Greek OCR «Μόκ ἴσχυσεν», securely = «Οὐκ ἴσχυσεν» ("he had not the strength / he was not able"). The verb *ἰσχύω* is repeated in the next clause; rendered consistently as "have the strength / be able."
[^3]: Greek *φάρμακον* — a drug, which may be a remedy, a love-charm, or a poison. In this context (women who adorn to allure) it carries the sinister sense of a poison/philtre mixed and served; the metaphor is that the guilt lies in the mixing, not in whether anyone "drinks." Rendered "potion" to keep the ambiguity.
[^4]: Greek *σκανδαλίζῃ* — "causes you to stumble / entraps / gives you offense." Kept as "scandalize" (its liturgical-English calque) to preserve the single recurring verb across both clauses; "cause to stumble" is the plain sense.

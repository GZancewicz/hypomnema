# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 53 · Matt. 5:40

> **And to him who wishes to go to law with you and to take your tunic, let him have also *your* cloak.** (Matt. 5:40)[^1]

If he drags you to a court and harasses you, let him have also *your* cloak — not if he simply asks for it. Now "tunic," properly, is what among us is called the undershirt;[^2] and "cloak," what is thrown over. Except that the names are also used one for the other.

[^1]: Greek «τῷ θέλοντί σοι κριθῆναι» — κριθῆναι is the passive/middle of κρίνω used in the legal sense "to go to law, to be judged *against* someone," i.e. to sue; rendered "to go to law with you." χιτών and ἱμάτιον are rendered "tunic" and "cloak," the inner and outer garments, as Theophylact himself distinguishes them just below.
[^2]: Greek ὑποκάμισον, the inner garment worn next to the skin (whence English "chemise"); rendered "undershirt." Theophylact's added «πλὴν καὶ ἀντ᾿ ἀλλήλων τὰ ὀνόματα λέγονται» concedes that in ordinary usage the two names are interchanged.

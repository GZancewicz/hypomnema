# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 17 · Matt. 5:13–14

> **But if the salt be made foolish, wherewith shall it be salted? It is no longer of any strength, except to be cast outside and to be trodden down by men. (Matt. 5:13)**[^1]

For the teacher, if he be made foolish[^1]—that is, if he does not reprove and draw tight, but grows slack—wherewith shall he be salted, that is, shall he be set right? From then, therefore, he is cast outside the teacher's dignity,[^2] and is trodden down—that is, is despised.

> **Ye are the light of the world. (Matt. 5:14)**

First salt, and then light. For the one who reproves the things done in secret, that man is light; for everything that is made manifest is light.[^3] And these did not enlighten a single nation, but the world.

[^1]: Greek *μωρανθῇ*, from *μωραίνω*—literally "be made foolish / dull / insipid," used of salt losing its savor. The rendering "be made foolish" is kept because Theophylact deliberately extends the same verb to the teacher himself, who "is made foolish" when he ceases to reprove; the pun on salt-losing-savor and teacher-turned-fool would be lost by rendering it "lose its savor." *ἀλισθήσεται* ("shall be salted," from *ἁλίζω*) he glosses in the exposition as *διορθωθήσεται* ("shall be set right").
[^2]: Greek *τοῦ διδασκαλικοῦ ἀξιώματος*—"the teacher's rank / dignity / office." OCR corrections in this lemma: «τουτι ἐτιν» = *τουτέστιν*, «ἐστετὸ» = *ἐστε τὸ*, «ἀλλὴ» = *ἀλλὰ*—all secure.
[^3]: Cf. Eph. 5:13, *πᾶν γὰρ τὸ φανερούμενον φῶς ἐστιν* ("for everything that is made manifest is light"), which Theophylact quotes here verbatim to explain why the reprover is called "light."

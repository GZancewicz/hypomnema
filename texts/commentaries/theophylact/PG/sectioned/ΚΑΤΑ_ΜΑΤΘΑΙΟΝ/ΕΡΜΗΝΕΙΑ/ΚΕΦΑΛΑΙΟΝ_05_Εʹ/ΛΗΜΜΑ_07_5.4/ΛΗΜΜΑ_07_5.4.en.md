# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 5:4–5

> **Blessed *are* they that mourn, for they shall be comforted. (Matt. 5:4)**

Those who mourn over their sins, not over any of the *things* of *this* life; and he said "mourning," that is, always and not once *only*; and not on behalf of our own sins only, but also on behalf of those[^1] of the neighbor. And they shall be comforted even here; for he who mourns on account of sin rejoices spiritually; and there, on the other hand, much more.

> **Blessed *are* the meek, for they shall inherit the earth. (Matt. 5:5)**

Some say the earth *is* the intelligible *earth*, that is, heaven; but nevertheless understand this earth also. For since[^2] the meek are reckoned *to be* despised and deprived of their possessions, he says that rather these possess all things. And the meek are, not those who are in no way angered — for such *men are* insensible[^3] — but those who, on the one hand, have anger,[^4] but, on the other hand, master *it*, and are angered when it is necessary.

[^1]: Greek τῶν τοῦ πλησίον, "those [sc. sins] of the neighbor"; the noun "sins" (ἁμαρτημάτων) is carried over from the preceding clause and supplied here.
[^2]: The OCR reads «Ἐκεὶ γὰρ νομίζονται οἱ πραεῖς», which as printed would be *ἐκεῖ* ("there"). The sense requires a concessive/causal connective — most plausibly **ἐπεὶ** ("since / seeing that"): "since the meek are reckoned *to be* despised… he says that rather they possess all things." Translated on that reading; the point is that the worldly estimate of the meek is inverted by Christ.
[^3]: Greek ἀναίσθητοι, "without perception / feeling," hence "insensible, unfeeling" — men incapable of anger are not virtuous but defective.
[^4]: Greek θυμὸν μὲν ἔχοντες, κρατοῦντες δέ. *θυμός* here is the "spirited" passion, anger/wrath as a capacity of the soul; meekness is not its absence but its mastery — having it, ruling it, and deploying it "when it is necessary" (ὅτε δεῖ).

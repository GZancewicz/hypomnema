# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 55 · Matt. 5:42

> **To *him* that asks you, give; and *him* that wishes to borrow from you, turn not away. (Matt. 5:42)**

Even if an enemy, even if a friend, even if an unbeliever; whether he seeks money, or some other help. But by "borrowing" he means not that *which is* with interest, but simply the lending.[^1] Since in the Law also they used to lend without interest.

[^1]: Greek *Δάνεισμα δὲ, οὐ τὸ σὺν τόκῳ λέγει, ἀλλὰ τὴν ἁπλῶς χρῆσιν*. The lemma's verb is *δανείσασθαι* ("to borrow"); in the comment Theophylact glosses the cognate noun *δάνεισμα* ("a loan / lending"). He distinguishes it from a loan *σὺν τόκῳ* ("with interest") and defines it as *ἡ ἁπλῶς χρῆσις* — "the plain/simple lending" or "the mere granting of use" (*χρῆσις* = "use, employment, a lending for use"). "*which is*" is supplied for the elided article-clause *τὸ σὺν τόκῳ* ("the *loan* with interest").

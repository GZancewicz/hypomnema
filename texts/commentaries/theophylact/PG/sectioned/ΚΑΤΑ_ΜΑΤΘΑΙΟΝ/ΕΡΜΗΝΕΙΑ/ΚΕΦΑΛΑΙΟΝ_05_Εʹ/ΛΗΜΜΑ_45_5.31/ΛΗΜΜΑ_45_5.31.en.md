# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 45 · Matt. 5:32

> **But I say to you, that every one who puts away his wife, apart from a matter of fornication,[^1] makes her to commit adultery; and whoever shall marry her who has been put away, commits adultery. (Matt. 5:32)**[^2]

But he does not abolish the Mosaic *precepts*, but sets them right, striking fear into the man, so that he may not without reason hate his wife. For if he should put her away with good reason — that is, when she has committed fornication — he is uncondemned; but if apart from fornication, condemned; for he compels her to commit adultery. But also the one who receives her is an adulterer; for if he had not received her, she would have turned back, and been made subject to her husband. For the Christian must be a peacemaker both toward the others, and by much more toward his own wife.

[^1]: Greek *παρεκτὸς λόγου πορνείας* — literally "apart from *the* word/account of fornication." Here *λόγος* means the "matter," "cause," or "ground" of fornication (i.e., unless she has been unfaithful); rendered "a matter of fornication" to stay close to the noun without importing a fixed English-Bible phrasing.
[^2]: The folder metadata assigns this lemma to 5:31, but the Greek here is the antithesis of Matt. 5:32 (Theophylact's «Ἐγὼ δὲ λέγω ὑμῖν …»), which the exposition expounds; the header is corrected accordingly.

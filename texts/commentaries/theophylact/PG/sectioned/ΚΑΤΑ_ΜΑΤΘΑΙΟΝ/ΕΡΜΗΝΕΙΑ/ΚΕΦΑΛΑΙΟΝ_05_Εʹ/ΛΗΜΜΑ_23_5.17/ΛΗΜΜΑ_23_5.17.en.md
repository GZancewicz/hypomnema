# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 5:17

> **For he did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth. (1 Pet. 2:22)**[^1]

And in another way too he fulfilled the law — that is, he filled *it* up;[^2] for whatever that one[^3] sketched *only* in outline, these things this one painted to perfection.[^4] That one *set forth* the:[^5]

[^1]: Greek «ἁμαρτίαν γὰρ οὐκ ἐποίησεν οὐδὲ εὑρέθη δόλος ἐν τῷ στόματι αὐτοῦ.» — 1 Peter 2:22, itself citing Isa 53:9. The source drops the opening syllable, reading OCR *μαρτίαν* = *ἁμαρτίαν* ("sin"), and *ολδὲ* = *οὐδὲ* ("neither").
[^2]: Theophylact glosses the verb of 5:17, *ἐπλήρωσε* ("he fulfilled"), with a near-synonym *ἀνεπλήρωσεν* ("he filled up completely, supplied in full"), sharpening "fulfill" toward "bring to completeness."
[^3]: Greek *ἐκεῖνος* ("that one") = the law (of Moses); *οὗτος* ("this one") just below = Christ. The demonstratives run through this lemma and the next.
[^4]: The wordplay is central and lost in English: *ἐσκιαγράφησε* (from *σκιαγραφία*, "shadow-painting, a rough outline sketch") versus *ἐζωγράφησεν* (from *ζωγραφία*, "painting from life, a full living portrait"; a *ζωγράφος* is a painter). The law made only the shadow-outline; Christ filled in the finished, lifelike picture. The source reads OCR *ταῦταοὗτος* (run together) = *ταῦτα οὗτος*, and *ἐωγράφησεν* = *ἐζωγράφησεν*.
[^5]: The sentence breaks off at *Ἐκεῖνος τὸ* ("That one *set forth* the," a verb of speaking supplied) and is completed in the next lemma (24), which supplies the commandment «Μὴ φονεύσῃς».

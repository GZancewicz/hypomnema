# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 5:22

> **«Raca».**[^1] (Matt. 5:22)

«Raca» signifies the equivalent of saying 'you'[^2] — just as we have the custom of saying to someone despised by us, "Get away, you." Wishing therefore to train us to be exact even about the small things, and to honor one another, he exhorts these things. But according to a certain *interpreter*, the *word* —[^3]

[^1]: This lemma has no fresh «…» quote at its head; it continues the exposition of the same clause of Matt. 5:22, «Ὃς δ᾽ ἂν εἴπῃ τῷ ἀδελφῷ αὐτοῦ, Ῥακά» ("But whoever shall say to his brother, Raca"), quoted in the previous lemma. *Ῥακά* is itself an Aramaic/Syriac term of contempt left untranslated in the Gospel; here Theophylact glosses its force.
[^2]: Greek *σημαίνει ἀντὶ τοῦ, σύ*. The idiom *ἀντὶ τοῦ* means "that is to say / is equivalent to"; the point is that «Raca» functions as a scornful, dismissive "you," used in place of a person's name. Rendered to keep the idiom visible rather than smoothing to "means 'you'".
[^3]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at *Τινὸς δὲ τὸ* ("But according to a certain *interpreter*, the *word*—"); the clause is completed in the next lemma, which supplies the Syriac gloss of «Raca» as "contemptible." The neuter article *τὸ* introduces the quoted word «Ῥακά» taken up there.

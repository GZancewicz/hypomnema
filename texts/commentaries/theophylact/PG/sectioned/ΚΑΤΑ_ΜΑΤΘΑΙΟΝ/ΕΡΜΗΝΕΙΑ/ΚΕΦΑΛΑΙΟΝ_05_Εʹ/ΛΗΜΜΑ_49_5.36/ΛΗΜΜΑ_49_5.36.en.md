# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 49 · Matt. 5:36–37

> **…by thy head shalt thou swear, because thou canst not make one hair white or black.** (Matt. 5:36)[^1]

God alone swears by himself, as being subject to no one; but we, not having authority over ourselves, how could we swear by our head? For the possession is another's. For if the head is yours, change, if you can, one hair.

> **But let your word be yea, yea; nay, nay.** (Matt. 5:37a)[^2]

So that you may not say, "And how shall I be believed?" — he says: If you always speak truth, and never swear. For no one is so disbelieved as the one who swears readily.

[^1]: This file opens in mid-quotation: the head of the lemma, «Μήτε ἐν τῇ» ("Neither by thy…"), stands at the close of the preceding lemma (48). The whole verse is «Μήτε ἐν τῇ κεφαλῇ σου ὀμόσῃς, ὅτι οὐ δύνασαι μίαν τρίχα λευκὴν ἢ μέλαιναν ποιῆσαι» (Matt. 5:36). "By" renders the explicit preposition ἐν; the Greek is anarthrous where English wants "one hair." Stray capital "Κ" letters at several line-openings in the OCR (e.g. «Κμίαν», «ὁμόσαιΚμεν») are scanning artifacts and have been dropped.
[^2]: A second lemma embedded within this file. Greek «Ἔστω δὲ ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν, ναὶ, ναί· οὒ, οὔ» — the doubled ναὶ ναί / οὒ οὔ ("yea yea, nay nay") is Theophylact's text; rendered as it stands rather than smoothed to the more familiar "let your yea be yea." The δέ ("but/and") opening the verse is present and roman.

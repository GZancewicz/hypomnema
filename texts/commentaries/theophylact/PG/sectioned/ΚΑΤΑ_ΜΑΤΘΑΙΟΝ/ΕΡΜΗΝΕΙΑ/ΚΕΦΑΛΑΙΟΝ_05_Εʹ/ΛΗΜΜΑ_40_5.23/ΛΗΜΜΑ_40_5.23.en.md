# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 40 · Matt. 5:23-24

> **If, therefore, thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother has something against thee, leave there thy gift before the altar, and go, first be reconciled to thy brother, and then, having come, offer thy gift.** (Matt. 5:23-24)

God thinks little of his own honor,[^1] that we may only love. And in saying, "If thy brother has something against thee," he added nothing else. For whether he has *it* justly or unjustly, be reconciled. And he did not say, "If thou hast *something* against him," but, "If he has something against thee" — be eager to make him friendly toward thee.[^2] And he commands *thee* to leave the gift, that he may lay upon thee the necessity of being reconciled; for thou, wishing thy gift to be offered, wilt be compelled to be reconciled. And at the same time he shows that love is the true sacrifice.

[^1]: Greek *καταφρονεῖ ὁ Θεὸς τῆς οἰκείας τιμῆς* — literally "God despises his own honor," i.e. sets aside / makes light of the honor due to him (the offered gift), so that reconciliation may take precedence. Rendered "thinks little of" to avoid the harsher English overtone of "despise."
[^2]: Greek *σπούδασον αὐτὸν φιλιῶσαί σοι*. The verb *φιλιόω* means "to make friendly, to reconcile"; the sense is "make him a friend to thee again." The phrase reads as the point of the Lord's clause, hence set off after the dash.

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 5:19

> **…of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, shall be called least in the kingdom of the heavens.**[^1]

He calls "least commandments" those which he himself is about to hand down; for not the commandments of the law.[^2] But he names them "least" through humility, so that he may train you also to be modest-minded[^3] regarding *your* teachings. And "he shall be called least in the kingdom" *stands* instead[^4] of *this*: in the resurrection *he shall be* last and cast away into Gehenna. For he shall not enter into the kingdom of the heavens — away with *it*![^5] — but understand "kingdom" *as* the resurrection.

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 27 (Matt. 5:19); the opening words «Ὃς ἐάν οὖν λύσῃ μίαν τῶν» are quoted there.
[^2]: Greek *οὐ γὰρ τὰς νομικάς*, "for not the legal ones," i.e. not the commandments of the Mosaic law but the new commandments Christ is himself about to give (in the Sermon that follows). The supplied "*the commandments*" is italicized.
[^3]: Greek *μετριοφρονεῖν*, "to be moderate-minded / modest / unassuming," from *μέτριος* ("measured, moderate") + *φρονέω* ("to think, be minded"); the same humility (*ταπείνωσις*) Theophylact has just named as the reason Christ himself calls the commandments "least."
[^4]: Greek *ἀντὶ τοῦ*, "instead of *this*," Theophylact's standard formula introducing a paraphrase ("that is to say"); rendered literally here to keep his gloss-marker visible.
[^5]: Greek *ἄπαγε*, literally "away!" (imperative of *ἀπάγω*), an exclamation of rejection — "away with it / far be it / God forbid." The supplied "*it*" is italicized.

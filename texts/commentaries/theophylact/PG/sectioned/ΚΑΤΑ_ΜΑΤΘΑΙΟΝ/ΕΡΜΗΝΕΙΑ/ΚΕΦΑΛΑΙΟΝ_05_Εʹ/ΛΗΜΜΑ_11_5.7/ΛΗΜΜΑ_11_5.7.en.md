# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 5:8–9

> **Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God. (Matt. 5:8)**[^1]

There are many who neither plunder, but rather even show mercy; yet they commit fornication, and are unclean in other ways. Christ therefore commands *them* to be, along with the other virtues, also pure — that is, chaste[^2] — not only in the body, but also in the heart. For apart from sanctification — that is, from chastity — no one shall see the Lord.[^3] For just as the mirror, if it be clean, then receives the reflections;[^4] so also the pure soul receives the sight of God, and the knowledge of the Scriptures.

> **Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God. (Matt. 5:9)**

Not only those who are themselves at peace toward all, but also those who reconcile others at strife. And peacemakers are also those who through teaching turn back the enemies of God. And such are sons of God. For indeed the Only-begotten also came down[^5]

[^1]: The folder name assigns this lemma to 5.7, but the Greek expounds two later beatitudes — «οἱ καθαροὶ τῇ καρδίᾳ» (5:8) and «οἱ εἰρηνοποιοί» (5:9); the header is corrected accordingly. "In heart" renders the dative τῇ καρδίᾳ (of respect).
[^2]: Greek καθαροὺς, ἤτοι σώφρονας: Theophylact glosses "pure" (καθαρός) by "chaste / self-controlled" (σώφρων), and just below by the noun σωφροσύνη ("chastity, temperance"). The word-group spans "chastity" and the broader "temperance / soundness of mind"; rendered "chaste / chastity" to keep the purity-theme, but the wider sense of disciplined self-mastery is present.
[^3]: An allusion to Heb. 12:14 (τὸν ἁγιασμόν, οὗ χωρὶς οὐδεὶς ὄψεται τὸν Κύριον, "the sanctification without which no one shall see the Lord"), here glossed with ἤτοι τῆς σωφροσύνης ("that is, chastity").
[^4]: Greek τὰς ἐμφάσεις, "the appearances / images / reflections" cast in a mirror (κάτοπτρον). The optical metaphor: a clean mirror mirrors images, a clean soul mirrors the sight of God.
[^5]: The sentence breaks off at κατήλ[θεν] ("came down"): "For indeed the Only-begotten also came down…" — completing the thought that peacemakers are called sons of God because the Son himself came down to make peace (between God and men). The remainder is lost at the section boundary.

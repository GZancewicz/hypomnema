# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 5:11–12

> **And *when* they shall say every evil word against you, lying, for my sake. (Matt. 5:11)**[^1]

Not the one who is simply reviled is blessed, but *only* when it is both for Christ's sake and falsely. But if these two things are not present, wretched is that man, as one who scandalizes many.

> **Rejoice and exult, for your reward is great in the heavens. (Matt. 5:12)**[^2]

In the case of the others[^3] he did not say "a great reward"; but here *he does*, showing that to endure reproach is a great and most grievous thing. For many have even hanged themselves. And Job too, having endured the other trials, then most of all was troubled, when his friends reproached him, as one suffering because of sin.

[^1]: The lemma is OCR-corrupt in the source—«εἴπωσι πᾶν πονηρὸν ἐῆμα κσθ´ ὑμῶν ψευδόμενοι, ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ»—securely recoverable as **«καὶ εἴπωσι πᾶν πονηρὸν ῥῆμα καθ᾽ ὑμῶν ψευδόμενοι, ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ»** (*ἐῆμα* → *ῥῆμα*, *κσθ´* → *καθ᾽*). The received text adds *ψευδόμενοι* ("lying / speaking falsely"), which Theophylact's comment expressly turns on ("both for Christ's sake and falsely").
[^2]: OCR «εαίρετε καὶ ἀγαλλιᾶσθε, ὅτι ὁ μισθὸς ὑμῶν πολὺς ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς» = **«Χαίρετε καὶ ἀγαλλιᾶσθε…»**. *ἀγαλλιᾶσθε* is stronger than plain "rejoice"—"exult / be exceedingly glad"—hence the two distinct verbs. Note *πολύς* ("much / great") rather than the common text's *πολύς… ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς*; rendered "great" per his wording.
[^3]: That is, in the other Beatitudes preceding this one. OCR «ὄνειος» in the following clause = *ὄνειδος* ("reproach"); «ἁπηγχόνισαν» = *ἀπηγχόνισαν* ("hanged / strangled themselves"); «φλοι» = *φίλοι* ("friends"); «δ ἁμαρτίας» = *δι᾽ ἁμαρτίας* ("because of sin"). All are secure, low-stakes OCR corrections.

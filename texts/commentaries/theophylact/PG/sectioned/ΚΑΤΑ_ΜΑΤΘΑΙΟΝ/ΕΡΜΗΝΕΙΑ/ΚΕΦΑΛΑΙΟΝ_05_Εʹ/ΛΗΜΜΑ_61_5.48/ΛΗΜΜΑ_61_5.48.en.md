# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 61 · Matt. 5:48

> **You shall be therefore perfect, just as your Father who is in the heavens is perfect. (Matt. 5:48)**[^1]

For the loving of some of men — namely those purportedly[^2] friends — but the hating of the others, is imperfect; but perfect is the loving of all.

[^1]: The comment turns on a single word-family: *τέλειος* ("perfect / complete / brought-to-its-end") and its privative *ἀτελής* ("imperfect / incomplete"). Greek *τέλειος* carries the sense of a thing having reached its proper end or completion, not merely moral flawlessness; rendered "perfect" throughout to keep the pair *τέλειος* / *ἀτελής* visible in the exposition below. The verb *ἔσεσθε* is future indicative ("you shall be / you will be"), which can also carry imperatival force ("be ye"); translated as a plain future per the Greek form. The clause *ὁ ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς* is "the *one* in the heavens" (the article standing for a relative); "who" renders the article and "*is*" is supplied.
[^2]: Greek *τοὺς δῆθεν φίλους*. The particle *δῆθεν* ("supposedly, ostensibly, forsooth, as it is claimed") colors the phrase: these are friends "so-called" or "as one reckons them," marking the friend/enemy division as a merely apparent or conventional one. Rendered "purportedly"; alternatively "those *who are* supposedly friends" or "one's so-called friends." The particle is a present word, hence roman, not italic.

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 52 · Matt. 5:39

> **But I say to you, not to resist the evil one, but whoever shall strike you on the right cheek, turn to him also the other.** (Matt. 5:39)[^1]

By "the evil one" here he means the devil, the one working through the man. Should we not, therefore, resist the devil? Yes — but not through striking back, but through enduring. For fire is not quenched by fire, but by water. But do not suppose that he is speaking about the blow to the cheek alone, but also about every other *blow*, and, simply, about every hardship,[^2]

[^1]: Greek «μὴ ἀντιστῆναι τῷ πονηρῷ». The dative τῷ πονηρῷ is ambiguous — neuter "evil / the evil *thing*" or masculine "the evil one." Theophylact resolves it explicitly in the comment as the devil (τὸν διάβολον), so the lemma is rendered "the evil one" to match his reading. The infinitive ἀντιστῆναι ("to resist") is retained as an infinitive, governed by "I say."
[^2]: The sentence breaks off with a comma, the thought (that the precept covers every kind of injury, not the slap alone) continuing beyond this lemma. ῥαπίσει denotes a slap or open-handed blow.

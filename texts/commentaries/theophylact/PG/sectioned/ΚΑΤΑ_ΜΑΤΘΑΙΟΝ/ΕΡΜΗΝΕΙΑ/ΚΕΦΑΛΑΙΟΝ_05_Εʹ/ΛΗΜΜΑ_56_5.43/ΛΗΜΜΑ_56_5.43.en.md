# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 56 · Matt. 5:43–44

> **You heard that it was said: You shall love your neighbor, and you shall hate your enemy; but I say to you: Love your enemies. (Matt. 5:43–44)**[^1]

He has arrived at the very summit of the virtues; for what is greater than this? But *it is* not impossible. For Moses and Paul loved the Jews *who were* raging against them[^2] beyond their own selves,[^3] and all the saints *loved* their enemies,

[^1]: The clause "and you shall hate your enemy" (*καὶ μισήσεις τὸν ἐχθρόν σου*) is not written in the Law; it represents the popular addition or inference that Christ here corrects. The lemma runs continuously from "was said" through the first clause of the Lord's reply, "Love your enemies" (*Ἀγαπᾶτε τοὺς ἐχθροὺς ὑμῶν*, Matt. 5:44a), which the exposition then expounds.
[^2]: Greek *λυττῶντας* (from *λυττάω*, "to rage, to be furious," properly of the madness of a rabid dog); rendered "raging" to keep the violence of the image.
[^3]: Greek *ὑπὲρ ἑαυτοὺς ἠγάπησαν* — literally "loved *them* above/beyond themselves," i.e., more than their own selves. The allusion is to Moses (Exod. 32:32) and Paul (Rom. 9:3), each of whom wished himself blotted out or accursed for the sake of the very people who turned on him. The sentence breaks off with "and all the saints *loved* their enemies," the verb "loved" being carried over from the preceding clause; the citation resumes in the next lemma.

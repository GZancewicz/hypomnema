# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 41 · Matt. 5:25-26

> **Be well-disposed toward thine adversary quickly, while thou art in the way with him, lest the adversary deliver thee to the judge, and the judge deliver thee to the officer, and thou be cast into prison. Verily I say to thee, thou shalt by no means come out thence, until thou hast paid the last kodrantes.**[^1] (Matt. 5:25-26)

Some think that by "adversary" is meant the devil, and by "way" *this* life; and that the Lord exhorts thus: "As long as thou art in this life, be reconciled with the devil, that he may not have *ground* afterward to convict thee concerning sin, as one having something of his, and that thou then be delivered to punishment, being called to account down to the very last transgressions."[^2] For a kodrantes is two lepta.[^3] But do thou understand that he says this also concerning the adversaries here, exhorting us not to go to law and to be dragged away from divine works. For even if thou wast wronged, he says, do not go off to a tribunal; but in the way be reconciled, lest thou suffer worse through the power of the adversary.

[^1]: Greek *κοδράντην* (Latin *quadrans*), the smallest Roman coin, left transliterated as "kodrantes" rather than converted to "farthing," since Theophylact expressly glosses its value below. *οὐ μὴ ἐξέλθῃς* is an emphatic double negative, "thou shalt by no means come out."
[^2]: Greek *ἄχρι καὶ τῶν ἐσχάτων ἁμαρτημάτων εὐθυνόμενος* — "being examined / held to account even as far as the last sins," parallel to the Gospel's "last kodrantes": the debtor is exacted down to the smallest transgression. *εὐθύνω* is a judicial term, "to call to account, audit."
[^3]: Greek *λεπτά*, the smallest Greek coins (cf. the widow's two mites, Mark 12:42). Theophylact's equation "a kodrantes is two lepta" fixes the point of the parable: even the least debt must be paid in full.

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 5:17

> **Do not think that I came to destroy the law or the prophets; I came not to destroy, but to fulfill. (Matt. 5:17)**[^1]

Since he himself was about to introduce new laws, in order that they might not think that he is opposed to God,[^2] he says — forestalling[^3] the supposition of the many — that: "I did not come to destroy the law, but rather to fulfill *it*." And how did he fulfill *it*? First, in that whatever the prophets said concerning him, he did.[^4] Wherefore also the evangelist frequently says:[^5]

[^1]: Greek *καταλῦσαι* ("to destroy, dissolve, abolish, break up") is deliberately opposed throughout this run to *πληρῶσαι* ("to fulfill, fill up, complete"); the antithesis drives lemmata 21–24.
[^2]: Greek *ἀντίθεός ἐστι* — literally "he is an anti-god," i.e. a rival or adversary of God, one set over against him. Rendered "opposed to God."
[^3]: Greek *προθεραπεύων* ("tending/treating beforehand"), i.e. remedying or conciliating the popular opinion in advance, before it can take hold.
[^4]: Greek *ἐποίησε* ("he did / made / accomplished"): whatever the prophets foretold of him, he brought to pass.
[^5]: Greek *Διὸ καὶ ὁ εὐαγγελιστὴς συχνάκις λέγει·* ("Wherefore also the evangelist frequently says:"), a lead-in to the quotation expounded in the next lemma (22). The source reads OCR *iῶς δὲ* for *Πῶς δὲ* ("And how…") earlier in the paragraph, securely reconstructed.

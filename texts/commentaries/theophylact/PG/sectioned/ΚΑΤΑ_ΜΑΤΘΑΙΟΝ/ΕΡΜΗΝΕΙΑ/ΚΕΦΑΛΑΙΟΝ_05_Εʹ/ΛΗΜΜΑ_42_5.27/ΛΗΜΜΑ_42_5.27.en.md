# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 5:27

> **Ye have heard that it was said: «Thou shalt not commit adultery.»** (Matt. 5:27)

Adultery is one thing, and fornication another: adultery, on the one hand, *is that* with a married woman;[^1] but fornication, on the other hand, *is that* with a woman *who is* loosed.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *ἡ εἰς ὕπανδρον*, literally "the *sin* against a woman *who is* under a husband," i.e. with a married woman. The article *ἡ* stands for "the *act*/*sort of unchastity*"; the noun is supplied.
[^2]: Greek *ἡ εἰς ἀπολελυμένην*. The perfect passive participle *ἀπολελυμένη* ("released, dismissed, set free") is genuinely ambiguous: it can mean a woman *not bound* to any husband (unmarried, free), contrasted with the *ὕπανδρος* just named — or, more technically, a woman who has been *divorced/dismissed*. Given the antithesis "married vs. not-under-a-husband," "loosed" (i.e. unbound, free of a husband) is the more likely sense here; but "divorced" cannot be excluded, so the neutral "loosed" is kept in the body.

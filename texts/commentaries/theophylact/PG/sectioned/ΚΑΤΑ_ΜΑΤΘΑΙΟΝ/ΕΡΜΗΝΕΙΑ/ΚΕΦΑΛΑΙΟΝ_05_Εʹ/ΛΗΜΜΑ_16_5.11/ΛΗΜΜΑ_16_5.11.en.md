# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 5:13

> **Ye are the salt of the earth. (Matt. 5:13)**

The prophets were sent[^1] to a single nation, but you are the salt of the whole earth, through your teaching and your reproofs drawing tight[^2] the slack, so that they may not breed the never-ending worms;[^3] so that you must not cast away the astringency[^4] of your reproofs, even if you be reviled, even if you be persecuted. Wherefore he says:

[^1]: OCR «ἀπευιάλησαν» = *ἀπεστάλησαν* ("were sent"). Also «ἀστὲ» = *ἐστὲ*, «ἐτελευτήτους» = *ἀτελευτήτους*—secure corrections.
[^2]: Greek *στύφοντες*, from *στύφω*—"to contract, draw tight, make astringent." The salt-image runs through the whole passage: salt dries and contracts flesh, staving off rot. Here it is applied to teachers who, by reproof, "tighten up" *τοὺς χαύνους*, "the slack / flabby / dissolute."
[^3]: Greek *τοὺς ἀτελευτήτους σκώληκας*—"the unending worms," an allusion to the worm that dies not (cf. Mark 9:48, Isa. 66:24). Salt, by preventing corruption, keeps flesh from breeding worms.
[^4]: Greek *τὴν στρυφνότητα*—"astringency, sharpness, harshness," cognate with *στύφω* above; the biting quality of true reproof, which the teacher must not lose.

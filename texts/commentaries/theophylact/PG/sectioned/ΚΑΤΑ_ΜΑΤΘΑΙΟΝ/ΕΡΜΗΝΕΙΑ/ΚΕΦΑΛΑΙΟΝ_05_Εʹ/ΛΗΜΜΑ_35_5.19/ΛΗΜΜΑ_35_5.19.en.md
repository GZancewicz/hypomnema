# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 5:22

> **But I say to you, that everyone who is angry with his brother without cause shall be liable to the judgment. (Matt. 5:22)**[^1]

The prophets, being about to prophesy, would say: "Thus says the Lord"; but Christ says, "I say," showing the authoritative *character* of *his* divinity.[^2] For they were servants; but he is the Son, and one having all the things of the Father. And the one who is angry with his brother without cause, that man is condemned; but if someone is angry with good reason and for *the sake of* correction[^3] and according to a spiritual zeal, he shall not be condemned. For Paul also uttered words of anger toward Elymas the magician,[^4] and toward the high priest,[^5] yet surely not without cause, but according to zeal. But we are angry "without cause" then, whenever it is on account of money or glory.[^6]

[^1]: Greek *Ἐγὼ δὲ λέγω ὑμῖν* — "But I say to you" (*δὲ* setting his word over against "it was said to the ancients"). The key word is *εἰκῆ*, rendered "without cause": it can mean "in vain, rashly, heedlessly, to no purpose" as well as "without cause / groundlessly." It marks a textual point: *εἰκῆ* stands in the majority text (so KJV "without a cause") but is omitted by some early witnesses; Theophylact both reads it in the lemma and builds his whole comment on it, so it is retained. OCR *ἀλφῷ* = *ἀδελφῷ* ("brother"); *ἔνοχος ἔσται τῇ κρίσει* = "shall be liable / subject to the judgment."
[^2]: Greek *τὸ ἐξουσιαστικὸν τῆς θεότητος* — the substantival neuter adjective, literally "the authoritative *thing* / that-which-has-authority, of *his* divinity," i.e. the sovereign authority proper to *his* Godhead. The prophets speak as heralds ("Thus says the Lord"); Christ speaks in his own name.
[^3]: Greek *ἐπὶ παιδείᾳ* — "for correction / discipline / instruction / training"; the range covers both chastening and teaching.
[^4]: Cf. Acts 13:8–11, where Paul rebukes Elymas (Bar-Jesus) the magician, calling him "full of all deceit."
[^5]: Cf. Acts 23:3, where Paul says to the high priest Ananias, "God shall strike you, *you* whitewashed wall."
[^6]: The comment breaks off here. Greek *διὰ χρήματα ἢ δόξαν* — "on account of money or glory"; *δόξα* may equally be "reputation / esteem."

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 5:19

> **But whoever shall do and shall teach, this one shall be called great in the kingdom of the heavens.**

First the "shall do," and then the "shall teach"; for how shall I guide another into a way which I have not traveled?[^1] But again, if I myself do but do not teach,[^2] I do not have so great a reward; and often *I incur* even condemnation,[^3] when through envy[^4] or sloth I do not teach.

[^1]: OCR «ὁθηγήσω» = *ὁδηγήσω* ("I shall guide / lead the way," from *ὁδός*, "way, road"); and «Ἀιώκευσα» is corrupt for a first-person aorist of travelling, most likely *διώδευσα* ("I have journeyed through / traversed") — the reading followed here — or *ὥδευσα* ("I have travelled"). Either way the sense is secure: one cannot lead another along a road one has not oneself walked. The whole clause plays on *ὁδηγέω* / *ὁδός* / the verb of journeying.
[^2]: Greek *ἐὰν ποιῶ μὲν ἐγώ, οὐ διδάσκω δέ* (OCR «ἐλν» = *ἐάν*); the contrast is carried by the plain "but" for *δέ*, and *μέν* is left untranslated. Both particles are present in the Greek.
[^3]: OCR «κατάκριαιν» = *κατάκρισιν* ("condemnation, sentence of guilt"). The verb "*I incur*" is supplied for sense and italicized; the Greek has the bare accusative *κατάκρισιν* carried over from "I have" (*οὐκ ἔχω … πολλάκις δὲ καὶ κατάκρισιν*).
[^4]: OCR «πθόνον» = *φθόνον* ("envy, grudging"). The pairing *φθόνον ἢ ὄκνον* ("envy or sloth/shrinking") names the two motives for withholding teaching.

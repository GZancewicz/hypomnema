# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 5:22

> **But whoever shall say, «Thou fool,» shall be liable *to be cast* into the gehenna of fire.**[^1] (Matt. 5:22)

Many say and think this to be heavy and hard, but it is not. For he who deprives his brother of reason and understanding[^2] — the *very things* by which we differ from the beasts — how is he not worthy of gehenna? For he who reviles and insults dissolves love; and love, once dissolved, takes away together *with it* the virtues, just as, being present, it also holds them together. Therefore he who insults tears down all the virtues, in tearing love apart. So that fittingly *he is* worthy of the fire.

[^1]: Greek *Ὃς δ᾽ ἂν εἴπῃ, Μωρέ, ἔνοχος ἔσται εἰς τὴν γέενναν τοῦ πυρός*. The construction *ἔνοχος … εἰς* is elliptical ("liable *unto* the gehenna of fire," i.e. liable *so as to be cast* into it); the supplied "*to be cast*" fills the sense. *Μωρέ* is the vocative "fool" (whence Theophylact's play on depriving a man of *reason* below); *γέεννα* is retained transliterated as "gehenna" rather than rendered "hell."
[^2]: Greek *λόγου καὶ φρονήσεως* ("of reason/speech and of understanding"). The point turns on a pun lost in English: to call a man *μωρός* ("fool") is to deny him *λόγος*, the rational faculty proper to man; *λόγος* here carries both "reason" and "speech." Rendered "reason" to match "fool"; *φρόνησις* = "practical understanding, good sense."

# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 5:21

> **You have heard[?] that it was said to the ancients: "You shall not kill; but whoever shall kill shall be liable to the judgment." (Matt. 5:21)**[^1]

By whom it was said, he does not say. For if he had said, "My Father said to the ancients, but I say to you," he would have seemed to legislate against the Father.[^2] Again, if he had said, "I said to the ancients," it would have become hard to accept.[^3] Indefinitely, therefore, he speaks, saying that—[^4]

[^1]: The opening word is OCR-garbled in the source as «κοιλαατε ὄτι ἐῤῥέθη»; it is securely recoverable as **«Ἠκούσατε ὅτι ἐρρέθη»** ("You have heard that it was said"), the standard opening of Matt. 5:21, which this comment expounds. The reconstruction is marked [?] only to flag the corruption. Greek *Οὐ φονεύσεις* ("You shall not kill," future indicative with imperatival force); *ὃς δ᾽ ἂν φονεύσῃ, ἔνοχος ἔσται τῇ κρίσει* ("but whoever shall kill shall be liable to the judgment").
[^2]: Greek *ἀντινομοθετεῖν τῷ Πατρί* — a single compound verb, "to enact counter-legislation against the Father," i.e. to set up a law opposed to the Father's. The hypothetical *Ὁ μὲν Πατήρ μου εἶπε … ἐγὼ δὲ λέγω* carries a μὲν … δὲ antithesis, here rendered as a plain contrastive (μέν left untranslated, *δὲ* = "but").
[^3]: Greek *δυσπαράδεκτος ἂν ἐγένετο* — "it would have become hard to accept." The subject may be the saying (as translated) or Christ himself ("he would have become hard to receive"); the verb form is neutral. OCR *ἱδοξεν* above = *ἔδοξεν* ("he would have seemed").
[^4]: The sentence breaks off at *ὅτι* ("that"); lemma 33 supplies the words «It was said to the ancients».

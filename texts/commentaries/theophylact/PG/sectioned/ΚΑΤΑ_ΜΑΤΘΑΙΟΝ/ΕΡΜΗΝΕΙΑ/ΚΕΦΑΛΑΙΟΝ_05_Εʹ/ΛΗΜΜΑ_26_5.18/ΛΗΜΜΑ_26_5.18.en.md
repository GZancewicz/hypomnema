# Matthew 5 (Εʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 5:18

> **Until heaven and earth pass away, one iota or one tittle[^1] shall by no means pass away from the law, until all things come to pass.**

He shows[^2] here that the world passes away and is transformed *in figure*.[^3] He says, then, that until the universe holds together,[^4] not even the least letter would pass away from the law.[^5] But others say the iota and the tittle are the ten commandments of the law; and others, the cross. For of the cross,[^6] the iota is the upright wood, and the tittle the transverse.[^7] He says, accordingly, that the things spoken concerning the cross shall be fulfilled.

[^1]: Greek *κεραία*, literally "little horn," the small stroke or serif that distinguishes one Greek (or Hebrew) letter from another; conventionally "tittle." Retained as "tittle" throughout because the whole comment turns on the shape of the strokes.
[^2]: OCR reads «Λ ἴκνυσιν»; securely = *Δείκνυσιν* ("he shows / demonstrates"). The reconstruction is certain and noted only for the record.
[^3]: Greek *μετασχηματίζεται*, "is changed in outward form / re-figured," the same verb Paul uses of the passing "figure" (*σχῆμα*) of this world (cf. 1 Cor. 7:31). The rendering "transformed *in figure*" keeps the *σχῆμα*-root visible; the supplied "*in figure*" is italicized as not separately present in the Greek. The point is not annihilation but a change of form.
[^4]: Greek *ἕως οὗ συνίσταται τὸ πᾶν*, "as long as the universe subsists / holds together" (*συνίσταμαι*, "to stand together, cohere, subsist").
[^5]: OCR «παωέλθοι» = *παρέλθοι*, aorist optative of *παρέρχομαι* ("would pass away / pass by"); the optative gives a potential sense, "would pass away."
[^6]: OCR here is corrupt: «φῆ γὰρ σταυροῦ» stands for something like *τῆς γὰρ σταυροῦ* / *τοῦ γὰρ σταυροῦ* (*σχήματος*), "for of the cross *(in its shape)*." The sense is secure from what follows; the genitive "of the cross" governs the two members named next. Also «ἄλλρι» = *ἄλλοι* ("others") and «αταυροῦ» / «λησὶ» = *σταυροῦ* / *φησί* just below.
[^7]: Greek *τὸ ὀρθὸν ξύλον* … *τὸ πλάγιον*, "the upright wood … the transverse [wood]"; the iota (a single vertical stroke, Ι) is likened to the vertical beam of the cross, and the *κεραία* (a cross-stroke) to the horizontal beam. The English cannot reproduce the visual pun on the letter-shapes; hence this note.

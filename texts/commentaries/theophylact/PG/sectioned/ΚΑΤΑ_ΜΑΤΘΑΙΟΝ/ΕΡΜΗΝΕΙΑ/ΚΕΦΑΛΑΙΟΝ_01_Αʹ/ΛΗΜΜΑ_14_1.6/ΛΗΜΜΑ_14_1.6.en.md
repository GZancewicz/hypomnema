# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 1:6

> **Jesse;[^1] and Jesse begot David the king; and David the king begot Solomon of the *wife* of Uriah.[^2] (Matt. 1:6)**

And again he makes mention of the *wife* of Uriah, so that he might indicate that one ought not to be ashamed of *one's* forefathers, but rather to be eager, through *one's* own virtue, to make even those *forefathers* illustrious; and that all are acceptable to God, even if they be born of a harlot, only if they have virtue.

[^1]: The bare "Jesse" completes the closing clause of the previous lemma ("and Obed begot Jesse," Matt. 1:5); the exposition of this lemma turns on the following clauses (David → Solomon), which belong to Matt. 1:6.
[^2]: Greek *ἐκ τῆς τοῦ Οὐρίου*, literally "of the *[woman]* of Uriah" — the noun *γυναικός* ("wife") is elided, carried by the feminine article *τῆς* alone; hence the supplied italic *wife*. Theophylact does not name Bathsheba, following Matthew's own reticence, which becomes the point of the comment.

# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 1:17

> **All the generations, therefore, from Abraham until David *are* fourteen generations; and from David until the removal to Babylon fourteen generations; and from the removal to Babylon until Christ fourteen generations. (Matt. 1:17)**[^1]

Into three states[^2] he divided the generations, that he might show the Jews that, both *when* being ruled by judges — as those up to David — and *when* ruled by kings — as those up to the removal — and *when* ruled by priests — as those up to Christ — they were nevertheless profited nothing toward virtue; and that they had need of the true one, both judge and king and priest, who is Christ. For, the rulers having failed, Christ came according to the prophecy of Jacob.[^3]

But how are there, from the removal to Babylon until Christ, fourteen generations, where thirteen persons are found? For if, on the one hand, a woman were genealogized, we would have *cause* to rank Mary also *in the list* and to fill out the number; but as it is, on the other hand, a woman is not genealogized. How, *then*, is this resolved? Some say that he set the removal itself also as holding the rank of a person.[^4]

[^1]: Greek *μετοικεσία Βαβυλῶνος*, "the removal / migration / deportation to Babylon" (the Babylonian exile); rendered "removal to Babylon." *δεκατέσσαρες* = "fourteen"; the genitive «τῆς μετοικεσίας» is "of the removal," i.e. the exile-event marks the boundary. Minor OCR corruptions in the lemma (e.g. «γενκαὶ δεναπέσσαρες», «μετοικεσίᾳς») are securely restored to *γενεαὶ δεκατέσσαρες* / *μετοικεσίας*.
[^2]: Greek *καταστάσεις* — "states, conditions, constitutions, regimes." The word carries a political nuance here (three successive forms of governance: judges, kings, priests); rendered "states" to keep it neutral.
[^3]: The prophecy of Jacob at Gen. 49:10 ("The ruler shall not fail from Judah…"), read as fulfilled when the native rulers gave out and Christ came.
[^4]: The puzzle: the third set yields only thirteen named persons, not fourteen. Theophylact reports (without fully endorsing) the solution that the *removal to Babylon* itself is counted as though it were one of the "persons" in the list, making up the fourteen. His own preferred grounds — that a woman (Mary) is not counted — he has just excluded. Greek: «φασί τινες ὅτι καὶ τὴν μετοικεσίαν ὡς προσώπου τάξιν ἔχουσαν ἔθηκεν», *πρόσωπον* meaning both "person" and "personage/figure."

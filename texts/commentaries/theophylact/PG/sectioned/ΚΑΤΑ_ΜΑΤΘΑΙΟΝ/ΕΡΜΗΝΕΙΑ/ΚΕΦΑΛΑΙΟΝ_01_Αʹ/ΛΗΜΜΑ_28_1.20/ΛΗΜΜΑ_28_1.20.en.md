# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 1:20

> **But while he was considering these things, behold, an angel of *the* Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying: (Matt. 1:20)**

When the just man was at a loss,[^1] then the angel stood by, teaching him what he should do. And he appears to him in a dream, because he was exceedingly faithful; for to the shepherds the angel spoke openly, as to rustics, but to this man, as to one just and faithful, in sleep. And how was he not going to believe, when the angel was teaching him about the things which he himself had in his mind, and about the things which he had told out to no one? For when he had considered *them*, but had not spoken *them* out, the angel stands by him; reasonably, therefore, he believed[^2] that it is from God — for it belongs to God to know the unspeakable things.[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἠπόρει* (OCR *ἠπάοει*), securely = "was at a loss, was perplexed, was in doubt *what to do*"; the reconstruction is certain from the context and the following *διδάσκων … τί ποιήσοι* ("teaching him what he should do").
[^2]: Greek OCR reads *Ἀπίστευσεν*; the sense and the preceding *εἰκότως οὖν* ("reasonably, therefore") require *ἐπίστευσεν* ("he believed"), not the alpha-privative *ἠπίστησεν* ("he disbelieved"). The whole train of thought — the angel revealing Joseph's unspoken deliberations — is adduced precisely to explain his belief; rendered "he believed."
[^3]: Greek *τὰ ἀπόρρητα*, "the unspeakable / secret / unutterable things"; here Joseph's undisclosed thoughts, knowledge of which Theophylact marks as proper to God alone.

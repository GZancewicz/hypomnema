# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 36 · Matt. 1:22

> **Now this whole *thing* came to pass, that there might be fulfilled that which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying: (Matt. 1:22)**[^1]

Do not think that these things seemed good to God recently, but from above and from the beginning. For thou knowest the prophets, O Joseph, as one learned in the law. And take to heart that which was spoken by the Lord: he did not say "that which was spoken by Isaiah," but "by the Lord." For not a man spoke, but God through the mouth of the man; so that the oracle *is* trustworthy.[^2] [Since,] then, the Hebrews *say* that in the prophet it does not stand "a virgin," but "a young woman"[^3] — to whom it must be said that "young woman" and "virgin" are the same thing in the Scripture; for it names the undefiled *woman* a "young woman." Then, if a virgin did not give birth, how was it going to be a sign and a marvel? For hear Isaiah saying:[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Τοῦτο δὲ ὅλον γέγονεν, ἵνα πληρωθῇ τὸ ῥηθὲν ὑπὸ τοῦ Κυρίου διὰ τοῦ προφήτου, λέγοντος». The particle *δὲ* is rendered "now."
[^2]: Greek *ὥστε ἀξιόπιστος ὁ χρη-*. The OCR here collapses two clauses: the noun breaks off (securely *χρησμός*, "oracle") and runs directly into «Ἑβραῖοι» with intervening words lost — the text should read approximately *ὥστε ἀξιόπιστος ὁ χρησμός. Ἐπειδὴ δὲ λέγουσιν οἱ Ἑβραῖοι…* ("…so that the oracle is trustworthy. But since the Hebrews say…"). The bracketed "[Since,] then, … *say*" supplies the reconstructed connective and verb; the sense is secure.
[^3]: The dispute is over Isa. 7:14: Greek *παρθένος* ("virgin," the Septuagint reading) versus *νεᾶνις* ("young woman," the reading the Hebrews urge, corresponding to Heb. *ʿalmah*). Both Greek words are kept untranslated-in-force ("virgin" / "young woman") because the whole argument is about the two terms as words.
[^4]: The lemma ends by introducing the Isaiah citation, which is quoted in the following lemmata (1:22–23; Isa. 7:14). *ἀδιάφθορον* ("undefiled, uncorrupted") is Theophylact's gloss on *νεᾶνις*.

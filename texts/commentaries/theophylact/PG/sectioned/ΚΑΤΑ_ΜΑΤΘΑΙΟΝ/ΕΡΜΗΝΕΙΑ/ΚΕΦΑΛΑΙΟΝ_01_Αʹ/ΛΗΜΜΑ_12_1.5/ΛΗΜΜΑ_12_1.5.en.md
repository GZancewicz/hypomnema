# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 1:4–5

> **… *Naasson begat* Salmon; and Salmon begat Booz, of Rahab. (Matt. 1:5)**[^1]

Some suppose Rahab to be that Raab, the harlot,[^2] who received the spies of Joshua the *son* of Nun[^3] and brought them safely through, and was herself also brought safely through. And he made mention of her, so that he might show that just as she was a harlot, so also *was* the congregation of the nations;[^4] for they played the harlot in their pursuits. But having received the spies of Jesus — that is, the apostles — and having been persuaded by the words of those *men*, all those from among the nations were saved.

[^1]: The lemma opens mid-sentence: the subject of "begat Salmon" is *Naasson*, carried over from the previous lemma (supplied in italics). Greek: «Σαλμών· Σαλμὼν δὲ ἐγέννησε τὸν Βοὸζ ἐκ τῆς Ῥαχάβ». Names in conventional English biblical forms (Salmon, Booz, Rahab).
[^2]: Theophylact preserves a spelling distinction the Greek marks: Matthew writes **Ῥαχάβ** (here "Rahab"), whereas the harlot of Jericho is **Ῥαάβ** in the Septuagint (Josh. 2; here "Raab"). He reports that "some suppose" (*νομίζουσί τινες*) the two to be the same woman, without fully committing. The two forms are kept distinct in translation to show the identification he is reporting rather than asserting.
[^3]: Greek *τοῦ Ἰησοῦ τοῦ Ναυῆ* = Joshua son of Nun, but in Greek his name is literally *Ἰησοῦς* ("Jesus"). This grounds the typology that follows: "the spies of Jesus" (*τοὺς τοῦ Ἰησοῦ κατασκόπους*) — glossed by Theophylact as the apostles — trades on the single Greek name shared by Joshua and Jesus Christ, a wordplay lost in English where the two are distinguished. "Joshua" is used for the son of Nun and "Jesus" where the type shifts to Christ, following the Greek's own single word.
[^4]: Greek *ἡ τῶν ἐθνῶν συναγωγή* — "the gathering / congregation / synagogue of the nations (Gentiles)." Rendered "congregation of the nations." Below, *πάντες οἱ ἐξ ἐθνῶν* = "all those from among the nations (Gentiles)."

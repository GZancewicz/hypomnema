# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 1:20

> **Do not be afraid to take *her*.[^1] (Matt. 1:20)**

He shows from this that he was afraid to have her, lest somehow he give offense to God, as having an adulteress. Or also otherwise: "do not be afraid" — that is, you, on the one hand, are afraid to touch her, as *one who has* conceived from *the* Holy Spirit; but do not be afraid to take *her*, that is, to hold *her* within;[^2] for in *his* mind and *his* thought he had already released her.

[^1]: Greek *παραλαβεῖν*, "to take to *oneself*, to take *along*, to receive *into one's house/keeping*." Theophylact himself glosses it below as *ἔνδον κατέχειν* ("to hold within"); "take *her*" keeps the verb's sense of receiving Mary into his home as wife.
[^2]: Greek *ἔνδον κατέχειν*, "to keep / hold within *doors*," i.e. to retain her in his household rather than send her away. The point of the second reading: Joseph's fear is not of blame but of reverent hesitation to touch one made holy by the Spirit, so the angel bids him keep her.

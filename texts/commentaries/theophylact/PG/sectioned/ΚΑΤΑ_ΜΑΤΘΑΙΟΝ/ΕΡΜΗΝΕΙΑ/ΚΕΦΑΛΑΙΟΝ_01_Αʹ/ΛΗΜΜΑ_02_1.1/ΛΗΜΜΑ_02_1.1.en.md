# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 1:1

> **Book of *the* generation of Jesus.**[^1] (Matt. 1:1)

The name "Jesus" is not Greek, but Hebrew; and it is interpreted "Savior." For "Iao" is what salvation is called among the Hebrews,[^2]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence broken off at the end of Lemma 1 (which ended at *ἀλλά*, "but,"): it now supplies the scriptural words «Βίβλος γενέσεως» ("Book of generation") together with the following genitive Ἰησοῦ ("of Jesus"). The phrase is anarthrous; the supplied "*the*" is italicized, as in Lemma 1.
[^2]: Greek *Ἰαὼ γὰρ ἡ σωτηρία παρὰ τοῖς Ἑβραίοις λέγεται* ("for 'Iao' is what salvation is called among the Hebrews"). Theophylact is giving the Hebrew etymology of the name Ἰησοῦς (Yeshua/Yehoshua), which combines the divine-name element with the root for "save/salvation"; hence his gloss "Savior" (*Σωτήρ*). The form *Ἰαὼ* (Iao) is a Greek transcription associated with the Hebrew divine name and the salvation-root; the OCR is legible here. The lemma-file breaks off mid-sentence after this clause (ending with a comma), so the etymological explanation is left incomplete in the source.

# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 3 · Matt. 1:1

> **Of Christ.** (Matt. 1:1)

"Christs"[^1] were the kings and the priests[^2] called. For they were anointed with the holy oil, the one gushing up from the horn placed upon the head.[^3] The Lord, therefore, is called "Christ," both as king — for he reigned against sin — and as priest — for he brought himself forward as a sacrifice on our behalf; and he himself also was anointed, properly speaking, with the true oil, the Holy Spirit. For who else had the Spirit as the Lord did? For in the saints the grace of the Holy Spirit was working; but in Christ the grace of the Spirit was not working, but Christ, together with the Spirit who is of one essence *with him*,[^4] was working the miracles.

[^1]: Greek *Χριστοί* ("anointed ones"), the plural of *Χριστός*; the same word as the title "Christ" in the lemma. The whole comment turns on this: *Χριστός* means literally "anointed," and the argument shows in what senses the Lord is "the Anointed." Rendered "Christs" to keep the wordplay visible.
[^2]: The OCR reads *μκρεῖς*, securely = *ἱερεῖς* ("priests"); the reconstruction is certain from the sense and from the parallel with "priest" (*ἱερεύς*) just below.
[^3]: Greek *τῷ ἁγίῳ ἐλαίῳ τῷ ἀπὸ τοῦ κέρατος τοῦ ἐπιτιθεμένου τῇ κεφαλῇ ἀναβλύζοντι*. The syntax is compressed: the oil "gushes up" (*ἀναβλύζοντι*) from the horn, and the horn is "placed upon the head" (*ἐπιτιθεμένου τῇ κεφαλῇ*) — i.e. the anointing-horn from which oil was poured on the head. An alternative construal would take "placed upon the head" with the oil rather than the horn, but the genitive *τοῦ ἐπιτιθεμένου* agrees with "horn." (OCR *ἀλαίῳ* = *ἐλαίῳ*, "oil.")
[^4]: Greek *ὁμοουσίῳ ὄντι* — the technical Nicene term *ὁμοούσιος* ("consubstantial / of one essence / of the same substance"). Theophylact's point is that in the saints the *grace* of the Spirit merely operated, whereas in Christ the Spirit is not an external grace but works with him as one consubstantial with him.

# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 1:3

> **And Judah begat Phares and Zara, of Tamar. (Matt. 1:3)**

Judah had Tamar as a daughter-in-law for Er, one of his sons. But this one having died childless, he joined her to Onan,[^1] this one also being his son; and this one too having been reaped from life[^2] because of his wickedness, Judah no longer joined her to anyone. But she, desiring to bear children from the seed of Abraham, put off the *garments* of widowhood;[^3] and, having disguised herself as a harlot, mingled with her father-in-law, and conceived from him two twin children. And in the bearing of these, the first of the children put forth his hand from the womb, as though about to be born first; and the midwife with a scarlet thread quickly sealed the hand of the child that had come out, so that the one born first might be recognized. But the child drew back the hand inside the womb, and the other child was born first, and then thus that one who had put forth the hand. Therefore the one born first was called Phares, which is "a cutting-through";[^4] for he cut through the natural order; but the one who drew back the hand was called Zara.

And this history signifies a certain mystery. For just as Zara first showed the hand, but was drawn back again, so also the way of life according to Christ[^5] appeared in the saints before the law and circumcision — for all these were justified not from the observances and commandments of the law, but from living according to the evangelical way of life; and consider Abraham, who left father and house for the sake of God, and renounced even nature; consider Job, Melchizedek — but when the law came, such a way of life was drawn back. And just as there, Phares having been born, later Zara again came out, so, the law having been given, later the evangelical way of life shone forth, the one sealed with the scarlet thread — that is, with the blood of Christ.

For on this account, indeed, the Evangelist also made mention of these two children, since their birth reveals something mysterious. And otherwise too, he makes mention of Tamar — although she seems not praiseworthy, because of her mingling with the father-in-law — so that he might show that Christ, having accepted all things for our sake, accepted also to have such forebears; rather, so that, even through this very thing, namely being born from them,[^6] he might sanctify them. For he came not to call the righteous, but sinners.[^7]

[^1]: Greek OCR *τῷ Ἀθνᾷ*; securely = **Αὐνάν** (Onan), Judah's second son (Gen. 38:8–10). Rendered by the conventional English biblical form "Onan." Likewise "Er" for *Ἢρ* (Gen. 38:6–7).
[^2]: Greek *ἐκθερισθέντος … τῆς ζωῆς*, literally "having been reaped/harvested away from life" — a metaphor for death (the verb *ἐκθερίζω*, "to reap out"); Onan too was cut off. Rendered literally to keep the agricultural image.
[^3]: Greek *ἀπέθετο … τὰ τῆς χηρείας*, "put off the things of widowhood," i.e. her widow's garb (cf. Gen. 38:14). "*garments*" is supplied for the bare neuter article *τά* ("the things").
[^4]: Greek *διακοπή* ("a cutting-through, cleaving, breach"), the etymology Theophylact gives for Φαρές (Phares/Perez): *διέκοψε γὰρ τὴν φυσικὴν τάξιν*, "for he cut through the natural order" (by being born first though his hand emerged second). Zara (Ζαρά) is left unglossed here.
[^5]: Greek *ἡ κατὰ Χριστὸν πολιτεία* — "the way of life / manner of citizenship / commonwealth according to Christ"; *πολιτεία* recurs throughout (also *ἡ εὐαγγελικὴ πολιτεία*, "the evangelical way of life"). Rendered consistently "way of life" to keep the term visible; it carries the sense of a whole ordered mode of living, not merely private conduct.
[^6]: Greek OCR *τοῦ κπ´ αὐτῶν γεννηθῆναι*; the corrupt *κπ´* stands for a preposition governing the genitive — securely *ἐξ* or *ἀπ´ αὐτῶν* ("from them") — giving "the being born from them." The sense is secure; the exact preposition is not recoverable from the OCR.
[^7]: Cf. Matt. 9:13 / Mark 2:17. Theophylact quotes the saying loosely (*οὐ γὰρ ἦλθε καλέσαι δικαίους, ἀλλὰ ἁμαρτωλούς*), without «εἰς μετάνοιαν» ("to repentance"); rendered as he gives it.

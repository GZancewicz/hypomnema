# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 38 · Matt. 1:23

> **Behold, the Virgin,**[^1] and what follows.

So that, if a virgin were not going to give birth, it would not be a sign. Therefore the Hebrews, being willfully malicious,[^2] falsify the Scripture, and instead of "virgin" they put "young woman"; but whether "young woman" stands there or "virgin," in any case she who is going to give birth must be understood to be a virgin, so that indeed this might be a portent.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Ἰδοὺ ἡ Παρθένος» — the opening of the prophecy of Isa. 7:14 as quoted at Matt. 1:23. The folder metadata assigns this to 1:22, but the words expounded are those of v. 23. The comment turns on «παρθένος» ("virgin") as the load-bearing word.
[^2]: Greek *ἐθελοκακοῦντες*, from *ἐθελοκακέω* — "to be bad on purpose, to act with deliberate ill-will / play false willingly." The point is that the falsification is not error but chosen malice.
[^3]: Greek OCR *τεοάστιον*, securely = *τεράστιον* ("portent, marvel, prodigy"). The reasoning: only a *virgin's* giving birth constitutes the "sign" (*σημεῖον*) Isaiah announced; a mere young woman bearing a child is no marvel at all.

# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 1:1

> **Book of *the* generation.**[^1]

For what reason did he not say "a vision" or "a word," as the prophets did? For those men would write at the outset thus: "A vision which Isaiah saw,"[^2] and "The word that came to Isaiah."[^3] For what reason did he not? — do you seek to learn? Because the prophets were conversing with people hard-hearted and disobedient, and for this reason they said that it is a divine vision and that it is a word from God, so that the people might be afraid and might not despise the things spoken. But Matthew had to do with people who had believed, and were well-disposed and obedient; and for this reason he said beforehand nothing of the sort, such as the prophets did.

But I have also something else[^4] to say: that the prophets beheld intellectually[^5] the things which they beheld, picturing these through the Holy Spirit — wherefore they also called these things "visions"; but Matthew did not behold Christ intellectually and picture him, but was present with him perceptibly and heard him perceptibly, seeing him in the flesh. Therefore, accordingly, he did not say, "A vision which I saw" — that is, an appearance to the imagination — but,[^6]

[^1]: The lemma is OCR-garbled in the source as «ΧίΚλρς τενέαεως»; it is securely recoverable as **«Βίβλος γενέσεως»** ("Book of generation/origin"), the opening words of Matt. 1:1 — which is precisely the phrase this comment expounds (why Matthew opens with "book," not "vision" or "word"). Greek *γένεσις* spans "origin, coming-into-being, birth, generation, genealogy"; rendered "generation" for the plain sense, without importing a fixed English-Bible wording. The phrase is anarthrous (no article); the supplied "*the*" is italicized.

[^2]: Greek *ὅρασις ἣν εἶδεν Ἡσαΐας* — the opening formula of the prophet Isaiah (cf. Isa. 1:1).

[^3]: Greek *ὁ λόγος ὁ γενόμενος πρὸς Ἰσαΐαν* ("the word that came **to Isaiah**"). The "word that came to…" opening-formula normally names Jeremiah or Hosea, not Isaiah; this second "Isaiah" is likely an OCR error (or a slip in Theophylact's text) for another prophet. Translated as it stands, per the rule of following the text on the page.

[^4]: Greek OCR *Κτε.ρόν*, securely = *ἕτερόν* ("something else / other"); the reconstruction is certain and low-stakes, noted only for consistency with the other flagged OCR corruptions.

[^5]: Theophylact turns on a fixed antithesis of two adverbs: *νοητῶς* ("intellectually / with the mind / noetically") and, just below, *αἰσθητῶς* ("perceptibly / by the senses / sensibly"). It is the standard intelligible-vs-sensible contrast: the prophets saw only in the mind, whereas Matthew was with Christ bodily. Rendered "intellectually" / "perceptibly" to keep the pair visible; the related noun *φαντασία* ("appearance to the imagination," below) belongs to the same complex.

[^6]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at *ἀλλά* ("but,"); the sentence is completed in the next lemma, which supplies the quoted words «Βίβλος γενέσεως».

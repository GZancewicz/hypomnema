# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 1:19

> **But Joseph her husband, being just, and not willing to make her a public example,[^1] wished to release her secretly. (Matt. 1:19)**

How was Joseph just, when — the law commanding that the adulteress be made a public example, that is, be exposed and punished — he himself was about to cover over the sin and to transgress the law? But this is resolved: first, on the one hand,[^2] because for this very thing he was just. For he did not wish to be harsh, but he was acting humanely out of much kindness, showing himself above the law, and already living beyond the legal commandments. Then, on the other hand, because he himself also knew that she had conceived from *the* Holy Spirit, and for this reason he was not willing to make a public example of and to ill-treat her who had conceived from the Holy Spirit, and not from an adulterer. [^1]: Greek *παραδειγματίσαι* (from *παραδειγματίζω*), "to hold up as a public example, to expose to open disgrace." Theophylact's whole comment turns on this verb, glossing it below as *φανεροῦσθαι καὶ κολάζεσθαι* ("to be exposed and punished"); "make a public example" keeps that legal-disgrace force rather than a softer "put to shame."
[^2]: Theophylact's *πρῶτον μέν … Ἔπειτα* ("first, on the one hand … then [second]") frames a two-part resolution; the *μέν* is answered by the second clause's sense rather than by a matching *δέ*. Both correlatives are rendered to keep the structure visible.
[^3]: The lemma breaks off at *ὅρα γὰρ τί φησιν·* ("for see what he says:"), which introduces the re-quoted words «Εὑρέθη ἐν γαστρὶ ἔχουσα» taken up in the next lemma.

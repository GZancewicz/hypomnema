# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 1:2

> **Abraham begat Isaac.**[^1] (Matt. 1:2)

From Abraham he begins the genealogy, because he himself was father of the Hebrews, and because he himself first received the promises, that in his seed all the nations would be blessed.[^2] Fittingly, therefore, from this *one* he traces the genealogy of Christ; for the Christ is the seed of Abraham, in whom we all were blessed — we who are from the nations, who were formerly accursed. And Abraham is interpreted "father of nations";[^3] and Isaac, "joy, laughter";[^4] but the evangelist does not make mention of the illegitimate children of Abraham — such as Ishmael and the rest — because the Jews were not descended from these, but from Isaac.

[^1]: The folder metadata assigns this lemma to Matt. 1:1, but the Greek quotes Matt. 1:2 («Ἀβραὰμ ἐγέννησε τὸν Ἰσαάκ»); the header verse is corrected accordingly. (OCR of the lemma reads *Ἀραὰμ* for *Ἀβραάμ*.)
[^2]: Cf. Gen. 22:18; the "seed" is read christologically, as the next sentence makes explicit.
[^3]: Greek *πατὴρ ἐθνῶν* ("father of nations / of gentiles"), the traditional etymology of the Hebrew name Abraham (*av-hamon-goyim*, "father of a multitude of nations," Gen. 17:5). The same word *ἔθνη* ("nations") renders "the gentiles" just above, tying the etymology to Theophylact's point about the nations being blessed in Abraham's seed.
[^4]: Greek *χαρά, γέλως* ("joy, laughter"), the etymology of the Hebrew name Isaac (*yiṣḥaq*, "he laughs / laughter"; cf. Gen. 17:17, 21:6).

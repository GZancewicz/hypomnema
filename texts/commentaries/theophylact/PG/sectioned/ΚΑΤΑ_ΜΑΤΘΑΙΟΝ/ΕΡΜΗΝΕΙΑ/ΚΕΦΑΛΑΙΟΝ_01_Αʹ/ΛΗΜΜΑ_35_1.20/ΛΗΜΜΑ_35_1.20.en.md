# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 35 · Matt. 1:21

> **For he himself shall save his people from their sins. (Matt. 1:21)**[^1]

Here he interprets what "Jesus" signifies — that *it means* "Saviour." For he himself, he says, *shall save*[^2] his people; not only the Jewish, but also the Gentile *people*, that which was eager to believe and to become his people. But from what shall he save? Is it, then, from wars?[^3] No, but from their sins; whence *it is* clear that the one who is to be born is God, for it belongs to God alone to forgive sins.

[^1]: Greek «Αὐτὸς γὰρ σώσει τὸν λαὸν αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν αὐτῶν» (OCR «ἀπὸ εν ἀαμρτιῶν αὐτων»). The name *Ἰησοῦς* (Jesus) is here connected with *σωτήρ* (Saviour), the etymology the whole comment turns on.
[^2]: The verb is OCR-garbled as «μπει»; securely *σώσει* ("shall save"), resumed from the lemma. Supplied in italics as reconstructed.
[^3]: Greek *Ἆρα ἀπὸ πολέμων;* (OCR «ἀπλ ολεμων»). The interrogative particle *ἆρα* introduces the question; rendered "then."

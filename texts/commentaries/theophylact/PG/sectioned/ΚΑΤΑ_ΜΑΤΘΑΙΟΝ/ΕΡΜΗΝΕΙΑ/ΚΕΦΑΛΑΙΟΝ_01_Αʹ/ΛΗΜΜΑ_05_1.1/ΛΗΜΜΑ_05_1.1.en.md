# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 1:1

> **Of *the* son of Abraham.** (Matt. 1:1)

For what reason did he place David before Abraham? Because among the Hebrews David was rather more spoken of,[^1] both as being later than Abraham, and as having become more illustrious from the kingship; for among the kings, this one first was well-pleasing to God; and he received a promise from God, that from his seed the Christ would arise. Whence also all called the Christ "son of David"; for indeed David in truth preserved a type[^2] of Christ. For just as that *one* reigned after Saul, *who had been* cast off by God and hated, so also the Christ, after Adam was deprived of the kingship and of the rule which he had over all things — both the animals and the demons — came, having been made flesh,[^3] and reigned over us.

[^1]: Greek *ἐφημίζετο* (from *φημίζω*), "was talked of, was celebrated, had *his* name on *people's* lips." Rendered "was spoken of" to keep the plain sense of report/renown.
[^2]: Greek *τύπον* ("type, figure, foreshadowing") — the standard typological term: David is a prefiguration of Christ, as the following comparison (David after Saul / Christ after Adam) spells out.
[^3]: Greek *σαρκωθείς* ("having been enfleshed / made flesh / incarnate"), the aorist passive participle of *σαρκόω*; the technical term for the Incarnation. The final clause *ἐβασίλευσεν ἡμῶν* takes a genitive object, "reigned over us."

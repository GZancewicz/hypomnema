# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 1:23

> **And she shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which is, being interpreted, God *is* with us.**[^1]

The Hebrews say: How then was he not called Emmanuel, but Jesus Christ? It must be said, therefore, that he did not say "Thou shalt call," but "They shall call,"[^2] that is, the very deeds themselves shall show him *to be* God[^3] and dwelling among us. For the divine Scripture assigns the names from the works, as *in* the *phrase*, "Call his name, Quickly-despoil";[^4] and yet, where was anyone ever called by such a name? But since, at the same time as the Lord's being born, the error[^5] was despoiled and taken captive, thus he is said to have been named, having obtained his appellation from the deed.

[^1]: Greek «Καὶ τέξεται υἱὸν, καὶ καλέσουσι τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Ἐμμανουήλ, ὅ ἐστι μεθερμηνευόμενον, Μεθ᾽ ἡμῶν ὁ Θεός» — the continuation of the Isa. 7:14 citation at Matt. 1:23. The supplied "*is*" renders the copula-less «Μεθ᾽ ἡμῶν ὁ Θεός».
[^2]: The whole comment turns on the plural «καλέσουσι» ("they shall call") over against a singular «καλέσεις» ("thou shalt call"): the naming is done impersonally by the events, not by a single namer. Kept literal to preserve the point.
[^3]: Greek OCR *Θελνὄντα*, securely = *Θεὸν ὄντα* ("being God"). The reconstruction is certain: the events show him "being God and dwelling among us," which unpacks *Ἐμμανουήλ* = "God with us."
[^4]: Greek «Κάλεσον τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ, ταχέως σκύλευσον» — an allusion to the symbolic name *Maher-shalal-hash-baz* of Isa. 8:1–3 ("swift the spoil, speedy the prey"), which Theophylact renders by the imperatives "quickly despoil / plunder." Translated from his Greek, not conformed to any English Bible's transliteration.
[^5]: Greek *ἡ πλάνη* — "the error / delusion / straying," here the reign of idolatrous deceit that was "despoiled and led captive" (*ἐσκυλεύθη καὶ ᾐχμαλωτίσθη*) at the Nativity, tying back to the "despoil" of the Isaiah name.

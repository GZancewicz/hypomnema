# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 22 · Matt. 1:16

> **…*Matthan begat* Jacob; and Jacob begat Joseph the husband of Mary, from whom was born Jesus, the one called Christ. (Matt. 1:16)**[^1]

For what reason does he trace the genealogy of Joseph, and not that of the Theotokos?[^2] For what participation[^3] had Joseph with that seedless[^4] birth? For was Joseph truly the father of Christ, that he should trace the genealogy of Christ from Joseph? Hear, then, that Joseph truly has no participation with the birth of Christ. And on this account it were fitting that the Theotokos be genealogized;[^5] but since it was a law that women not be genealogized, on this account he did not trace the genealogy of the Virgin. Yet, having traced the genealogy of Joseph, he traced hers also.

For there was a law not to take wives from another tribe, nor from another lineage — that is, kinship — but from the same, both tribe and lineage; so that, since such was the law, it is manifest that Joseph too, being genealogized, brings in together the genealogy of the Theotokos. For assuredly the Theotokos was of the same tribe and of the same lineage. For if she were not, how would she have been betrothed to him? So that the evangelist both kept the law which commands that women not be genealogized, and none the less traced the genealogy of the Theotokos, having traced the genealogy of Joseph.

And he named him "husband of Mary," according to the common custom; for indeed the betrothed man we have a custom of calling "husband" of the betrothed woman, even if marriage has not yet gone forward.

[^1]: The lemma's head is OCR-garbled as «Ἰακώβι Ἰακώβ»; it is securely «…τὸν Ἰακώβ· Ἰακὼβ δὲ ἐγέννησε τὸν Ἰωσὴφ τὸν ἄνδρα Μαρίας, ἐξ ἧς ἐγεννήθη Ἰησοῦς ὁ λεγόμενος Χριστός». The opening "Jacob" completes the verb left dangling at the end of the previous lemma («Ματθὰν δὲ ἐγέννησε τὸν», "and Matthan begat —"); the supplied "*Matthan begat*" is italicized. «ὁ λεγόμενος Χριστός» is rendered "the *one* called Christ" (i.e. "who is called Christ"), *ὁ λεγόμενος* being a substantival participle.
[^2]: Greek *Θεοτόκος*, "God-bearer" / "Mother of God"; retained throughout as the transliterated title **Theotokos**, since it is a fixed theological term with no single-word English equivalent.
[^3]: Greek *μετουσία* — "participation, partaking, share, communion." Theophylact repeats it just below; rendered "participation" both times to keep the link visible.
[^4]: Greek *ἄσπορος* ("without seed, sown by no seed"), i.e. conceived without human seed; a standard epithet of the virginal conception.
[^5]: Throughout this comment the single verb *γενεαλογεῖν* (active "to trace the genealogy of," passive *γενεαλογεῖσθαι* "to be genealogized / have one's genealogy traced") recurs insistently. English has no equally compact verb, so the rendering alternates between "trace the genealogy of" and the technical "genealogize"; both stand for the one Greek verb.

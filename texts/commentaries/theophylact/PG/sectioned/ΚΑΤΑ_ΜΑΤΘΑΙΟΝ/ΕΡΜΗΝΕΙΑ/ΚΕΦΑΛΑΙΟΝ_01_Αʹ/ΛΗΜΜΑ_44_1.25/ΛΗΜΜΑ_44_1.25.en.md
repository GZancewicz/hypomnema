# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 1:25

> **"I am with you until the consummation of the age." (Matt. 28:20)**[^1]

But after the consummation, then, will he not be *with them*? And how *could that be*? For then *he will be* all the more.[^2] So therefore also here you will understand the "until she brought forth"[^3] as *meaning*, instead, that neither before the birth nor after the birth did he know her. For how would he have touched that holy *woman*, after he had, most of all, come to know that ineffable birth?[^4]

[^1]: Greek «Μεθ᾽ ὑμῶν εἰμι ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος», the closing words of the Gospel (Matt. 28:20). Theophylact cites it as a second proof (alongside the raven of the previous lemma) that Scripture's "until" (*ἕως*) does not imply that the contrary holds afterward. The bracketed reference is the source of the quotation, not of the lemma, which continues to expound Matt. 1:25.
[^2]: Greek *Τότε γὰρ μᾶλλον* — elliptical: "for then *he is/will be with them* rather / all the more," i.e. after the consummation Christ's presence with his own is not withdrawn but intensified. The verb is supplied.
[^3]: Greek *Ἕως οὗ ἔτεκε* ("until she brought forth"), the phrase from the lemma of Matt. 1:25 («οὐκ ἐγίνωσκεν αὐτὴν ἕως οὗ ἔτεκεν») under discussion since Lemma 42.
[^4]: The verb *γινώσκω* is used here in both its senses at once, and the sentence turns on the play: to "know" a woman (*ἔγνω αὐτήν*, sexual union, above) versus to "come to know / understand" the ineffable birth (*ἔγνω τὸν ἄφραστον ἐκεῖνον τόκον*). The argument: having become fully aware of the miraculous, virginal birth, Joseph would all the less have "known" her carnally. Greek *Πῶς γὰρ ἂν ἥψατο τῆς ἁγίας ἐκείνης, μεθ᾽ ὃ μάλιστα ἔγνω τὸν ἄφραστον ἐκεῖνον τόκον;* The rendering keeps "come to know" to preserve the shared root; *ἥψατο* ("touched") is likewise a euphemism for sexual contact.

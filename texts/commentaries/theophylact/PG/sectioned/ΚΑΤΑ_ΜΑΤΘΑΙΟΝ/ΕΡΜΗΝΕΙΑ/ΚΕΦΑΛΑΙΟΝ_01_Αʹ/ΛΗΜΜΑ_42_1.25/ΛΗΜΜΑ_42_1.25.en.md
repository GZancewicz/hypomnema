# Matthew 1 (Αʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 1:25

> **…knew her not until she had brought forth.**[^1]

That is, he never at any time had intercourse with her; for the "until" here does not signify this, that up until the birth he knew *her* not, but that after these things he knew *her*; but *rather that* once for all he never at any time knew her. And the Scripture has such an idiom;[^2] as *in* the *phrase*,[^3]

[^1]: The lemma continues from Lemma 41: «…ἐγίνωσκεν αὐτὴν ἕως οὗ ἔτεκεν» (Matt. 1:25). Greek *ἐγίνωσκεν* is the euphemistic "knew" for marital relations, which Theophylact immediately glosses with the blunter *οὐκ ἐμίγη* ("did not have intercourse").
[^2]: Greek *ἰδίωμα* — a "property, peculiarity, idiom" of scriptural usage: namely that "until / *ἕως*" marks what did not happen up to a point without implying it happened afterward.
[^3]: The lemma breaks off as Theophylact introduces his proof-texts; these are supplied in the following lemmata (the raven of Gen. 8:7, and Matt. 28:20).

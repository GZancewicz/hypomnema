# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 45 · Matt. 12:45

> **it goes, and takes with itself seven other spirits more wicked than itself. And having entered, they dwell there, and the last *state* of that man becomes worse than the first; thus will it be also for this wicked generation.**[^1]

He shows *that* they are about to arrive at *the* utmost of destruction from *their* not receiving him. For just as those *who have been* freed from demons, if they grow slothful, suffer worse *things*, so also your generation was being held down by a demon, when you were worshipping the idols; but through the prophets I cast out this demon. And I also came myself, wishing to cleanse you further; but since you thrust me away, and rather hasten to slay *me*, as sinning worse, you will also be punished worse, and your last captivity will be heavier than the former *ones*.

But do you understand also this, that through baptism the unclean spirit is cast out, and it goes into the waterless and unbaptized souls; but in those it does not find rest. For rest, for the demons, *is* the troubling of the baptized through wicked deeds — since it already has the unbaptized. Therefore it returns back upon the baptized *one* with the seven spirits. For just as the gifts[^2] of the Spirit *are* seven, so on the contrary the spirits of wickedness *are* also seven. But when it enters into the baptized *one*, the calamity *is* worse. For formerly, on the one hand, there was hope of being cleansed through baptism; but from then on, on the other hand, there is no hope of a second baptism, except the *one* through repentance, which *is* exceedingly toilsome.[^3]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence broken off at *Τότε* ("Then") in Lemma 44. Greek *τὰ ἔσχατα τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐκείνου* is literally "the last *things* of that man"; rendered "the last state" for the plain sense (his final condition), the neuter plural being idiomatic. *γίνεται … χείρονα τῶν πρώτων* — "become worse than the first *things*." The OCR of this lemma is heavily corrupted but securely recoverable throughout: *καοικεῖ*→*κατοικεῖ* ("dwells"), *ἴσται*→*ἔσται* ("will be"), *Κσχατον*→*ἔσχατον* ("utmost"), *παραἄέξασθαι*→*παραδέξασθαι* ("to receive"), *λθον*→*Ἦλθον* ("I came"), *2κ*→*ἐκ*, *πνεύματε*→*πνεύματα* ("spirits"), *βαβαπτισμένον*→*βεβαπτισμένον* ("baptized").
[^2]: Greek *χαρίσματα* ("gifts of grace, charisms"); the seven gifts of the Spirit are set against seven answering spirits of wickedness.
[^3]: Greek *εἰ μὴ τοῦ διὰ μετανοίας, ὃ σφόδρα ἐπίπονον* — "except the *one* through repentance, which *is* exceedingly toilsome/laborious." Repentance is figured as a "second baptism," but a painful one.

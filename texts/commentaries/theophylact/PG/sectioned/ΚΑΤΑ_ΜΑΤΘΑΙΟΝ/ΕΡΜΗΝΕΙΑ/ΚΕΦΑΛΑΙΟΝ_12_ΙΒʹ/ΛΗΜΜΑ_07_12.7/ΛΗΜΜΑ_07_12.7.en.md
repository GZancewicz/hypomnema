# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 12:7–8

> **[*But if*] you had known what this is: "I desire mercy, and not sacrifice," you would not have condemned the guiltless. For the Son of man is Lord even of the Sabbath. (Matt. 12:7–8)**[^1]

He shows them to be also unlearned, as not knowing the prophetic *sayings*.[^2] For was it not necessary, he says, to have mercy on the people who were hungry? And besides, I the Son of man am Lord of the Sabbath, as being the Creator of all things and of the days as well, so that I loose the Sabbath as Master.[^3]

But understand *it* also according to the anagogical *sense*.[^4] For since the apostles were labourers, and the harvest and the ears *of grain* were those who believe, the apostles plucked these and ate, that is, they had as food the salvation of men; and this they did on the Sabbaths, in the resting from evil deeds; but the Pharisees were indignant. So also in the Church, when the teachers often teach and give benefit, those who act the Pharisee and the envious are displeased.[^5]

[^1]: The lemma is Matt. 12:7–8 (the folder name records only 12.7). The opening «Εἰ δὲ» ("But if") stands at the very end of the previous lemma (06), which breaks off there; it is supplied here in brackets so the conditional is intelligible. The quoted words *Ἔλεον θέλω, καὶ οὐ θυσίαν* ("I desire mercy, and not sacrifice") are Christ's citation of Hos. 6:6; *ἔλεος* is "mercy/pity," rendered "mercy." *ἀναιτίους* = "guiltless / blameless." In the second clause the Greek word order is *Κύριος γάρ ἐστι καὶ τοῦ Σαββάτου ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου* — literally "For Lord is even of the Sabbath the Son of man"; smoothed to normal English order while keeping the emphatic "even of the Sabbath."
[^2]: Greek *τὰ προφητικά*, "the prophetic *things*" — i.e. the prophetic writings/sayings; the noun is supplied.
[^3]: *Δεσπότης* ("Master / Sovereign Lord"), distinct from *Κύριος* just above ("Lord"); the verb *λύω τὸ Σάββατον* = "I loose/dissolve the Sabbath" (i.e. suspend its restriction).
[^4]: Greek *κατὰ ἀναγωγήν* — the "anagogical" (upward, spiritual) sense, Theophylact's technical term for the allegorical reading that follows; kept transliterated as a term of art.
[^5]: The end of this sentence is OCR-corrupt in the source: *«καὶ ὠπελρινπονν οἱ ο ρισαὶζοντες καὶ φθονεροὶ ἀπαρέσκουτοι»*. It is recoverable as *καὶ ὠφελούντων, οἱ φαρισαΐζοντες καὶ φθονεροὶ ἀπαρέσκονται* — "and [when the teachers] give benefit, those who play the Pharisee and *the* envious are displeased." *φαρισαΐζοντες* = "those who act/play the Pharisee." Translated on that reconstruction.

# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 12:18-19

> **…I chose; my beloved, in whom my soul was well pleased. I will put my Spirit upon him, and he shall proclaim judgment to the nations. He shall not strive, nor cry out. (Matt. 12:18-19)**[^1]

He brings in also the prophet as witness to the meekness of Jesus. For whatever, he says, the Jews wish, he will do; if they wish him not to appear, then this also he will do, and he will not resist like one who is a lover of glory, nor will he strive. But he will charge the crowds that they should not make him manifest — yet also to the nations he will proclaim judgment,[^2] that is, he will teach the Gentiles as well. For "judgment" is teaching,[^3] and knowledge and discernment of the good. Or else he will also proclaim to the Gentiles the coming judgment — those who never before heard concerning a coming judgment.

[^1]: The lemma completes the Isaian citation begun in Lemma 17 (Isa. 42:1-3, as quoted at Matt. 12:18-19). Greek *ᾑρέτισα· ὁ ἀγαπητός μου, εἰς ὃν εὐδόκησεν ἡ ψυχή μου. Θήσω τὸ πνεῦμά μου ἐπ' αὐτὸν, καὶ κρίσιν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν ἀπαγγελεῖ. Οὐκ ἐρίσει, οὐδὲ κραυγάσει.* The OCR *Θήσα.πὸ* is securely *Θήσω τὸ* ("I will put the"), and *κπαγγελεῖ* is *ἀπαγγελεῖ* ("he shall proclaim"). *πνεῦμά* rendered "Spirit" (the Holy Spirit put upon the Servant). *ἀπαγγελεῖ* is "announce / proclaim / report."
[^2]: Greek OCR *ἀλλὰ καὶ τοῖς ἔθνεσι ορίσιν ἀπαγγελεῖ*; *ορίσιν* is garbled and here read as *κρίσιν* ("judgment"), echoing the lemma's *κρίσιν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν ἀπαγγελεῖ* — "yet to the nations he will proclaim judgment."
[^3]: Theophylact glosses the lemma-word *κρίσις* ("judgment") in two ways. First, OCR *Κρίσις γὰρ κ Αασκαλία … κνῶσις καὶ διάκρισις τοῦ καλοῦ* is securely *Κρίσις γὰρ ἡ διδασκαλία … γνῶσις καὶ διάκρισις τοῦ καλοῦ* ("For 'judgment' *is* the teaching … knowledge and discernment of the good"). Theophylact's text stops at *τοῦ καλοῦ* ("of the good"); the fuller idiom *διάκρισις τοῦ καλοῦ καὶ κακοῦ* ("discernment of good and evil") is not written here, so we render only what stands and do not supply "and evil." Second (*Ἢ καί* … "Or else also"), he takes *κρίσις* in its eschatological sense, the "coming judgment" (*μέλλουσα κρίσις*) of which the Gentiles had never heard. The word *κρίσις* thus carries both "discernment/teaching" and "the Last Judgment," and Theophylact deliberately offers both.

# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 12:14

O *the* envy![^1] When they are being benefited, then they are driven mad. But Jesus withdraws, on account of the season of the passion not yet having arrived, and at the same time sparing them, lest they fall into a charge of murder. For the recklessly-daring *deed* is not well-pleasing to God.[^2] But observe the *word* "having gone out." For when they went out from God, then they willed to destroy Jesus. For no one who remains in God takes counsel for such things.

[^1]: Greek *Ὢ φθόνου!* — an exclamatory genitive ("Oh, *what* envy!" / "O *the* envy!"). The genitive is preserved by the supplied, italicized "*the*"; the sense is an outcry against the Pharisees' envy.
[^2]: Greek *Τὸ γὰρ ῥιψοκίνδυνον, οὐ θεάρεστον* — *ῥιψοκίνδυνον* is "reckless / foolhardy / rashly-daring"; *θεάρεστον* (= *θεῷ ἀρεστόν*) is "pleasing to God." Both the subject-adjective and the predicate are neuter substantives; a form of "to be" is supplied, and "*deed*" supplied for the neuter. The maxim: to court danger needlessly is not God-pleasing — Christ withdrew rather than provoke.

# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 12:22–23

> **Then there was brought to him one possessed by a demon, blind and dumb,[^1] and he healed him, so that the blind and dumb *man* both spoke and saw. And all the crowds were amazed, and said: Is this not[^2] the son of David? (Matt. 12:22–23)**

The demon had shut the roads *that lead* to faith — eyes, and hearing, and tongue. But Jesus heals, and is named "son of David" by the crowds. For the Christ was expected *to come* from *the* seed of David.[^3] And now, too, if you should see someone neither himself perceiving[^4] the good, nor receiving the words of another, consider this *man* blind and dumb, whom may God heal by touching his heart.[^5]

[^1]: Greek *κωφός* denotes deafness and/or muteness together; here paired with the man's speaking being restored, so "dumb / mute" is the operative sense, though "deaf" is included. The Gospel text as Theophylact quotes it lacks the article before *δαιμονιζόμενος* and reads simply "blind and dumb."
[^2]: Greek *Μήτι οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ υἱὸς Δαυίδ;* — *μήτι* introduces a question expecting a hesitant or negative answer ("Surely this is not…? / Can this be…?"); rendered "Is this not…?" to keep the tentative force the crowds express.
[^3]: Greek *προσεδοκᾶτο… ὁ Χριστὸς ἐκ σπέρματος Δαυΐδ* ("the Christ was being expected *to be* from David's seed"); the OCR *Προσοδοκᾶτο* is a plain error for *προσεδοκᾶτο*.
[^4]: The OCR reads *ὑοοῦντα*, securely = *νοοῦντα* ("perceiving / understanding *with the mind*"); Theophylact's point turns on the inward faculty, so "perceiving/understanding" rather than physical sight.
[^5]: Greek *ὃν ἰάσαιτο ὁ Θεός* (OCR *ὀάσαιτο*), an optative of wish — "whom may God heal"; alternatively it could be read as a relative-final ("that God may heal"). The body keeps the wish; the OCR form is reconstructed as *ἰάσαιτο*.

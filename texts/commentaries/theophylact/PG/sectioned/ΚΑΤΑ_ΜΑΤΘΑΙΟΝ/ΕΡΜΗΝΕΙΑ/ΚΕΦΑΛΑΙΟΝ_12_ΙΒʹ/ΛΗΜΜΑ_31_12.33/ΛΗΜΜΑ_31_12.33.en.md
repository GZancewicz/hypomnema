# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 31 · Matt. 12:33

> **Make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree corrupt, and its fruit corrupt. For from the fruit the tree is known. (Matt. 12:33)**

Since the Jews were not able to slander the miracles[^1] as evil, but were blaspheming Christ, who did these things, as demoniacal, he says: Either you say that I too, the tree, am good — and then assuredly my miracles also, which are the fruit, will be good; or, if you say that I am a corrupt tree, it is quite plain that the fruit also, that is, the miracles, will be corrupt. But indeed you say that the miracles, that is, the fruit, are good; and therefore I too am good, the tree. For assuredly from the fruit the tree is recognized, so that I too, from the miracles —[^2]

[^1]: The word is heavily OCR-garbled in the source as «αιίι απα» (with «τὰ μὲν … οὐκ ἠδύναντο διαβάλλειν ὡς κακά»). From the run of the argument — where the miracles are repeatedly equated with "the fruit" (τὰ θαύματά μου, ἅτινά εἰσιν ὁ καρπός) — the object is securely **τὰ θαύματα** ("the miracles / works"); rendered accordingly, with the OCR corruption flagged.
[^2]: The sentence breaks off at the section boundary: «ὥστε κἀγὼ ἐκ τῶν θαυμάτων …» ("so that I too, from the miracles [am recognized as good]"). The thought is completed by sense — Christ is known to be good from his miracles just as a tree is known from its fruit — but the Greek trails off where the next lemma begins its fresh scripture quotation.

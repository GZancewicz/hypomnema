# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 12:5-6

> **Or have you not read in the law, that on the Sabbaths the priests in the temple profane the Sabbath, and are without guilt? But I say to you, that *something* greater than the temple[^1] is here. (Matt. 12:5-6)**

The law forbade *one* to work on the Sabbath; the priests, therefore, on the Sabbath both split wood and kindled fire, so that they profaned the Sabbath — that is, defiled *it* — as you suppose. But you say to me that those were priests, whereas the disciples were not. I say, therefore, that *something* greater than the temple[^2] is here — that is: Since I, the Master greater than the temple, am also with the disciples, these have greater authority to loose the Sabbath than the priests. [^1]: The lemma's Greek reads «τοῦ καροῦ μεῖζόν ἐστιν ὧδε», where *καροῦ* is an OCR corruption of *ἱεροῦ* ("temple") — securely recovered from the parallel wording in Theophylact's own exposition below («τοῦ ἱεροῦ μεῖζόν ἐστιν ὧδε») and from Matt. 12:6. Likewise «Λέγω ἄὲ ὑμῖν» → *Λέγω δὲ ὑμῖν* ("But I say to you"). The neuter comparative *μεῖζον* ("something greater") is left indefinite in the Greek; the supplied "*something*" is italicized.
[^2]: Here Theophylact writes *τοῦ ἱεροῦ* ("the temple," τὸ ἱερόν, the temple precinct), then immediately glosses it with *τοῦ ναοῦ* ("the temple / shrine," ὁ ναός, the sanctuary proper). Both are rendered "temple"; the distinction (precinct vs. inner shrine) is not pressed in his argument.
[^3]: Greek *Εἰ δὲ* ("But if") breaks off here; the sentence is completed at the head of the next lemma («ἐγνώκειτε τί ἐστιν· Ἔλεον θέλω, καὶ οὐ θυσίαν …» — "But if you had known what it is: I desire mercy, and not sacrifice …", Matt. 12:7).

# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 12:31–32

> **Therefore I say to you: Every sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven to men, but the blasphemy of the Spirit shall not be forgiven to men. And whoever shall say a word against the Son of Man, it shall be forgiven him; but whoever shall say *a word* against the Holy Spirit, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this present age, nor in the *age* to come.**[^1]

This he says here: that every other sin, on the one hand, has at least some small defense — such as fornication, theft; for we take refuge in human weakness, and we are the more pardonable.[^2] But when someone sees miracles being accomplished through the Spirit, and slanders these as being done by a demon, what defense will such a one have? For it is evident that he knows, on the one hand, that they come from the Holy Spirit, but is willfully wicked.[^3] How, then, is such a one pardonable?

When, therefore, the Jews saw the Lord eating and drinking, and consorting with tax-collectors and harlots, and doing all the other things as a Son of Man, and then slandered him as a glutton and a wine-drinker — for this they are worthy of pardon, and they will not even be required *to make* repentance for this; for they seem to be reasonably scandalized.[^4] But when, seeing him working miracles, they slandered and blasphemed the Holy Spirit, saying it to be demoniacal, how shall this sin be forgiven them, unless they repent?

So that: know that he who, on the one hand, blasphemes against the Son of Man — seeing him living in human fashion, and calling him a friend of harlots, and a glutton, and a wine-drinker, because Christ does these things — such a one, even if he does not repent, shall not give account for this; for he is pardoned as not having perceived him to be God hidden.[^5] But he who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit — that is, the spiritual works of Christ — and says them to be demoniacal, unless he repents, shall not be pardoned. For did he have any reasonable occasion for slandering, as that other *had*, who, seeing Christ being with harlots and tax-collectors, then slandered *him*?

Therefore it shall not be forgiven to this one, neither here nor there; but both here and there he shall be punished. For many, on the one hand, are punished here, but there not at all — as the poor Lazarus.[^6] But others *are punished* both here and there — as those who blaspheme the Holy *Spirit*.[^7] But some, neither here nor there — as the apostles and the Forerunner. For even if they seem to be punished by being persecuted, yet those are not punishments for sins, but provings and crowns.[^8]

[^1]: Greek «οὔτε ἐν τῷ νῦν αἰῶνι, οὔτε ἐν τῷ μέλλοντι» — "neither in the now age, nor in the coming *age*"; rendered "this present age… the *age* to come," with the second "age" supplied. OCR «κεθήσεται»→«ἀφεθήσεται» ("shall be forgiven").
[^2]: Greek «κἂν μικράν γοῦν ἔχει ἀπολογίαν» — "has at least (γοῦν) at any rate some small defense/excuse (ἀπολογία)." «συγγνωστότεροι» = comparative, "more pardonable / more readily excused."
[^3]: Greek «ἐθελοκακεῖ» — a single verb, "acts wickedly on purpose, is willfully / deliberately evil"; the point is knowing culpability, not ignorance.
[^4]: Greek «δοκοῦσι… εὐλόγως σκανδαλίζεσθαι» — "they seem to be reasonably (with good cause) made to stumble / scandalized." «σκανδαλίζω» rendered "cause to stumble / scandalize."
[^5]: Greek «συγγινώσκεται γὰρ ὡς μὴ νοήσας αὐτὸν Θεὸν ὄντα κρυπτόν» — "he is pardoned as one who did not perceive/understand (νοέω) that he was God *while* hidden (κρυπτόν)," i.e. God concealed under the human manner of life.
[^6]: Greek «ὡς ὁ πτωχὸς Λάζαρος» — "as the poor Lazarus" (Luke 16); OCR «πτοωνὸς»→«πτωχὸς». The category is those punished (afflicted) here but not hereafter.
[^7]: The OCR is defective here: «Ἄλλοι δέ, καὶ ὧδε καὶ / ἅχιον βλασφημοῦντες.» Read «Ἄλλοι δέ, καὶ ὧδε καὶ ἐκεῖ· ὡς οἱ τὸ ἅγιον [Πνεῦμα] βλασφημοῦντες» ("others both here and there, as those who blaspheme the Holy [Spirit]") — restoring the three-way parallel (here-not-there / both / neither). «ἅχιον» is reconstructed as «ἅγιον».
[^8]: Greek «δοκιμασίαι καὶ στέφανοι» — "provings/tests and crowns." The exposition ends here; the following «Ἢ» in the source begins the next scripture lemma (Matt. 12:33, «Ἢ ποιήσατε τὸ δένδρον καλόν…»), expounded in Lemma 31.

# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 12:12

> **…does a man differ from a sheep? So that it is lawful to do well on the Sabbaths. (Matt. 12:12)**[^1]

He shows[^2] them, for the sake of love of money — and so that they might not be deprived of a sheep — dissolving the Sabbath, but, *when it comes* to a man's being healed, not enduring this to happen.[^3] So that at the same time he shows *them* both money-loving and harsh, and despisers of God. For they despise the Sabbath so that they might not be mulcted of a sheep, but they are merciless, because they do not want the healing of a man.

[^1]: This lemma continues the sentence broken off in lemma 10 at «How much then»; *διαφέρει ἄνθρωπος προβάτου;* completes it — "does a man differ from a sheep?" *Ὥστε ἔξεστι τοῖς Σάββασι καλῶς ποιεῖν* = "So that it is lawful to do well on the Sabbaths"; the source's *καλῶ- ποιεῖν* is an OCR break for *καλῶς ποιεῖν*.
[^2]: The exposition opens with *Δείκνυσιν* ("he shows / demonstrates"), which the source garbles as *Δείνω,σιν*; the reading is secure.
[^3]: The antithesis is *διὰ μὲν φιλοχρηματίαν … διὰ δὲ τὸ θεραπευθῆναι ἄνθρωπον* ("on account of love of money … but on account of a man's being healed"), the μέν left untranslated and δέ carried by the plain contrastive "but." The closing participle appears in the source as *μὴ ἀνενοένους*, an OCR error for *μὴ ἀνεχομένους* ("not enduring / not allowing"). The sense: they will break the Sabbath to save a sheep out of avarice, but will not allow it to be broken for a man's healing.

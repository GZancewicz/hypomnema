# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 12:38

> **"Then answered certain of the scribes and Pharisees, saying: Teacher, we wish to see a sign from you." (Matt. 12:38)**

The evangelist, marveling, added the *then*. For when it was necessary *for them* to submit because of the miracles that had happened before, then *it is that* they ask for a sign; but from heaven they wish to see a sign, as another evangelist says.[^1] For they thought that the miracles upon the earth he does from the power of the devil; for the devil is world-ruler.[^2] What, therefore, *does* the Saviour *say*?[^3]

[^1]: Greek *ἐξ οὐρανοῦ δὲ θέλουσιν ἰδεῖν σημεῖον, ὡς ἄλλος εὐαγγελιστὴς λέγει.* The reference is to the demand for "a sign from heaven," reported by Mark (8:11) and Luke (11:16); Matthew's text here does not itself add "from heaven," so Theophylact supplies it from the parallel.
[^2]: Greek *κοσμοκράτωρ* ("world-ruler," ruler of the cosmos) — a title applied to the devil (cf. *τὸν κοσμοκράτορα*, Eph. 6:12). Rendered literally to keep the technical term visible.
[^3]: The sentence *Τί οὖν ὁ Σωτήρ;* ("What, therefore, *does* the Saviour *say/do*?") breaks off here; the answer is supplied by the Gospel quotation opening the next lemma. The verb of saying is not expressed in the Greek and is supplied.

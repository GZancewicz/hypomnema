# Matthew 12 (ΙΒʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 12:4

> **how[^1] he entered into the house of God and ate the loaves of the presentation,[^2] which it was not permitted for him to eat, nor for those with him, but for the priests only? (Matt. 12:4)**

Again the Pharisees find fault with the natural affection[^3] — hunger — themselves sinning worse; but the Lord, from the history concerning David, puts them to shame. For that man, he says, on account of hunger, dared even something greater. And the loaves of the presentation are those set forth each day; for twelve were set forth on the table — six from the right side, and six from the other. And although David was a prophet, not even so ought he to have eaten; for it was permitted to the priests only. But much more was it not permitted even to those with him — nevertheless, on account of hunger, he is worthy of pardon; thus, therefore, also the disciples.

[^1]: This lemma continues directly from the *πῶς* ("how") broken off at the end of the previous lemma (Matt. 12:3-4): "how he entered into the house of God …".
[^2]: Greek *τοὺς ἄρτους τῆς προθέσεως* — literally "the loaves of the setting-forth," i.e. the shewbread (the twelve loaves set out on the table in the sanctuary, LXX for Hebrew *leḥem ha-panim*). Rendered "loaves of the presentation" to keep transparency to *πρόθεσις* ("a setting-before / presentation"); Theophylact's own gloss below ("those set forth each day … twelve on the table") unpacks the term.
[^3]: Greek *τὸ φυσικὸν πάθος* — "the natural passion / affection / condition," here specified as hunger. *πάθος* is a bodily affection one undergoes, not a moral fault; rendered "affection" to avoid the pejorative overtone of "passion."

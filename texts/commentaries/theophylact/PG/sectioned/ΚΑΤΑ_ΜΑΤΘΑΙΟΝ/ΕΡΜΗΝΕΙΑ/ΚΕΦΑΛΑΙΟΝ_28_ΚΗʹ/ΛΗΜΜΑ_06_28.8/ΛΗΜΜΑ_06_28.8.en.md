# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 6 · Matt. 28:1 (three-days computation, continued)

> **On the *day* dawning toward *the* first *day* of *the* week;**[^1] (Matt. 28:1)

— for from the dawn the whole day also is counted: behold, a third day-and-night.[^2] And you can also count the three days in another way: on the Preparation he gave up the spirit[^3] — this *is* one day; on the Sabbath he was in the tomb — this *is* a second day; and in the night of the Lord's day he rose, so *the* Lord's day too is counted, from a part, as another day; so that — behold — three days. For indeed, in the case of those who have fallen asleep[^4] too, if someone die about the tenth hour of the day, and another about the first hour of the same day, both are said to have died on the same day.

I have also another reason to tell you for this, how three days and three nights will be counted. Attend, therefore: on the fifth *day*, at evening,[^5] the Lord made the supper, and said to the disciples:[^6]

[^1]: Greek «Τῇ ἐπιφωσκούσῃ εἰς μίαν Σαββάτων·» (Matt. 28:1). *τῇ ἐπιφωσκούσῃ* is a substantival participle, "on the *day* that was dawning / growing light"; the supplied nouns "*day*" and "first *day*" are italicized. For *μία τῶν σαββάτων* = "the first day of the week," see Lemma 5, note 6. In the source the lemma is interrupted by an OCR-garbled running page-header — «ΟΑΡΙΤ ἦΧΝΙΙ.» — which is not part of the text and is omitted.

[^2]: Greek *ἡμερονύκτιον* (day-and-night); see Lemma 5, note 8. This completes the first method of counting, begun in Lemma 5.

[^3]: Greek *τῇ Παρασκευῇ παρέδωκε τὸ πνεῦμα* — "on the Preparation (Friday) he gave up the spirit / breath." *τὸ πνεῦμα* is "spirit" or "breath"; the phrase echoes the passion narrative (cf. *παρέδωκε τὸ πνεῦμα*, John 19:30).

[^4]: Greek *οἱ κεκοιμημένοι*, "those who have fallen asleep," the Christian euphemism for the dead (perfect participle of *κοιμάομαι*); kept literal.

[^5]: Greek *τῇ πέμπτῃ ἑσπέρας*, "on the fifth *day* (Thursday), at evening." *τῇ πέμπτῃ* ("the fifth") counts from the first day of the week; the supplied "*day*" is italicized.

[^6]: The sentence breaks off at *καὶ τοῖς μαθηταῖς ἔλεγε* ("and to the disciples he said:"); the quotation it introduces — «Λάβετε, φάγετε τὸ σῶμά μου» ("Take, eat my body") — is supplied by Lemma 7.

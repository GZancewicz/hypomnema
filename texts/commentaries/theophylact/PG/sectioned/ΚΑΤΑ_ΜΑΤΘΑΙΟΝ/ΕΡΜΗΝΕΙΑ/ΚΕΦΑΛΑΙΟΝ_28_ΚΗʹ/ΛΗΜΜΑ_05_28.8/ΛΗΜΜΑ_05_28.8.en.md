# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 28:8 (with exposition of 28:1)

> **…having gone out quickly from the tomb with fear and great joy, they ran to announce *it* to his disciples.**[^1] (Matt. 28:8)

The *phrase* "late on *the* Sabbath"[^2] is equivalent to what is said in Luke, "at deep dawn,"[^3] and to *that which is said* in Mark, "very early, the sun having risen."[^4] For by "sun" here we ought to understand the early rays of the sun; for from *the time* when the eighth hour of the night sets in, from then *is* the beginning of the coming day, and the morning seems to be setting in. Then, accordingly, it was late on *the* Sabbath, on the one hand, but *it was* the beginning of the Lord's day,[^5] on the other, which he names "one of the sabbaths." For they used to call the days of the week "sabbaths," on the one hand; but "one," on the other, *means* the first — so that the Lord's day is "one of the sabbaths," that is, *the* first of the days of the week;[^6] for with respect to the first, straightway the second also is spoken of, then the third, and thereafter the rest.

The Lord rose, accordingly, while the stone was still lying upon the tomb; but after the Lord had risen, the angel also comes, that, having rolled away the stone, he might give the women a passage into the tomb. And an earthquake occurs, that the guards might be awakened out of sleep, and might learn the strange thing that had happened. The Lord rose, therefore, on the third day.[^7] But how are the three days counted? At the sixth hour of the Preparation he was crucified; from this *hour* until the ninth, darkness — reckon this to me *as* night. Again, from the ninth *hour*, light — this *is* day: behold, one day-and-night.[^8] Again, the night of the Preparation and the day of the Sabbath, a second day-and-night; and again the night of the Sabbath and the dawn of the Lord's day, according to Matthew, who says:[^9]

[^1]: Greek «ἐξελθοῦσαι ταχὺ ἀπὸ τοῦ μνημείου μετὰ φόβου καὶ χαρᾶς μεγάλης, ἔδραμον ἀπαγγεῖλαι τοῖς μαθηταῖς αὐτοῦ.» (Matt. 28:8). *ἐξελθοῦσαι* is the aorist participle (feminine plural), kept as a participle: "having gone out." A direct object for *ἀπαγγεῖλαι* ("to announce") is not expressed in the Greek; the supplied "*it*" is italicized.

[^2]: Greek *τὸ ὀψὲ Σαββάτων*, the opening of Matt. 28:1 (*ὀψὲ δὲ σαββάτων*). *ὀψέ* + genitive can mean "late on" or "after" the Sabbath; the phrase is notoriously hard, and Theophylact's whole point is to harmonize it with the other evangelists' time-notes. Rendered "late on *the* Sabbath." *Σαββάτων* here first denotes the Sabbath day; just below, Theophylact exploits its other sense ("week") — see note 6.

[^3]: Greek *ὄρθρου βαθέος* ("at deep dawn," i.e. the depth of early morning), Luke 24:1.

[^4]: Greek *λίαν πρωὶ ἀνατείλαντος τοῦ ἡλίου* ("very early, the sun having risen"), Mark 16:2. *ἀνατείλαντος τοῦ ἡλίου* is a genitive absolute, kept as such: "the sun having risen."

[^5]: Greek *ἡ Κυριακή* ("the Lord's day"), the standard name for Sunday; rendered literally rather than "Sunday."

[^6]: Wordplay untranslatable in English: *σάββατα* means both "the Sabbath" and, by extension, "week"; *μία τῶν σαββάτων* ("one of the sabbaths") is the Semitic idiom for "*the* first *day* of the week." Theophylact's gloss turns on this double sense — *Σάββατα … ἔλεγον τὰς ἡμέρας τῆς ἑβδομάδος* ("they used to call the days of the week 'sabbaths'"), *μίαν δέ, τὴν πρώτην* ("but 'one' *means* the first"). "One" (*μία*) stands for "first" (*πρώτη*) in this counting idiom.

[^7]: Greek *τριήμερος*, "on the third day / of three days," an adjective agreeing with "the Lord"; rendered adverbially for English.

[^8]: Greek *ἡμερονύκτιον* (nychthemeron), a single "day-and-night," a full 24-hour span reckoned as one unit; the argument counts three such units. Cf. the parallel count in Lemma 7.

[^9]: The sentence breaks off at *κατὰ τὸν Ματθαῖον, λέγοντα* ("according to Matthew, who says:"); the quotation it introduces — «Τῇ ἐπιφωσκούσῃ εἰς μίαν Σαββάτων» — is supplied by Lemma 6.

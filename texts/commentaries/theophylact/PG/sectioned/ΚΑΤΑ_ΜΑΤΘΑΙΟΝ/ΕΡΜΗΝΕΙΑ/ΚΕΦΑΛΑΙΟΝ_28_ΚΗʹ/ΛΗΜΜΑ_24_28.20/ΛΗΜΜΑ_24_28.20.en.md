# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 28:18-20 (conclusion of the commentary)

> **All the authority has been given to me,**[^1] (Matt. 28:18)

— in that both unwillingly and willingly they now recognize me *as* God, who formerly, according to the mode of involuntary subjection,[^2] were in bondage to me. But if *it be taken* as spoken from the human nature, understand *it* thus: that I, the formerly-condemned nature, but having become God according to the unconfused union[^3] with the Son of God, received the authority over all, so as to be worshipped by angels in heaven, and on earth to be glorified by all the ends *of the earth*.

He sends, therefore, the disciples no longer to the Jews alone, but — since he received the authority over all, having sanctified all human nature in himself — fittingly he sends *them* to all the nations, having commanded *them* to baptize into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let Arius, therefore, be ashamed, and Sabellius: Arius, on the one hand, in that Christ did not say to baptize "into the names," but "into the name";[^4] and *the* name of the three *is* one, the Godhead; one God, therefore, *are* the three. But Sabellius, on the other hand, in that the Lord made mention of three persons — not of one person, as that man foolishly prated,[^5] *a person* having three names and being called sometimes Father, and sometimes Son, and sometimes Spirit — but rather of three persons having one name, *namely* "God."

Then, since it does not suffice to be baptized only, but it is necessary also to work the good after the baptism, he says: Teaching them to keep all things whatsoever I commanded you[^6] — not one or two, but all my commandments. Let us shudder,[^7] therefore, brethren, considering that if one *thing* be lacking to us we are not perfect servants of Christ; for we are required to keep all things. And behold the discourse of the Lord, how it comprehends both the chief points[^8] of Christianity, theology and practical virtue: for having said, on the one hand, that one must baptize into the name of the Trinity, he delivered to us theology; but having said, on the other hand, to teach also the keeping of the commandments, he introduced to us practical virtue.

And heartening them, since he was sending them to nations and to slaughters and dangers, he says: Fear not; for I will be with you until the consummation of the age.[^9] And behold this too: how he made mention of the consummation, in order to rouse them the more to despise the terrors. "For be not vexed," he says: all things will have a consummation, whether the worldly things be painful, or good — so that ye may neither fall under the painful things, for they pass away; nor be deceived by the good things, for they come to an end. And not to the apostles alone did he promise this — *namely*, to be with them — but also to all his disciples simply. For surely the apostles were not going to live until the consummation. To us also, therefore, and to those after us, he promises this; and *it is* not *that*, being present *only* until the consummation, after the consummation he will be absent — away *with the thought*![^10] For from that time rather he will be present *with us* more purely and more clearly; for the *word* "Until" does not do away with the after-time, wherever it be found lying in the Scripture.[^11]

Having given thanks, therefore, to the Lord who is here present with us, and supplies every good thing, and will again be present more perfectly after the consummation, let us here bring the exposition to its completion; for to him belongs all thanksgiving, and glory, and honor, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.[^12]

[^1]: Greek «Ἐδόθη μοι ἡ πᾶσα ἐξουσία» — Matt. 28:18, re-cited a final time. Here Theophylact's text carries the article, *ἡ πᾶσα ἐξουσία* ("**the** all authority / all the authority"); rendered "all the authority" to show the article. This lemma completes the two-sided reading set up in Lemma 23: *εἰ μὲν … ἀπὸ τοῦ Θεοῦ Λόγου* ("if … from God the Word") is now answered by *εἰ δὲ … ἀπὸ τῆς ἀνθρωπίνης φύσεως* ("but if … from the human nature").

[^2]: Greek *κατὰ τὸν τῆς ἀκουσίου ὑποταγῆς τρόπον* — "according to the mode of involuntary subjection." *ἀκούσιος* ("involuntary, unwilling") is picked up from *ἄκοντες καὶ ἑκόντες* just above ("unwillingly and willingly"): those who once served God unwillingly now, willing or not, confess Christ as God.

[^3]: Greek *ἀσύγχυτον ἕνωσιν*, "unconfused union" — the technical term for the union of the two natures in Christ without confusion or blending (the Chalcedonian *ἀσυγχύτως*). The OCR *Θεὸς … εσα* is securely *Θεὸς γενομένη* ("having become God," feminine, agreeing with *φύσις*, "nature"); the human nature, hypostatically united to the Son, is called "God."

[^4]: Greek *οὐκ … εἰς τὰ ὀνόματα … ἀλλ' εἰς τὸ ὄνομα* — "not 'into the names' (plural), but 'into the name' (singular)." The anti-Arian argument turns entirely on the singular *ὄνομα* of Matt. 28:19: one name for the three shows one Godhead, against Arius's subordination of the Son. The point is untranslatable without preserving the singular/plural contrast, which is kept explicit here.

[^5]: Greek *ἐμφλυάρει* (from *ἐμφλυαρέω*), "talked nonsense, prated foolishly" — a contemptuous verb for Sabellius's teaching that Father, Son, and Spirit are one *person* (*πρόσωπον*) under three names. Theophylact answers: three *persons* with one *name* ("God"), not one person with three names.

[^6]: Matt. 28:20a, quoted by Theophylact within the exposition: Greek «Διδάσκοντες αὐτοὺς τηρεῖν πάντα ὅσα ἐνετειλάμην ὑμῖν». Left in running prose (not set as a head-lemma) since he cites it in the course of comment.

[^7]: Greek *φρίξωμεν* ("let us shudder / be struck with awe"), OCR-garbled as *θρίξωμεν*; the reconstruction is certain (*φρίσσω*, to shudder with fear or reverence).

[^8]: Greek *τὰ … κεφάλαια*, "the chief points / heads / main topics" (of Christianity) — here *θεολογία* ("theology," i.e. doctrine of God/the Trinity) and *πρακτικὴ ἀρετή* ("practical virtue," the active moral life). The two are the poles of Byzantine spiritual teaching.

[^9]: Matt. 28:20b, quoted within the exposition: Greek «Μὴ φοβεῖσθε· ἐγὼ γὰρ ἔσομαι μεθ' ὑμῶν ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος». *συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος* = "the consummation / completion of the age," the end of the world; the word *συντέλεια* recurs throughout what follows and is rendered consistently "consummation."

[^10]: Greek *ἄπαγε*, literally "away!" — an idiomatic expression of vehement rejection, "away with the thought! / God forbid! / by no means." The supplied "*with the thought*" is italicized.

[^11]: Theophylact's grammatical rule: the particle *ἕως* ("until") in Scripture marks a terminus without denying what lies beyond it (so "with you until the consummation" does not imply "not after"). Cf. the same argument applied elsewhere in patristic exegesis (e.g. on Gen. 8:7; Ps. 109:1 LXX; Matt. 1:25).

[^12]: The closing doxology of the whole commentary on Matthew. In the source it is followed by an OCR-garbled colophon — legible fragments «εἰς τὸ κατὰ Ματθαῖον Εὐαγγέλιον … Τέλος» ("…on the Gospel according to Matthew … End") — which is a scribal/printer's tailpiece, not part of Theophylact's text, and is omitted here.

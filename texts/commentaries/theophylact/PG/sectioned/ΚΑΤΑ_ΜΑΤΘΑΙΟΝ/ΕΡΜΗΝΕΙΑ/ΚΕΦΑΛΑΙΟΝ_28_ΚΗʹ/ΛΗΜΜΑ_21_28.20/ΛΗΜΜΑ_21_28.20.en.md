# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 28:18

> **Authority has been given to me (Matt. 28:18)**[^1]

— understand *it* thus: "Out of my own struggles and wrestlings I saved men, and there came to be for me henceforth an inheritance and a peculiar people."[^2] On earth, on the one hand, therefore, the Lord has the authority, in that all the earth recognized him; but in heaven, because the reward of those who believe in him and their commonwealth[^3] exists in the heavens. For indeed, otherwise too: since human nature, formerly condemned, being joined to God the Word according to hypostasis,[^4] sits in heaven, being worshipped by angels — fittingly he says that[^5]

[^1]: Greek «Ἐδόθη μοι ἐξουσία» (Matt. 28:18), re-quoted in abbreviated form as the peg for a fresh gloss (*οὕτω νόει*, "understand *it* thus"). Folder-name verse is 28.20; the Greek expounds 28:18.
[^2]: Greek *κλῆρος καὶ περιούσιος λαός*. *κλῆρος* = "lot / portion / inheritance"; *περιούσιος λαός* = "a people for *his own* possession, a special/peculiar people" (the LXX phrase, Exod. 19:5, Deut. 7:6; cf. Titus 2:14). *περιεγένοντό μοι* rendered "there came to be for me / accrued to me."
[^3]: Greek *πολίτευμα* — "commonwealth / citizenship / state"; cf. Phil. 3:20 (*ἡμῶν … τὸ πολίτευμα ἐν οὐρανοῖς ὑπάρχει*), which Theophylact is echoing almost verbatim.
[^4]: Greek *καθ᾽ ὑπόστασιν* — the technical Chalcedonian phrase, "according to hypostasis / hypostatically," i.e. the union of the human nature with God the Word in the single hypostasis of the Son. Kept literal.
[^5]: The sentence breaks off at *εἰκότως φησὶν ὅτι* ("fittingly he says that …"); the quotation it introduces is supplied by the next lemma, «Ἐδόθη μοι πᾶσα ἐξουσία ἐν οὐρανῷ».

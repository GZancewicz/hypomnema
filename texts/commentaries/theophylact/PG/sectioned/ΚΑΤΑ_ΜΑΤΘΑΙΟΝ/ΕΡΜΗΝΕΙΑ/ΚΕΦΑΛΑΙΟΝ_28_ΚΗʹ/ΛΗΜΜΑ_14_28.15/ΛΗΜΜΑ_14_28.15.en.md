# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 28:15

> **…having taken the pieces of silver, they did as they were taught; and this report was spread abroad among the Jews until the present day.[^1] (Matt. 28:15)**

Those of the guard,[^2] on the one hand, reported all things: that an earthquake had happened, that the stone had been lifted of its own accord,[^3] that they themselves, having been afraid, became as dead men. But the Jews, on the other hand, being shamed[^4] neither by the miracles that had happened at the Passion, nor by the things testified concerning the tomb by the soldiers, corrupt[^5] the soldiers by their own passion, *namely* the love of money, winning them over[^6] to utter the *thing* most impious of all and most senseless[^7] — that he was stolen. For how could the disciples have stolen *him*, O senseless ones — they who, on account of *their* fear, had shut themselves in and did not dare to go out at all? And how, if they had stolen *him*, would they afterward have been dying for his sake, proclaiming that he had risen, and being cut to pieces[^8] on behalf of the falsehood?

[^1]: The lemma continues from the «Οἱ δὲ,» ("But they,") of the previous lemma: «Οἱ δὲ λαβόντες τὰ ἀργύρια, ἐποίησαν ὡς ἐδιδάχθησαν· καὶ διεφημίσθη ὁ λόγος οὗτος παρὰ Ἰουδαίοις μέχρι τῆς σήμερον.» *τὰ ἀργύρια* = "the pieces of silver / the money"; *διεφημίσθη* = "was spread abroad / noised abroad / rumored"; *ὁ λόγος οὗτος* = "this report / this saying"; *μέχρι τῆς σήμερον* = "until the present *day*" (σήμερον, "today," with the noun ἡμέρας understood, hence the supplied "*day*" rendered here within "present day").
[^2]: Greek *οἱ ἀπὸ τῆς κουστωδίας* — "those from the guard." *κουστωδία* is a Latin loanword (*custodia*, "guard, watch"), the same term used at Matt. 27:65–66; retained as "guard."
[^3]: Greek *ὁ λίθος ἤρθη αὐτομάτως* — "the stone was raised automatically / of its own accord," i.e. without human agency.
[^4]: Greek OCR *υσωπηθέντες*, securely = *δυσωπηθέντες* (the initial δ dropped in the OCR), aorist passive participle of *δυσωπέω*: "being put to shame / abashed / prevailed upon *by*." The sense is that neither set of wonders shamed them into belief.
[^5]: Greek *ὑπονοθεύουσι* (historic present) — "corrupt underhandedly / suborn / bribe secretly"; the *ὑπο-* prefix carries the note of covert corruption. The instrumental phrase *τῷ ἰδίῳ πάθει τῇ φιλαργυρίᾳ* — "by their own passion, the love of money" — is the means by which the Jews corrupt the soldiers.
[^6]: Greek *παραπείθοντες* — "persuading amiss / talking *them* over / winning *them* round," with the *παρα-* prefix connoting misleading persuasion. OCR *αὐτολς* = *αὐτούς* ("them").
[^7]: Greek *τὸ πάντων ἀσεβέστερον φθέγξασθαι καὶ ἀνοητότερον* — literally "to utter the *thing* more impious than all and more senseless." The comparatives *ἀσεβέστερον* / *ἀνοητότερον* with the genitive *πάντων* ("than all") carry superlative force: "most impious of all and most senseless." *φθέγξασθαι* = "to utter / speak out."
[^8]: Greek *κατακοπτόμενοι* — present passive participle of *κατακόπτω*, "being cut down / cut to pieces / hacked apart," i.e. martyred. *ἀπέθνησκον* (imperfect) rendered "would … have been dying," preserving the durative aspect within the counterfactual.

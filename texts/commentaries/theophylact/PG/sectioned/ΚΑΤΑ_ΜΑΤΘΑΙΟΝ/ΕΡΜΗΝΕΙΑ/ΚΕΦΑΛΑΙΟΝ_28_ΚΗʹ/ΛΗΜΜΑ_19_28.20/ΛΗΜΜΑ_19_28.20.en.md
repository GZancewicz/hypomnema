# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 28:20

> **teaching them to keep all things whatsoever I commanded you; and behold, I am with you all the days, until the consummation of the age. (Matt. 28:20)**[^1]

According to John, Jesus was first seen by the disciples on the very day of the resurrection, the doors being shut;[^2] then after eight days, when Thomas also believed; then, since they were about to meet in Galilee, but not yet had all gathered together — rather some of them were fishing at Tiberias — he appeared then to those fishing alone, *they* being seven.[^3] Now, therefore, what Matthew says happened later, after the things narrated in John which had happened before; for often he appeared to them, throughout forty days coming near and again withdrawing, not being present with them always and throughout.

The eleven disciples, therefore, the chief ones,[^4] together with all the rest — with absolutely all who followed — worshipped Christ; but some doubted, that is, but certain ones doubted.[^5] It is likely, therefore, that *it* be understood thus: The eleven disciples went into Galilee, and the eleven worshipped him. But "some" — that is, "certain ones," perhaps of the seventy — doubted concerning Christ. Later, however, these also were confirmed. But some understand *it* thus: that Matthew, on the one hand, omitted to say who were those that doubted; but what this *evangelist* omitted, John said — that it was Thomas who doubted; and perhaps all *of them* doubted, which is what Luke says. You ought, therefore, to understand *it* thus: that, on the one hand, having come into Galilee, they worshipped him; but these who worshipped in Galilee had doubted before, in Jerusalem, as Luke says.[^6]

Jesus, therefore, spoke to them, *saying* that "All authority has been given to me in heaven and upon earth." And this is of such *a kind*: On the one hand, as God and Creator I had the authority over all things,[^7]

[^1]: The lemma is legible with only minor OCR slips (source: «ἱδοὺ ἐγὼν … συντεΧείας»); securely **«διδάσκοντες αὐτοὺς τηρεῖν πάντα ὅσα ἐνετειλάμην ὑμῖν, καὶ ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ μεθ᾽ ὑμῶν εἰμι πάσας τὰς ἡμέρας, ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος.»** This lemma continues the quotation begun in the previous folder (Matt. 28:19, «πορευθέντες μαθητεύσατε…»). *συντέλεια* = "completion / consummation / end"; rendered "consummation" throughout, and the word-play on it will recur at the close of Lemma 24.
[^2]: The sequence of resurrection appearances follows John 20–21: the day of the resurrection (John 20:19), after eight days with Thomas (John 20:26), and the appearance at the Sea of Tiberias (John 21).
[^3]: Greek OCR *ἐφάνη τότε τοῖς ἀλιεύουσι μόοις ἐπτὰ οὗσι* = *ἐφάνη τότε τοῖς ἁλιεύουσι μόνοις, ἑπτὰ οὖσι* ("he appeared then to those alone who were fishing, *they* being seven"); cf. John 21:2.
[^4]: Greek OCR *Ὁ εὖν Κνδ, μαθηταὶ οἱ κορυφαῖοι* is corrupt; securely **Οἱ οὖν ἕνδεκα μαθηταί, οἱ κορυφαῖοι** ("The eleven disciples, then, the chief ones").
[^5]: Theophylact glosses the Gospel's *οἱ δέ* ("but they / but some," Matt. 28:17) as *τινὲς δέ* ("but certain ones"): the substantival *οἱ δέ* need not mean "the *rest*" of the eleven, but "some *people*." Kept literal ("but some … that is, but certain ones") so the gloss is visible.
[^6]: The harmonization draws Luke 24 (the doubt in Jerusalem) together with Matthew's Galilee scene: those who doubted earlier in Jerusalem are the same who afterward worshipped in Galilee.
[^7]: The sentence breaks off at *Εἶχον μὲν ὡς Θεὸς καὶ Δημιουργὸς τὴν ἐξουσίαν πάντων* ("I had, on the one hand, as God and Creator, the authority over all things"); the *δέ*-clause answering this *μέν* is supplied in the next lemma (Lemma 20), which opens with the Davidic proof-text.

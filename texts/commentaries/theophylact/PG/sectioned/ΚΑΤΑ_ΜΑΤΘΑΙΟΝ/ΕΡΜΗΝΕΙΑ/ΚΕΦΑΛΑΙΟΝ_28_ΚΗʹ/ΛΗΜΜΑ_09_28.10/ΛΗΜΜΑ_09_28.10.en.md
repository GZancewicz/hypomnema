# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 28:9-10

> **the Jesus says to them: Fear not. Go, announce to my brethren that they should depart into Galilee, and there they shall see me. (Matt. 28:10)**[^1]

He addresses to the women the *word* "Rejoice."[^2] For since the female race was condemned to be grieved,[^3] the Lord, through his resurrection, procured joy beforehand for the female race, and blessed them. Wherefore they, out of *their* great reverence and honor toward him, take hold of his feet, not daring, out of piety, to touch any *other* part of *his* body, except the outermost parts of his body. But some say that they laid hold of his feet on purpose, so that they might learn whether he had truly risen and *was* not according to *an* appearance,[^4] nor is a spirit. For they suspected that it was a spirit. At that time, then, on the one hand, these two Marys touched his feet. But according to John, on the other hand, Mary Magdalene attempts, on the one hand, to touch *him*, but is not permitted, because she wished to be always with him just as also before. Or rather, for this reason she is not permitted to touch Jesus according to John, as *being* over-curious.[^5] For since, as Matthew says, she [had] touched his feet, what need was there still to touch *him* again? So then, as *she was* henceforth superfluous, he pushes her away.

[^1]: Theophylact's lemma agrees with the common text of Matt. 28:10. He retains *τοῖς ἀδελφοῖς μου* ("my brethren") — the risen Lord calling the disciples his "brethren" — which his comment on the honor and blessing of the women does not directly touch. The opening *ὁ Ἰησοῦς* is anarthrous-sounding in English; the Greek article is rendered woodenly as "the Jesus" only in the header blockquote, following the Greek.
[^2]: Greek *προσφωνεῖ… τὸ, Χαίρετε*: the article *τό* nominalizes the quoted word — "he addresses *the* '*Rejoice*.'" The supplied "*word*" carries the article's force. See the note on *χαίρετε* at the previous lemma.
[^3]: An allusion to Gen. 3:16 (*ἐν λύπαις τέξῃ τέκνα*, "in griefs shalt thou bear children"). Greek *λυπεῖσθαι* ("to be grieved / to suffer pain") is set against *χαρά* ("joy") — the grief of the first woman is reversed by the joy of the resurrection announced first to women.
[^4]: Greek *κατὰ φαντασίαν* — "according to an appearance / apparition," i.e. merely a phantasm of the imagination and not a real, risen body. The same *φαντασία*-complex (appearance vs. reality) recurs throughout Theophylact.
[^5]: Theophylact plays on two near-synonyms: *περίεργος* ("over-curious, meddlesome, busying oneself beyond need") and, in the next clause, *περιττή* ("superfluous, excessive"). Mary Magdalene's renewed attempt to touch is judged "*περίεργος*" — doing more than needed — because (per Matthew) she had already touched his feet; hence as "*περιττή*," redundant, he sets her aside. The pun is lost in English; "over-curious" / "superfluous" keeps the pair visible.

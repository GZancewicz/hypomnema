# Matthew 28 (ΚΗʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 28:4–5

> **… fear of it the keepers were shaken, and became as if dead. But the angel, answering, said to the women: Do not you[^1] be afraid. For I know that you seek Jesus the crucified *one*; not … (Matt. 28:4–5)**

*A continuation fragment of the running Gospel citation (see Lemma 1). It opens mid-phrase — "fear of it" completes the "And from the …" of Lemma 1 (i.e. «Ἀπὸ δὲ τοῦ φόβου αὐτοῦ», "And from the fear of it") — and breaks off at "not …," which is completed by "*he* is here" at the head of Lemma 3 (the negative «οὐκ» belongs to this fragment). No exposition of its own; the commentary begins at Lemma 5.*

[^1]: Greek «Μὴ φοβεῖσθε ὑμεῖς» — the pronoun *ὑμεῖς* ("you") is emphatic and grammatically redundant with the verb, hence rendered woodenly "Do not **you** be afraid." Theophylact himself later fastens on this emphatic "you" (at Lemma 7): the guards deserve to be afraid, but "you, the disciples of the Lord," need not be.

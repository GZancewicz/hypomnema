# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 45 · Matt. 2:20

> **For they have died who were seeking the life of the child.** (Matt. 2:20)[^1]

Where is Apollinaris, who used to say that the Lord did not take a human soul?[^2] For from this very *place* he is refuted.

[^1]: Greek *Τεθνήκασι γὰρ οἱ ζητοῦντες τὴν ψυχὴν τοῦ παιδίου*. The word *ψυχή* means both "life" and "soul." In the scriptural idiom "to seek the *ψυχή* of someone" (cf. Exod. 4:19) it means "to seek someone's *life*," i.e. to kill him — hence "the life of the child" in the lemma. But Theophylact's comment turns on the other sense, "soul": the very fact that the child has a *ψυχή* is his proof against Apollinaris. The pun is unavoidable in English, where one word cannot carry both senses; "life" is used in the lemma, and the "soul" sense is drawn out in the note below and in the exposition.
[^2]: Apollinaris of Laodicea held that in Christ the divine Logos took the place of the human rational soul; hence he denied that the Lord assumed a full *human soul* (*ψυχὴν ἀνθρώπου*). Theophylact's point: since Scripture speaks of "the *ψυχή* (soul) of the child," the child truly had a human soul, and Apollinaris is refuted "from this very place" (*ἐντεῦθεν*, i.e. this passage). *ἔλεγε* is imperfect, "used to say / kept saying."

# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 2:20

> **saying: Having arisen, take the child and his mother, and go into *the* land of Israel.** (Matt. 2:20)[^1]

He does not say, "Flee," but, "Go." For *there* was no longer fear,[^2]

[^1]: This lemma continues the sentence begun in the previous one ("Behold, an angel of the Lord appears in a dream to Joseph in Egypt, **saying**…"). *Ἐγερθεὶς* is an aorist passive participle, kept as a participle: "having arisen / having been raised." *γῆν Ἰσραήλ* is anarthrous, so the supplied "*the*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek *Φόβος γὰρ οὐκ ἔτι ἦν* — "For fear no longer was/existed"; the existential "there" is supplied and italicized. The clause breaks off, and its reason is supplied by the scripture citation heading the next lemma: «Τεθνήκασι γὰρ οἱ ζητοῦντες τὴν ψυχὴν τοῦ παιδίου» ("For they have died who were seeking the life of the child"). Note the contrast Theophylact draws: the earlier command (Matt. 2:13) was «Φεῦγε» ("Flee"), whereas now it is «Πορεύου» ("Go").

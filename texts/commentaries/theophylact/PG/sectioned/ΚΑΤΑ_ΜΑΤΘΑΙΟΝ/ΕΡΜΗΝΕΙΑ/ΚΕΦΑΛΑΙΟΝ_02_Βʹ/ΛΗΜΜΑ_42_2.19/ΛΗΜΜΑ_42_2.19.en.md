# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 42 · Matt. 2:19

> **But when Herod had died. (Matt. 2:19)**[^1]

A bitter end did Herod have: by fever, and by dysentery, and by itching, and by swelling of the feet, and by putrefaction of the private part breeding worms,[^2] and by shortness of breath, and by trembling, and by convulsion of the limbs, having burst out his wicked soul.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Τελευτήσαντος δὲ Ἡρώδου» — a genitive absolute, "but Herod having died"; rendered "But when Herod had died." The lemma cites Matt. 2:19.
[^2]: Greek «σήψει τοῦ αἰδοίου σκώληκας γεννώσῃ» — "putrefaction of the genital (part) that breeds worms"; *τὸ αἰδοῖον* is the euphemism for the private/genital part, and the dative participle *γεννώσῃ* agrees with *σήψει* ("putrefaction… breeding worms"). The OCR reads «σκώηκας», securely = **«σκώληκας»** ("worms"). The catalogue of afflictions echoes the death of Herod described by Josephus (*Antiquities* 17.6.5).
[^3]: Greek *ἀποῤῥήξας τὴν πονηρὰν ψυχήν* — aorist participle, literally "having broken/snapped off his wicked soul," i.e. expiring; the vivid verb *ἀποῤῥήγνυμι* ("break off, tear away") is retained in the rendering "having burst out his wicked soul." The whole clause has Herod as subject of the participle.

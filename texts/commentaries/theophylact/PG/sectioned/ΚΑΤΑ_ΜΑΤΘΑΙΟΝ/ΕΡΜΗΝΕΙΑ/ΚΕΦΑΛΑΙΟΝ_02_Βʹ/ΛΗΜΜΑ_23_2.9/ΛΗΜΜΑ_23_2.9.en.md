# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 2:9

> **Until, having come, it stood over where the child was. (Matt. 2:9)**[^1]

And this too is strange. For the star came down from the height, and having become nearer to the earth, it showed them the place. For if it had appeared to them from on high, how would they have been able to recognize precisely the place where Christ was? For the stars encompass much space. Whence you too see *it* over your own house, *while I see it over* my house alone.[^2] And all men, quite simply, each one thinks that the moon or some other star stands over himself alone.[^3] That star, therefore, would not have shown Christ, had it not come down along *with them* and stood, all but,[^4] over the head of the child.

[^1]: OCR «Ἔως ἐλθὼν ἔστη ἐπάνοω. οὗ Κν τὴ παιΒίον» is securely **«Ἕως ἐλθὼν ἔστη ἐπάνω οὗ ἦν τὸ παιδίον»** — "Until, having come, it stood over *the place* where the child was." The subject throughout is "the star" (grammatically neuter *ὁ ἀστήρ* is masculine, but rendered "it" for the star in English); the participle *ἐλθών* ("having come") is kept.
[^2]: Greek *Ὅθεν καὶ σὺ ἐπάνω τῆς σῆς οἰκίας βλέπεις, τῆς ἐμῆς οἰκίας μόνης.* The clause is elliptical: "Whence you too see *it* over your own house, [and I over] my house alone." Theophylact's illustration is that the same heavenly body seems to each observer to stand directly over his own dwelling; the second half supplies the implied contrasting observer ("*while I see it over*"). Rendered to make the ellipsis intelligible; the supplied words are italicized.
[^3]: Greek *καὶ πάντες ἁπλῶς, δοκοῦσιν ἕκαστος ἐπάνω αὐτοῦ μόνου ἵστασθαι τὴν σελήνην ἢ ἄλλον ἀστέρα* — "and all, simply *put*, each one thinks the moon or another star stands over himself alone." The shift from plural "all" to singular "each one" is Theophylact's own; retained.
[^4]: Greek *μονονουχί* ("all but, well-nigh, as good as"): the star did not literally rest on the child's head, but came so low as to be virtually over it. *συγκατέβη* ("came down together *with them* / condescended in descending") rendered "came down along *with them*."

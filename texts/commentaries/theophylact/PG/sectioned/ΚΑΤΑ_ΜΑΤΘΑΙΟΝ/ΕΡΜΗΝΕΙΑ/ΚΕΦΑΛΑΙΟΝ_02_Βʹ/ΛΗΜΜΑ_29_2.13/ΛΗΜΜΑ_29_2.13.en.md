# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 2:13

> **But when they had departed, behold, an angel of the Lord appears in a dream to Joseph, saying: "Having risen, take the child and his mother." (Matt. 2:13)**

Did you see for what cause God permitted the Virgin to be betrothed? For here it is shown forth to you: *namely, that it was* on account of *Joseph's* caring for her and taking thought *for her*.[^1] But he did not say, "take your wife," but "the mother of the child." For since the suspicion had been dissolved, and the just *man* had been fully assured, from the wonders at the birth, that all things are from *the* Holy Spirit, he no longer names her his wife.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *προφαίνεταί σοι, διὰ τὸ ἐπιμελεῖσθαι ταύτης καὶ κήδεσθαι* — the sense is compressed: the reason God allowed the betrothal "appears" here, in that Joseph would now *care for* (*ἐπιμελεῖσθαι*) and *take thought for / provide for* (*κήδεσθαι*) Mary and the child in the flight to Egypt. The supplied "*namely, that it was*" makes the causal *διὰ τό* + infinitive explicit.
[^2]: Cf. Matt. 1:18–25, where the "suspicion" (*ὑποψία*) about Mary was resolved. Theophylact's point is that the angel's wording here—"the mother of the child," not "your wife"—reflects Joseph's now-settled assurance of the virginal conception.

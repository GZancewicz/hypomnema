# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 2:8

> **And having sent them to Bethlehem, he said, "Having gone, search out carefully concerning the child." (Matt. 2:8)**[^1]

He did not say "concerning the king," but "concerning the child"; for he does not even endure the name;[^2] whence he shows that he is raging against him,[^3]

[^1]: The folder-name verse is 2.7, but the Greek quoted here is Matt. 2:8. The lemma is lightly OCR-garbled: «Καὶ σπάμψας αὐτοὺς εἰς Βηθλεὲμ» is securely **«Καὶ πέμψας αὐτοὺς εἰς Βηθλεέμ»** ("And having sent them to Bethlehem"). *Πορευθέντες* ("having gone") is kept as a participle; the standard text has *ἐξετάσατε ἀκριβῶς*, here *ἀκριβῶς ἐξετάσατε* — word order retained. The supplied object of "find" is not yet in view; "the child" (*τοῦ παιδίου*) is the object of the verb rendered "search out concerning."
[^2]: Greek *οὐδὲ γὰρ τὸ ὄνομα ἀνέχεται* — literally "for he does not even endure the name." The point turns on the contrast just stated: Herod said "the child," not "the king," because he cannot bear even *the name* — i.e. that the infant should be called king. The subject "*he*" (Herod) is supplied from the sense; "*the*" before "name" renders the Greek article.
[^3]: The exposition breaks off with a comma after *κατ᾽ αὐτοῦ* ("against him") in the source; the following lemma (20) opens with fresh scripture rather than completing the clause, so the thought "he shows that he rages against him" stands essentially as the close of this comment.

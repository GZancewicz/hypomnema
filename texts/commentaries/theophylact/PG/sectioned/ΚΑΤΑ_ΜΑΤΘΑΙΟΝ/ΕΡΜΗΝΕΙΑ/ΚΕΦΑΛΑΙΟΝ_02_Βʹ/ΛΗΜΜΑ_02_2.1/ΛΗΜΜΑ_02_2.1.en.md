# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 2:1

> **In *the* days of Herod. (Matt. 2:1)**

He makes mention of Herod, so that you may learn that the rulers and kings from the tribe of Judah had failed,[^1] and necessarily Christ came. For Herod was not a Jew, but an Idumean, a son of Antipater, from an Arabian woman. And the rulers having failed, the expectation of the nations came, as Jacob prophesied.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *ἐξέλιπον* ("had failed / given out / come to an end") — the verb of Gen. 49:10 LXX, on which the whole comment turns. Kept as "had failed" to preserve the allusion.
[^2]: Jacob's prophecy over Judah, Gen. 49:10: "A ruler shall not fail from Judah… until there come the things laid up for him; and he *is* the expectation of *the* nations" (*προσδοκία ἐθνῶν*). Theophylact reads Herod's non-Judahite kingship as the sign that the Judahite line had "failed," and hence that the Messiah — the "expectation of the nations" — had come.

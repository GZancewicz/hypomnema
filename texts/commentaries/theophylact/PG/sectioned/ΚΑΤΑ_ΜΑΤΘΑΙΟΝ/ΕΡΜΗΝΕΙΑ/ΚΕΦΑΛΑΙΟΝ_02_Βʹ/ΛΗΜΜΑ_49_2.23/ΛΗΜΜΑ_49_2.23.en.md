# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 49 · Matt. 2:23

> **So that there might be fulfilled the thing spoken through the prophets, that "*He* shall be called *a* Nazarene." (Matt. 2:23)**

And which prophet said this? *It* is not found now.[^1] For on account of the carelessness of the Hebrews, many books were lost — also from the continual captivities; but perhaps *it* was said unwritten[^2] among the Jews. But the *word* "Nazarene" signifies "sanctified."[^3] Since, accordingly, Christ *is* holy, *he* would with reason be called *a* Nazarene. For "Holy *One* of Israel"[^4] the Lord is called by many prophets.

[^1]: Greek OCR «οὐχ κὐρίσκεται νῦν», securely = **«οὐχ εὑρίσκεται νῦν»** ("it is not found now"); the initial *κ* is an OCR corruption for *εὑ*. The point is that no surviving prophetic book contains the words "He shall be called a Nazarene."
[^2]: Greek *ἀγράφως … ἐλέγετο* — "was spoken unwrittenly," i.e. transmitted orally rather than in a written text. The adverb *ἀγράφως* stands in deliberate contrast to the lost written *βιβλία* ("books"); rendered "unwritten" to keep that contrast visible. *τυχόν* ("perhaps / as it may be") is retained.
[^3]: Greek *ἡγιασμένος*, perfect passive participle of *ἁγιάζω*, "having been sanctified / made holy, consecrated." Theophylact derives *Ναζωραῖος* from a Hebrew root meaning "consecrated / set apart" (cf. the Nazirite, נָזִיר), hence the gloss "sanctified"; the etymology is the pivot of the whole comment and is not readily reproducible in English.
[^4]: Greek *Ἅγιος … τοῦ Ἰσραήλ* ("Holy One of Israel"), the recurrent title of the Lord in the prophets (e.g. Isa. 1:4). The connective *γὰρ* ("for") is kept; the predicate noun *Κύριος* ("the Lord") is the subject of the passive *καλεῖται* ("is called").

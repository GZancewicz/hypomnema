# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 2:18

> **A voice was heard in Rama. (Matt. 2:18)**[^1]

Rama was a high place of Palestine. For this is what the name means, "high";[^2] and it fell as an inheritance to the tribe of Benjamin, who was the son of Rachel; but Rachel was buried in Bethlehem. The prophet therefore calls Bethlehem "Rachel," because she was buried in it; and the weeping and the lamentation, *he says,* were heard in the high place.[^3] Hear, then, the prophet:

[^1]: The folder metadata assigns this to 2:17, but the words Theophylact quotes here — «Φωνὴ ἐν Ῥαμᾷ ἠκούσθη» — belong to Matt. 2:18; the header and blockquote are corrected accordingly. The OCR reads «Χωνή», securely = **«Φωνή»** ("A voice").
[^2]: Theophylact's etymology: the place-name *Ῥαμά* (Rama) is derived from the Hebrew sense "height, lofty place," hence *ὑψηλόν* ("high"). The OCR of the gloss is corrupt («ὑύόολύν»), but the reading is fixed by the surrounding argument (the name = "high," and the lamentation is heard "in the high place"). Retained inline as an etymological point.
[^3]: Greek accusative-and-infinitive continuing the verb "the prophet says/calls" (*Λέγει*): «τὸν δὲ κλαυθμὸν καὶ τὸν θρῆνον, ἀκουσθῆναι ἐν τῇ ὑψηλῇ» — "*and* [he says] the weeping and the lamentation to have been heard in the high *place*." The supplied "*he says*" carries the governing verb into English; "the high *place*" renders the feminine substantivized *τῇ ὑψηλῇ*, i.e. Rama.

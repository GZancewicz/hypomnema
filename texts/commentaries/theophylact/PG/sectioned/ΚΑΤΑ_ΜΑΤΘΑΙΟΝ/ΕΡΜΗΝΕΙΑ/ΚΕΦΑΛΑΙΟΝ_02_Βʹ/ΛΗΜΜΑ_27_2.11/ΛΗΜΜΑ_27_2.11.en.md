# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 27 · Matt. 2:11

> **And having opened their treasures, they offered to him gifts: gold, and frankincense, and myrrh. (Matt. 2:11)**

The gold, on the one hand, they brought to him as to a king; for to the king we who are under *his* hand render gold *as tribute*.[^1] But the frankincense, as to God; for to God we burn frankincense as incense. But the myrrh, as to one about to taste death; for with myrrh the Jews prepare *bodies* for burial, on account of the body remaining undecayed. For myrrh, being dry, dries up the moisture, and does not allow worms to be generated.

Do you see the faith of the Magi? Since indeed also from the prophecy of Balaam they had been taught that the Lord is both God and king, and one who would die on our behalf. But hear the prophecy: "Having lain down," it says, "he rested as a lion, and as a whelp."[^2] Behold the royal *aspect*, through the lion; but through the "having lain down," the putting-to-death.[^3] "Those who bless you, have been blessed";[^4] behold the divinity. For the power of blessing the divine nature alone has.

[^1]: Greek *τελοῦμεν οἱ ὑπὸ χεῖρα* — *τελέω* here in its fiscal sense, "pay (as tax/tribute)"; *οἱ ὑπὸ χεῖρα* ("those under *the* hand") = subjects, those under *a king's* authority.
[^2]: Balaam's oracle, Num. 24:9 (LXX): *κατακλιθεὶς ἀνεπαύσατο ὡς λέων καὶ ὡς σκύμνος*. Greek *σκύμνος* = the young of a lion (a whelp/cub), here paralleled with the full-grown *λέων*.
[^3]: Greek *ἡ νέκρωσις* — "the putting-to-death, deadening, mortification"; here it denotes Christ's *death* (signified by the lion's "lying down"). Rendered "the putting-to-death" to keep the abstract noun visible; the sense is his dying.
[^4]: Num. 24:9b (LXX): *οἱ εὐλογοῦντές σε εὐλόγηνται* ("those who bless you have been blessed"). Theophylact takes the power to bless as a mark of divinity, since (he adds) only the divine nature possesses it.

# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 22 · Matt. 2:9

> **And behold, the star which they saw in the East went before them. (Matt. 2:9)**[^1]

The star was hidden for a little while by *divine* dispensation,[^2] so that they themselves might question the Jews, and Herod might be troubled, and thus the truth might become more manifest. But to them, having gone out from Jerusalem, *the star* appeared again, leading them by the hand.[^3] Whence *it is* clear that the star was a divine power.[^4]

[^1]: OCR «Καὶ ἰδοὺ ὁ ἀστὴρ ἂν εἶδον ἐν τῇ Ἀνατολῇ, προῆγον αὐτοός» is securely **«Καὶ ἰδοὺ ὁ ἀστὴρ ὃν εἶδον ἐν τῇ Ἀνατολῇ, προῆγεν αὐτούς»** — "And behold, the star which they saw in the East went before them." The source's *προῆγον* is an OCR slip for the singular *προῆγεν* ("it went before"), the subject being "the star." *τῇ Ἀνατολῇ* ("the East / the rising") rendered "the East" per convention.
[^2]: Greek OCR *Ἀπακρόη* = **ἀπεκρύβη** ("was hidden"). *οἰκονομικῶς* ("by *divine* dispensation / providentially / by the [divine] economy"): the adverb marks a purposeful arrangement of providence; "*divine*" is supplied to convey the theological force the word carries here.
[^3]: Greek OCR *ιραγωγῶν αὐτούς* is best restored as **χειραγωγῶν αὐτούς** ("leading them by the hand"), a present participle; the initial *χε-* is lost in the OCR. An alternative restoration *παράγων* ("leading along") would give much the same sense.
[^4]: Greek *θεία δύναμις* (OCR *δεία*) — "a divine power"; Theophylact's point is that the star's descent and guidance prove it no ordinary heavenly body.

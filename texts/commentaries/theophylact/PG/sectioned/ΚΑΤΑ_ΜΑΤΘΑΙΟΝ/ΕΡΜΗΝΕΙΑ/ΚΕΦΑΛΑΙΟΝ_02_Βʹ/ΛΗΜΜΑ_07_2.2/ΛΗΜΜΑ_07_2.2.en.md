# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 7 · Matt. 2:2

> **For we saw his star in the East. (Matt. 2:2)**

When you hear "a star," do not suppose that one to be such as those we see, but a divine and angelic power, appearing in the form of a star. For since the Magi were astrologers, the Lord drew them by means of what was familiar to them, just as also he drew Peter, being a fisherman, whom he astonished by the multitude of fishes which he netted at the word[^1] of Christ. But that the star was an angelic power is clear from the fact that it shone exceedingly even in the day, and from its moving when the Magi moved but standing still when they rested; but also from its moving from the northern region, which is that of Persia, toward the southern, which is that of Jerusalem. But never does a star move from the north toward the south.

[^1]: Greek OCR reads *ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματι τοῦ Χριστοῦ* ("at/in the name of Christ"), but the allusion is to the miraculous catch of Luke 5:5–6, where Peter lets down the nets *ἐπὶ τῷ ῥήματί* σου ("at your word"). The variant *ῥήματι* ("word") is likely the intended reading; rendered "word" here, with the difficulty flagged. The phrase *ἐπὶ τῷ ῥήματι/ὀνόματι τοῦ Χριστοῦ* attaches to the fishes Peter caught, the sensible sign by which the Lord drew a fisherman — the parallel to the star drawing astrologers.

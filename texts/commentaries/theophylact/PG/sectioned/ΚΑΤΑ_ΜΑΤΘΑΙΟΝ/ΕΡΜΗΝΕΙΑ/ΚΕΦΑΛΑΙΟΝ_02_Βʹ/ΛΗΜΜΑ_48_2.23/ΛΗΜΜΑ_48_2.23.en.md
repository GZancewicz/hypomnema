# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 48 · Matt. 2:23

> **And having come, he dwelt in a city called Nazareth.** (Matt. 2:23)

And how *is it that* Luke says that, after the being born, having completed the forty days and having been held up by Simeon,[^1] the Lord went down to Nazareth; whereas here Matthew says that after the return from Egypt he came into Nazareth? Learn, then, that the things which Matthew passed over in silence, Luke stated. Such *is* what I mean: after the being born, he completed the forty days; then he went down to Nazareth — these things Luke says; but Matthew says the things after these: that he fled into Egypt, then went up from Egypt into Nazareth. Therefore they do not contradict one another; but the one, on the one hand — Luke — tells the going-down from Bethlehem to Nazareth; while Matthew, on the other hand, *tells* the going-up afterward from Egypt to Nazareth.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *ἀπὸ τοῦ Συμεὼν βασταχθεὶς* — literally "having been held/carried up by Simeon," referring to Simeon taking the child into his arms at the presentation in the temple (Luke 2:28, «ἐδέξατο αὐτὸ εἰς τὰς ἀγκάλας»). *τὸ γεννηθῆναι* is an articular infinitive, "the being born"; kept in that form for transparency. The "forty days" are the days until the purification and presentation (Luke 2:22; cf. Lev. 12:2-4).
[^2]: The two nouns are a deliberate pair: *κάθοδος* ("going-down / descent," from Bethlehem to Nazareth in Luke) and *ἄνοδος* ("going-up / ascent," from Egypt to Nazareth in Matthew); rendered "going-down" / "going-up" to keep the antithesis visible. The correlative *ὁ μὲν … ὁ δὲ* ("the one … the other") is rendered "on the one hand … on the other hand"; Theophylact identifies "the one" as Luke by apposition.

# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 2:7

> **He ascertained exactly from them the time of the appearing star. (Matt. 2:7)**[^1]

That is, he learned *it* exactly. For the star, before the Lord was born, appeared to the Magi; for since they were about to consume much time on the journey, for this reason the star appeared long beforehand, so that they might worship him while he was still in the swaddling clothes. But some say that at the same time as Christ was born, the star appeared, and the Magi came after two years,[^2] and did not find the Lord in swaddling clothes, nor in the manger, but in a house with his mother, being two years old. But do you hold the previously stated *view as* the better *one*.[^3]

[^1]: Greek «Ἠκρίβωσε παρ᾽ αὐτῶν τὸν χρόνον τοῦ φαινομένου ἀστέρος». The verb *ἠκρίβωσε* ("he ascertained/determined exactly") and its gloss *ἀκριβῶς ἀνέμαθεν* ("he learned exactly") share the root *ἀκριβ‑*; the exactness is kept in both.
[^2]: Greek «διὰ δύο ἐτῶν ἦλθον» — either "came after *an interval of* two years" or "came in the course of two years"; rendered "came after two years." This clause reports the alternative opinion, that the star appeared only at the Nativity and the Magi arrived when the child was already two years old (cf. Matt. 2:16).
[^3]: Greek «Ἀλλὰ σὺ τὸ προρρηθὲν ἔχε κρεῖττον». *τὸ προρρηθέν* = "the thing said before," i.e. the first explanation (that the star appeared before the Nativity); *κρεῖττον* ("better") is here predicative — "hold the earlier *view* as the better *one*."

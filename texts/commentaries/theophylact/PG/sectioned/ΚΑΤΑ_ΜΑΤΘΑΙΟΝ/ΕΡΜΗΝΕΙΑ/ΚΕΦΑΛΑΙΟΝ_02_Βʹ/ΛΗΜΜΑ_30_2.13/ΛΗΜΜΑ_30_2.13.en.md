# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 2:13

> **And flee into Egypt. (Matt. 2:13)**

The Lord also flees, in order that it might be made credible that he truly was also man. For if, having come into the hands of Herod, he had not been cut down, he would have seemed to have been made flesh according to appearance *only*.[^1] But into Egypt he flees, in order that he might sanctify that *land* also. For there were two lands that were workshops of all evil: Babylon and Egypt. Babylon, therefore, he received worshipping *him* through the Magi;[^2] but Egypt he sanctified by his own presence.

[^1]: Greek *κατὰ φαντασίαν σαρκωθῆναι* — "to have been made flesh in appearance/imagination *only*," the Docetic error that Christ's body was not real. The flight proves the reality of his human flesh, which was genuinely vulnerable to Herod.
[^2]: Theophylact treats the Magi as coming from Babylon (the East, land of the Chaldeans), so that in receiving their worship Christ already "received Babylon"; by fleeing to Egypt he sanctifies the second of the two archetypal "workshops of evil."

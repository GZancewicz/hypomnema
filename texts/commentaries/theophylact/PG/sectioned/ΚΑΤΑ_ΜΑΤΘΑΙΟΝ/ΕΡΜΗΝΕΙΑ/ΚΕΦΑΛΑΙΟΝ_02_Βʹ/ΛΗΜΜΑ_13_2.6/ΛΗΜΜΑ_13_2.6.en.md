# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 2:6

> **For out of you shall come forth a ruler.**[^1]

Well did he say "shall come forth," and not "shall remain in you." For Christ did not remain in Bethlehem, but out of it he went forth after being born, whereas in Nazareth he spent most of his time. But the Jews say that this was prophesied concerning Zerubbabel. But they lie manifestly. For indeed Zerubbabel was not born in Bethlehem, but in Babylon. For behold his name: "Zoro" signifies the sowing and the birth, and "Babel" *signifies* Babylon — that is, the one sown or begotten in Babylon.[^2] But the prophecy also plainly refutes them, saying:[^3]

[^1]: The lemma is the continuation of the Micah 5:2 citation quoted at Matt. 2:6. The OCR reads «Ἐκ τοῦ γὰρ ἐξελεύσεται ἡγούμενος»; **τοῦ** is a corruption of **σοῦ** ("out of *you*"), as is confirmed by Theophylact's own comment below, which contrasts "shall come forth" with «ἐν σοὶ μενεῖ» ("shall remain **in you**"). Rendered "For out of you shall come forth a ruler."
[^2]: The etymology is Theophylact's own word-play on the name Ζοροβάβελ: *Ζορό* he glosses as *σπορά/γέννησις* ("sowing / birth"), *Βαβέλ* as *Βαβυλών* ("Babylon"), yielding «ὁ ἐν Βαβυλῶνι σπαρεὶς ἢ γεννηθείς» — "the one sown or begotten in Babylon." The point is that the name itself locates Zerubbabel's origin in Babylon, not Bethlehem.
[^3]: The sentence breaks off with «λέγουσα·» ("saying:"); the prophetic words are supplied at the head of the next lemma, «Αἱ ἔξοδοι αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς…».

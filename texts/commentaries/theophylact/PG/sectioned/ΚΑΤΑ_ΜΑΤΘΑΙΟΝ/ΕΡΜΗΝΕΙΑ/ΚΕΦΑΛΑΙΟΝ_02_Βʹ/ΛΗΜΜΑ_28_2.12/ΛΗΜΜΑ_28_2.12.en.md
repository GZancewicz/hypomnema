# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 2:12

> **And having been divinely warned in a dream not to turn back to Herod, by another way they departed into their country. (Matt. 2:12)**[^1]

See the sequence: first, through a star, God led them by the hand into the faith; then, when they had come into Jerusalem, he taught them through the prophet that he is born in Bethlehem; and lastly, through the angel. And they obey the divine warning[^1]—that is, the divine converse. Having been divinely warned, therefore, that is, having received a revelation from God, they deceived Herod; and they did not fear lest they be pursued, but took courage in the power of the one who had been born—thus were they true witnesses.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *χρηματισθέντες* (and the cognate noun *χρηματισμός*, "the divine warning," below): *χρηματίζω* in the passive is a technical term for receiving a divine oracle, warning, or instruction (cf. Matt. 2:22; Heb. 8:5). Rendered "having been divinely warned" / "the divine warning"; Theophylact himself glosses it as *ἡ θεία ὁμιλία* ("the divine converse/communication") and as *ἀποκάλυψις* ("a revelation").
[^2]: Greek *μάρτυρες ἀληθεῖς* — *μάρτυς* means both "witness" and "martyr." The primary sense here is "witnesses" (to Christ, by their faithful obedience and refusal to fear), but the later Christian resonance of "martyrs" cannot be excluded.

# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 2:6

> **His goings forth *are* from *the* beginning, from *the* days of eternity.**[^1]

And of whom else are the goings forth both from *the* beginning and from *the* days of eternity than of Christ? Who had two goings forth, that is, births; but the first birth, on the one hand, *is* from *the* beginning, from the Father, and the second, on the other hand, from *the* days of eternity[^2] — beginning from the Theotokos, and being under time. Let the Jews say, then, whether Zerubbabel came to be from *the* beginning? But they have nothing to say.

[^1]: A continuation of the Micah 5:2 citation (Matt. 2:6). Greek «Αἱ ἔξοδοι αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς, ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος». *ἔξοδοι* ("goings out, issues, departures") Theophylact glosses below as *γεννήσεις* ("births"); rendered literally "goings forth." The lemma is verbless; the supplied "*are*" is italicized.
[^2]: Greek *αἰών* is genuinely ambiguous — "eternity" or "an age / the world / a span of time." Theophylact exploits the ambiguity: the *same* phrase «ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος» that marks the eternal generation is applied to the second, temporal birth, which he immediately qualifies as «ἀπὸ τῆς Θεοτόκου ἀρξαμένη, καὶ ὑπὸ χρόνον οὖσα» ("beginning from the Theotokos, and being under time"). The paradox — "days of eternity" that nonetheless begin in time — is the point; the phrase is kept identical in lemma and comment so the seam stays visible. An alternative rendering of the second occurrence would be "from *the* days of *the* age."

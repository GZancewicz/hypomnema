# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 2:6

> **His goings forth are from the beginning, from the days of eternity.**[^1]

And of whom else are the goings forth both from the beginning and from the days of eternity than of Christ? Who had two goings forth, that is, births; but the first birth is from the beginning, from the Father, and the second from the days of the age[^2] — beginning from the Theotokos, and being under time. Let the Jews say, then, whether Zerubbabel came to be from the beginning? But they have nothing to say.

[^1]: A continuation of the Micah 5:2 citation (Matt. 2:6). Greek «Αἱ ἔξοδοι αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς, ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος». *ἔξοδοι* ("goings out, issues, departures") Theophylact glosses below as *γεννήσεις* ("births"); rendered literally "goings forth." The lemma is verbless; "are" is supplied.
[^2]: Greek *αἰών* is genuinely ambiguous — "eternity" or "an age / the world / a span of time." In the prophecy (the lemma) the phrase «ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος» carries the traditional sense "from days of eternity / of old," standing in parallel to «ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς». Theophylact, however, splits the two clauses between Christ's two births: «ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς» = the eternal generation from the Father, and «ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος» = the temporal birth from the Theotokos, which he at once qualifies «ἀπὸ τῆς Θεοτόκου ἀρξαμένη, καὶ ὑπὸ χρόνον οὖσα» ("beginning from the Theotokos, and being under time"). Because he applies the phrase to the time-bound birth, *αἰῶνος* is here rendered "the age" rather than "eternity," so the comment does not call the temporal birth "eternal."

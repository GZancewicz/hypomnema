# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 2:1

> **And Jesus having been born in Bethlehem of Judea. (Matt. 2:1)**[^1]

Bethlehem is interpreted "house of bread," and Judea "confession."[^2] May it come to pass, then, that we also now, through confession, become a house of the spiritual bread.

[^1]: Greek «Τοῦ δὲ Ἰησοῦ γεννηθέντος ἐν Βηθλεὲμ τῆς Ἰουδαίας» — a genitive absolute ("Jesus having been born…"), kept as a participle. δέ rendered "and."
[^2]: Theophylact glosses the two place-names. Βηθλεέμ (Hebrew *Bêṯ leḥem*) is indeed "house of bread." For Ἰουδαία he gives *ἐξομολόγησις*, the standard etymology of Judah/Ἰούδα, whose range is "confession, acknowledgment, thanksgiving/praise"; "confession" is chosen in the body to keep the wordplay he then builds on ("that we too, through *ἐξομολόγησις*…"). The verb ἑρμηνεύεται ("is interpreted") governs both names; "*is*" is supplied for the second.

# Matthew 2 (Βʹ) — Lemma 46 · Matt. 2:21-22

> **But he, having arisen, took the child and his mother, and came into *the* land of Israel. And having heard [that Archelaus was reigning over Judaea in place of][?] Herod his father, he was afraid to go away thither.** (Matt. 2:21-22)[^1]

Three sons Herod left behind: Philip, Antipas, and Archelaus; Archelaus, on the one hand, therefore, he ordered to be king, but the rest, on the other hand, *to be* tetrarchs. And Joseph was afraid to go away into *the* land of Israel — that is, into Judaea — because Archelaus also was like his father. But Antipas, he is the younger Herod, the *one* who slew the Forerunner.[^2]

[^1]: The OCR reads «Ἀκούσας [ΑΡΠΤΙi.] Ἡρώδου τοῦ πατρὸς αὐτοῦ, ἐφοβήθη ἐκεῖ ἀπελθεῖν». The garbled fragment «ΑΡΠΤΙi.» (partly a stray running-header artifact) stands where the words of Matt. 2:22 «ὅτι Ἀρχέλαος βασιλεύει τῆς Ἰουδαίας ἀντὶ» ("that Archelaus was reigning over Judaea in place of") belong; these are securely recoverable from the received text and from Theophylact's own exposition (which turns on Archelaus succeeding Herod), and are supplied in brackets with a [?] flag. *ἐκεῖ* is rendered "thither" ("to go away *thither*," i.e. to that place).
[^2]: "The younger Herod" (*ὁ νέος Ἡρώδης*) is Herod Antipas, who beheaded John "the Forerunner" (*ὁ Πρόδρομος*, John the Baptist; cf. Matt. 14:10). The OCR «ἀνε´ ὡν» is securely *ἀνελών*, aorist participle of *ἀναιρέω*, "having done away with / having slain." The exposition breaks off here and continues in the next lemma with the citation «Χρηματισθεὶς δὲ κατ᾽ ὄναρ…».

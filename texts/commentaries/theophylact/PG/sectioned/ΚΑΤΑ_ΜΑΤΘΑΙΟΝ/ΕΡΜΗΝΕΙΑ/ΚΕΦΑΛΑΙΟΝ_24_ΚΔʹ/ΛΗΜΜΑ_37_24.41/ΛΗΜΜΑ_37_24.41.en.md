# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 37 · Matt. 24:40–41

> ***Two women* grinding at the mill; one is taken, and one is left. (Matt. 24:41)**[^1]

Then, he says, when all are without care[^2] and working their own works, the one, on the one hand, is taken — that is, the righteous *man* — unto *the* meeting of the Lord in *the* air,[^3] but the other one, evidently the sinner, is left below.

But even if some are slaves and are grinding *at the mill*, even among these some are taken, as many as *are* worthy, but others are left, as many as *are* unworthy; so that from here also we learn that both slaves and women are not hindered with respect to virtue.

[^1]: The lemma continues the Gospel quotation begun in the previous lemma («Τότε δύο ἔσονται ἐν τῷ ἀγρῷ· ὁ εἷς παραλαμβάνεται, καὶ ὁ εἷς ἀφίεται. Δύο …», Matt. 24:40), which breaks off at *Δύο* ("Two"); this file carries its completion, «ἀλήθουσαι ἐν τῷ μύλωνι· μία παραλαμβάνεται, καὶ μία ἀφίεται» (Matt. 24:41). The participle *ἀλήθουσαι* is feminine plural ("*women* grinding"), so the supplied "*Two women*" and the feminine "one" are warranted by the Greek gender; hence Theophylact's remark below about "slaves and women."
[^2]: Greek *ἀμερίμνων ὄντων πάντων* — "all being free of care / without anxiety," i.e. absorbed in their ordinary occupations and off their guard.
[^3]: Greek *εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ Κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα* — an allusion to 1 Thess. 4:17 (*εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ Κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα*, "to meet the Lord in the air"). "*The*" is supplied for the anarthrous *ἀέρα*.

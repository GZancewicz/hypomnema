# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 44 · Matt. 24:51

> **…he will cut him asunder,[^1] and his part he will set with the hypocrites; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.**

Having said how the faithful servant shall be honored, now he says how the wicked one shall be punished. For if someone, having been entrusted with the stewardship of some gift, should despise the tribunal there,[^2] and should say that "My lord tarries" — that is, does not impose the punishment at once and immediately[^3] — and should make the longsuffering of God an occasion for wickedness, and should beat his fellow servants (I mean, should give offense,[^4] and should beat their conscience; for those who are ruled, on the one hand,[^5] seeing the rulers using badly the things that were given, are struck, being scandalized, and are harmed) — such a one, therefore, shall be cut asunder, that is, shall be stripped of the gift belonging to him, and then he shall appear such as he was, and shall be cast into the darkness. For formerly, on the one hand, he was deceiving through the outward guise — such as *were* many of the high priests, seeming holy on account of *their* rank; but then, on the other hand, the grace shall be taken away from them. And they shall be punished as hypocrites, being one thing and appearing another.[^6]

[^1]: Greek *διχοτομήσει αὐτόν* — literally "he will cut him in two, dichotomize him" (the whole scripture end: «διχοτομήσει αὐτὸν, καὶ τὸ μέρος αὐτοῦ μετὰ τῶν ὑποκριτῶν θήσει· ἐκεῖ ἔσται ὁ κλαυθμὸς καὶ ὁ βρυγμὸς τῶν ὀδόντων»). Rendered "cut asunder" to keep the violent literal image, since Theophylact's exposition turns on it: below he glosses *διχοτομηθήσεται* not as bodily bisection but as *γυμνωθήσεται τοῦ … χαρίσματος*, "shall be stripped of the gift." I keep "cut asunder" in the body at both places so the reader sees the word Theophylact is re-interpreting.
[^2]: Greek *τοῦ ἐκεῖθεν δικαστηρίου* — literally "the tribunal from there / thence," i.e. the judgment-seat of the world to come. *ἐκεῖθεν* ("from that place, yonder") is Theophylact's regular shorthand for the age to come; rendered "the tribunal there."
[^3]: Greek *οὐ παρὰ πόδας καὶ εὐθέως τὴν τιμωρίαν ἐπιτίθησι*. The idiom *παρὰ πόδας* is literally "beside the feet," i.e. "on the spot, forthwith, immediately"; paired here with *εὐθέως* ("straightway"). Rendered "at once and immediately" to carry both members.
[^4]: Greek *φημὶ δὴ σκανδαλίζῃ* — "I mean [namely], should scandalize / cause to stumble." *σκανδαλίζω* is the technical Gospel term for placing a stumbling-block; here (and in *σκανδαλιζόμενοι* just below) rendered "give offense" / "being scandalized" to keep the cognate visible. Theophylact reads the "beating of fellow servants" figuratively as the wounding of their *conscience* (*συνείδησις*) through scandal.
[^5]: Theophylact opens a *μὲν … δὲ* here (*οἱ γὰρ ἀρχόμενοι … πλήσσονται* stands as the "on the one hand" member) but the answering *δὲ* is absorbed into the resumptive *ὁ τοιοῦτος οὖν* ("such a one, therefore"). I mark the *μὲν* with "on the one hand," the sentence's structure making the antithesis with the ruler's abuse. *οἱ ἀρχόμενοι* = "those who are ruled, the subjects" (present participle of *ἄρχομαι*), set against *οἱ ἄρχοντες*, "the rulers." (OCR: *βλέοντες* → *βλέποντες*; *ποὺς ἄρχοντας* → *τοὺς ἄρχοντας*; both secure.)
[^6]: Greek *ἄλλο ὄντες καὶ ἄλλο φαινόμενοι* — "being one thing (*ἄλλο*) and appearing another (*ἄλλο*)." This closes the play on *ὑποκριταί*: a *ὑποκριτής* is both "hypocrite" and, literally, a stage-actor who is other than the mask he wears — precisely the person "one thing in being, another in appearance." (OCR in this lemma, all secure: *ὧποκριτῶν* → *ὑποκριτῶν*; *γυμνωθήσεσαι* → *γυμνωθήσεται*; *φα2ήσεται* → *φανήσεται*; *xρονίζει* → *χρονίζει*.)

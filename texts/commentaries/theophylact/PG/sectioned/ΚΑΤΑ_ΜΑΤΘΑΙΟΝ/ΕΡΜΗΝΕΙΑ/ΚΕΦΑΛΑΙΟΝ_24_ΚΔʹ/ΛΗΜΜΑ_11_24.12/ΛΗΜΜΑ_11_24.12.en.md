# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 11 · Matt. 24:9–13 (exposition)

*This lemma carries no scripture quotation of its own — only a stray opening quotation-mark stands at its head; it is Theophylact's exposition of the words quoted in Lemmata 8–10 (Matt. 24:9–13).*[^1]

He foretells the evils that are to befall, emboldening the disciples through the foretelling. For the unexpected is wont to terrify and to trouble. Therefore he smooths away[^2] the fear beforehand through announcing in advance the terrible things to come — the envyings, the enmities, the stumblings, the false prophets, who are forerunners of the Antichrist, leading the many astray, so as to hurl them headlong[^3] toward every form of lawlessness; and because of the multiplying of the lawlessness *arising* from the deception of the Antichrist, men will be made brutish,[^4] so as not to preserve even toward their nearest *kin* any trace of love,[^5] but to be delivered up by one another. But he who endures unto *the* end, and bears *it* steadfastly, and does not yield to the things brought upon *him*, will be saved, having shown himself an approved soldier.[^6]

[^1]: The source begins with a lone «, evidently the misplaced remnant of the closing quotation-mark of the scripture block (Matt. 24:9–13) whose fragments run across Lemmata 8, 9, and 10. No Gospel words are quoted here; the file is pure exposition.
[^2]: Greek *προομαλίζει* ("levels/smooths in advance"), from *ὁμαλός* ("even, level") — he flattens the ground of fear ahead of time so that it will not shake the disciples when the events arrive.
[^3]: Greek *ἐκτραχηλίζειν* — literally "to break the neck, throw headlong" (as a horse throws its rider); metaphorically to plunge someone recklessly into ruin.
[^4]: Greek *θηριωθήσονται* ("will be turned into beasts, made savage"), from *θηρίον* ("wild beast") — men lose their humanity under the Antichrist's deception.
[^5]: Greek *ἰκμάδα τινὰ ἀγάπης* — *ἰκμάς* is "moisture, sap, vital fluid"; the image is that not even a drop or trace of the sap of love is kept. Rendered "any trace of love" for sense; the underlying metaphor is of moisture drying up.
[^6]: Greek *ὡς δόκιμος στρατιώτης ἀναφανείς* — *δόκιμος* means "tested and approved, proven" (as metal assayed and found genuine); *ἀναφανείς* is "having been revealed/shown forth." The one who endures is revealed as a proven soldier.

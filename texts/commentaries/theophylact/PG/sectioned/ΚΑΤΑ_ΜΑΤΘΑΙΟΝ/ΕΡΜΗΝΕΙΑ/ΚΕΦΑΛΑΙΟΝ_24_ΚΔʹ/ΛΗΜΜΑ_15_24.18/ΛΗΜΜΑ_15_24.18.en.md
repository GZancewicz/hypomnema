# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 24:18b

> **…turn back to take his garments. (Matt. 24:18)**[^1]

Hinting at the inescapable excess of the calamities, he says that *one* must flee without turning back, giving thought to none of the *things* that are in the houses, neither garments nor other utensils.

But some, understanding the abomination of desolation *to be* the Antichrist — as *one* who shall come for the desolation of the world and the overthrow of the churches, and *who shall* sit in the temple — understand also these *things* thus: that the *one* on the housetop, that is, *the one* being in the height of the virtues, let him not come down from the height of the virtues so as to take up the *things* of the body. For the body *is* the house of the soul.[^2] And *one* must also withdraw from the field, that is, from the earthly *things*; for the field *is* this life,[^3] and *one must* not even take up the garment, *that is,* the old wickedness which we have put off.

[^1]: This lemma opens by completing the sentence broken off at the end of the previous lemma (Matt. 24:17-18): *…καὶ ὁ ἐν τῷ ἀγρῷ, μὴ ἐπιστρεψάτω ὀπίσω ἆραι τὰ ἱμάτια αὐτοῦ* ("…and let the *one* in the field not turn back to take his garments"). Only the tail *ἐπιστρεψάτω ὀπίσω ἆραι τὰ ἱμάτια αὐτοῦ* stands at the head of this file.
[^2]: Greek *Οἰκία γὰρ τῆς ψυχῆς, τὸ σῶμα* — a verbless sentence; the copula "*is*" is supplied. The word order ("For the house of the soul, the body") is kept close to the Greek.
[^3]: Greek *ἀγρὸς γὰρ ὁ βίος* — again verbless; *βίος* here in the sense of the present worldly life. "*is*" is supplied.

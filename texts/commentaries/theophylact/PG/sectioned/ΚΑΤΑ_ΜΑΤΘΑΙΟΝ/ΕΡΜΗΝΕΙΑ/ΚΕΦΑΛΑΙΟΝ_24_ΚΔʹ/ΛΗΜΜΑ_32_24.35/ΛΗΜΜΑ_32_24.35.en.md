# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 32 · Matt. 24:34–35

> **…heaven and the earth shall pass away, but my words shall by no means pass away.**[^1] (Matt. 24:35)

By "generation" here he means not the one existing then, but that of the faithful; as if saying this: The generation of the faithful shall by no means pass away, until all these things come to pass. For do not, because you hear of famines and pestilences, suppose that the generation of the faithful will be destroyed by these evils, but it will remain, and nothing terrible will overpower it.

But some understood the *all these things* as concerning only the capture of Jerusalem, and not also concerning the second coming; whence they interpret thus: "This generation shall by no means pass away" — that is, the generation of you the apostles will see the calamities of Jerusalem.[^2] And confirming the things *he had* said, he says: It is easier for heaven and earth to be made to vanish — these fixed and immovable elements — than for any of the things spoken by me to prove false.

But concerning the[^3]

[^1]: Greek «ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ ἡ γῆ παρελεύσονται, οἱ δὲ λόγοι μου οὐ μὴ παρέλθωσιν». This lemma opens by completing the citation broken off in Lemma 31 (which ended at the bare article «ὁ»); the leading ellipsis marks that join. The particle *δὲ* ("but") is rendered as a present word (roman); the emphatic *οὐ μὴ* is again given as "by no means."
[^2]: Theophylact reports two readings of «ταῦτα πάντα» ("all these things") without fully adjudicating: (a) his own — "this generation" = the enduring generation of the faithful, who survive all the eschatological woes; (b) that of "some" (*τινές*) — the phrase refers to Jerusalem's fall alone, so "this generation" = the generation of the apostles, who would live to witness it. The OCR reads «Δμῶν», securely = *ὑμῶν* ("of you," i.e. you apostles); the reconstruction is certain.
[^3]: The exposition breaks off at «Περὶ δὲ τῆς» ("But concerning the…"). This is in fact the opening of the next Gospel citation (Matt. 24:36, «Περὶ δὲ τῆς ἡμέρας ἐκείνης καὶ τῆς ὥρας…» — "But concerning that day and hour…"), which is taken up and expounded in Lemma 33.

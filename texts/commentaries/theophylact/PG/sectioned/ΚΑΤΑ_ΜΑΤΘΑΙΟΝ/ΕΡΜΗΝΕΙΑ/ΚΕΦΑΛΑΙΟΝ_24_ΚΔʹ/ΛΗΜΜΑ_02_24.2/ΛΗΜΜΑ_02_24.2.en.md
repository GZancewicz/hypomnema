# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 2 · Matt. 24:2

> **…*Jesus* said to them: Do you see all these things?[^1] Amen I say to you, there shall by no means be left here stone upon stone, which shall not be thrown down. (Matt. 24:2)**

Having gone out from the temple, he showed that he would remove *himself* from the Jews. And just as he said, «Your house is left to you desolate,»[^2] so indeed he also does *it*. And he foretells to the disciples the destruction of the temple; for since those *men*, minding earthly things, were carried away by the beauties of the buildings, and were, as it were, pointing out to Christ, "Behold, of what a house you are leaving the beauties desolate," he leads them away from being nailed *fast* to the earthly things, and sends them onward to the Jerusalem above, saying, "There shall by no means remain here stone upon stone." But this he said, hinting at the utter destruction of the building, and making the statement hyperbolic.[^3]

[^1]: «Βλέπετε ταῦτα πάντα;» — Theophylact's text lacks the «οὐ» ("Do you not see all these things?") of the common Gospel reading. Without the negative, «Βλέπετε» can be read either as an indicative question ("Do you see all these things?") or as an imperative ("See all these things"); rendered here as a question, following his wording as it stands.
[^2]: Greek «Ἀφίεται ὑμῖν ὁ οἶκος ὑμῶν ἔρημος» (cf. Matt. 23:38). The OCR reads «καθὼς εἶπεν ὅτε» ("just as he said when"); «ὅτε» is almost certainly a slip for the recitative «ὅτι» introducing the quotation ("just as he said, that…"), and is so rendered.
[^3]: «ὑπερβολικὸν τὸν λόγον ποιούμενος» — i.e. casting the saying in the form of hyperbole; «αἰνιττόμενος» = "hinting at / darkly signifying." The phrase «τοῦ μὴ προσηλῶσθαι τοῖς ἐπιγείοις» ("from being nailed fast to earthly things") uses the perfect passive of «προσηλόω» ("nail to, rivet").

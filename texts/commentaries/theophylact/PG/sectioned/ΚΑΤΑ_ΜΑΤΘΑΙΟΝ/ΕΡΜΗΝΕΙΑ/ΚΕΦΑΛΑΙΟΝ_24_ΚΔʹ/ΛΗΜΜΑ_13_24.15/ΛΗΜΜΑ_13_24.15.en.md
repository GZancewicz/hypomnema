# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 13 · Matt. 24:15

> **When therefore you see the abomination of desolation, the *thing* spoken through Daniel the prophet, standing[^1] in *the* holy place, let the *one* reading understand. (Matt. 24:15)**

By "abomination of desolation" he means the statue[^2] of the *one* who had gained possession of the city, which he set up in the inner sanctuary[^3] of the temple. "Of desolation," on the one hand, therefore, because the city was laid waste; but "abomination," on the other hand, because the Hebrews used to call the statues and the images of men "abominations," hating idolatry.

[^1]: Greek *ἑστὼς* is masculine, while its antecedent *βδέλυγμα* ("abomination") is neuter — the grammatical mismatch, present already in the Gospel tradition (Mark 13:14 has the neuter *ἑστηκότα*/*ἑστὸς* in various witnesses, and the participle is variously construed with the masculine person represented by the statue). Rendered neutrally as "standing"; the concord is left as it stands in the text.
[^2]: Greek *στήλη* — a set-up block, pillar, or monument, here a commemorative/honorific statue of the conqueror. Rendered "statue"; "pillar" or "monument" would also be defensible.
[^3]: Greek *ἐν τοῖς ἀδύτοις τοῦ ναοῦ* — literally "in the innermost/not-to-be-entered *parts* of the temple," i.e. the sanctuary (the Holy of Holies), rendered "in the inner sanctuary."

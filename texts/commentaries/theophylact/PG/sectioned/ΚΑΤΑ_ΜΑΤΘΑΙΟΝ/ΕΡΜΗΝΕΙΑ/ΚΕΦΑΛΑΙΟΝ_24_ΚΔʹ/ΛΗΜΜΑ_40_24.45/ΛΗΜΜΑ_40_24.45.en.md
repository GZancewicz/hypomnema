# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 40 · Matt. 24:45–47

> **Who then is the faithful and prudent slave, whom his lord set over his household attendants,[^1] to give them the food in season? Blessed *is* that slave, whom his lord, having come, will find doing thus. Truly I say to you that he will set him over all his possessions. (Matt. 24:45–47)**

The Lord raises *the question*, in perplexity,[^2] as to who will be the faithful and prudent slave, whom his lord set over his household attendants, in order that he might show that such a *man is* rare and hard to find. And two *things* are demanded of every steward: faith[fulness] and prudence.[^3] For whether, on the one hand, he is faithful and steals nothing but is not prudent, but in vain squanders the money; or, on the other hand, being supposedly prudent, he himself steals — *there is* no benefit. Accordingly, the one who will then be found faithful and prudent, he will attain also the more perfect *things* — I mean indeed the kingdom of the heavens. For the saints will be heirs of all the things belonging to God.

And a faithful and prudent slave *is* also every teacher who in season gives the food to each of those taught by him — such as Paul, now on the one hand giving milk to drink,[^4] now on the other hand speaking wisdom;[^5] and being a faithful minister, he who *was* formerly a blasphemer,[^6] and prudent, as not being ignorant of the devices of the enemy.[^7] But also everyone who has received anything whatever from God — whether money, whether dominion, whether office — ought to administer these things both faithfully and prudently, as *one who will* give account.

[^1]: Greek *ἐπὶ τῆς θεραπείας αὐτοῦ*. *θεραπεία* here means the body of household attendants, the domestic staff; the concrete sense is required by the following *τοῦ διδόναι αὐτοῖς τὴν τροφήν* ("to give *them* the food"), the attendants set under the slave's charge. The Byzantine text has *θεραπείας*, where the Alexandrian text reads *οἰκετείας* ("household").
[^2]: Greek *διαπορεῖ ὁ Κύριος* — *διαπορέω*, "to be at a loss, to be perplexed"; here of a deliberately posed, searching question ("Who *then* is …?") whose difficulty itself makes the point that such a servant is rare.
[^3]: Greek *πίστιν καὶ φρόνησιν* — the two nouns answering to the adjectives *πιστός* ("faithful") and *φρόνιμος* ("prudent") of the lemma. *πίστις* is rendered "faith[fulness]" to keep the connection to the "faithful slave" while marking that here it is trustworthiness in stewardship.
[^4]: An allusion to 1 Cor. 3:2 (*γάλα ὑμᾶς ἐπότισα*, "I gave you milk to drink").
[^5]: An allusion to 1 Cor. 2:6 (*σοφίαν … λαλοῦμεν*, "we speak wisdom").
[^6]: An allusion to 1 Tim. 1:13 (Paul "formerly a blasphemer," *πρότερον … βλάσφημον*).
[^7]: Greek *τὰ νοήματα τοῦ ἐχθροῦ* — an echo of 2 Cor. 2:11 (*οὐ … ἀγνοοῦμεν τὰ νοήματα αὐτοῦ*, "we are not ignorant of his devices/thoughts"), of Satan; *νοήματα* = "designs, thoughts, schemes."

# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 33 · Matt. 24:36

> **…*that* day and the hour no one knows, not even the angels of the heavens, except my Father alone.**[^1] (Matt. 24:36)

He teaches the disciples here not to seek to learn the things that surpass human knowledge. For in saying "not even the angels," he held them back from learning now that which even the angels do not know; and in saying that "my Father alone," he prevented them even from seeking to learn *it* for the time thereafter. For if he had said, "On the one hand I know, but on the other I do not wish to tell you," they would have been grieved, as though *being* scorned by him; but now, in saying that "Not even the Son knows, except the Father alone," he prevents them from seeking[^2] — just as fathers also, often holding something in their hands, then being asked for it by their children and not wishing to give it, hide it, saying, "We do not have what you seek," and thus the children cease their whimpering. So also the Lord, in order to stop the apostles who wished to learn about the day and the hour, said that "Not even I know, except the Father alone."

For that he also himself knows that hour and that day is clear indeed from many other *things*. For all things whatsoever the Father has are the Son's; and *the Father* also has the knowledge of the day;[^3] therefore the Son too has this. But *it is* more evident from this: for how is it possible for the Son to be ignorant of the day, to whom the things before the day are thus known? For he who has led *us* up to the vestibule, *it is* plain, knew the door also;[^4] but expediently he did not open it — for it is not expedient for us to know when the consummation *is*, lest we grow slack. For ignorance makes us alert.

[^1]: Greek «…ἡμέρας ἐκείνης καὶ τῆς ὥρας οὐδεὶς οἶδεν, οὐδὲ οἱ ἄγγελοι τῶν οὐρανῶν, εἰ μὴ ὁ Πατήρ μου μόνος». The citation opens mid-phrase because its head, «Περὶ δὲ τῆς» ("But concerning…"), was carried onto the end of Lemma 32; the supplied "*that*" renders the demonstrative *ἐκείνης* and the ellipsis marks the join. Theophylact's text here reads "except my Father alone," but immediately below he quotes the clause in the fuller form «Οὐδὲ ὁ Υἱὸς οἶδεν» ("Not even the Son knows"), the reading (cf. Mark 13:32) on which his whole argument turns.
[^2]: The whole passage hinges on the variant "Not even the Son knows, except the Father alone" (*Οὐδὲ ὁ Υἱὸς οἶδεν, εἰ μὴ ὁ Πατὴρ μόνος*). Theophylact takes it not as real ignorance in the Son but as a pedagogical withholding, which he then argues against on doctrinal grounds; the rendering keeps his quoted clauses verbatim rather than harmonizing them.
[^3]: The Greek as transmitted is elliptical: «καὶ τὴν γνῶσιν δὲ ἔχει τῆς ἡμέρας· καὶ ὁ Υἱὸς ἄρα ἔχει ταύτην». The subject of the first clause is unexpressed and is the Father (the syllogism runs: all the Father has is the Son's; the Father has knowledge of the day; therefore the Son has it). "*the Father*" is supplied for sense; the reconstruction is secure from the argument.
[^4]: A vestibule/door image: *πρόθυρα* ("forecourt, the space before the door") set against *θύρα* ("the door" itself). Knowing "the things before the day" is the vestibule; the "day" is the door. The one who brought us as far as the forecourt plainly knew the door too — hence the Son, who foretold the signs preceding the day, is not ignorant of the day, but chooses not to "open" it. Greek «ὁ γὰρ ἐπὶ τὰ πρόθυρα ἀγαγών… καὶ τὴν θύραν ἠπίστατο· ἀλλὰ συμφερόντως οὐκ ἤνοιξε ταύτην».

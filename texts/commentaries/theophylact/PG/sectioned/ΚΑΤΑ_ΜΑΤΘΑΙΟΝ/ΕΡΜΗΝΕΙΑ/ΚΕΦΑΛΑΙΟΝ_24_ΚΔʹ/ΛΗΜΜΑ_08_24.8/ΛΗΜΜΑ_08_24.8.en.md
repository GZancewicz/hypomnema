# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 24:6–8

> **But all these *things are the* beginning of birth-pangs. (Matt. 24:8)**[^1]

By "wars" he means those of the Romans at Jerusalem. And he says that not only wars will come to be, but also famines and pestilences, showing that the wrath against the Jews will be God-driven.[^2] For the wars, on the one hand, one might say come about from the malice of men;[^3] but the famines and pestilences, on the other hand, *come* from nowhere else at all than from God. Then, so that they might not suppose that the world will be consummated before they preach, he says: "Do not be troubled."[^4] For the consummation *is* not yet; for neither will the common consummation[^5] be at the same time as the razing of Jerusalem. And he says that nation will be raised up against nation, and kingdom against kingdom, on account of the evils that are to come upon the Jews, which are *the* beginning of birth-pangs. For just as the birth-pangs come upon the woman giving birth, and thus she brings forth, so also the present age, after the confusions and the wars, will give birth to the things to come,

> **Then shall they deliver you up unto affliction, and shall kill you; and you shall be hated by all the nations on account of my name; and then (Matt. 24:9)**[^6]

[^1]: The lemma-head carries the closing words of the scripture block begun in Lemmata 6–7: Greek *πάντα δὲ ταῦτα ἀρχὴ ὠδίνων* ("but all these things *are the* beginning of birth-pangs"). The sentence is verbless in Greek; the supplied forms of "to be" and the article "*the*" are italicized. *ὠδῖνες* are specifically the pangs of childbirth, a metaphor Theophylact develops below.
[^2]: Greek *θεήλατος* ("driven/sent by God, God-inflicted"), of the *ὀργή* ("wrath") against the Jews — that is, the divine visitation is not merely human misfortune.
[^3]: Greek *ἀπὸ τῆς τῶν ἀνθρώπων ἐπηρείας* — *ἐπήρεια* denotes "malice, spiteful injury, insolent abuse"; here the wars are traced to human spite, in contrast to the famines and pestilences, which come from God.
[^4]: Greek *Μὴ θορυβεῖσθε* ("Do not be thrown into an uproar / troubled"), Theophylact's paraphrase of the Lord's *μὴ θροεῖσθε* ("be not alarmed," Matt. 24:6, Lemma 6).
[^5]: Greek *ἡ κοινὴ συντέλεια* ("the common/general consummation") — the end of the world, as distinct from the local "end" (the fall of Jerusalem); *συντέλεια* is the word rendered "consummation" throughout.
[^6]: The lemma closes by opening the next scripture block (Matt. 24:9), breaking off at "and then"; the quotation continues in Lemma 9, and its exposition is given in Lemma 11.

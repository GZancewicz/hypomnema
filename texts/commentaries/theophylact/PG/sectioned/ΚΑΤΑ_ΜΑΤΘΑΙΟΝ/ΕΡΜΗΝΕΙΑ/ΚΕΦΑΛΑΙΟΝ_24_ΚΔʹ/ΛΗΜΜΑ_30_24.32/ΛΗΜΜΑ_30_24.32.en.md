# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 30 · Matt. 24:32–33

> **…*the* fig tree learn the parable: When already its branch becomes tender, and it puts forth the leaves, ye know that summer *is* near. So also ye, when ye see all these things, know that it is near, at *the* doors. (Matt. 24:32–33)**[^1]

When, he says, all these things happen, the interval between the consummation and my coming will not be great. And by "summer" he means the age to come, and *a* calm after *the* storm — but for the righteous; since for the sinners, at least, it is rather winter and turmoil. As therefore, he says, seeing the branches and the leaves of the fig tree, ye hope for summer, so also, seeing those signs which I spoke of — the sun and the moon being altered — look forward to my coming.

[^1]: The lemma begins mid-quotation with Greek *συκῆς* ("fig tree"); the preceding words *Ἀπὸ δὲ τῆς* ("But from the—") stand at the end of Lemma 29. Restored, Matt. 24:32 opens «Ἀπὸ δὲ τῆς συκῆς μάθετε τὴν παραβολήν». The blockquote gives the words this folder's `.txt` actually carries, beginning at "*the* fig tree." *Ἐπὶ θύραις* ("at *the* doors") is idiomatic for "close at hand, imminent"; rendered literally to preserve the image.

# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 39 · Matt. 24:43–44

> **But know this, that if the master of the house had known in what watch the thief comes, he would have watched, and would not have allowed his house to be broken into. On account of this you also, become ready, for in *the* hour you do not think, the Son of man comes. (Matt. 24:43–44)**[^1]

He enjoins *us* to keep awake and to prepare *ourselves* — that is, to have the works of virtue stored up beforehand — so that, when the Lord comes demanding some of the things which he wills, we may have *wherewith* to give. But see how he did not say, "I do not know in what hour the thief comes," but, "You do not know"; and by "thief" he names the consummation,[^2] and the death of each *person*; and he hints from this that his coming will be also by night. Just as, therefore, the thief comes unseen, so also *is* my coming[^3] — so that do not be slack, but be sober. For if we knew when our end will be, we would be eager to be well-pleasing to God on that day alone; but now, being ignorant, we are ever sober toward the works of virtue.

[^1]: Minor OCR corruptions in the lemma are securely recoverable: *ἐγρηγόρησεν ἂν* ("he would have watched"), *διορυγῆναι τὴν οἰκίαν αὐτοῦ* ("that his house be broken into/dug through"; OCR *ρἰκίαν* → *οἰκίαν*), *Διὰ τοῦτο καὶ ὑμεῖς γίνεσθε ἕτοιμοι* ("on account of this you also become ready"; OCR *Αιὲ … ὑιμεῖς*), *ᾗ ὥρᾳ οὐ δοκεῖτε* ("in the hour you do not think"), *ὁ Υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἔρχεται* ("the Son of man comes"). *φυλακή* is the night-"watch," retained here since Theophylact's point below is that the coming will be "by night."
[^2]: Greek OCR *τὴν αυντίληιαν* = *τὴν συντέλειαν*, "the consummation / end (of the age)"; secured by the immediately following *καὶ τὸν ἑκάστου θάνατον* ("and the death of each *person*"). Theophylact glosses the "thief" as both the general consummation of the world and each individual's death.
[^3]: Greek *ἡ ἐμὴ παρουσία* — Theophylact shifts abruptly into Christ's own voice ("my coming"), so the sentence is the Lord speaking in the first person; retained as it stands.

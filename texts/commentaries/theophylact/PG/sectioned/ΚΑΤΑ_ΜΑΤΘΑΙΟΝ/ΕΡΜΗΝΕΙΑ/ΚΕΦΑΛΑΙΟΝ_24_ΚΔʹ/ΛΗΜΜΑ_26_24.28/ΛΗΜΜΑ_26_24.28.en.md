# Matthew 24 (ΚΔʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 24:28

> **…*the* corpse, there shall the eagles be gathered together. (Matt. 24:28)**[^1]

If the deceivers come, he says, saying that, "Christ has come, but he is hidden in the wilderness, or in some house, and in the chambers and the more inward places of it," do not be deceived; for the coming of Christ does not need the one who will point *it* out, but will be manifest to all, just as also *the* lightning *is*. For this too is both sudden and plainly evident to all; so therefore that *coming* also will be manifest to all those in the world. For not as in the former coming, *when* he passed from place to place, *will it be* so also in the second; but in *the* twinkling of an eye he will be present.

And just as upon a dead body the eagles gather swiftly — the vultures, that is — so also, wherever Christ may be, all the saints will come, those who fly to the heights and who will be caught up in the clouds as eagles. And Christ *is* by all means a "corpse,"[^2] on account of his falling for *the* world and dying; as also Simeon says: "This *one* is set for *the* falling."[^3]

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence broken off at the end of Lemma 25: full Matt. 24:28 reads «ὅπου γὰρ ἐὰν ᾗ τὸ πτῶμα, ἐκεῖ συναχθήσονται οἱ ἀετοί» ("For wherever the corpse may be, there shall the eagles be gathered together"). Greek *πτῶμα* is literally "a fallen thing," hence "corpse / carcass" — a sense Theophylact exploits below (see note 2). The blockquote gives only the words this folder's `.txt` actually carries.
[^2]: Theophylact turns on a word-chain lost in English: *πτῶμα* ("corpse," lit. "a fallen thing") is linked to *πεσεῖν* ("to fall") and to *πτῶσιν* ("falling") in the Simeon citation. His point: Christ is called the "corpse" to which the eagle-saints flock because he "fell" (died) for the world. The clause is OCR-corrupt in the source — «διὰ τὸ πεσεῖν ὑπὰρ κοῶπν αὶ ἀποθανεῖν» — securely reconstructed as **«διὰ τὸ πεσεῖν ὑπὲρ κόσμου καὶ ἀποθανεῖν»** ("on account of falling for *the* world and dying").
[^3]: Luke 2:34, Simeon's words over the infant Christ: «οὗτος κεῖται εἰς πτῶσιν καὶ ἀνάστασιν πολλῶν» ("this *one* is set for the falling and rising of many"). Theophylact cites only the first half, «οὗτος κεῖται εἰς πτῶσιν», for the sake of the *πτῶμα / πτῶσις* echo.

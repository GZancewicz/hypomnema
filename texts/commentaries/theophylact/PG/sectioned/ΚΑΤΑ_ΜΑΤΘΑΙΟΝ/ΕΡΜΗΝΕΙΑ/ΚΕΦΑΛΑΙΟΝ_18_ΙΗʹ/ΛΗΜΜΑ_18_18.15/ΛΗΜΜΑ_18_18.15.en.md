# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 18:22

> **Jesus says to him: I say not to you, Until seven times, but, Until seventy times seven. (Matt. 18:22)**

This is what Peter asks: How often, if he sin, then come and ask pardon, repenting, shall I forgive him?[^1] And he added the *phrase* "if he sin against me"; for if he sin against God, I the layman[^2] cannot forgive him — unless perhaps the priest, who holds the rank of God;[^3] but if he sin against me, then *if* I forgive him, he will be forgiven, even though I am a layman and not a priest.

And the *phrase* "until seventy times seven" he said, not that he might enclose forgiveness within a number. For it is irrational for someone to sit counting until four hundred and ninety be reached (for so many[^4] *are* the seventy times seven), but here he signifies the unlimited — as if he were saying: As often as, having stumbled, he repents, forgive him. For this too he says through the parable that follows:[^5] that we must be compassionate.[^6]

[^1]: Theophylact recasts Peter's question (from Lemma 17, Matt. 18:21) to make explicit that forgiveness presupposes the offender's repentance and request for pardon.
[^2]: Greek *ἰδιώτης* — "a private person, a layman," one without office, here specifically a non-cleric, contrasted with the priest (*ἱερεύς*). Not "idiot / ignorant" in the modern sense.
[^3]: Greek *ὁ Θεοῦ τάξιν ἔχων ὁ ἱερεύς* — "the priest, who has the rank / order / office of God," i.e. who stands in God's place (with authority to forgive sins against God). *τάξις* is "rank, order, appointed station."
[^4]: Greek *τετρακόσια ἐννενήκοντα … τοσαῦτα γὰρ τὰ ἑβδομηκοντάκις ἑπτά* — Theophylact takes "seventy times seven" as 70 × 7 = 490 ("four hundred ninety"); he then denies that any literal count is meant, the number standing for the infinite.
[^5]: The reference is forward to the parable of the unforgiving servant (Matt. 18:23ff.), expounded in the following lemma.
[^6]: Greek *συμπαθεῖς εἶναι* — "to be compassionate / sympathetic," literally "to suffer-with, to feel-with" others. The opening words are OCR-garbled (*το γὰρ καὶ διὰ τῆς ἐξῆς παραβολῆς, λέγει, ὅτι…*); the sense — "for [this] too he says through the following parable, that we must be compassionate" — is secure, and the clause closes the lemma as it turns toward the coming parable.

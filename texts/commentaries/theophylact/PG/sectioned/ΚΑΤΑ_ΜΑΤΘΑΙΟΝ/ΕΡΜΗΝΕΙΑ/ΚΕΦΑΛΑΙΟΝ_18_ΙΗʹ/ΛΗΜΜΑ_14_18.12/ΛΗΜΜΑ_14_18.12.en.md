# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 18:12-14

> **What think you? If a hundred sheep come to be to some man, and one of them go astray, does he not, leaving the ninety-nine upon the mountains, going *forth* seek the one going astray? And if it come to pass to find it, amen I say to you, that he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that have not gone astray. So there is no will before your Father who *is* in the heavens, that one of these little ones should perish.[^3] (Matt. 18:12-14)**

To what man did a hundred sheep come to be? To Christ. For all the rational creation, both angels and men, are the hundred sheep; of whom Christ *is* shepherd, not a sheep. For he is not a creature, but Son of God. This one, therefore, left the ninety-nine in heaven; but having taken *the* form of a servant, he came to seek the one sheep, which is human nature. And he rejoices over this one, more[^1] than over the steadfastness of the angels. This *parable*, in a comprehensive way,[^2] hints that God takes care for the conversion of sinners, and rejoices over these more than over those who have virtue *as* permanent.

[^1]: Greek *χαίρει ἐπὶ τούτῳ, ἢ ἐπὶ τῇ τῶν ἀγγέλων στερρότητι* — the comparative *μᾶλλον* ("more") is not written but is carried by the construction *χαίρει … ἢ* ("rejoices over … than"), echoing the lemma's *χαίρει … μᾶλλον ἢ*; supplied in italics.
[^3]: Greek «οὐκ ἔστι θέλημα ἔμπροσθεν τοῦ Πατρὸς ὑμῶν» — literally "there is not a will before your Father." *ἔστι θέλημα* is existential ("there is a wish"), not a copula ("it is a will"); θέλημα is anarthrous, and *ἔμπροσθεν* ("before, in the presence of") reflects a Hebraic idiom (a wish "before / on the part of" someone). The sense: it is not your Father's will that one of these little ones perish.
[^2]: Greek *συλληπτικῶς αἰνίττεται* — the adverb *συλληπτικῶς* means "in a comprehensive / summary / all-embracing way, taking things together"; the sense is that the parable gathers up and intimates a general truth (God's care for sinners) rather than spelling it out. Alternatively "summarily, by way of summary."

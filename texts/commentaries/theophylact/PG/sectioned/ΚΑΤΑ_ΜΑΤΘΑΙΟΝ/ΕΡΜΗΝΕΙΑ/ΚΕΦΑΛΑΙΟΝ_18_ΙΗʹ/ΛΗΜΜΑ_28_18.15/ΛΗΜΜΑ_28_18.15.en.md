# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 18:34

> **…his lord, being angered, delivered him to the tormentors, until he should pay back all that was owed to him. (Matt. 18:34)**[^1]

The master enters into judgment[^2] with the servant on account of *his* love of mankind, so that he might show that it is not he, but the servant's cruelty that overturns the gift, and *his* ingratitude. But to what tormentors does he deliver *him*? To certain punitive powers,[^3] so that he may be punished eternally. For the *saying*, "Until he should pay back what is owed," means this: that let him be punished until then, until he pays back; but truly he will never pay back what is owed — that is, the owed and deserved punishment; therefore he will be punished forever.[^4]

[^1]: The lemma corresponds to Matt. 18:34; it opens with the *Καὶ* ("And") carried over from the end of Lemma 27, here rendered as the resumed clause. Greek *ὀργισθεὶς* ("having been angered, in anger"); *βασανισταί* ("tormentors, torturers, those who put to the test"), whence *βασανισταῖς* below; *ἕως οὗ ἀποδῷ πᾶν τὸ ὀφειλόμενον* ("until he should pay back all that is owed").
[^2]: Greek *Κρίνεται… πρὸς τὸν δοῦλον*. The verb *κρίνεται* is ambiguous: it can be passive ("is being judged, is called to account") or middle ("submits *himself* to judgment, enters into a reckoning"). The point is that the master, out of *φιλανθρωπία* ("love of mankind"), lets his dealing with the servant be examined, to show the fault lies with the servant. Rendered "enters into judgment with"; alternatively "is brought to judgment in regard to the servant."
[^3]: Greek OCR «Τιμωρητικαῖς τάνα δυνάμεσιν», securely = *τιμωρητικαῖς τισι δυνάμεσιν* ("to certain punitive/avenging powers"). Theophylact glosses the "tormentors" of the parable as spiritual powers charged with retribution.
[^4]: Theophylact reads the "until" (*ἕως οὗ*) as marking not a term that will be reached but one that never can be: since the debt of "the owed and deserved punishment" can never be discharged, the punishment is unending. Greek *ἀεὶ ἄρα κολασθήσεται* — "therefore he will be punished always/forever."

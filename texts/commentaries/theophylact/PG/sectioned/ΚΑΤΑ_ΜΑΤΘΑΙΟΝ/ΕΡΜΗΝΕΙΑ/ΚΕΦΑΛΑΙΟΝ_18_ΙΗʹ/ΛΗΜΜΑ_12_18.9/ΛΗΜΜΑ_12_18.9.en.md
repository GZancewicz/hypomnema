# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 18:9

> **… eye cause thee to stumble, pluck it out, and cast *it* from thee: it is good for thee to enter one-eyed[^1] into life, than, having two eyes, to be cast into the gehenna of fire. (Matt. 18:9)**

Understand *by* "hand" and "foot" and "eye" *thy* friends, whom we hold in the rank of members.[^2] If therefore those who harm us should be some such persons—that is, household[^3] friends—we must despise them as rotten[^4] members, and cut *them* off, lest they harm others also. So that from these things it is clear that, even if it is necessary that the stumbling-blocks come—that is, those who harm *us*—yet it is not necessary that we be harmed. For if we do as the Lord said, and cut off from ourselves those who harm us, even if they be friends, we shall not be harmed.

[^1]: Greek *μονόφθαλμον* — "one-eyed"; the received text (KJV) reads "with one eye." Theophylact's clause order is *μονόφθαλμον εἰς τὴν ζωὴν εἰσελθεῖν*, "one-eyed to enter into life."
[^2]: Greek *ἐν τάξει μελῶν* — "in the rank / position of members (limbs)," i.e. we count our dear friends as parts of our own body. *μέλη* = "limbs, members."
[^3]: Greek *οἰκιακοὶ φίλοι* — "friends of the household, domestic / intimate friends," those closest to us.
[^4]: Greek *σεσηπότων* (perfect participle of *σήπω*) — "having rotted, putrefied"; the diseased member must be amputated lest the infection spread.

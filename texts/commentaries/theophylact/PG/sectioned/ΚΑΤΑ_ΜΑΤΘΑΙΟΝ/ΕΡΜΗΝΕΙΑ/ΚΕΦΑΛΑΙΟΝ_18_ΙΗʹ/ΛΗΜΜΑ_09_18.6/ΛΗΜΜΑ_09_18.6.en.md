# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 18:6

> **it is profitable for him that an ass-*driven* millstone be hanged about his neck.[^1] (Matt. 18:6)**

For he brings a perceptible[^2] punishment into the midst, wishing to show that those who insult and cause to stumble the humble ones in Christ will undergo much retribution.[^3] But do thou understand that, even if someone should cause to stumble him who is little according to truth—that is, the weak *one*—and should not by every means bear him up,[^4] he will be punished. For the great *man* is not easily made to stumble,[^5] but the little *one is*.

[^1]: Greek «συμφέρει αὐτῷ ἵνα μύλος ὀνικὸς κρεμασθῇ εἰς τὸν τράχηλον αὐτοῦ». *μύλος ὀνικός* is literally "a donkey-millstone," i.e. the large upper millstone turned by a donkey, as opposed to a small hand-mill; rendered "ass-*driven* millstone." Theophylact's wording here reads *εἰς τὸν τράχηλον* ("about/onto the neck"); in Lemma 6 the same citation appeared with *περὶ τὸν τράχηλον*.
[^2]: Greek *αἰσθητήν* — "perceptible, sensible, apprehended by the senses," the standard antithesis to *νοητός* ("intelligible"). The point is that the Lord adduces a bodily, tangible punishment as an image of the spiritual one.
[^3]: Two near-synonyms for "punishment" occur: *κόλασις* (here rendered "punishment") and *τιμωρία* (here "retribution"), the latter picked up by the cognate verb *τιμωρηθήσεται* ("he will be punished"). The distinction is not pressed by Theophylact.
[^4]: Greek *μὴ πάντα τρόπον ἀναβαστάσῃ* — "should not in every way lift up / support / bear him up." *ἀναβαστάζω* = "to bear up, hold up," here of morally supporting the weak.
[^5]: Greek *οὐκ εὐσκανδάλιστος* — a single adjective, "not easily-caused-to-stumble," coined on *σκανδαλίζω*; the antithesis is left implied for "the little one," so "*is*" is supplied.

# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 25 · Matt. 18:31

> **But when his fellow-servants saw the things that had happened, they were grieved exceedingly. And having come, they made plain to their own lord all the things that had happened. (Matt. 18:31)**[^1]

The angels are shown from this *to be* haters of evil and lovers of good; for these are the fellow-servants.[^2] But they do not tell these things to the Lord as to one *who is* ignorant, but so that you might learn that angels are overseers[^3] of us, and that they are indignant against the inhuman *ones*,[^4]

[^1]: The lemma corresponds to Matt. 18:31. The OCR carries several minor corruptions, all securely recoverable: «ατοῦ» = *αὐτοῦ* ("his"); «ἀλυπήθησαν» = *ἐλυπήθησαν* ("they were grieved"); «ὃιεσάφησαν» = *διεσάφησαν* ("they made plain, reported"). Greek *διασαφέω* means "make clear, declare distinctly, report in full"; rendered "made plain."
[^2]: That is, the "fellow-servants" of the parable signify the angels. Greek *οὗτοι γὰρ οἱ σύνδουλοι* — literally "for these *are* the fellow-servants," the supplied verb "are" being italicized.
[^3]: Greek OCR «φοροι», securely = *ἔφοροι* ("overseers, guardians, watchers"). The word denotes those who watch over or superintend; here of the angels as guardians who observe human conduct.
[^4]: The lemma breaks off mid-sentence at a comma; the thought continues into the following lemma. Greek *ἀπάνθρωπος* ("inhuman, without human feeling, merciless"), cognate with the *φιλανθρωπία* ("love of mankind") that governs this whole exposition.

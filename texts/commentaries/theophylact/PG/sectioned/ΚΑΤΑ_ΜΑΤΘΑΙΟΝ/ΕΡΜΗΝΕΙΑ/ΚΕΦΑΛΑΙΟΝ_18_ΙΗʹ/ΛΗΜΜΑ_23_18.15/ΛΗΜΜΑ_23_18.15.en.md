# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 18:27–28

> **And the Lord of that servant, being moved with compassion,[^1] released him, and forgave him the loan. (Matt. 18:27)**

Behold the strength of repentance, and the loving-kindness[^2] of the lord. For repentance, on the one hand, made the servant fall away from *his* wickedness; for he who stands firmly in wickedness is not to be forgiven. But the loving-kindness of God, on the other hand, forgave completely even the loan, although indeed the servant was not seeking complete forgiveness, but *only* a set respite.[^3] Learn accordingly that God gives even more *things* than *those* we ask for. Since so great is his loving-kindness, that even that which seems harsh — the commanding *that* he be sold — he said not from harshness, but in order that he might strike the servant with terror, and might persuade him to look toward supplication and entreaty.

> **And that servant, having gone out, found one of his fellow-servants, who owed him a hundred denarii, and having seized him he throttled *him*, saying: Pay back to me whatever you owe.[^4] (Matt. 18:28)**

[^1]: Greek *σπλαγχνισθεὶς* — "having been moved in the inward parts (σπλάγχνα)," the visceral verb for compassion; governs the genitive *τοῦ δούλου ἐκείνου* ("with compassion on that servant").
[^2]: Greek *φιλανθρωπία*, literally "love of humankind"; rendered "loving-kindness" throughout. The word anchors the whole comment (repeated of the lord/God) and stands in deliberate contrast to the *ἀπανθρωπία* ("inhumanity") of the servant in Lemma 24.
[^3]: Greek *προθεσμία*, an appointed/fixed term or deadline — here a stay of payment. The point: the servant asked only for *time*, but received total remission.
[^4]: This scripture quotation (Matt. 18:28) stands at the end of the lemma with no exposition; the comment on it opens the next lemma («Ἐξελθὼν ὁ συγχωρηθείς, οὕτω πνίγει τὸν σύνδουλον…»). *ἔπνιγε* is literally "was choking/strangling him"; "throttled" keeps the violence.

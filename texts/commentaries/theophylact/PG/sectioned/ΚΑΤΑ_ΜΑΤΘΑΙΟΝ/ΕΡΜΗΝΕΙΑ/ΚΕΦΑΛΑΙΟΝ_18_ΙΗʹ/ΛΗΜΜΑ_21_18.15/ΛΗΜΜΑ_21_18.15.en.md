# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 18:25

> **and as he did not have *wherewith* to pay, his lord commanded him to be sold, and his wife, and the children and all that he had, and *payment* to be made. (Matt. 18:25)**

Debtors of ten thousand talents *are* we, inasmuch as we are, on the one hand, benefited every *day*, but, on the other hand, give back nothing good to God. Debtors of ten thousand talents *are* also those who have received the presidency over a people and over many men; for each man *is* a talent, according to him who says, "A great thing *is* a man,"[^1] and who then have not used *their* office well. And the debtor's being sold together with *his* wife and his children signifies the estrangement from God; for he who is sold becomes *the property* of another master.

But might not the wife, on the one hand, *be* the flesh, as being consort of the soul; and the children, on the other hand, *be* the deeds wickedly wrought by the soul and the body? He commands, therefore, the flesh to be given to Satan for destruction — that is, to be handed over to diseases or to *the* affliction of a demon; but also the children, I mean indeed the operations of the wicked *deeds*, to be racked.[^2] For example: there is someone's stealing hand; this God withers, or constricts by means of some demon. Behold, then, the wife *is* the flesh, and the children *the deeds*, *chastened*[^3] so that the spirit may be saved; for that man will no longer work thievishly.

[^1]: Greek *Μέγα ἄνθρωπος* ("Great *is* man"), from Prov. 20:6 LXX (*μέγα ἄνθρωπος, καὶ τίμιον ἀνὴρ ἐλεήμων*, "A great thing *is* a man, and an honorable thing a merciful man"). Cited to ground the equation "each man = a talent."
[^2]: Greek *στρεβλωθῆναι*, "to be twisted / racked / tortured" (as on the rack). The verb continues the judicial-punishment imagery: the flesh is "handed over," the deeds "racked."
[^3]: The OCR here reads *Ἰδοὺ οὖν ἡ γυνὴ ἡ σὰρξ, καὶ τὰ τέκνα, ἤτοι τὸ πνεῦμα σωθῇ*, which lacks a main verb and cannot stand as written: *ἤτοι* ("that is to say") ill fits the subjunctive *σωθῇ* ("may be saved"), which requires a purpose particle (ἵνα). The clause plainly means that the flesh and its wicked deeds are given over/chastened *in order that* the spirit may be saved; a verb such as *κολάζονται* ("are chastened") has dropped out. "*chastened* so that" supplies the sense; the exact lost word is uncertain.

# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 4 · Matt. 18:4

> **Whoever therefore shall humble himself as this child, this one is the greater in the kingdom of the heavens;**

The Lord, having seen the disciples gripped[^1] by the passion of vainglory,[^2] restrains them, showing them, through the unpretentious[^3] child, humble-mindedness's[^4] way. For we must be children according to the lowliness of the disposition, not according to the infantile *quality* of the reasoning; and according to guilelessness, not according to senselessness. But *by* the *phrase*, "Unless you turn back," having said *it*, he showed that they had come from humble-mindedness into vainglory. Accordingly, *they* must turn again thither, that is, into the humble-mindedness from which you went out.[^5]

> **And whoever should**[^6]

[^1]: The OCR reads «κραξ τηθέντας»; securely *κρατηθέντας* ("having been held/gripped/mastered").
[^2]: Greek *φιλοδοξία* — literally "love of glory/opinion," i.e. vainglory, love of honor; the passion driving the "Who is greater?" of Lemma 1.
[^3]: Greek *ἄτυφος* — "free from *τῦφος* (vanity, conceit, puffed-up-ness)," hence "unpretentious, unassuming." The OCR «διὰ ποῦ ἀτύφου παιδίοὺ» is securely *διὰ τοῦ ἀτύφου παιδίου*.
[^4]: Greek *ταπεινοφροσύνη* — "lowliness/humility of mind," rendered "humble-mindedness" consistently to keep it distinct from the plain *ταπεινόν* ("lowliness") in the next sentence.
[^5]: The Greek shifts person mid-sentence: the main clause is impersonal/third-person ("one must turn again"), but the relative clause ends *ἀφ᾽ ἧς ἐξήλθετε* — second person plural, "from which **you** went out," addressing the disciples directly. The shift is preserved rather than smoothed.
[^6]: The next Gospel quotation (Matt. 18:5) begins here, «Καὶ ὃς ἐὰν», but is cut by the sectioning tool and continued in Lemma 5 («…should receive one such child…»). Rendered as a fragment; the exposition belongs to the following lemmata.

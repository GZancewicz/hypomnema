# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 18:29–30

> **Therefore his fellow-servant, having fallen at his feet, besought him, saying: Be patient with me, and I will pay back to you all *things*. But he would not; but having gone away he cast him into prison, until he should pay back what was owed. (Matt. 18:29–30)**

The *one* who had been forgiven, having gone out, thus throttles his fellow-servant; for no one who abides in God *is* without compassion, but *only* he who has gone out from God, and is a stranger to him. And so great *was his* inhumanity,[^1] that, though the greater *debt* had been forgiven — for *it was* ten thousand talents — in the case of the lesser — for *it was* a hundred denarii — he did not show mercy at all, nor grant a postponement.[^2] And yet indeed the fellow-servant speaks the very same words, reminding him through *the words* by which he himself had been saved; for "Be patient," he says, "with me, and I will pay back to you."

[^1]: Greek *ἀπανθρωπία*, "inhumanity / want of human feeling" — the deliberate antithesis of the *φιλανθρωπία* ("loving-kindness") of the lord in Lemma 23.
[^2]: The OCR of this sentence is corrupt (*ὥστε τὰ μείζω συγμωρηθέντα … ἐπὶ τοῖς ἐλάττοαινκατὸν γὰρ δηάρια μὴ συγγνωμονῆααι Σλως, μηδὲ ἀναβολὴν δοῦναι*) but securely recoverable as *ὥστε τὰ μείζω συγχωρηθέντα (τάλαντα γὰρ μύρια) ἐπὶ τοῖς ἐλάττοσιν (ἑκατὸν γὰρ δηνάρια) μὴ συγγνωμονῆσαι ὅλως, μηδὲ ἀναβολὴν δοῦναι*: "that, the greater *debt* having been forgiven (for *it was* ten thousand talents), over the lesser (for *it was* a hundred denarii) he showed no mercy at all, nor gave a postponement." The two parenthetical glosses are Theophylact's own. *ἀναβολή* = "delay, deferral," echoing the *προθεσμία* ("respite") the servant himself had begged.

# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 29 · Matt. 18:35

> **So also my heavenly Father will do to you, if you do not forgive, each one his brother from your hearts, their trespasses. (Matt. 18:35)**[^1]

He did not say, "your Father," but, "my Father"; for such people are unworthy to have God *as* father. And he wishes *them* to forgive from the heart, but not from the lips only. And consider also how great an evil is the remembrance of wrongs,[^2] when it overturned the gift of God — and yet the gracious gifts of God are without repentance,[^3] but nevertheless they were overturned.

[^1]: The lemma corresponds to Matt. 18:35. OCR «Οῆσω» = *Οὕτω* ("So, thus"); «ἔὰν» = *ἐὰν* ("if"). Note the number-shift within the verse, present in the Greek: "each one *his* brother… their trespasses" (*ἕκαστος… τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν*), the singular "each one" governing a plural "their," preserved as it stands.
[^2]: Greek *μνησικακία* — literally "remembrance of evil (suffered)," i.e. holding a grudge, harboring resentment, vindictiveness. Rendered "remembrance of wrongs" to keep the etymology visible; the single English word "resentment" or "malice" would flatten it.
[^3]: Greek *ἀμεταμέλητα τὰ χαρίσματα τοῦ Θεοῦ*, echoing Rom. 11:29 (*ἀμεταμέλητα γὰρ τὰ χαρίσματα καὶ ἡ κλῆσις τοῦ Θεοῦ*). The adjective *ἀμεταμέλητος* means "not to be repented of, not regretted, irrevocable" — God does not take back or regret his gifts. Rendered "without repentance" (as at Rom. 11:29 in the older versions); i.e. "irrevocable." The paradox Theophylact presses: gifts that God himself never revokes were nonetheless forfeited by the servant's own unforgivingness.

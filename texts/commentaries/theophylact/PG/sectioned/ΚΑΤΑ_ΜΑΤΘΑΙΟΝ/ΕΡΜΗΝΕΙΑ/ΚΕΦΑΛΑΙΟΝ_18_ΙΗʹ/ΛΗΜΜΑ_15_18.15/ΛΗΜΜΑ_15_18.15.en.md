# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 18:15-17

> **But if your brother sin against you, go and reprove him between you and him alone; if he hear you, you have gained your brother. But if he hear *you* not, take with you yet one or two, that upon the mouth of two witnesses or three every word may stand; but if he disregard them, tell *it* to the Church; but if he disregard also the Church, let him be to you just as the heathen and the publican. (Matt. 18:15-17)**

Having extended a vehement discourse against those who give offense,[^1] now he sets right also those who are offended. For lest you, the one being offended, collapse[^2] utterly — since the one who gives offense has the "woe"[^3] — he says that: I wish you, the one being offended, that is *the one* being harmed, to reprove those who wrong you and harm *you*, if they be Christians. For see what he says: If your brother sin against you — that is, the Christian. For if, on the one hand, an unbeliever wrongs *you*, then withdraw even from the things belonging to you. But if, on the other hand, a brother *wrongs you*, reprove this one; for he did not say, Insult *him*, but, Reprove *him*.

"If he hear you" — that is, condemn himself; for privately he wills that the sinners first be reproved, lest, having been reproved before many, they become more shameless; but if, having been reproved even before two witnesses or three, he be not put to shame,[^4] then before the presidents[^5] of the Church make public the fault. For since he did not hear the two or three — although indeed the law says, that upon two witnesses or three every word stands, that is, is confirmed — let him henceforth be brought to his senses[^6] by the Church; but if he hear not even this, then let him be cast out, lest he impart of his own wickedness also to the others.

And he likens such brothers to the publicans; for the publican *is* a thing held in ill repute;[^7] and *it is* a consolation to the one wronged, that the one wronging be reckoned a publican and a heathen — that is, a sinner and an unbeliever. Is this, then, the only punishment of the one wronging? No. But hear also the things that follow.

[^1]: Greek *σκανδαλίζειν / σκανδαλιζόμενος* — the *σκάνδαλον* word-group runs through the whole passage. It is "to cause to stumble, to trip up, to be a stumbling-block," and by extension "to give / take offense." Theophylact plays the active (*τῶν σκανδαλιζόντων*, those who trip others up) against the passive (*τοὺς σκανδαλιζομένους*, those tripped up / offended / harmed), which he glosses just below with *βλαπτόμενον* ("being harmed"). Rendered "give offense" / "be offended" throughout for consistency.
[^2]: Greek *ἀναπέσῃς* — literally "fall back, sink down, give way, lose heart." The sense is despondency: lest the injured party collapse in discouragement because the offender, not he, bears the "woe."
[^3]: An allusion to Matt. 18:7, *οὐαὶ τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ δι᾽ οὗ τὸ σκάνδαλον ἔρχεται* ("woe to the man through whom the offense comes"): the "woe" falls on the offender, so the offended is not to lose heart.
[^4]: Greek *δυσωπηθῇ* — "be made ashamed, be abashed, be shamed into yielding" (literally "have one's face put out of countenance"). The reproof aims to shame the offender into repentance.
[^5]: Greek *τῶν τῆς Ἐκκλησίας προέδρων* — "the presidents / presiders of the Church," i.e. those who preside (bishops, church rulers). Theophylact glosses the lemma's "tell it to the Church" as bringing the matter before the church's presiding authorities.
[^6]: Greek *σωφρονισθήτω* — "let him be brought to a sound mind, be chastened, be admonished into self-control" (from *σωφροσύνη*). The verb combines correction and the restoration of good sense.
[^7]: Greek *διαβεβλημένον … χρῆμα ὁ τελώνης* (OCR *ιαεβλημένον*) — *διαβεβλημένον* is the perfect passive participle of *διαβάλλω*, "slandered, discredited, held in ill repute"; *χρῆμα* here is loosely "a thing, a business, a matter." The clause: "the publican is a thing/figure of ill repute."

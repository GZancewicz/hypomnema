# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 10 · Matt. 18:7

> **Woe to the world because of the stumbling-blocks! for it is necessary that the stumbling-blocks come; nevertheless woe to that man through whom the stumbling-block comes! (Matt. 18:7)**

He laments the world, as *one who is* loving-of-mankind,[^1] since it is about to be harmed by the stumbling-blocks. But someone might say: And what need is there to lament, when it were fitting *rather* to help and to stretch out a hand? We shall say, therefore, that this very thing—to lament someone—is *also* an *act* of help. For often those whom our exhortation did not benefit, these we benefit when we lament over them, *as they* come to *their* senses.[^2] "And if it is necessary," he says, "that the stumbling-blocks come, how shall we flee them?" It is necessary, on the one hand, that *they* come, but it is not necessary *for us* to perish. For it is possible to set oneself against[^3] the stumbling-blocks; and understand *as* "stumbling-blocks" those who hinder *us* toward the good things,[^4] and *as* "the world" the low and earth-creeping[^5] men, who also are easily hindered.

[^1]: Greek *ὡς φιλάνθρωπος* — "as *one who is* benevolent / loving-of-mankind," a fixed epithet of Christ. The subject of "he laments" (*θρηνεῖ*) is the Lord.
[^2]: Greek *εἰς αἴσθησιν ἐρχομένους* — "coming to perception / awareness," i.e. coming to their senses. The participle agrees with the objects "these" (those lamented over).
[^3]: Greek *ἀντιτάξασθαι* — "to array oneself against, to set oneself in opposition"; a military metaphor.
[^4]: Greek *τοὺς ἐμποδίζοντας πρὸς τὰ καλά* — "those who impede / put an obstacle in the way *of others* toward the good (noble) things." *ἐμποδίζω* ("to entangle the feet, to hinder") echoes the "stumbling" imagery. *τὰ καλά* = "the good/noble things."
[^5]: Greek *χαμερπεῖς* — literally "creeping on the ground, groveling," of men whose minds are earthbound; paired with *τοὺς κάτω ἀνθρώπους* ("the men *who are* below / lowly").

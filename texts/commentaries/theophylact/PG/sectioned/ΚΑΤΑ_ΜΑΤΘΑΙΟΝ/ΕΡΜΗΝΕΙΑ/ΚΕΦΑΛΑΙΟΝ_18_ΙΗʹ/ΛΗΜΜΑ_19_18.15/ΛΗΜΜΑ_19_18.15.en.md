# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 18:23

> **For this reason the kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man, a king, who wished to settle a reckoning[^1] with his servants. (Matt. 18:23)**

The general sense of the parable, on the one hand, teaches *us* to forgive the things sinned against us by our fellow-servants, and especially if those *men*, being in need of forgiveness *themselves*, fall down *before us*. But to expound the parable in detail, on the other hand, would belong only to one having the mind of Christ; nevertheless we too will set our hand *to it*.

The kingdom *is* the Word of God; but *it is* not a kingdom of certain small things, but of *the* heavens. Therefore this *one* was likened to a man, a king, having been made flesh for our sake and having come to be in *the* likeness of men; and he settles a reckoning with his servants, as a good *one* rendering judgment to them. For he does not punish without judgment; for this *is a mark* of savagery.[^2]

[^1]: Greek *συνᾶραι λόγον* (aorist of *συναίρω λόγον*), literally "to take up an account/word together *with*" — the technical idiom for settling accounts or auditing a reckoning. Rendered "settle a reckoning" throughout the parable; the same phrase recurs just below as *συναίρει λόγον*.
[^2]: Greek *ὠμότητος γὰρ τοῦτο* ("for this *is* of savagery/rawness"). *ὠμότης* is literally "rawness," used figuratively for cruelty or brutality; "savagery" preserves the physical metaphor better than the flatter "cruelty."

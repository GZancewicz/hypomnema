# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 1 · Matt. 18:1

> **In that hour the disciples came *to* Jesus, saying: Who then is greater[^1] in the kingdom of the heavens?[^2] (Matt. 18:1)**[^3]

Since they saw Peter honored by Christ — for this one was honored, having been commanded to give the stater[^4] on behalf of Christ and on behalf of himself — for this reason they suffered something human, and, being bitten by envy, they approach obliquely,[^5] asking the Lord: Who is greater?

[^1]: Greek *μείζων* is a comparative, "greater," here functioning as a superlative ("greatest"); rendered "greater" throughout to keep the form Theophylact repeats (the disciples' word and the Lord's answer in Lemma 4 share it).
[^2]: Greek *ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν* — "in the kingdom of the heavens," with *οὐρανῶν* plural, as Theophylact writes it; kept literal rather than the singular "heaven."
[^3]: The lemma is OCR-garbled in the source — «τῷ Ὑησοβ» for *τῷ Ἰησοῦ* ("to Jesus") and «βασιλε ᾳ» for *βασιλείᾳ* ("kingdom"); both are securely recoverable and low-stakes.
[^4]: Greek *τὸν στατῆρα* — the stater, the coin Peter was told to take from the fish's mouth and pay "for me and for you" (Matt. 17:27). That singling-out is the honoring which provokes the others' envy here.
[^5]: Greek *πλαγίως* — "sidelong, obliquely, indirectly"; they do not ask openly but by a slanted, envy-driven approach.

# Matthew 18 (ΙΗʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 18:18-20

> **Amen I say to you, whatsoever you bind upon the earth, shall be bound in heaven, and whatsoever you loose upon the earth, shall be loosed in heaven. (Matt. 18:18)**

If you, he says, the one who has been wronged, hold the one who wrongs *you* as a publican and a heathen, he will be such also in heaven. But if you loose him — that is, forgive *him* — he will be forgiven also in heaven; for not only whatsoever the priests loose are loosed, but whatsoever we too, the ones who have been wronged, either bind or loose, those also will be bound or loosed.

> **Again, amen I say to you, that if two of you agree upon the earth, concerning every matter whatsoever they ask, it shall come to be for them from my Father who *is* in *the* heavens; for where two or three are gathered into my name, there am I in the midst of them. (Matt. 18:19-20)**

He gathers us together, through such words, into love; for since he forbade us from giving offense to one another, and from harming and being harmed, now he speaks also about the concord[^1] toward one another. And "those who agree" are understood *to be* not those who come together for evil, but those *who come together* for good. For see what he said: "If two of you" — that is, of the believing, of the virtuous; for Annas and Caiaphas also agreed,[^2] but blameworthily; so that, on account of this, often when praying we do not receive, because we do not even have concord toward one another.

And he did not say, "I will be"; for he neither delays nor tarries; but, "I am" — that is, straightway I am found there. And you will understand that also, when the flesh and the spirit agree, and the flesh does not lust against the spirit,[^3] then the Lord is in the midst. And the three powers of the soul also agree — the rational, the spirited, and the appetitive.[^4] But also the old and the new *Covenant*[^5] — the two agree, and in the midst of them Christ is found, being proclaimed by both.

[^1]: Greek *συμφωνία* — "concord, harmony, agreement" (literally "sounding together"; the musical metaphor underlies the whole passage, and its verb *συμφωνέω* renders the lemma's "if two of you agree"). Rendered "concord / agree" consistently.
[^2]: Cf. John 18:13-14, 24: Annas and Caiaphas, the high priests, "agreed" against Christ — an agreement for evil, hence "blameworthily" (*ψεκτῶς*).
[^3]: An allusion to Gal. 5:17, *ἡ σὰρξ ἐπιθυμεῖ κατὰ τοῦ πνεύματος* ("the flesh lusts against the spirit").
[^4]: Greek *τὸ λογικόν, τὸ θυμικόν, καὶ τὸ ἐπιθυμητικόν* — the standard Platonic tripartition of the soul: the rational, the spirited (the seat of *θυμός*, anger/spiritedness), and the appetitive (the seat of desire).
[^5]: Greek *ἡ παλαιὰ καὶ ἡ νέα* (OCR *ἡ παλαιὰ ταὶ ἡ ψέα σιλιμφωνοῦσιν*), securely = *ἡ παλαιὰ καὶ ἡ νέα συμφωνοῦσιν* — the feminine adjectives "old" and "new" stand with an unexpressed noun, understood as *διαθήκη* ("covenant / testament"): the Old and New Testaments "agree," and Christ, proclaimed by both, is found between them. The supplied "*Covenant*" is italicized.

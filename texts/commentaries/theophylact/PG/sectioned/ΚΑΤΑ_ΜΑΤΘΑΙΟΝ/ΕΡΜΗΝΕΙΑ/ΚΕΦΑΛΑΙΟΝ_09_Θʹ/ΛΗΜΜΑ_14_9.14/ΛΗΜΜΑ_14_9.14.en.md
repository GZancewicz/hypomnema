# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 14 · Matt. 9:14

> **Then the disciples of John come to him, saying: "Why do we and the Pharisees fast much, but your disciples do not fast?" (Matt. 9:14)**

The disciples of John, envying the glory of Christ, bring a charge against him for not fasting; for they were perhaps at a loss how, without ascetic discipline,[^1] he conquers the passions — which John was not strong enough *to do*; for they were ignorant that John, on the one hand, *was* a mere man,[^2] and righteous out of virtue, but Christ, on the other hand, *was* virtue-itself,[^3] as God.

[^1]: Greek *ἄσκησις*, "ascetic exercise / discipline / training" — the bodily labor of the ascetic life. The disciples of John wondered how Christ mastered the passions without such discipline.
[^2]: Greek *ἄνθρωπος ψιλός*, "a bare / mere man," a plain human being and nothing more — set against Christ, who is God.
[^3]: Greek *αὐτοαρετή*, "virtue-itself" (self-virtue), a coined abstraction: Christ does not become righteous by acquiring virtue, as John did "out of virtue" (*ἐξ ἀρετῆς*), but is Virtue itself in his very being, as God.

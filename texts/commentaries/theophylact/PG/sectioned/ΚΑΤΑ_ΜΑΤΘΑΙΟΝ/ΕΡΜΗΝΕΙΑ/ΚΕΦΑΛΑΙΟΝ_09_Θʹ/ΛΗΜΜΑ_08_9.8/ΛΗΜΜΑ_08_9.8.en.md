# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 8 · Matt. 9:8

> **But the crowds, having seen *it*, marveled,[^1] and glorified God who had given such authority to men. (Matt. 9:8)**

He commanded him to carry the bed, so that it might not seem *to be* an illusion,[^2] and at the same time so that the crowds might see the sign — who considered him, on the one hand, a mere man,[^3] yet greater than all.

[^1]: Greek *ἐθαύμασαν* ("they marveled / were amazed"), the Byzantine reading which Theophylact quotes and expounds; a large part of the manuscript tradition reads instead *ἐφοβήθησαν* ("they were afraid"). Translated as he has it, per the rule of following the text on the page.
[^2]: Greek *ἵνα μὴ φαντασία δόξῃ* — literally "so that it might not be thought *an* appearance/imagination." *φαντασία* is the "appearance to the imagination / phantom, illusion" term (the same word-complex noted at Matt. 1:1, Lemma 1); here the point is that a genuinely healed man carrying his own bed proves the cure was no mere apparition.
[^3]: Greek *ψιλὸν ἄνθρωπον* — "a bare/mere man," i.e. a man and nothing more. The antithesis is *μὲν … μέντοι*: rendered "on the one hand … yet." *κρείττονα πάντων* is "greater/mightier than all," left without a supplied noun as the Greek leaves it.

# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 26 · Matt. 9:27

> **And as Jesus passed on from there, two blind men followed him, crying and saying: Have mercy on us, Son of David. (Matt. 9:27)**

The blind men, on the one hand, as to God said the "Have mercy"; but on the other hand, as to a man, the "Son of David."[^1] For it was commonly noised abroad[^2] among the Jews that the Messiah would come from *the* seed of David.

[^1]: The definite article *τὸ* before each quoted phrase substantivizes it ("the *Have mercy*," "the *Son of David*"), i.e. the very words they uttered. The point of the μὲν … δὲ antithesis: the plea for mercy addresses Christ as God, while the title "Son of David" addresses him as man.
[^2]: Greek *ἐθρυλλεῖτο* (from *θρυλλέω*), "it was continually repeated / commonly talked of / on everyone's lips"; the imperfect denotes an established, ongoing rumor.

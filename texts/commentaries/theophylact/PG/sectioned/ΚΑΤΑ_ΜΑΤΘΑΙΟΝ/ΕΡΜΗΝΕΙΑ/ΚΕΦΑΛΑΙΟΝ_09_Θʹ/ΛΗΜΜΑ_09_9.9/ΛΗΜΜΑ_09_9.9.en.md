# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 9 · Matt. 9:9

> **And Jesus, passing on from there, saw a man sitting at the tax-office,[^1] called Matthew, and says to him: Follow me. And having risen up, he followed him. (Matt. 9:9)**

He did not call this man together with Peter and John, but when he knew *that he* would be persuaded. For thus also he called Paul later, when *it was the* time.[^2] And marvel,[^3] moreover, at the evangelist — how he parades[^4] his own life *for all to see*, although the others concealed his name, calling him Levi; but *it is* God's work, by the[^5]

[^1]: Greek *ἐπὶ τὸ τελώνιον* — the *τελώνιον* is the toll-house / customs-post / tax-office where the *τελώνης* (tax-collector, "publican") sat to collect duties.
[^2]: The OCR here is corrupt: the source reads «ὅτε καιρὸς Χν αὶ / μασον», which is securely recoverable as *ὅτε καιρὸς ἦν. Καὶ θαύμασον* — "when *it was the* time. And marvel" — the sentence-break and the following imperative *θαύμασον* being split across the line ("θαύ-|μασον"). Rendered on that reconstruction.
[^3]: Greek *θαύμασον* is an imperative addressed to the reader ("marvel! / be amazed!"); *δέ* here is continuative, rendered "moreover."
[^4]: Greek *ἐκπομπεύει* — literally "leads *it* out in procession," hence "parades, publishes, makes a public spectacle of." The sense is that Matthew, far from hiding it, openly advertises that he had been a tax-collector; the point turns on the contrast with the other evangelists, who spared him by naming him "Levi" (cf. Mark 2:14; Luke 5:27).
[^5]: The sentence breaks off here mid-clause at *τῷ* ("by the"); it is completed in Lemma 10, which supplies «λόγῳ μόνῳ μεταστρέψαι αὐτόν» ("by *the* word alone to convert him").

# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 21 · Matt. 9:22

> **…Jesus, having turned and having seen her, said: Take courage, daughter, your faith has saved you.[^1] And the woman was made whole from that hour.[^1] (Matt. 9:22)**

Being unclean because of her affliction, the woman did not come forward openly, being wary lest she be hindered; and even though she thought to escape notice, yet she hoped to obtain health, if only she should touch the edge of the garment. For this is the hem.[^2] But the Saviour makes her manifest — not as one desiring glory, but so that he might display her faith for our benefit, and so that the ruler of the synagogue too might be confirmed *in faith*.[^3] And "Take courage," he says to her, because she had been afraid, as one who had stolen the gift; and he calls her daughter, as being faithful. And he shows that, if she had not brought faith to it, she would not have received the grace, even though the garments were holy.

And they say that this woman fashioned a statue of the Saviour, at whose feet there grew up a herb that gave aid to women with a flow of blood — which *statue*, in the times of Julian, the impious men shattered.[^4]

[^1]: The verb *σῴζω* recurs twice, deliberately spanning "save" and "heal": *ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέ σε* ("your faith has saved you") and *ἐσώθη ἡ γυνή* ("the woman was made whole"). Rendered "saved" in the saying (where the theme is faith) and "made whole" in the narrative statement (bodily cure), keeping both facets in view.
[^2]: Greek *τὸ κράσπεδον* — the tasseled fringe or border of the garment (the ritual tassel of Num. 15:38). Theophylact glosses "the edge of the garment" as being precisely "the hem."
[^3]: Greek *ἵνα πιστωθῇ καὶ ὁ ἀρχισυνάγωγος*. Theophylact here calls the father "the ruler of the synagogue" (*ἀρχισυνάγωγος*), the title given him in Mark 5:22 / Luke 8:41, though Matthew's own text calls him only *ἄρχων* ("a ruler," Matt. 9:18) — a harmonizing detail carried over from the parallel accounts. The verb *πιστωθῇ* ("be made to believe / be confirmed in faith") plays on *πίστις* ("faith").
[^4]: The statue at Caesarea Philippi (Paneas), reported by Eusebius, *Church History* 7.18, and said to have been destroyed under the emperor Julian "the Apostate."

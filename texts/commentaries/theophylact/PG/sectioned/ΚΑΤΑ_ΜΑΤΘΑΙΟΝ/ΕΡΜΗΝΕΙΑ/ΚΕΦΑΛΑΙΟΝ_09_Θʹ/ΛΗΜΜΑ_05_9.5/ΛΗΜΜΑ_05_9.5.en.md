# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 9:5

> **… is easier to say, "Your sins have been forgiven you," or to say, "Rise up and walk"? (Matt. 9:5)**[^1]

From the *fact of* knowing their thoughts,[^2] he shows himself *to be* God. He refutes them, then, saying such things as these: "You, on the one hand, consider me a blasphemer, as *one* having promised to forgive sins — which *thing* on the one hand is great; but on the other hand you suppose that I take refuge in this so that I might not be refuted. But I, from the healing of the body, will give assurance also of the *healing* of the soul, confirming — through the *thing* that is on the one hand the lesser, but on the other hand seems the more difficult — also the forgiveness of sins, which on the one hand is great, but on the other hand seems to you the easier because of *its* being unseen."

[^1]: This lemma opens with the completion of the scripture quotation broken off at the end of Lemma 4 («Τί γάρ» + «ἐστιν εὐκοπώτερον…»). The OCR «Ἔγειοαι» is securely *Ἔγειραι* ("Rise up"). The perfect «Ἀφέωνται» is again rendered "have been forgiven" (cf. Lemma 3).
[^2]: A stray OCR line, «ΛΑΡΟΤ ΓΚ.», intrudes at this point in the source between "God" and the following sentence; it is a page/column running-header artifact of the Migne print, not part of the text, and is omitted.

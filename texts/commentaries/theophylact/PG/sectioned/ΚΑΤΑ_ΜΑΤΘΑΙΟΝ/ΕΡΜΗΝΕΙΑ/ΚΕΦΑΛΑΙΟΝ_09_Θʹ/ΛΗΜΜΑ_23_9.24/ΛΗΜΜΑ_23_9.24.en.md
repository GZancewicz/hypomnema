# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 23 · Matt. 9:24

> **…he says to them: Withdraw, for the girl did not die, but sleeps. And they laughed him to scorn.[^1] (Matt. 9:24)**

Since she was unmarried, they were mourning her with the marriage-flutes, doing this contrary to the law.[^2] And he says that she sleeps, because with him death was sleep, since he was able easily to raise her up. And if they laugh him to scorn, do not wonder;[^3] for they rather bear witness to the miracle, as of one who raised her who was in truth dead. For, lest anyone should say that she had merely fallen into a trance,[^4] on account of this it was confessed by all that she had died.

[^1]: This lemma opens mid-sentence, completing the clause left hanging in Lemma 22 ("And Jesus, having come into the house of the ruler…"). Greek *τὸ κοράσιον* — "the little girl / maiden," rendered "the girl."
[^2]: Greek «μετὰ τῶν γαμικῶν αὐλῶν αὐτὴν ἐθρήνουν, παρὰ τὸν νόμον τοῦτο ποιοῦντες». The point turns on *γαμικῶν αὐλῶν*, the "wedding flutes": to lament an unmarried girl with the flutes of a marriage-feast was itself out of place, "contrary to the law/custom" (*παρὰ τὸν νόμον*). *νόμος* here may mean established custom or usage rather than the Mosaic Law strictly.
[^3]: OCR: *μὴ αυμᾶσης* → **μὴ θαυμάσῃς** ("do not wonder / be not astonished"), securely recoverable.
[^4]: Greek *κάτοχος* — one "held fast," i.e. in a cataleptic swoon or coma, not truly dead. The mockers' laughter unwittingly guarantees that she was really dead and not merely comatose, so that the raising cannot be explained away. Rendered "fallen into a trance"; alternatively "become comatose / seized *by catalepsy*."

# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 16 · Matt. 9:16

> **But no one puts a patch of unfulled cloth[^1] upon an old garment, for its filling[^2] takes away from the garment, and a worse tear comes to be. Nor[^3] (Matt. 9:16)**

*This lemma carries only the Gospel text, which breaks off at "Nor"; Theophylact's comment on the two similes (the patch and the wineskins) is given together under the following lemma.*

[^1]: Greek *ἐπίβλημα ῥάκους ἀγνάφου*, "a patch of unfulled rag/cloth." *ἄγναφος* is new cloth not yet fulled (i.e. not yet shrunk and dressed); sewn onto old cloth it shrinks and rends the garment.
[^2]: Greek *αἴρει γὰρ τὸ πλήρωμα αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ τοῦ ἱματίου*, "for its filling/fullness takes away from the garment" — *τὸ πλήρωμα* is the new patch itself, that which fills the hole; being new, it pulls away from the old cloth. (OCR *σκίσαα* is securely *σχίσμα*, "tear, rent.")
[^3]: The sentence breaks off at «Οὐδὲ» ("Nor…"); it is completed at the head of the next lemma ("…do they put new wine into old wineskins").

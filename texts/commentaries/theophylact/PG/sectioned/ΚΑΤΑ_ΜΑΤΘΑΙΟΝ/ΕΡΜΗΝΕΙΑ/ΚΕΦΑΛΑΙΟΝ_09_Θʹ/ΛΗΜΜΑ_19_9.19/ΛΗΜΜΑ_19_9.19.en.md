# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 19 · Matt. 9:19

> **…*and* Jesus, having arisen, followed him, and his disciples *did*.[^1] (Matt. 9:19)**

This *man* appears to have faith, even if not great; for he entreats Jesus not to speak by word only, but also to come and to lay on *his* hand. But he says that the daughter has died, although Luke says that she had not yet died[^2] — either as conjecturing *it*, because he had left her at *her* last breaths, or *else* magnifying the misfortune, so as to draw Christ to pity.

[^1]: This lemma opens mid-sentence, continuing the previous lemma (the ruler's request, «Ἡ θυγάτηρ μου ἄρτι ἐτελεύτησεν…»). The Greek here is «ἐγερθεὶς ὁ Ἰησοῦς ἠκολούθησεν αὐτῷ, καὶ οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ». The verb *ἠκολούθησεν* ("followed") is singular, governed by "Jesus"; the closing clause "and his disciples" carries no verb of its own, so a form of "did/followed" is supplied.
[^2]: Greek «καίτοι τοῦ Λουκᾶ λέγοντος ὡς οὔπω τετελεύτηκεν». A harmonization of the Gospels: in Luke (8:42, 49) the girl is at first only dying, and word of her death arrives later, whereas in Matthew the father says outright that she has just died (*ἄρτι ἐτελεύτησεν*). Theophylact offers two reconciliations, both kept in the body: the father either *guesses* she is dead (having left her drawing her last breaths) or deliberately *heightens* the calamity to move Christ.

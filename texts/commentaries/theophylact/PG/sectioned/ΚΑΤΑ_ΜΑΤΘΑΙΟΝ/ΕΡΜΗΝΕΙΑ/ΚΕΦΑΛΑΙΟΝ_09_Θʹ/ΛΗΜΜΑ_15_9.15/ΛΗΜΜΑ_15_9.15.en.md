# Matthew 9 (Θʹ) — Lemma 15 · Matt. 9:15

> **Jesus therefore said to them: "Can the sons of the bridechamber[^1] mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them? But days will come when the bridegroom shall be taken away from them, and then they will fast." (Matt. 9:15)**

The present season, he says, is *one* of joy, as long as I am with the disciples. For he names himself bridegroom, as betrothing[^2] to himself the new congregation,[^3] the old *one* having died; and *by* "sons of the bridechamber," the apostles. There will be, then, a season, he says, when, I having suffered and been taken up, they will fast, being persecuted in hunger and thirst. And, showing the imperfection of the disciples, he adds:

[^1]: Greek *οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ νυμφῶνος*, "the sons of the bridechamber," a Semitic idiom for the bridegroom's wedding companions or guests. Theophylact takes it below of the apostles.
[^2]: Greek *ἀρμοζόμενον*, "joining / fitting to himself," a verb used of betrothal: Christ espouses the new congregation to himself as a bride.
[^3]: Greek *νέαν συναγωγήν*, literally "new synagogue / assembly." *συναγωγή* is used here in its general sense of "gathering, congregation," set against the "old *one*" (*τῆς παλαιᾶς*) that has died; rendered "congregation" to avoid the narrowly Jewish institutional sense.

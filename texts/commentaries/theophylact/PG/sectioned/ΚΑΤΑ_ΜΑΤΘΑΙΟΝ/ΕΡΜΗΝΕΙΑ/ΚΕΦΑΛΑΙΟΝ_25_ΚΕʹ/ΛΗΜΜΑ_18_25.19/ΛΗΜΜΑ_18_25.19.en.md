# Matthew 25 (ΚΕʹ) — Lemma 18 · Matt. 25:19

> **a long time, the lord of those servants comes, and settles accounts with them.**[^1]

Having said that "you do not know the day when the Lord will come," he adds also this parable, showing that he will come suddenly; for as if a man going abroad, so indeed also the Lord called his own servants, and appointed to them this and that.[^2] Christ, therefore—he who for our sake became man—is said "to go abroad" either in that he ascended into the heavens, or in that he is long-suffering and does not immediately demand the work, but waits. But his servants are those entrusted with the ministry of the word, such as bishops,[^3] priests, deacons, and those who have received spiritual gifts—some greater, others lesser, each according to his own ability, that is, according to the measure of faith and of purification. For in whatever vessel I shall present to God, into that same one he will pour his gift:[^4] if I present a small *vessel*, *he gives* little; if a great *one*, much.

And immediately he who received the five talents, having gone away, worked. See his readiness,[^5] how he neglected nothing, but immediately worked, doubling what he received. For he who benefits not only himself—whether he have the word *of teaching*, or wealth, or power with kings, or any other ability or skill whatsoever—but wishes to benefit others also, such a one doubles what was given to him. But he who buried the talent is he who takes thought for his own benefit only, and not also for that of others;[^6] *such a one* is ever *idle* and is condemned. But also, if *there be* some naturally gifted and capable man who has misused his natural gift for *worldly* business dealings and cunning and earthly affairs, say that such a one has buried the talent in the earth—that is, in the affairs of the earth. And after a long time comes the one who gave the silver—or *rather* his oracles;[^7] for the oracles of God are "silver refined by fire,"[^8] or, simply, any gift whatsoever that makes its possessor bright and glorious might be called "silver." And he settles accounts, demanding a reckoning and calling to account those who received *them*.[^9]

[^1]: This lemma completes the sentence begun in Lemma 17 ("...But after [Lemma 18:] a long time, the lord of those servants comes..."). Greek «…ἔρχεται ὁ κύριος τῶν δούλων ἐκείνων, καὶ συναίρει μετ' αὐτῶν λόγον». The idiom «συναίρει λόγον» = "settles / reckons accounts."
[^2]: Greek «τάδε καὶ τάδε αὐτοῖς διέθετο» — "he appointed to them this-and-that (such-and-such)"; «διατίθεμαι» = "arrange, dispose, assign."
[^3]: Greek «ἀρχιερεῖς» (OCR «ἀρχιαρεῖς»), literally "chief-priests / high-priests." In the fixed threefold order of Byzantine clergy (ἀρχιερεῖς, ἱερεῖς, διάκονοι) the word denotes bishops (hierarchs), the order to which Theophylact himself belonged; rendered "bishops" to give the referent, not the Old-Testament/Jewish high priest that "high priest" would evoke in English.
[^4]: Greek «Ἐν γὰρ τῷ ἀγγείῳ ᾧ δώσω ἐγὼ τῷ Θεῷ, ἐν ἐκείνῳ ἐπιβαλεῖ μοι τὴν δωρεὰν αὐτοῦ» — lit. "For in the vessel which I shall give to God, in that one he will lay upon me his gift." The image: the measure / receptacle one offers to God is the measure in which God pours back his grace. Rendered "present" for «δώσω» to keep the metaphor legible; «ἐπιβαλεῖ» = "will impose / bestow / pour in."
[^5]: Greek «ἐντρέχεια» — "adroitness, quickness, readiness, diligence."
[^6]: The OCR is corrupt here: «καὶ μὴ καὶ τῆς τῶν Καλων ἂε καὶ κατακρίνεται». Best reconstruction «καὶ μὴ καὶ τῆς τῶν [ἄλλων· ὁ τοιοῦτος ἀργὸς] ἀεὶ καὶ κατακρίνεται» ("and not also for that of others; such a one, ever idle, is also condemned"). «Καλων» is read as «ἄλλων» ("of others") and «ἂε» as «ἀεὶ» ("ever"); the words rendered "such a one" and "idle" are conjectural supply, marked italic. The sense is secure even where the exact words are not.
[^7]: The OCR runs «ὁ δούς» together as one word, «ὁδοὺς». Read «ἔρχεται ὁ δοὺς τὸ ἀργύριον» = "the one who gave the silver comes" (ὁ δούς, aorist participle of δίδωμι): the master who entrusted the silver returns to reckon. The gloss «ἦ τὰ λόγια αὐτοῦ» ("or his oracles") identifies the silver with God's oracles (see the citation that follows). The verb of demanding, «ἀπαιτῶν», belongs to the next sentence, «συναίρει λόγον ἀπαιτῶν…».
[^8]: Greek «ἀργύριον πεπυρωμένον» — an allusion to Ps. 11:7 LXX (12:6 Heb.): «τὰ λόγια Κυρίου λόγια ἁγνά, ἀργύριον πεπυρωμένον δοκίμιον τῇ γῇ» ("the oracles of the Lord are pure oracles, silver tried by fire"). «πεπυρωμένον» = "refined / tried by fire."
[^9]: Greek «συναίρει λόγον ἀπαιτῶν καὶ λογοπραγῶν τοὺς λαβόντας»; «λογοπραγέω» = "to exact an account, call to reckoning." The final two OCR lines of the source file («θΙΦ οΩΙ οὰ οἰαιωμὴ…») are unrecoverable print-noise (a running header / Latin footer) and are not translated.

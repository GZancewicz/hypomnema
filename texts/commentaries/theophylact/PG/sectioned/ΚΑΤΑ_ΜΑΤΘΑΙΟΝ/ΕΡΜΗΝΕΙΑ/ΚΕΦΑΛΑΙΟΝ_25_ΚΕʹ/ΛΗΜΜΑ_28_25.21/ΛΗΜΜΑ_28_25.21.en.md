# Matthew 25 (ΚΕʹ) — Lemma 28 · Matt. 25:31–33

> **...he shall set the sheep, on the one hand, at his right, but the kids on the left.**[^1] (Matt. 25:33)

Since the first coming of the Lord came about in dishonor and outrage,[^2] he says the *phrase*, "When he shall come in his glory." For in the second *coming* he shall come with glory, angels attending him as *his* bodyguard.[^3] But first he separates the holy *ones* from the sinners, freeing them from anxiety, and sets *them in place*, and then he speaks to them. Sheep, on the one hand, therefore, he calls the holy *ones*, on account of *their* gentleness, and because they give us fruit and benefit like the sheep — both supplying wool, that is, a divine and spiritual covering, and milk, that is, fitting nourishment; but goats,[^4] *he calls* the sinners; for they walk along precipices, and they are disorderly and unfruitful like the goats.

[^1]: Greek «στήσει τὰ μὲν πρόβατα ἐκ δεξιῶν αὐτοῦ, τὰ δὲ ἐρίφια ἐξ εὐωνύμων.» The *μὲν … δὲ* construction is rendered "on the one hand … but." *ἐρίφια* is the diminutive, "kids / young goats," whereas the shepherd-image just above (Lemma 27) and the exposition below use *ἔριφοι* ("goats"); the referent is the same, and the plain "goats" is retained in the exposition. *ἐξ εὐωνύμων* literally "from the left-hand *parts*"; *εὐώνυμος* ("of good name") is the euphemistic Greek term for the ill-omened left side. This lemma completes the scripture quotation running from Lemma 25.
[^2]: The word rendered "outrage" is OCR-garbled in the source as «βόρει»; it is best recovered as **ὕβρει** (dat. of *ὕβρις*, "insolent outrage, humiliation"), pairing naturally with *ἀτιμίᾳ* ("dishonor"): "in dishonor and outrage." Greek «ἐν ἀτιμίᾳ γέγονε καὶ [ὕβρει?]». Marked as uncertain, though the sense ("lowliness/humiliation") is secure whatever the exact noun.
[^3]: Greek *δορυφορούντων* — from *δορυφόρος*, a spear-bearing bodyguard; the participle pictures the angels forming an armed escort round Christ. Rendered "attending him as *his* bodyguard" to keep the martial image.
[^4]: Greek *ἐρίφους* (acc. pl. of *ἔριφος*, "goat"); the object "the sinners" (*τοὺς ἁμαρτωλούς*) follows, with "*he calls*" supplied by parallel from "he calls the holy ones" above.

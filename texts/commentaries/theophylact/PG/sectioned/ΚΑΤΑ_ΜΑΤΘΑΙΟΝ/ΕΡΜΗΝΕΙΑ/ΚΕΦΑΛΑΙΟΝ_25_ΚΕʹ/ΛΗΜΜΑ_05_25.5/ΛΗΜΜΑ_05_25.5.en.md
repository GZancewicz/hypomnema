# Matthew 25 (ΚΕʹ) — Lemma 5 · Matt. 25:5

> **But the bridegroom tarrying, they all slumbered and slept.[^1] (Matt. 25:5)**

Under the person of the virgins[^2] he frames the parable concerning almsgiving, in order that — since it is a great matter[^3] — someone succeeding in this alone might not neglect the others; but learn that, apart from almsgiving, even if you should have virginity, you shall be cast out with the fornicators. And reasonably is the unsympathetic and unmerciful *one* cast out, even should he be a virgin; for the fornicator, on the one hand, was overcome by a passion both tyrannical and natural, but the unmerciful *one*, on the other hand, by *a passion* for money. By how much, therefore, the adversary[^4] *is* the weaker, by so much *the more* unpardonable *is* he who has been conquered by the passion of love-of-money; and such a one *is* foolish, moreover, for this very *reason* — that he conquered, on the one hand, natural burning,[^5] but was overcome, on the other hand, by the small passion of money. And the slumbering[^6] *is* death; and the tarrying of the bridegroom *is* the second coming's not happening speedily.

[^1]: Greek *Χρονίζοντος δὲ τοῦ νυμφίου, ἐνύσταξαν πᾶσαι καὶ ἐκάθευδον* — a genitive absolute, "the bridegroom tarrying (delaying)," kept as a participial phrase; *ἐνύσταξαν* ("they slumbered / nodded off") and *ἐκάθευδον* ("they slept," imperfect) are distinguished, the pair picked up in the exposition (*νυσταγμός* = "slumbering").
[^2]: Greek *Ἐπὶ τοῦ προσώπου τῶν παρθένων* — "upon/under the person (*πρόσωπον*, face, character, dramatic role) of the virgins." The idiom is theatrical: he casts the lesson "in the character of" the virgins.
[^3]: Greek *μέγα πρᾶγμά ἐστι* ("it is a great matter") — the "it" is virginity, the great achievement of which one must not rest content; supplied from the context.
[^4]: Greek *ἀνταγωνιστής* — "adversary, opponent, rival contestant," here the opposing passion one wrestles against (the athletic/agonistic image): the weaker the opponent, the less excusable defeat by it. Love-of-money (*φιλοχρηματία*) is the weaker adversary than natural desire.
[^5]: Greek *φυσικὴ πύρωσις* — "natural burning / inflammation," the standard term for the heat of sexual desire (cf. *πυροῦσθαι*, 1 Cor. 7:9). Rendered "burning" to preserve the fire-image against which virginity is measured.
[^6]: Greek *νυσταγμός* ("slumbering, drowsiness"), resuming *ἐνύσταξαν* from the lemma; *χρονισμός* ("tarrying, delay"), resuming *χρονίζοντος*. Theophylact glosses each term of the verse allegorically: the drowsiness = death, the bridegroom's delay = the deferral of the Second Coming. The OCR *λρονισμὸς* is securely *χρονισμός*.

# Matthew 25 (ΚΕʹ) — Lemma 24 · Matt. 25:28–30

> **and the unprofitable servant cast out into the outer darkness; there shall be the weeping and the gnashing of teeth.**[^1] (Matt. 25:30)

The ones, on the one hand, who worked with the things given are praised by the master, both alike, each of them hearing "Well *done*,[^2] good and faithful servant." And by "good" here we understand altogether the one *who is* loving-of-mankind, the ungrudging *one*,[^3] who spreads out his own goodness also to *his* neighbors; and having shown *themselves* faithful over few things, they are set over many. For indeed, even if here we are deemed worthy of gracious gifts,[^4] nevertheless these are nothing in comparison with the good things to come. And the joy of the Lord is the unceasing gladness which God has, rejoicing over his works, as David says.[^5] Such gladness, therefore, do the saints also feel over their works; just as, then, sinners are grieved over their own works and feel remorse, so the saints, having the Lord *as their* wealth, rejoice in him.

But observe that both the one who received the five talents and the one *who received* the two are deemed worthy of the same good things. For whenever someone, even having received few things, nevertheless administers well[^6] the grace given to him — though it be small — he will receive back honor equal to the one deemed worthy of great *things* and *who* accomplished *them*. For each *man*, according to what he received, according to this also is reckoned excellent,[^7] if he accomplishes *it*.

But the well-disposed servants, on the one hand, *are* such; the wicked and slothful *one*, on the other hand, defends himself otherwise, and in a manner worthy of himself. For he names the master "hard," just as even now many of those who teach say: "It is hard to seek obedience from people in whom God did not implant the obedient *disposition*, nor sowed in them the ready-compliance;"[^8] for he hints at this by saying, "You reap where you did not sow" — that is, *that though* you did not sow the ready-compliance, you demand the compliance from that *man*. But the servant, by calling the master "hard," condemns himself. For since he was hard, he ought rather to have taken care, as having a hard and harsh master. For if he demands what belongs to others, much more will he demand what is his own; so that you too ought to have multiplied what you received, and to have made disciples, from whom he would have demanded what was owed. For he names the disciples "bankers," as those who approve or reject the word;[^9] and as interest he demands from the disciples the demonstration of works. For the disciple, receiving the word from the teacher, on the one hand has it himself and renders it back whole; but on the other hand he also gives in addition, *as* interest,[^10] the working of the good.

The gift, therefore, is taken away from the wicked and slothful servant. For the one who has received some gift for the benefiting of others, and has not used it, loses even it; but the one who has made greater diligence draws to himself also the greater bounty. For to the one having diligence more grace shall be given, and he shall have abundance; but from the one not having diligence, even the gift which he seems to have shall be taken away. For the one not having diligence, nor having worked further upon the things he received, does not have the gift, but *only* seems to have *it*; for he has darkened it[^11] through negligence.

[^1]: The lemma completes the sentence begun at the end of lemma 23 (*καὶ τὸν* …): *καὶ τὸν ἀχρεῖον δοῦλον ἐκβάλλετε εἰς τὸ σκότος τὸ ἐξώτερον* ("and cast the unprofitable servant into the outer darkness"). *ἀχρεῖον* = "useless, unprofitable." Minor OCR slips silently corrected: *ἀκεῖ* → *ἐκεῖ*, *ἕσται* → *ἔσται*.
[^2]: Greek *Εὖ* — the bare adverb "well"; "*done*" supplied (see lemmata 19–20).
[^3]: Theophylact glosses *ἀγαθόν* ("good") with two terms: *τὸν φιλάνθρωπον* ("the man-loving / benevolent one") and *τὸν ἄφθονον* ("the ungrudging, unstinting, unenvious one"), the *one* "who spreads out (*ἐφαπλοῖ*) his own goodness also to his neighbors."
[^4]: Greek *χαρισμάτων* — "gifts of grace, charismata." *πρὸς τὰ μέλλοντα ἀγαθά* rendered "in comparison with the good things to come."
[^5]: An allusion to Ps. 103:31 LXX (104:31 MT), *εὐφρανθήσεται Κύριος ἐπὶ τοῖς ἔργοις αὐτοῦ* ("The Lord shall be glad in his works"). Greek *ἄληκτος εὐφροσύνη* = "unceasing / never-ending gladness."
[^6]: Greek *οἰκονομήσῃ* — "manage, administer, dispense as a steward." The metaphor of stewardship is retained in "administers."
[^7]: Greek *ἄκρος δοκεῖ* — "is deemed / reckoned topmost (consummate, excellent)." *δοκεῖ* here "is accounted," not "seems falsely."
[^8]: Greek *τὸ ὑπήκοον* ("the obedient *disposition*") and *τὸ εὐπειθές* ("the ready-obedience, docility, compliance"); Theophylact plays the two near-synonyms against the parable's "sowing." The rendering keeps both as substantivized neuters ("the obedient *disposition*" / "the ready-compliance").
[^9]: Greek *δοκιμάζοντας ἢ ἀποδοκιμάζοντας* — "approving/testing or rejecting/disapproving," a play on the pair *δοκιμάζω / ἀποδοκιμάζω*.
[^10]: The OCR reads *προσδίδωσι δὲ καὶ πότον τὴν ἐργασίαν τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ*; *πότον* is a garble for *τόκον* ("interest"), matching the just-stated equation of the "interest" with the "working of the good" (*τὴν ἐργασίαν τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ*). Rendered "gives in addition, *as* interest, the working of the good."
[^11]: Greek *ἠμαύρωσε* — "darkened, dimmed, made faint / obscured." The verb echoes the parable's burial of the talent in the earth. *ἀμελείας* = "negligence, carelessness."

# Matthew 25 (ΚΕʹ) — Lemma 12 · Matt. 25:13

> **Watch therefore, for you do not know the day nor the hour in which the Son of man comes. (Matt. 25:13)**

At midnight[^1] he says that a cry arose, in order that he might show that the Lord comes unlooked-for, just as, about midnight, we are all already sleeping in deep sleep. But he comes with a cry; for a trumpet will sound at the second coming. And *the* lamps — both our souls and the mind of each one is a lamp[^2] — which is then kindled, when it has the oil of the virtues and mercy.[^3] But the virgins are foolish indeed in this respect also, in that they then sought the oil, seeing that it is no longer a season for trafficking.[^4]

But the wise say: Lest it not suffice for us and for you. For the virtue of my neighbour will scarcely suffice for that one himself for a defense,[^5] much less also for me; for each will be justified out of his own works, not out of those of *his* neighbour. But the foolish go away to those who sell, that is, to the poor. And what he says is something of this sort: [they repent of not having shown][^6] mercy, and now for the first time they know that it is from the poor that we must obtain the oil. This, therefore, is what he says — that they went away to those who sell to buy oil; that is, they went away in *their* mind to the poor, and reckoned what a good thing mercy *is*; but indeed the door was shut to them, for there is no time for repentance and for working after the departure from this life.[^7] The Lord, at any rate, says to them: I do not know you. For he who is loving-of-mankind and merciful does not know the unmerciful *women*. For how *could he know* those *who are* alien to him and unlike *him*?

But know that every soul also received a lamp and illumination from God, and all are raised up for a meeting with the Lord; for all desire to meet and to be joined to God. But, God having given illumination and a lamp, the wise on the one hand, through well-doing, pour on oil; but the foolish on the other hand, leaving their lamps without oil, are shut out, not having good works through which the light in them will be kindled; for if we do not work the good things, we have quenched the light of God *that is* in us.

[^1]: Greek *μεσούσης τῆς νυκτὸς*, a genitive absolute, "the night being at its middle." *ἀνέλπιστος* (of the Lord) = "unhoped-for, unlooked-for, unexpected"; rendered adjectivally with "comes unlooked-for."
[^2]: The clause *καὶ ὁ νοῦς ἑκάστου λαμπάς ἐστιν* is grammatically singular ("the mind of each one is a lamp"), loosely apposed to the plural "lamps … our souls" that opens the sentence; the anacoluthon is preserved.
[^3]: Greek *ἐλεημοσύνη* spans "mercy / compassion / almsgiving"; here paired with "the oil of the virtues," and below made explicit as giving to the poor, so it shades toward "almsgiving." Rendered "mercy" for consistency with *ἐλεήμων* / *ἀνελεήμων* ("merciful" / "unmerciful") below, where the wordplay turns on the same root.
[^4]: Greek *πραγματεία* = "business, trading, transaction"; the metaphor is commercial (buying oil), so "trafficking / trading." *ἅτε* = "seeing that, inasmuch as."
[^5]: Greek *πρὸς ἀπολογίαν* (OCR *πρὸξ ἀσολογίαν*) = "for a defense / an accounting"; *μήτι γε* = "much less, let alone."
[^6]: The OCR has a lacuna: *Ὁ δὲ λέγει τοιοῦτόν […] ἐλεημοσύνην*. Words are lost between "something of this sort" and "mercy/almsgiving." The bracketed "[they repent of not having shown]" is a supplied conjecture to bridge the gap, given the drift of the sentence (the foolish only now learn that the oil is to be had from the poor). The surviving word ἐλεημοσύνην is accusative, so it is the object of a transitive verb of *giving/showing* almsgiving (which governs the accusative), not of "neglecting" (ἀμελέω, which would take the genitive); and μεταμέλεια ("repentance") recurs a few lines below, favouring a verb of repenting here. Greek as printed: «Ὁ δὲ λέγει τοιοῦτόν … ἐλεημοσύνην, καὶ νῦν πρῶτον γινώσκουσιν ὅτι ἀπὸ τῶν πενήτων δεῖ ἡμᾶς τὸ ἔλαιον ἔχειν.»
[^7]: Greek *μεταμελείας … καὶ ἐργασίας καιρὸς οὐκ ἔστι μετὰ τὴν ἐνθένδε ἀποβίωσιν* — "there is no season for repentance and for working after the departure hence from life." *ἀποβίωσις* = "departure from life, death"; *ἐνθένδε* = "from here, hence."
